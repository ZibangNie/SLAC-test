A cardinal invariant related to homogeneous families

Martin Goldstern Saharon Shelah

Feb 23, 1997

Definition 1 We call a family F ⊆ [ω] ω independent if every boolean intersection
A∈F0 A ∩ B∈F1 (ω \ B) F 0 , F 1 ⊆ F , disjoint, finite is infinite.
We call F homogeneous iff: for every finite partial injective functions f : ω → ω and g : F → F that satisfies
∀n ∈ dom(f ) ∀A ∈ dom(g) : n ∈ A ⇔ f (n) ∈ g(A)
there is a permutation π of ω such that π[A], π −1 [A] ∈ F for all A ∈ F , π extends f and π[A] = g(A) for all A ∈ dom(g).

Explanation, motivation... Definition 2 Let ex = min{|F | : F is an independent family on ω that cannot be extended to a homogeneous family.

It is easy to see that ex ≥ cov(M) (see below). (This was also pointed out by Brendle).

We show here that in fact ex = cov(M). We will use the following characterisation of cov(M):


Definition and Fact 3

• If A ⊆ ω ω , and g ∈ ω ω , we say that g "diagonalizes" F if ∀f ∈ F ∃ ∞ n f (n) = g(n).

• By a theorem of Bartoszynski, κ < cov(M) iff every family F ⊆ ω ω of size κ can be diagonalized. * The first author was supported by an Erwin Schroedinger fellowship from the Austrian Science Foundation. † Publication E14.

• If there is no g which diagonalizes F , |F | = κ then we say that F "witnesses" cov(M) ≤ κ.

Definition 4 Define κ < ex * iff: For any independent family A ⊆ [ω] ω of cardinality κ there is a permutation π : ω → ω such that for all A ∈ A the set
{n ∈ A : π(n) ∈ A \ {n}} is infinite.
We will show
(1) ex ≤ ex * (2) ex * ≤ cov(M) (3) cov(M) ≤ ex
(1) and (3) are easy.
