Cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning With Approximate Model Learning

In multi-agent reinforcement learning, it is essential for agents to learn communication protocol to optimize collaboration policies and to solve unstable learning problems. Existing methods based on actor-critic networks solve the communication problem among agents. However, these methods have difficulty in improving sample efficiency and learning robust policies because it is not easy to understand the dynamics and nonstationary of the environment as the policies of other agents change. We propose a method for learning cooperative policies in multi-agent environments by considering the communications among agents. The proposed method consists of recurrent neural network-based actor-critic networks and deterministic policy gradients to centrally train decentralized policies. The actor networks cause the agents to communicate using forward and backward paths and to determine subsequent actions. The critic network helps to train the actor networks by sending gradient signals to the actors according to their contribution to the global reward. To address issues with partial observability and unstable learning, we propose using auxiliary prediction networks to approximate state transitions and the reward function. We used multi-agent environments to demonstrate the usefulness and superiority of the proposed method by comparing it with existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods, in terms of both learning efficiency and goal achievements in the test phase. The results demonstrate that the proposed method outperformed other alternatives.INDEX TERMS Reinforcement learning, model-free method, multi-agent system, multi-agent cooperation, actor-critic method, deterministic policy gradient.

I. INTRODUCTION

Recently, reinforcement learning algorithms have garnered attention with their ability to accomplish a wide variety of tasks, such as game playing [26], [33], complex continuous control tasks involving locomotion [22], and those in industrial applications [21]. The majority of the success in reinforcement learning has been in single-agent environments [24]. However, many real-world problems require multiple agents to interact and cooperate. Cooperative multiagent systems can naturally model many complex problems, including the coordination of autonomous vehicles [5], network packet delivery [44], distributed logistics [45], the control of multiple robots [20], [25], and multiplayer games [16]. Therefore, multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms The associate editor coordinating the review of this manuscript and approving it for publication was Sungroh Yoon .

have been actively studied to enable agents to collaborate and achieve common goals [4].

In some multi-agent systems, certain modifications to the single-agent reinforcement learning methods can be used directly [14]. One of the most straightforward approaches is to train several agents independently to maximize their reward and treat others as part of the environment [36], [37]. However, this approach violates the underlying assumption in reinforcement learning that the environment should be stationary and Markovian. In this case, the environment is dynamic and nonstationary because of the changing policies of other agents [15]. Another basic approach is to design multiple agents into a single agent that becomes the joint action space for all the agents [12]. While this method enables the coordinated behavior of multiple agents, it is not scalable because the size of the action space exponentially increases with the increasing number of agents. The model involving the centralized training of decentralized policies has created considerable demand in the efficient training of multiple agents [19], [29]. This model can address the challenge of non-Markovian and nonstationary environments during learning [15] and can access the additional state information of other agents while promoting communication [31]. The decentralized policies also address the issue of partial observability because they are only conditioned on the local action and observation history of each agent. As a result, in the test phase, an agent only receives information based on its observations and can thus execute an action without one being initiated by other agents. This centralized training of decentralized policies has recently attracted attention from the multi-agent reinforcement learning community [13], [15], [24], [31]. However, many challenges remain in how to best exploit centralized training. In the recent paradigm of centralized training of decentralized execution, multi-head attention mechanisms are applied to integrate global rewards and train well-coordinated decentralized policies. During centralized training, joint actions and additional state information are accessed to provide an update signal to the decentralized policies. Subsequently, during decentralized execution, the importance of each agent is correctly verified to approximate a global reward [43].

Another challenge is with the multi-agent credit assignment [9]. In cooperative settings, joint actions usually only generate global rewards, making it difficult for each agent to estimate its own contribution to the team's success. In some cases, it is possible to design individual reward functions for each agent using human knowledge. However, in cooperative settings, these rewards are generally not available to encourage individual agents to sacrifice for the greater good [6]. A centralized action-value function can capture the effects of the agents' actions based on the global state and joint actions. However, such a function is difficult to train as the number of agents increases. Even when it can be learned, it offers no obvious way to extract decentralized policies that allow each agent to select a single individual action based on individual observation [31].

In this study, we propose an actor-critic method for learning policies in a cooperative multi-agent environment, while simultaneously considering the communication among agents. To learn multiple policies capable of communication, we propose utilizing recurrent actor-critic networks trained by deterministic policy gradients. The proposed method can help to preserve the assumption of a stationary and Markovian environment because the gradient flows allow centralized training of the decentralized policies for the recurrent actor-critic networks. Moreover, to increase the robustness of the learning and promote its full utilization, we propose an auxiliary predictive network that can be readily adapted to model-free methods for approximate model learning. This allows the agents to be well-trained even in environments with partial observability.

The main contributions of this study can be summarized as follows:

1) We propose multi-agent deterministic policy gradients with recurrent layers to handle cooperative multi-agent tasks. Recurrent layers help agents to not only learn differentiable communication protocols but also train based on the global reward while alleviating multi-agent credit assignment. We hypothesize that if the recurrent critic can assess the actor network by considering the contribution of each agent, then the deterministic policy gradient has trained each agent appropriately through the backward path. We demonstrate this hypothesis through experiments.

2) We propose using the auxiliary prediction network to address the issues with partial observability and the variance increase of the learned policy. The auxiliary prediction network can estimate approximate state transitions and the reward function to improve utilization and robustness. Moreover, the auxiliary prediction network can be straightforwardly combined with model-free reinforcement learning methods, without any assumptions about the environment.


II. BACKGROUND

In this study, we consider a fully cooperative multi-agent task with both multiple agents and partial observability that can be described as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (Dec-POMDP), called partially observable Markov games G. Multi-agents cooperate to solve a given task by receiving local observations and individually selecting and executing actions over a sequence of time steps. Execution is decentralized because each agent must select its own action at each time step, using information from other agents. The Dec-POMDP for N agents is defined by a set of states S describing the possible configurations of all agents. In a multi-agent framework, each agent receives a private observation associated with s. Each agent a ∈ A ≡ {1, . . . , N } simultaneously chooses an action u a ∈ U in each time step to form a joint action u ∈ U ≡ U N . Agents all share the same reward function r (s, u) : S × U → R, and the discount factor γ ∈ [0, 1), the hyperparameter of the reward function, determines the degree to which the policy favors immediate reward over long-term benefits [1], [29].

We consider a partially observable scenario in which each agent can obtain individual observations z ∈ Z from the observation O (s, a) : S × A → Z . Each agent has an action-observation history τ a ∈ T ≡ (Z × U ) * based on a stochastic policy π a (u a | τ a ) : T × U → [0, 1] . The joint policy π has a Q π (s t , u t ) = E s t+1:∞ ,u t+1:∞ [R t |s t , u t ] joint action-value function where R t = ∞ i=0 γ i r t+i is the discounted return. In this scenario, the agents learn the policy aimed at maximizing their expected discounted returns.


A. DEEP Q-LEARNING

Deep Q-learning [26] is one of the most popular methods in reinforcement learning and has been applied to multi-agent settings. Q-learning uses an action-value function for policy π , denoted as Q π (s, u). The action-value function can be recursively rewritten as Q π (s, u) = E s r (s, u) + γ E u ∼π Q π s , u using the Bellman equation. The state s is observed after taking the action u and receiving a reward r. In deep Q-learning, an action-value function is a deep neural network, parameterized by θ . It is trained by sampling batches of transitions from the replay memory and minimizing the loss as follows:
L (θ) = E s,a,r,s y DQN − Q (s, u | θ) 2 ,(1)
where the target y DQN = r +γ max u Q (s , u |θ − ). Here, θ − are the parameters of a target network that remains fixed during several iterative updates of the online network Q (s, u|θ ).


B. POLICY GRADIENTS

Policy gradients are another popular method used in a variety of reinforcement learning tasks. This method aims to estimate the gradient of an agent's expected returns to maximize the objective J (θ ) = E s∼p π ,u∼π θ [R] by taking steps in the direction of ∇ θ J (θ ). Using the previously defined Q function, the gradient of the policy can be written as:
∇ θ J (θ) = E s∼p π ,u∼π θ ∇ θ log (π θ (u | s)) Q π (s, u) ,(2)
where p π = ∞ t=0 γ t Pr{s t = s|s 0 , π} is the distribution of states under π , assuming that it is independent of s 0 and exists for all policies [35]. One could learn an approximation of the true action-value function Q π (s, u) through temporal-difference learning by minimizing Equation (1). This Q π (s, u) is called the critic and is the basis for a variety of actor-critic algorithms. In these algorithms, the actor, the policy, is trained by following the gradient that is calculated using the critic. The actor-critic methods can address a high variance in the estimates of policy gradients [32].

Therefore, it is possible to estimate gradients efficiently by replacing the non-differentiable samples, such as argmax, from a discrete distribution with differentiable samples through the Gumbel-Softmax distribution [17], [24].


III. RELATED WORK A. MULTI-AGENT REINFORCEMENT LEARNING FOR COMMUNICATION

One of the crucial components of multi-agent reinforcement learning is having the agents learn a communication protocol to optimize the collaboration policies [10], [27]. Moreover, in any partially observed environment, the agents must determine the communication protocol that can coordinate behaviors and solve the given task [2]. One of the methods for learning the agent's communication protocol is a value-based method that induces communication among agents by passing gradient signals through communication channels [46]. A hierarchical communication network, which was recently introduced in the hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning framework, achieves a communication protocol that extracts the hidden state information of the agents in the communication layer and broadcasts a number of agents [38].

In this study, we focus on actor-critic methods involving centralized learning and decentralized execution, a standard paradigm in multi-agent reinforcement learning problems. The bidirectionally-coordinated networks (BiCNets) are based on actor-critic policy gradient methods using bidirectional recurrent networks to connect each agent's policy and value networks. The agents can learn communication protocol through the recurrent networks [30]. However, this work assumes that agents know the individual rewards for the environment, which is not frequently encountered in practice.

To foster communication among agents, some researchers have studied the use of explicit communication actions that deliver messages to other agents. The multi-agent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) is an extension of a deep deterministic policy gradient algorithm (DDPG) [22]. Similarly, like the DDPG, the MADDPG uses an actor-critic model with feed-forward neural networks, representing policy and action-value functions [24]. However, because the neural networks for all agents are independent, predefined explicit communication actions are required to communicate in the execution phase. In addition, the complexity of training the neural networks increases quadratically as the number of agents increases. The multi-actor-attention critic (MAAC) is an actor-critic method based on counterfactual multi-agent policy gradients that aim to address the issues with multi-agent credit assignment [9]. The main concept of the MAAC is to learn a centralized critic with an attention mechanism by selectively paying attention to the actions of other agents [15]. However, training the fully centralized critic becomes impractical as the number of agents increases. The Q-value path decomposition (QPD) is a value-based method that also aims to address the issues with a multi-agent credit assignment. The QPD method proposes a network that integrates information from each agent into a multi-channel critic network and decomposes the global reward into individual rewards [42]. However, as the number of agents increases, centralized training has difficulties learning the increased agent information and encounters increasing computational costs for Q-value decomposition.

The recently proposed recurrent MADDPG (R-MADDPG) uses recurrent neural networks based on the MADDPG to handle partially observable environments. Much the same as our work, although the authors used recurrent actor-critic networks, their recurrent layers had the purpose of remembering the messages from previous time steps to solve certain domains that are not allowed to communicate every time step. However, we use recurrent layers for the inter-agent communication channel [41].


B. APPROXIMATE MODEL LEARNING

Recent model-based reinforcement learning approaches have promised to substantially improve the sample efficiency of deep reinforcement learning. There have been numerous attempts in model-based reinforcement learning to understand the dynamics of an environment. Reference [3] proposed a method that predicts the subsequent observation and reward, an approach used as a proxy for fully observable MDP dynamics. Reference [8] reported a method that has proven to be highly sample-efficient in environments that improve sample efficiency in the model-based method; [18] proposed a model-based method that improves the sample efficiency by predicting the subsequent states and rewards. However, the optimization processes for these methods have a high computational cost because they are based on modelbased methods. Additionally, these studies aimed to solve single-agent tasks.

A portion of the recent research has investigated predictive models to increase the learning efficiency of learning algorithms on single-agent tasks. Reference [39] proposed a two-step learning of value function decomposition using VAE to predict future expected trajectory and accumulated reward function separately. Reference [23] proposed an energy management system that consisted of the predictive model and deep Q-learning. However, these methods have a high computational cost because the learning processes of the predictive model are separated from the agent training. In contrast, this study's proposed method is based on a model-free method combined with the prediction of the subsequent states and rewards for multi-agent tasks. Therefore, it can not only improve the sample efficiency but also reduce the computational cost, such as the optimization process in multi-agent domains.


IV. PROPOSED METHOD

In this study, we investigated the issues with having multiple agents perceiving and acting in environments, attempting to maximize shared rewards. Learning communication among agents is essential to share the information required to accomplish their tasks. In this section, we derive an algorithm that performs well under the following three constraints:

1. The learned policies can only use the observations of each agent at the execution phase.

2. Agents can understand the dynamics of the environment based on model-free methods. 3. Cooperation can be learned via a communication channel without explicit communication actions. The above considerations lead to a multi-agent learning algorithm that can be applied to cooperative tasks with explicit communication actions as well as to tasks with no explicit communication actions.


A. MULTI-AGENT DETERMINISTIC POLICY GRADIENTS WITH RECURRENT LAYERS

To foster cooperation among the agents, we propose multiagent deterministic policy gradients with recurrent layers (MADR). The proposed method is based on differentiable agent-to-agent learning with a combination of centralized training and decentralized execution. Thus, recurrent layers can be used to generate end-to-end learning among agents through a differentiable communication channel. These settings can share parameters and deliver gradients from one agent to the next using the communication channel.

In this study, we accomplish communication among agents through long short-term memory (LSTM) layers in both the actor and critic networks. In particular, in the actor networks, the LSTM layers are bidirectional to reduce the dependency on the sequences of agents. In the execution phase, the actor network generates actions for each agent using a sequence of observations from each agent. In the training phase, the critic networks estimate the Q-value that evaluates the current states with a sequence of observations of all agents and the actions generated from each agent. The input sequence that contains the agents' information is sequentially accumulated through the recurrent layer, which can be used as a communication channel as well as a local memory saver, allowing each agent to maintain its internal state and to share information with its collaborators.

B. APPROXIMATE MODEL LEARNING USING AUXILIARY PREDICTION NETWORKS (AMLAPN)

Including auxiliary prediction tasks for the learning, environment dynamics can help avoid local optima in complex environments. These tasks can be considered to be linked to two supervised learning problems: the prediction of the next states and the rewards based on the observed state transitions. These predictive tasks are inspired by model-based reinforcement learning algorithms aiming to exploit the dynamics of the environment, such as the state transitions. Although model-based algorithms have the advantage of learning a robust policy and sample efficiency, they are computationally more complex than model-free methods. Thus, we attempted to embed the advantages of model-based algorithms in our model-free algorithm [7], [11], [28]. Figure 1 shows the overall architecture of the proposed method. In our work, the auxiliary next states prediction networks (f a ) reside within the actor networks to help understand the influence of agents' actions within the environment. More specifically, the auxiliary networks predict the next observations of all the agents based on their current observations and actions. Furthermore, the auxiliary rewards prediction networks (f c ) belong to the critic networks to inference the reward function of the environment associated with the states and actions of all agents. Thus, the auxiliary reward prediction networks estimate the reward using the current observations and actions of all the agents. As a result, the actor and critic networks learn the dynamics of the environment more easily than with pure model-free learning algorithms. We use a multi-agent particle environment framework to evaluate the capabilities of the proposed method.

The environment consists of N agents and L landmarks with two-dimensional continuous space and discrete-time. Agents can take both physical and communication actions. Physical actions allow the agents to move throughout the environment, and communication actions consist of sending messages to other agents. To further simplify the control problem, we use five-dimensional discrete action spaces, enabling agents to accelerate along four directions or to stay still. Thus, agents may not be moved by the given discrete action because of their momentum [15], [24]. In our experiments, we consider four cooperative multi-agent scenarios in which all agents should maximize a shared reward. In addition, some scenarios require explicit communication among agents to achieve the best reward, whereas others require agents to only perform physical actions. We provide details for each scenario below.


1) COOPERATIVE NAVIGATION

This scenario consists of three agents and three landmarks. Agents have to cooperate to reach a set of landmarks through physical actions. Agents observe the relative positions of other agents and landmarks and get rewards based on the proximity of each agent to the nearest landmark. In other words, the agents should optimally cover all of the landmarks. Furthermore, the agents occupy significant physical space and are penalized when they collide. Our agents must learn to cover the landmarks while avoiding each other.


2) COOPERATIVE COMMUNICATION

This task consists of two cooperative agents, a speaker, and a listener, which are placed in an environment with three different color landmarks. The listener is tasked with navigating to a landmark of a particular color at each episode, where it receives a reward based on its distance from the correct landmark. While the listener can observe the relative position and color of the landmarks, it does not know which landmark is the correct one. In contrast, the speaker can observe the correct landmark color and then create a communication output that the listener can observe at each step.


3) TREASURE COLLECTION

This scenario includes eight agents, six treasure hunters, and two treasure banks, each of which corresponds to a different color of treasure. The treasure hunters are tasked with collecting a treasure of any color and then depositing it in the bank with the matching color. The role of each bank is to simply gather as much treasure as possible from the hunters. The treasures are randomly generated as they are collected, maintaining a total of six. All agents can see the relative positions of the others and receive a global reward when the treasure is deposited. In addition, the hunters receive a reward when the treasure is successfully collected and are penalized for colliding with each other. However, the agents can only observe shared rewards, which are the sum of the individual rewards. Consequently, the task contains a mixture of shared and individual rewards, which makes it essential for agents to infer the different contributions of their rewards based on their state.


4) ROVER-TOWER

This scenario consists of a total of eight agents, namely four rover-tower pairs. At each episode, rovers and towers are randomly paired. This task can be thought of as an extension of the cooperative navigation scenario. The rovers cannot see their surroundings and must rely on communication from the towers. The towers can direct the rovers to their destinations using discrete communication messages. Each pair of rover and tower receives increasing rewards as the rover moves closer to its goal.


B. RESULTS

We evaluate the proposed method in two sets of experiments and compare their performance in centralized training with decentralized execution algorithms based on the actor-critic method, such as MADDPG [24], MAAC [15], and BiCNet [30]. These have demonstrated their performance on similar multi-agent control tasks in previous studies. The first set of experiments consists of the four environments described in Section V.A. In these environments, all agents observe the positions of others from their viewpoints and use explicit communication actions to deliver messages to cooperate. In contrast, in the second set, we modify the original environments so that agents observe a limited portion of the original states. None of the agents can observe the positions of the others or receive communication messages from speaker agents. Thus, agents are required to cooperate and exploit the limited available information to achieve goals, such as avoiding a collision or moving to the target positions. We hypothesize that the auxiliary prediction networks show better performance because they can perceive the intrinsic mechanisms in the environments. Further details on the differences between the experiments in the first set (full observations) and the second (partial observations) can be found in Table 1.  In our experiments, the number of layers, hidden dimensions, or learning parameters of all methods tested is set to be identical to our method. We use three-layer neural networks to represent the actor and critic networks. The rectified linear unit activation function is applied to the three-layer neural networks, and each network is trained with ten random seeds. All methods are limited to experiencing 40,000 episodes; the size of the replay buffer is 10 6 . We update the network parameters after every hundred samples added to the replay buffer, and all methods are trained using the same amount of experience. In addition, we use Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001 and τ = 0.001 to update the target networks and set a batch size of 1,024. The discount factor γ of the reward function is set to 0.95.

We implement the MADDPG and the MAAC using git repositories, github.com/openai/maddpg and github.com/ shariqiqbal2810/MAAC, respectively, written by the authors of the MADDPG and MAAC papers [15], [24]. In addition, we implement BiCNet by referring to [30], using individual rewards for each agent.

We compare the cumulative episode rewards of each learning method that was trained on the full-observation environments as in the originally proposed scenarios. Under the same learning conditions, using 40,000 episodes, the MADR with auxiliary prediction networks for approximate model learning (MADR + AMLAPN) outperforms the competitors by a significant margin for all scenarios. Figure 2 shows that the proposed method can produce the optimal policy, better and faster than the other tested methods. In most scenarios, the proposed MADR shows similar or better performance, without approximate model learning, than the BiCNet, which does not require credit assignments because it utilizes individual rewards. In particular, the MADR + AMLAPN shows the highest performance with the approximate model learning, in all cases. However, in the cooperative communication scenario, the proposed methods and the BiCNet offer comparable performance. In this scenario, all agents observe a sufficient amount of information and receive the same rewards, implying that this situation does not allow any opportunity for improvement by credit assignment from critic networks or approximate model learning. Consequently, we can conclude that the proposed multi-agent actor-critic algorithm can learn the given information pertaining to the observations and rewards as well as the other methods. Furthermore, we find that the superior performance of the MADR + AMLAPN indicates that approximate model learning is helpful to fully utilize the information given.

C. EFFECT OF APPROXIMATE MODEL LEARNING

We hypothesize that the auxiliary prediction networks improve learning performance by inferring the model mechanism that agents are not able to observe. First, we compare MADR + AMLAPN with MADDPG + AMLAPN to identify performance improvements, regardless of the use of different multi-agent reinforcement learning methods. In Figures 2 and 3, the AMLAPN is seen significantly improving the MADR; however, MADDPG is not improved. These results imply that approximate model learning is not helpful in methods that do not use recurrent layers to share the prediction results from each agent. In addition, we conduct experiments to compare the contributions of the next states and rewards predictions. In Figure 4, the proposed AMLAPN shows the fastest convergence performance with the next states and rewards prediction networks. VI. CONCLUSIONS

In this study, we examine the issues with coordinating and communicating among multiple agents in cooperative environments in which agents can only access their observations and receive a global reward. In this setting, learning algorithms are required to cope with the following three challenges: (1) partial observability, (2) credit assignment issues related to the global reward, and (3) performance degradation issues of learning algorithms due to nonstationary and non-Markovian environments. We propose the MADR to address these issues in multi-agent environments, based on the paradigm of centralized training and decentralized execution. We hypothesize that if the LSTM in the critic network can assess the actor network by considering the contribution of each agent, then the deterministic policy gradient has trained each agent appropriately through the LSTM's backward path. We demonstrate the superiority of the proposed MADR over other multi-agent reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically the MADDPG, one of the representative methods. Moreover, we propose that the auxiliary prediction networks, MADR + AMLAPN, can improve performance and understand environment dynamics based on approximate model learning. Our experiments with four multi-agent environments demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method over the competitors, in terms of goal achievement and policy robustness. Compared to other multitasking methods, the MADR + AMLAPN leads to outstanding performance in most cases.

There are a couple of interesting directions for future research. One is to extend our approach to competitive multi-agent environments in which multiple agents learn behaviors while they contend with one another. Another interesting direction is to use the transformer [40], an alternative recurrent neural network, in the actor-critic architecture, to handle the sequence of agents with no arbitrary orders.
