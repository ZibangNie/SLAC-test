Sonenberg and Money and their co-workers (l-3) have reported that preparations of the thyrotropic hormone, after being treated with acetic anhydride, exhibit no thyroid-stimulating activity but exert an inhibitory action toward the thyrotropic hormone.

These reports suggested to us the possibility that acetyl derivatives of the posterior pituitary hormones might be devoid of some or all of the posterior pituitary hormonal activities, and in addition might be capable of inhibiting the biological effects of oxytocin and the vasopressins.

Exploratory experiments' were carried out in which crude products from the treatment of oxytocin and %argininevasopressin with acetic anhydride were tested first for their ability to exert certain biological effects of the parent hormones and then for their ability to inhibit these effects. The product obtained from oxytocin was devoid of avian depressor activity, but was capable of inhibiting to some extent the avian depressor effect of oxytocin.

The product obtained from %arginine-vasopressin exhibited no pressor activity but caused a reduction in the normal pressor response to %arginine-vasopressin in the rat. The observations with the crude acetylation products prompted us to undertake a more extensive study of acetyl derivatives of the posterior pituitary hormones. The present paper describes the synthesis of the derivative of %argininevasopressin in which the amino group of the half-cystine residue in the l-position bears an acetyl substituent (1-(N-acetylhemi-L-cystine)-8-arginine-vasopressin, hereafter in this paper referred to as I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin) (Fig. I), and also describes the effects of the acetylated hormone in the rat pressor assay.

The synthesis of I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin involved the preparation of the protected nonapeptide, N-acetyl-X-benzyl-Lcysteinyl-L-tyrosyl-L-phenylalanyl-L -glutaminyl -L -asparaginyl-X-benzyl-~-cysteinyl-~-prolyl-~-arginylglycinamide hydrobromide. The hexapeptide dihydrobromide, prepared by the decarbobenzoxylation of carbobenzoxy-L-glutaminyl-L -asparaginyl -S -benzyl -L -cysteinyl -L -prolyl -L -arginylglycinamide hydrobromide (5), was utilized in the preparation of the protected nonapeptide.

The dihydrobromide was treated with triethylamine, and the resulting monohydrobromide was coupled with the azide of N-acetyl-S-benzyl-L-cysteinyl-L-tyrosyl-L-phenyl- alanine according to the procedures used in the synthesis of 8-arginine-vasopressin (5). The protected nonapeptide was treated with sodium in liquid ammonia in order to remove the benzyl groups from the half-cystine residues (6). Oxidation of the sulfhydryl nonapeptide to the cyclic octapeptide was accomplished by aeration in dilute aqueous solution at pH 6.7 (5).
*
The crude acetylated hormone was purified first by countercurrent distribution and then by zone electrophoresis. Analysis of the purified material indicated the presence of the eight amino acids and ammonia in the correct molar ratios.

The purified I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin produced no detectable effect upon the blood pressure of the rat when administered intravenously in amounts up to 200 pg. However, it inhibited the pressor effect of 8-arginine-vasopressin in the rat. A dose of 200 pg of the acetylated hormone injected intravenously 1 minute before the injection of 10 to 20 milliunits (0.025 to 0.050 pg) of 8-arginine-vasopressin lowered the pressor effect of the hormone to 10 to 307, of the normal level. The normal pressor response was not regained until 30 to 60 minutes had elapsed. EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURE'

The reaction mixture was stirred at 5" for 16 hours. The product was precipitated with ether, collected by filtration, and dried; weight 1.05 g. The crude protected nonapeptide was treated in four equal portions with sodium in liquid ammonia according to the procedure employed in this laboratory for the removal of benzyl groups attached to sulfur (6). The residue from each portion after evaporation of the ammonia was dissolved in 500 ml of 0.1% acetic acid, and the pH was adjusted to 6.7 with ammonium hydroxide. Each solution was aerated with a slow stream of COz-free air for 1 hour.

The four solutions then were combined, concentrated in a rotary evaporator, and lyophilized.

The resulting material showed no detectable pressor activity but inhibited the pressor response to 8-arginine-vasopressin in the rat. The crude 1-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin was placed in the first 8 tubes of a 200-tube countercurrent distribution apparatus (8) and distributed in the system 2-butanol-0.1% acetic acid. The progress of the purification was followed by measurement of both the Folin-Lowry color (9) and the inhibition of the pressor effect of 8-arginine-vasopressin in the rat. After 767 transfers, the inhibitory activity was concentrated in only one of the Folin-Lowry color peaks. This peak appeared in a position that indicated a partition coefficient (K) of 0.39 for the substance. The contents of the tubes that contained the inhibitory product were pooled, concentrated in a rotary evaporator, and lyophilized.

The weight of the resulting dry powder was 100 mg. Further purification was achieved by subjecting 40 mg of this material to electrophoresis on a cellulose block (10) in a pyridine-acetate buffer at pH 4.0 for 44 hours at 2" with a potential of 400 volts. The weight of the I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin after electrophoresis was 25 mg. A sample of the product was hydrolyzed, and the amino acid and ammonia composition was determined by the starch column chromatographic method (11). The following molar ratios were obtained (the value for phenylalanine being taken arbitrarily as 1.0): phenylalanine 1.0, tyrosine 0.9, proline 0.9, glutamic acid 1.2, aspartic acid 1.0, glycine 1.1, arginine 1.1, cystine 1.2, and ammonia 3.5.

Effects of I-Acetyl-S-arginine-vasopressin in Rat Pressor Assay-The purified I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin was tested for its effect upon the blood pressure of rats by modifications of the methods of Dekanski (12) and of Lindquist and Rowe (13). Male rats that weighed between 300 and 400 g were anesthetized with chlorobutanol and treated with Dibenamine. Intravenous injections of the acetylated hormone into these rats in amounts up to 200 pg produced no detectable change in blood pressure.

In the tests for inhibitory activity, doses of 10 to 20 milliunits of highly purified 8-arginine-vasopressin were injected intravenously until reproducible pressor responses were obtained. Then 200 pg of 1-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin, contained in 0.10 ml of water, were injected by the same route.

One minute later, 8-arginine-vasopressin was administered in the same dose as was given before injection of the acetyl derivative. The pressor responses before and after injection of I-acetyl-8-arginine-vasopressin were compared. In six rats given injections of 2OO pg of the acetyl derivative, the pressor responses were lowered to 25, 26, 32, 23, 16 and 10% of the initial responses. The time required for recovery of the initial response to the hormone was 30 to 60 minutes in most rats. in which the terminal a-amino group bears an acetyl substituent has been synthesized. The intermediates, N-acetyl-S-benzyl-L-cysteinyl-n-tyrosyl-nphenylalanine methyl ester and L-glutaminyl-L-asparaginyl-Sbenzyl-L-cysteinyl-L-prolyl-L-arginylglycinamide dihydrobromide, were utilized, the azide of the protected tripeptide being coupled with the hexapeptide monohydrobromide to yield the desired nonapeptide intermediate. The crude nonapaptide was treated with sodium in liquid ammonia in order to remove the benzyl groups, and the resulting sulfhydryl peptide was oxidized to the cyclic disulfide by aeration.

The material thus obtained was purified by countercurrent distribution and zone electrophoresis.

The purified I-acetyl-%arginine-vasopressin, when tested in the rat, exerted no detectable effect upon the blood pressure but inhibited the pressor effect of %arginine-vasopressin.
