7 Nov 2007arXiv:0706.3725v2 [math.QA] WEYL MODULES AND OPERS WITHOUT MONODROMY
We prove that the algebra of endomorphisms of a Weyl module of critical level is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on the space of monodromy-free opers on the disc with regular singularity and residue determined by the highest weight of the Weyl module. This result may be used to test the local geometric Langlands correspondence proposed in our earlier work.

Introduction

Let g be a simple finite-dimensional Lie algebra. For an invariant inner product κ on g (which is unique up to a scalar) define the central extension g κ of the formal loop algebra g ⊗ C((t)) which fits into the short exact sequence
0 → C1 → g κ → g ⊗ C((t)) → 0.
This sequence is split as a vector space, and the commutation relations read (1.1) [x ⊗ f (t), y ⊗ g(t)] = [x, y] ⊗ f (t)g(t) − (κ(x, y) Res f dg)1, and 1 is a central element. The Lie algebra g κ is the affine Kac-Moody algebra associated to κ. We will denote by g κ -mod the category of discrete representations of g κ (i.e., such that any vector is annihilated by g ⊗ t n C [[t]] for sufficiently large n), on which 1 acts as the identity. Let U κ ( g) be the quotient of the universal enveloping algebra U ( g κ ) of g κ by the ideal generated by (1 − 1). Define its completion U κ ( g) as follows:
U κ ( g) = lim ←− U κ ( g)/U κ ( g) · (g ⊗ t n C[[t]]).
It is clear that U κ ( g) is a topological algebra, whose discrete continuous representations are the same as objects of g κ -mod.

Let κ crit be the critical inner product on g defined by the formula κ crit (x, y) = − 1 2 Tr(ad(x) • ad(y)).

In what follows we will use the subscript "crit" instead of κ crit . LetǦ be the group of adjoint type whose Lie algebraǧ is Langlands dual to g (i.e., the Cartan matrix ofǧ is the transpose of that of g).

Let Z g be the center of U crit ( g). According to a theorem of [FF,F2], Z g is isomorphic to the algebra Fun Opǧ(D × ) of functions on the space ofǧ-opers on the punctured disc D × = Spec(C((t))) (see [BD1,FG2] and Sect. 2 for the definition of opers).

It is interesting to understand how Z g acts on various g crit -modules. The standard modules are the Verma modules and the Weyl modules. They are obtained by applying the induction functor Ind : g -mod → g crit -mod,
M → U ( g crit ) ⊗ U (g[[t]]⊕1) M,
where g [[t]] acts on M via the projection g [[t]] → g and 1 acts as the identity. For λ ∈ h * let M λ be the Verma module over g with highest weight λ. The corresponding g crit -module M λ = Ind(M λ ) is the Verma module of critical level with highest weight λ.

For a dominant integral weight λ let V λ be the irreducible finite-dimensional gmodule with highest weight λ. The corresponding g crit -module V λ = Ind(V λ ) is the Weyl module of critical level with highest weight λ. The module V 0 = Ind(C 0 ) is also called the vacuum module.

It was proved in [FF,F2] that the algebra of g crit -endomorphisms of V 0 is isomorphic to the algebra Fun Op reǧ g of functions on the space Op reǧ g ofǧ-opers on the disc D = Spec(C [[t]]). Moreover, there is a commutative diagram
Z g ∼ − −−− → Fun Opǧ(D × )     End b g crit (V 0 ) ∼ − −−− → Fun Op reǧ g
We have shown in [FG2], Corollary 13.3.2, that a similar result holds for the Verma modules as well: the algebra of g crit -endomorphisms of M λ is isomorphic to Fun Op RS,̟(−λ−ρ) g , where Op RS,̟(−λ−ρ) g is the space ofǧ-opers on D × with regular singularity and residue ̟(−λ−ρ), ̟ being the natural projection h * → Spec Fun(h * ) W (see [FG2], Sect. 2.4, for a precise definition). In addition, there is an analogue of the above commutative diagram for Verma modules.

In this paper we consider the Weyl modules V λ . In [FG2], Sect. 2.9, we defined the subspace Op λ,reǧ g ⊂ Op RS,̟(−λ−ρ) g of λ-regular opers (we recall this definition below). Its points are those opers in Op RS,̟(−λ−ρ) g which have trivial monodromy and are thereforě G(K) gauge equivalent to the trivial local system on D × . In particular, Op 0,reǧ g = Op reǧ g . According to Lemma 1 below, the disjoint union of Op λ,reǧ g , where λ runs over the set P + of dominant integral weights of g, is precisely the locus ofǧ-opers on D × with trivial monodromy. The main result of this paper is the following theorem, which generalizes the description of End b g crit V 0 from [FF,F2] to the case of an arbitrary dominant integral weight λ.

Theorem 1. For any dominant integral weight λ the center Z g maps surjectively onto End b g crit V λ , and we have the following commutative diagram
(1.2) Z g ∼ − −−− → Fun OpǦ(D × )     End b g crit V λ ∼ − −−− → Fun Op λ,reǧ g
For g = sl 2 this follows from Prop. 1 of [F1]. This statement was also independently conjectured by A. Beilinson and V. Drinfeld (unpublished).

In addition, we prove that V λ is a free module over End b g crit V λ ≃ Fun Op λ,reǧ g . Theorem 1 has important consequences for the local geometric Langlands correspondence proposed in [FG2]. According to our proposal, to each "local Langlands parameter" σ, which is aǦ-local system on the punctured disc D × (or equivalently, ǎ G-bundle with a connection on D × ), there should correspond a category C σ equipped with an action of G((t)). Now let χ be a fixedǧ-oper on D × , which we regard as a character of the center Z g . Consider the full subcategory g crit -mod χ of the category g crit -mod whose objects are g crit -modules, on which the Z g acts according to this character. This category carries a canonical action of the ind-group G((t)) via its adjoint action on g crit . We proposed in [FG2] that g crit -mod χ should be equivalent to the sought-after category C σ , where σ is theǦ-local system underlying the oper χ. This entails a far-reaching corollary that the categories g crit -mod χ 1 and g crit -mod χ 2 for two different opers χ 1 and χ 2 are equivalent if the underlying local systems of χ 1 and χ 2 are isomorphic to each other.

In particular, consider the simplest case when σ is the trivial local system. Then, by Lemma 1, χ must be a point of Op λ,reǧ g for some λ ∈ P + . Theorem 1 implies that the quotient V λ (χ) of the Weyl module V λ by the central character corresponding to χ is non-zero. Therefore V λ (χ) is a non-trivial object of g crit -mod χ and also of the
corresponding G[[t]]-equivariant category g crit -mod G[[t]] χ .
In the case when λ = 0 we have proved in [FG1] that this is a unique irreducible object of g crit -mod
G[[t]] χ
and that this category is in fact equivalent to the category of vector spaces. Therefore we expect the same to be true for all other values of λ. This will be proved in a follow-up paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sect. 2 we recall the relevant notions of opers, Cartan connections and Miura transformation. In Sect. 3 we explain the strategy of the proof of the main result, Theorem 1, and reduce it to two statements, Theorem 2 and Proposition 1. We then prove Proposition 1 assuming Theorem 2 in Sect. 4. Our argument is based on the exactness of the functor of quantum Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction, which we derive from [Ar]. In Sect. 5 we compute the characters of the algebra of functions on Op λ,reǧ g and of the semi-infinite cohomology of V λ (they turn out to be the same). We then give two different proofs of Theorem 2 in Sect. 6. This completes the proof of the main result. We also show that the natural map from the Weyl module V λ to the corresponding Wakimoto module is injective and that V λ is a free module over its endomorphism algebra.


Some results on opers

In this section we recall the relevant notions of opers, Cartan connections and Miura transformation, following [BD1,F2,FG2], where we refer the reader for more details.

Let g be a simple Lie algebra and G the corresponding algebraic group of adjoint type. Let B be its Borel subgroup and N = [B, B] its unipotent radical, with the corresponding Lie algebras n ⊂ b ⊂ g.

Let X be a smooth curve, or the disc D = Spec(Ô), whereÔ is a one-dimensional smooth complete local ring, or the punctured disc D × = Spec(K), whereK is the field of fractions ofÔ.

Following Beilinson and Drinfeld (see [BD1], Sect. 3.1, and [BD2]), one defines a g-oper on X to be a triple (F G , ∇, F B ), where F G is a principal G-bundle F G on X, ∇ is a connection on F G , and F B is a B-reduction of F G which is transversal to ∇, in the sense explained in the above references and in [FG2], Sect. 1.1. We note that the transversality condition allows us to identify canonically the B-bundles F B underlying all opers.

More concretely, opers on the punctured disc D × may be described as follows. Let us choose a trivialization of F B and a coordinate t on the disc D such thatÔ = C[[t]] andK = C((t)). Let us choose a nilpotent subalgebra n − , which is in generic position with b and a set of simple root generators f ı , ı ∈ I, of n − . Then a g-oper on D × is, by definition, an equivalence class of operators of the form
(2.1) ∇ = ∂ t + ı∈I f ı + v, v ∈ b(K),
with respect to the action of the group N (K) by gauge transformations. It is known that this action is free and the resulting set of equivalence classes is in bijection witĥ K ⊕ℓ , where ℓ = rank(g). Opers may be defined in this way over any base, and this allows us to define the ind-affine scheme Op g (D × ) of g-opers on D × (it is isomorphic to an inductive limit of affine spaces). LetP + be the set of dominant integral coweights of g. Forλ ∈P + we define a g-oper withλ-nilpotent singularity as an equivalence class of operators
(2.2) ∇ = ∂ t + ı∈I t αı,λ · f ı + v(t) + v t , v(t) ∈ b(Ô), v ∈ n,
with respect to the action of the group N (Ô) by gauge transformations (see [FG2], Sect. 2.9). The corresponding scheme is denoted by Opλ ,nilp g . According to Theorem 2.9.1 of [FG2], the natural map Opλ ,nilp g → Op g (D × ) is injective, and its image is equal to the space of g-opers with regular singularity and residue ̟(−λ −ρ) (hereρ is the half-sum of positive coroots of g and ̟ is the projection h → Spec Fun(h) W ). Now we define the space Opλ ,reg g ofλ-regular opers as the subscheme of Opλ ,nilp g corresponding to those operators (2.2) which satisfy v = 0 (so that ∇ is regular at t = 0). In particular, ifλ = 0, then Op 0,reg g is just the space of regularǧ-opers on the disc D. The geometric significance ofλ-opers is explained by the following Lemma 1. Suppose that a g-oper χ = (F G , ∇, F B ) on D × is such that the corresponding G-local system is trivial (in other words, the corresponding operator (2.1) is in the G(K) gauge equivalence class of ∇ 0 = ∂ t ). Then χ ∈ Opλ ,reg g for someλ ∈P + .

Proof. It is clear from the definition that any oper in Opλ ,reg g is regular on the disc D and is therefore G(K) gauge equivalent to the trivial connection ∇ 0 = ∂ t . Now suppose that we have an oper χ = (F G , ∇, F B ) on D × such that the corresponding G-local system is trivial. Then ∇ is G(K) gauge equivalent to a regular connection on D. We have the decomposition G(K) = G(Ô)B(K). The gauge action of G(Ô) preserves the space of regular connections (in fact, it acts transitively on it). Therefore if an oper connection ∇ is gauge equivalent to a regular connection under the action of G(K), then its B(K) gauge equivalence class must contain a regular connection. The oper condition then implies that this gauge class contains a connection operator of the form (2.2) with v(0) = 0, for some dominant integral coweightλ. Therefore χ ∈ Opλ ,reg g . Let us choose a coordinate t on D. The vector field L 0 = −t∂ t then acts naturally on Opλ ,reg g and defines a Z-grading on the algebra of functions on it. In Sect. 5 we will compute the character of the algebra Fun Opλ ,reg g of functions on Opλ ,reg g with respect to this grading.

Next, we introduce the space of H-connections and the Miura transformation. Let X be as above. Denote by ω X the C × -torsor corresponding to the canonical line bundle on X. Let ωρ X be the push-forward of ω X to an H-torsor via the homomorphismρ : C × → H. We denote by Conn H (ωρ X ) the affine space of all connections on ωρ X . In particular, Conn H (ωρ D × ) is an inductive limit of affine spaces Conn H (ωρ D ) ord k of connections with pole of order ≤ k. We will use the notation Conn H (ωρ D ) RS for Conn H (ωρ D ) ord 1 . A connection ∇ ∈ Conn H (ωρ D ) RS has a well-defined residue, which is an element of h. Forμ ∈ h we denote by Conn H (ωρ D ) RS,μ the subspace of Conn H (ωρ D ) RS consisting of connections with residueμ.

The Miura transformation is a morphism
(2.3) MT : Conn H (ωρ D × ) → Op g (D × ),
introduced in [DS] (see also [F2] and [FG2], Sect. 3.3). It can be described as follows.

If we choose a coordinate t on D, then we trivialize ω D and hence ωρ D . A point of Conn H (ωρ D × ) is then represented by an operator
∇ = ∂ t + u(t), u(t) ∈ h(K).
We associate to ∇ the g-oper which is the N (K) gauge equivalence class of the operator
∇ = ∂ t + ı∈I f ı + u(t).
The following result is a corollary of Proposition 3.5.4 of [FG2]. Let F B,0 be the fiber at 0 ∈ D of the B-bundle F B underlying all g-opers. We denote by N F B,0 the F B,0 -twist of N .
Lemma 2. Letλ be a dominant integral coweight of g. The image of Conn H (ωρ D ) RS,−λ in Op g (D × ) under the Miura transformation is equal to Opλ ,reg g . Moreover, the map Conn H (ωρ D ) RS,−λ → Opλ ,reg g is a principal N F B,0 -bundle over Opλ ,reg g .
In particular, this implies that the scheme Opλ ,reg g is smooth and in fact isomorphic to an infinite-dimensional (pro)affine space.

Exactness

In this section we prove Proposition 1 assuming Theorem 2. The proof will rely on some properties of the semi-infinite cohomology functors.

Let g crit -mod I,Z be the category of g crit -modules which are equivariant with respect to the Iwahori subgroup I ⊂ G((t)) and equipped with a Z-grading with respect to the operator L 0 = −t∂ t which commutes with g crit in the natural way. Since I = H ⋉ I 0 , where I 0 = [I, I], the first condition means that n + = Lie(I 0 ) acts locally nilpotently, and the constant Cartan subalgebra Lie(H) = h ⊗ 1 ⊂ g ⊗ 1 ⊂ g crit acts semi-simply with eigenvalues corresponding to integral weights. The second condition means that we have an action of the extended affine Kac-Moody algebra g ′ κ = CL 0 ⋉ g κ . The category g crit -mod I,Z is therefore the product of the blocks of the usual category O −h ∨ of modules over the extended affine Kac-Moody algebra at the critical level k = −h ∨ , corresponding to the (finite) Weyl group orbits in the set of integral weights. Let g crit -mod G[[t]],Z be the category of g crit -modules which are equivariant with respect to the subgroup G [[t]] and equipped with a Z-grading with respect to the operator L 0 = −t∂ t . This is the full subcategory of the category g crit -mod I,Z , whose objects are modules integrable with respect to the constant subalgebra g = g ⊗ 1 ⊂ g crit .
