Surgical outcomes among spinal tuberculosis patients: a study from a provincial reference hospital in Indonesia

Spinal tuberculosis is one of the most common forms of extrapulmonary tuberculosis, which can cause a significant loss of functional ability and decrease in quality of life.Surgery has become an integral component in the current management of spinal tuberculosis that could improve neurological deficits and functional status, yet it may expose patients to surgical site infection (SSI).The aim of the study was to observe the incidence of SSI and improved neurological deficits and functional status post-surgery among patients with spinal tuberculosis.Methods: The medical records (n=91) were collected retrospectively from patients admitted between January 2019 and June 2022.Characteristic data collected in this study included age, gender, education, occupation, domicile, history of smoking, history of pulmonary tuberculosis, presence of comorbidity.Patients were contacted for history of SSI, postoperative neurological deficits, and functional status assessments through interview using Frankel Grade and Barthel Index instruments, respectively.Results:The results revealed that only two percent of the total patients developed SSI.Number of patients with Frankel Grade E increased from 0 to 72 (79.1%), and only three patients (3.3%) had persistent Frankel Grade B after the surgery.Sixty-eight patients (74.7%) who underwent the surgery enjoyed independent state of functional status, whereas prior to the surgery, the number was smaller (n=18, 19.8%).The favorable surgical outcomes were found predominantly in female patients (83.9%), 17-25-year-old patients (93.3%), and those having no history of smoking (82.5%-84.2%)and comorbid disease (81.9%-83.3%).The recovery was mostly observed 1-2 years post-surgery (87.2%).Conclusion: Spinal surgery for patients with spinal tuberculosis improves neurological deficits and functional status.The risk of contracting SSI after the surgery is considered to be low.

INTRODUCTION

Tuberculosis is a highly infectious disease and continues to be a major global cause of mortality, surpassing other infectious agents in death toll prior to the COVID-19 pandemic. 1,2According to the World Health Organization (WHO), although there was an 18% decrease in tuberculosis cases from 7.1 million to 5.8 million in 2019, the number of tuberculosis patients rose to 10 million worldwide in 2020. 1 Notably, Southeast Asia accounted for the highest proportion of global tuberculosis cases, including Indonesia. 3,4n 2020, Indonesia reported a total of 393,323 tuberculosis cases, with 91% classified as pulmonary tuberculosis and 9% as extrapulmonary tuberculosis.5 The Indonesia Basic Health Research (RIKESDAS) in 2018 revealed that the prevalence of tuberculosis in Indonesia was 0.42%, and it was higher in Aceh Province (0.49%).6 Spinal tuberculosis can lead to structural damage in the vertebrae, intervertebral discs, and paravertebral soft tissues, resulting in compression of the spinal cord.7 Additionally, the proximity of the spinal cord infection to the nerves can trigger neurological complications, including pain, weakness, and numbness in the lower limbs.Without proper treatment, spinal tuberculosis can cause significant morbidity, persistent neurological deficits, and severe deformities, leading to impaired mobility, difficulties in self-care and disrupted sphincter control.Prolonged neglect of the condition can result in further physical, mental, emotional, and social consequences that profoundly impact the patient's quality of life.8 Spinal surgery is widely recommended for patients with spinal tuberculosis to address spinal deformities, improve neurological function, and restore bone stability.9 The treatment is particularly recommended in cases of spinal instability, spinal cord compression, sequestration, paravertebral abscesses, and sinus formation.10 However, it's important to consider the risk of surgical site infection (SSI), a common concern in spinal surgery patients, with reported SSI rates ranging from 2% to 20%. 112 Therefore,

MATERIAL AND METHODS


Study design


Eligibility criteria

The research subjects were patients diagnosed with spinal tuberculosis based on radiological presentation and positive mycobacterial culture or polymerase chain reaction.To be included the medical record should contain the report on Frankel Grade and Barthel Index.Incomplete medical records and patients who were unable to be contacted were excluded.


Data collection and synthesis

Data collection was carried out by looking at the patient's medical records directly and conducting interviews with the patients using the Barthel Index and Frankel Grade as research instruments.Characteristics of the patients collected in this study included gender, age, occupation, living address, level of education, history of pulmonary tuberculosis, comorbid diseases, history of smoking and length of time after surgery.Functional status of the patient was assessed by using the Barthel Index.The instrument provides comprehensive assessment tool that evaluates functional status by examining a patient's abilities in areas such as bowel


RESULTS


Characteristics of patients

Out of 91 patients included in this study, 60 (65.9%) were men (Table 1).The patients were mostly in the age range of 17-55 years (from 16.5% to 19.8%).

Those with occupations as housewives and farmers/fishermen predominated the total patients (n=16, 17.6%).The patients mostly had completed Senior High School (n=48, 52.7%).Most of the patients were from Banda Aceh (n=12, 13.2), Aceh Besar (n=10, 11%), and Pidie (n=9, 9.9%).Sixtynine patients (75.8%) had never been diagnosed with pulmonary tuberculosis.Seventy-two patients (79.1) and 57 patients (62.6%) did not have comorbidity and smoking history, respectively.Most of the patients had the surgery in the last 1-3 years ago (n=76, 83.6%).Only two patients developed surgical site infections (SSI) (Table 1).

Functional status

Herein, higher number of patients with independent functional status was observed after the surgery.In a previous study, improvement in the Barthel Index was not observed in patients with metastatic spinal infections that did not undergo the surgery, and they were more likely to have worsening progression. 25rogress on the functional status might be observable after the patients underwent 2-year rehabilitation along with regular check-up following the surgery. 26As a comparison, in the present study, most patients underwent the surgical treatment in the last 1 or up to 3 years ago.Based on a previous study, patient's characteristics such as age, gender, and the presence of comorbidity and diabetes mellitus are determinants for the post-surgical functional status. 27However, they also found that patients aged <58 years were likely to experience an increased functional status following the rehabilitation as compared to the older population. 27


Neurological deficit

In the present study, most of the patients after the surgery had Frankel Grade E suggesting that the normal sensory and motor functions had been achieved, although there might be some reflex abnormalities.Our finding is in line with a previous study reporting a high proportion of patients (35 out of 46) experiencing neurological improvement following the surgery. 28Similarly, findings from a study in Taiwan suggest that spinal tuberculosis patients who underwent surgery were likely to have neurological improvement, and complete recovery was achieved after >2 years of the surgery. 29onetheless, the recovery might vary among different populations.A study revealed that the Frankel Grade E was achieved after one year of surgery, where patients aged <50 years were likely to have faster recovery. 30This is due to the fact that older patients might have age-related arteriosclerosis that could compromise arteriolar dilation, exacerbating ischemic the surgery (from n=18 to 68).Higher percentage of patients with Frankel Grade E (normal) were observed during the postsurgery as opposed to during pre-surgery (0% versus 74.7%).After the surgery, patients aged 17-25 years were likely to achieve independent functional status conditions (93.3%) as compared to those aged 36-45 years (62.5%).Moreover, independent functional status was likely to be achieved after the surgery in patients who were female (83.9%), housewives (93.2%), graduated from junior high school (80%), having no history of pulmonary tuberculosis (76.8%), having no comorbidity (81.9%), and having no history of smoking (82.5%).Patients receiving the surgical treatment of more than 3 years were likely to achieve the independent state.Additionally, those achieving the independent state most of them had pre-operative Frankel Grade D during pre-surgery (89.5%).

Following the spinal surgery, as many as 93.3% of patients aged 17-25 years reached Frankel Grade E. Female patients (83.9%), housewives (93.8%), and those with bachelor certificates (83.3%) were found to have Frankel Grade E. Patients with no history of pulmonary tuberculosis (79.7%), no comorbid diseases (83.3%), no history of smoking (84.2%), and 1-2 years length of time after surgery (87.2%) were likely to achieve Frankel Grade E after spinal surgery.


DISCUSSION


Surgical site infection (SSI)

Patients diagnosed with this disease may require spinal surgery to alleviate neurological deficits, decompress the spinal cord or nerves, stabilize the spine, and address complications or failed pharmacological therapy. 16In such case, SSI is found common, where the incidence rates vary from 2% to 20%. 11,12,17Herein, only 2 out of 91 total patients (2.2%) were diagnosed having SSI.The two patients, 36-55 years, had worse functional status (severe and slight dependency) and neurological deficits (Frankel Grade B and C) as compared to their non-SSI counterparts.The SSI cases were observed within the span of 1-2 years post-surgery.Both SSI patients had a history of smoking, while one of them had type 2 diabetes mellitus.

The number of SSI incidence might vary across studies, depending on the number of patients and length of observation.In a previous report, the incidence reached 9.1% among 318 patients observed after 8 years post-surgery. 18It is worth mentioning that in a study with a larger sample size (n=289), the difference in functional status and neurological deficits between those with and without SSI is not significant. 19Generally, the increase in the incidence of SSI is influenced by the time span between the injury and surgery, duration of the surgery, length of hospitalization after surgery and resurgery. 19,20 Nonetheless, in a study observing medical records from 817 patients, age over 70 years was a conditions and eventually prolonging the neurological function restoration. 31n a previous study, the recovery of neurological function post-spinal surgery was not dependent on the patient's age, gender, comorbid diseases, history of tuberculosis, history of smoking and diabetes mellitus. 27However, findings from another study suggested that the being young, having partial paraplegia, and undergoing surgical management are contributing factors to significant improvement of neurological function among spinal tuberculosis patients. 32No association was found between gender and the improved state of neurological function. 32It is worth noting that early diagnosis and management have been attributed to higher changes of nervous and spinal regeneration. 33s the limitation, we were unable to retrieve missing data and contact the patients for interview, which might contribute to selection bias.Limited number of patients might result in the biased result, hinting the urgency of employing a larger sample size in the future research.Further, due to insufficient data, analytical statistics were not viable.This warrants an improvement in archiving medical records in the hospital.


CONCLUSIONS

Operative management of spinal tuberculosis could improve the functional status and neurological functions which were worse before the surgery.The successful outcomes were observed predominantly among female patients and 17-25 years old patients.Those without smoking history and comorbidity enjoyed a higher chance of successful outcomes.The risk for developing SSI following the surgery was low.Taken altogether, spinal surgery is a promising management to improve the neurological deficits and functional status among patients with spinal tuberculosis.However, the present study was limited by the small sample size which warrants future research with higher number of samples.
