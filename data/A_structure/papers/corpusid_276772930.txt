This study was conducted in order to find the role of biochar and added irrigation water level in some physical soil properties and some properties, growth and yield of Barley.It was conducted in the fields of agriculture college / university of Anbar -Iraq.The study included two factors, biochar (B) which was added with the soil with a depth of 0.15 m and three replicates; B0 with no addition; B1 by adding 2.0 g for each kilogram; and B2 by adding 4.0g for each kilogram.As for the second factor, irrigation water (I), it was added two levels; IF by adding the whole irrigation depth, and IH by adding half of it.The results of the study showed an improvement in the values of bulk density, as the values decreased from 1.41 to1.39 Mg m -3 when decreasing the water level from (IF) to (IH) and by increasing B0, B1 and B2, and the reduction percent from 1.40 to 2.80%.The results of the study also showed the ability of biochar to increase the availability of water and to hold and retain it when the minimum levels of added water are adopted.It has been possible to save a quality of water, its percentage has reached 50% at addition 50% from available water.

Introduction

Barley is one of the important crops, ranked fifth in the world as a strategic crop, and has the ability to grow in dry conditions as well as at low and high temperatures and in saline soils.Therefore, this study aims to know the role of biochar and the depth of added irrigation water in some of the physical properties of the studied soil and some of the growth characteristics of the barley crop.

Biochar is known as a carbon product obtained through the thermal decomposition of biomass in conditions of no or little oxygen at high temperatures.In order to improve the physical properties of the soil and its fertility, there was an urgent need to increase its organic carbon content, and for this purpose the integrated use of organic and inorganic fertilizers became an urgent necessity [1].Where organic fertilizers are used, such as compost, waste resulting from the manufacture of biogas, and other sources of organic matter, which are a source of organic carbon, but they are criticized for their lack of stability in the soil due to their very high rate of decomposition [2], hence the need to use soil amendments that are resistant to decomposition for a long period of time to maintain the organic carbon state in the soil.

Biochar has been used successfully by generations of indigenous farmers in the Amazon basin, and the accumulation of large quantities of it in the soil has increased its agricultural quality and the sustainability of its production and formed what is known as Amazonian dark land (Terra Pretta), which is still of great importance in agricultural use [3].[4] mentioned that biochar works as an amendment to make the soil retain 15% of its moisture content compared to not adding biochar.Adding biochar increases the ability of the soil to hold water, 2 and this depends on the nature of the type of texture and the ability of the soil to retain water.Water increases significantly in sandy soils when biochar is added to them, but it is affected less than in clay-mixed soils.[5] described that the increase in the ability of the soil to hold water when biochar was added to it was due to the increase in its porosity and the nature of biochar adsorption.[6] noticed that the addition of biochar reduced the values of saturated water conductivity by 92% in sandy lands and 67% in organic soils, and in return, its value increased by 32.8% in clay-rich soils.The researchers themselves indicated that biochar works to change the way water moves.Water in the soil voids, as water molecules make exceptional passages in the soil and thus it helps reduce water flow in sandy soils and increase it in clay soils.In addition, it contributes to increasing the humidity percentage by 92% in sandy soils.[7] indicated that the water retention capacity of the soil is an important characteristic from the farmers' point of view, which has an important role in plant growth.If the soil holds a larger amount of water, this reduces the number of irrigation successive times for the crop in addition to achieving good growth.For plants, this depends on the type of soil.Maximizing the addition of biochar may achieve a ready water content of 97% and a saturated water content of 65%.[7] showed that by increasing the biochar addition rate, there will be a significant decrease in bulk density values.[8] showed that a specific type of biochar possesses specific properties that have the potential to improve soil properties under certain climatic conditions.[9] indicated that the presence of soil with good structure, porosity and water conductivity, as well as good bulk density, in addition to having a good porous medium suitable for the growth of microorganisms and suitable for providing nutrients and easy movement of water in it, all of this work to increase the effectiveness of biochar in improving Those physical characteristics of the soil that were mentioned above.

In a study [10] which was conducted to determine the effect of biochar on barley growing in sandy lands, the results of which showed that adding biochar had a significant effect on soil salinity, pH, organic matter, and nutrients, which were treated with amounts of 1% and 3%. of biochar with the weight of the soil, which was irrigated with 13.8% saline water, according to the sequence.This study confirmed that adding biochar to the soil increased barley production and improved the properties of the soil, as well as conserving and rationalizing water, thus confronting the crisis of food demand from During the expansion of agricultural lands that were not previously exploited.[11] described that soil porosity would be inconsistent when biochar was added due to pore blockage by biochar dust.[12] indicated that there is an inverse relationship between the level of biochar added to the soil and its bulk density values, as it contributed to significantly reducing the values according to the values of the least significant difference at the 5% level, and the relationship was linear and strong of the first degree (R 2 = 0.997).While the relationship with soil porosity and field capacity was positive and significant differences were recorded compared to each other and to the comparison treatment, as the porosity increased from 43.68% in the comparison treatment to 47.83% at the highest level of biochar added 4% by weight, the same researchers indicated that there was consistency with the changes in soil porosity, as the field capacity recorded an increase from 41.32% in the comparison treatment to 44.25% at the level of 4% of added biochar.

[13] pointed out that managing water efficiently is of great importance in arid and semi-arid regions, especially in light of the climatic variations that the Earth's climate is currently witnessing and water pollution and the demand for it.Therefore, attention to irrigation and its techniques is one of the most important successful methods of water management in light of the data referred to on the one hand and on the other hand is due to its extreme importance to irrigated land, which constitutes about 20% of the cultivated land in the world.


Materials and Methods


Experiment location

A field experiment was conducted in the fields of the College of Agriculture -Anbar University, located at latitude 33.42802° north and longitude 43.33541° east for the period from 1/11/2023 to 24/3/2024, in mixed soil, to study the effect of biochar and the amount of irrigation water in Some physical properties, growth and yield of barley plant, Abaa 99.The study soil was classified within the 80 MW series [14] according to the American classification (USDA, 2010).


Physical and chemical characteristics for the study soil

A soil sample representative of the field was taken, air-dried, then passed through a sieve with holes diameter of 2 mm.The size distribution of the particles was estimated using the volumetric pipette method to determine the texture type.The bulk density values of the soil were estimated using the cylinder method (Core method) described in [15].As for the true density, it was estimated using the pycnometer method and according to the method given in [16].Soil porosity was calculated based on the bulk and particle density according to the Vomocil method presented in [16].The saturated water conductivity was estimated using the constant head method as stated in [18].(Table 1).Some chemical properties of soil samples were estimated.EC and pH were measured in a soil-water extract (1:1) using an EC meter and a pH meter respectively as described in [17].Dissolved ions were estimated according to the methods described in Black (1965), organic matter was estimated following the Walkley and Black method described in [19] (Table 1).


Study Factors 2.3.1 Biochar (B)

Biochar prepared from plant waste was added to the farm by heating it at a temperature of 400°C, mixed with the soil, to a depth of 0.15m, and at three levels, as following: No addition, B0, adding 2g for each 1kg soil, B1 and adding 4g for each 1kg soil, B2.Which were added to a depth of 0.15m.


Irrigation level (I)

Irrigation water was added for study treatments with two levels as following, full depth IF and half depth IH, with a factorial experimental, it included the design of randomized complete design.


Agriculture and Crop managements

A factorial experiment was carried out in a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with three replications.Plowing and field leveling operations were carried out.The field was divided into three blocks, leaving a 2-meter gap between one block and another.Each block was divided into 6 experimental units, 2 meters long and 1 meter wide, leaving a distance of 1.5 meters between one unit and another.Barley grains, type Abaa 99, were planted on 1/11/2023 on lines spaced 0.10 meters apart.


Irrigation

Water was added by irrigation using river water, the amount of water required was calculated, and the irrigation process was completed after 50% of the ready water in the soil was exhausted.The amount of water needed for each irrigation was calculated based on measuring the gravimetric moisture content of the soil and using the following equation [20] as following:  = (  −   ) *  *

Fertilization

The fertilization process was carried out according to the fertilizer recommendations of [21] (100 kg N, 100 kg P, and 80 kg K. Characteristics of growth and yield of plant 2.8.1 Plant height

Plant height was measured after the crop was fully grown by noting the appearance of final signs of plant growth.10 plants were selected for each treatment, and plant height was measured using a tape measure, starting from the surface of the soil to the end of the spike, and then the average was calculated.


Total yield

The total yield was estimated by taking random samples within the study's parameters.A wooden frame with an area of 1 m 2 was used.These squares were harvested when the crop was finally grown, and the yield was estimated and the average was taken in tons ha -1 .


Results and Discussion


The effect of biochar and irrigation level on some physical characteristics 3.1.1 Bulk density

Figure (1) shows the effect of biochar and the level of added water on bulk density values.In general, it is observed that there is a noticeable decrease in bulk density values with increasing levels of biochar addition and decreasing irrigation levels.When the levels of biochar are stable, the values are observed to decrease from 1.43 to 1.41 Mg m -3 when level B0 (comparison treatment) is adopted.For the half and full amounts of water, according to the sequence, the values reached 1.41 and 1.38 Mg m -3 at the level of 2.0 grams of biochar per kg of soil, and the values also reached 1.39 and 1.36 Mg m -3 when adding biochar at the level of 4.0 grams per kg of soil and the aforementioned water levels, respectively.

When the added water levels remained constant, the bulk density values decreased from 1.41 to 1.38 and 1.36, and the values also decreased from 1.43 to 1.41 and 1.39 Mg m 3 when the added water level was reduced from the full level (IF) to the half level (IH) and for the amounts of biochar B0, B1, B2 sequentially.The decrease rates were 2.13 and 3.55% when comparing the levels of biochar B1 and B2 with B0 (the comparison treatment), while the decrease rates were 1.40 and 2.80% for the same comparisons above when the lower water level was adopted, according to the sequence.This is consistent with what [7] indicated that by increasing the rate of biochar, there was a significant decrease in the bulk density values, and also as indicated by [4], who indicated that biochar has an important role in water movement and improving the physical properties of the soil.As for the interaction between the two study factors (biochar and irrigation levels) and their effect on bulk density values, it was not significant, as indicated by the L.S.D values shown in Figure (1), as well as the statistical analysis at a significant level of 0.05.


Saturated hydraulic conductivity

Figure (2) shows the effect of biochar and irrigation water level on saturated hydraulic conductivity values.It was found that there was a variation in saturated hydraulic conductivity values with increasing values of the added biochar and decreasing water levels from full to half.When the levels of biochar remain stable, a decrease in the levels of saturated water conductivity values is observed from 4.73 to 4.67 at the B0 level (comparison treatment) and for the levels of full and half added water, according to the sequence.As for the other two levels, B1 and B2, an increase in the water conductivity values was observed by reducing added water level from full to half, the values rose from 4.20 to 4.57 cm h -1 , and also rose from 4.00 to 4.30 cm h -1 .The effect was more pronounced at the highest addition level of 4.00 gm per kg, according to the sequence, and this is consistent with what was indicated by [5].The addition of biochar reduced the values of saturated hydraulic conductivity, especially in sandy soils.

Total yield

Figure (7) shows the effect of the levels of biochar and irrigation water on the total yield values, as it was observed that there was an increase in the yield values with an increase in both the levels of biochar and the levels of added water.It has been shown that the yield values increased more at half the water depth at levels B1 and B2 of the added biochar.When biochar levels remained stable, total yield values increased from 2,500 to 2,850 tons ha -1 and from 3,033 tons to 3.283 tons ha -1 at levels B1 and B2 and for water levels IF and IH, and the differences were highly significant.The opposite happened when the comparison treatment (B0) occurred, as the total yield values decreased from 2.217 to 2.027 tons ha -1 for the half and total water depth.This is attributed to the effect of adding biochar on barley growing in sandy lands, and this increased production and improved soil properties, as well as conserving and rationalizing water, and this is consistent with what was indicated by [19].

When the levels of added water remained constant, the values of the total yield increased from 2,217 to 2,500-and 3,033-ton ha -1 , and the values also increased from 2,027 to 2,850-and 3,283-ton ha -1 when the biochar levels graduated from the comparison treatment (B0) to the addition levels of 2.0 and 4.0 g charcoal per kg soil, according to sequence.The differences were not significant.The rates of increase in the total yield reached 12.76 and 36.81% when adopting the full level of water, while they reached 40.60 and 61.96% when adopting the half level of water, and when comparing the lower and upper levels of added biochar with the general comparison, respectively.As for the interaction between the study factors (biochar and added water levels), the differences were highly significant, according to the L.S.D values at a significance level of 0.05.This is attributed to the active role of the two study factors, whose effect was positive and in one direction, as biochar worked as an amendment to make the soil retain its moisture content as well as increase its ability to hold water, which improved the physical properties of the soil (bulk density, saturated water conductivity, and basic seepage rate).The plant growth characteristics also improved (plant height, spike length, and weight of 1000 grains), all of which contributed to increasing the total yield.


Effect of biochar and irrigation level in values of water use efficiency

Figure (8) shows the effect of biochar and irrigation levels in the values of water use efficiency, it was increased by the addition of biochar and the reduction of added water levels.It was shown that it was more increased at irrigation of (IH) and the levels B1, B2 of biochar.When the biochar levels are fixed, the efficiency values had increased from 341.10 to 623.70 g m -3 at (B0), then increased from 384.62 to 876.92 g m -3 , and from 466.63 to 1010.15 g m -3 for the levels B1 and B2 when reducing the amount of added water from the full to the half level, respectively.The variations were high significant.The increase percents in values of the efficiency were about 12.70% and 36.80%.While it reached 40.60% and 61.96% when comparing the biochar levels B1 and B2 with B0 and for the both added water depths (IH and IF), respectively.This is attributed to that the increase of biochar levels improved the soil physical properties and the ability to conserve high amounts of water, which is positively reflected on increasing the yield and therefore gave better values for the efficiency.This is consistent with what was mentioned by [24] that rationalizing water consumption contributed in the increase of the efficiency.8.The increase percents were 82.85%, 128.0% and 116.48% when compared with the full water levels (IF) and for the biochar B0, B1 and B2, respectively.This increase may be attributed to the effective and efficient management for water which is important in the arid and semi-arid areas, especially when adopting different levels of biochar under the current climate changes of the earth, this is consistent with what was mentioned by [12].

As for the interaction between biochar levels and added irrigation depths, the variations were high significant according to L.S.D values at 0.05.This is because of the mutual effect of the study factors, which their effect was positive and in one direction, which was the separation of the levels of biochar, which was an important factor in improving the physical properties of the soil by increasing its ability to conserve water and adding it when the plant needs it in order to increase the yield with the least amount of adopted irrigation water, and this is what is meant to be done in arid and semi-arid areas, in which water is the effective element, regardless of the land area.
