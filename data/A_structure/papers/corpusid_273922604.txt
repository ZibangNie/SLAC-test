Mannitol-evoked cough response is reduced by inhaled salbutamol
34F71D9FAE11A062D5FEEF5B5CE9518810.1183/23120541.00815-2024].Received: 26 Aug 2024 Accepted: 23 Sept 2024
This study demonstrated that salbutamol treatment inhibits measures of mannitol-evoked cough independent of changes in FEV 1 , suggesting that mechanisms unrelated to changes in airway tone can contribute to mannitol-evoked cough https://bit.ly/4h0ZRrY

Introduction

The pathological hallmarks of asthma are airway inflammation, variable airflow obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness (BHR) which are accompanied by symptoms including shortness of breath, wheeze and chest tightness as well as cough, which is reported in 37.6% of female and 27.6% of male asthmatic patients [1,2].Acute bronchoconstriction with methacholine has been shown to heighten cough responses to capsaicin with the proposed mechanism through sensitisation of airway nerves, which include the chemically sensitive C-fibres and potentially mechanosensitive Aδ fibres as well.[3].As such, therapies preventing bronchoconstriction would be expected to improve cough [4]; however, the interaction and relationship between airway smooth muscle and nerve function in the cough response is unclear.

Mannitol is a sugar alcohol developed as a diagnostic tool for asthma.Inhaled mannitol increases osmolarity in the airway surface liquid leading to activation of local inflammatory cells in the asthmatic airway [8,9], and it also causes both cough and bronchoconstriction in asthmatic patients, and cough in non-asthmatic patients [10].Previous studies have demonstrated that pretreatment with salbutamol in asthmatic patients reduces the cough response to inhaled challenges with hypertonic saline, histamine and mannitol [11,12], but it is unclear if this reduction was related simply to the prevention of bronchoconstriction, or if it included inhibition of airway sensory nerve excitation resulting from hyperosmolar airway challenges.We conducted mannitol inhaled cough challenge in allergic asthmatic patients (AAs) and healthy controls (HCs) to determine whether salbutamol would reduce the cough response to mannitol independent of changes in airway calibre.


Methods


Study eligibility

Participants with mild steroid-naïve allergic asthma (AAs) and HCs were recruited.Asthmatic participants had well-controlled asthma according to Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) classification.Forced expiratory volume in 1 s (FEV 1 ) was required to be ⩾70% predicted, with methacholine provocative concentration inducing a 20% fall in FEV 1 (PC 20 ) ⩽16 mg•mL −1 or mannitol provocative dose inducing 15% fall in FEV 1 (PD 15 ) ⩽635 mg or ⩾10% incremental fall in FEV 1 between consecutive mannitol doses.Allergic status was confirmed based on at least one positive skin-prick test (SPT) to an aeroallergen.AAs were not permitted any asthma medications other than short-acting β 2 -agonist (SABA) less than twice weekly to treat symptoms, and this was withheld at least 8 h prior to study visits.HCs were required to have FEV 1 ⩾80%, methacholine PC 20 >16 mg•mL −1 and <5% fall in FEV 1 to mannitol.We excluded current smokers, those with a recent asthma exacerbation or uncontrolled symptoms, and use of medication which may have altered cough responses (e.g., opiates, gabapentin, anticholinergics and theophylline).All subjects were required to demonstrate a cough response, defined as more than four coughs, to mannitol.


Study design

At McMaster University we conducted a double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, 2-way crossover study consisting of four visits.The study was registered (Clinicaltrials.govIdentifier: NCT03620422, NCT04565847 where the full protocol can be accessed).Eligibility was determined during the first two visits through medical history, allergen SPT, spirometry, and inhaled challenges with methacholine and mannitol.Eligible subjects were enrolled at Visit 3, and from a computer-generated allocation list they were randomised 1:1 to receive either salbutamol 2.5 mg or 0.9% saline placebo via an AeroEclipse nebuliser (Monaghan Medical, Plattsburgh, NY, USA), and the mannitol inhaled cough challenge began 15 min after dosing.After a period of 1-7 days, subjects crossed over to receive the other treatment at Visit 4 (figure 1).


Skin-prick testing

A panel of common aeroallergen extracts together with negative control and a positive histamine control were tested by skin-prick using a single head plastic lancet on the subject's forearm.A positive result was defined as a wheal of ⩾2 mm relative to the negative saline control.


Spirometry

Spirometry was performed according to American Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines using a handheld spirometer.To assess FEV 1 and forced vital capacity (FVC) the participants were asked to exhale forcefully until all air had been exhaled and a plateau was demonstrated.For baseline measurements this manoeuvre was repeated three times, and the highest reproducible values were recorded.


Methacholine challenge test

Methacholine is a synthetic form of acetylcholine and therefore causes smooth muscle contraction.It is commonly used in clinical practice to exclude asthma but also as a measure of airway hyperresponsiveness.The challenge was performed using guidelines produced by the ATS [13].Briefly, nebulised methacholine was inhaled in doubling concentrations from 0.0625 mg•mL −1 up to 16 mg•mL −1 via the 2-min tidal breathing method and a Wright nebuliser (Roxon Medi Tech Ltd., Montreal, ON, Canada) [13].At each concentration the FEV 1 was measured 30 s and 90 s after the end of each inhalation.The test was terminated once a 20% fall in FEV 1 was recorded.If the maximum concentration of 16 mg•mL −1 was reached without at least a 17% fall in FEV 1 , a PC 20 of 16 mg•mL −1 was assigned.


Mannitol inhaled cough challenge

Mannitol challenge was conducted according to guidelines set by ANDERSON and colleagues [14] using a single dose inhalator.The dose protocol consisted of 0 ( placebo capsule), 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 160 and 160 mg mannitol resulting in a maximum cumulative dose of 635 mg.The 80-160 doses were given in multiples of 40-mg capsules.Two FEV 1 measurements were performed 60 s after the last capsule of that dose was inhaled.The challenge was stopped if there was a 15% fall in FEV 1 reported or when a total cumulative dose of 635 mg had been administered.If FEV 1 did not fall by at least 15% when the cumulative dose of 635 mg was reached, a PD 15 of 1270 mg was assigned.A VitaloJAK cough monitor (Vitalograph Ltd., Buckingham, UK) was worn throughout the challenge.The number of coughs were counted for 60 s after each mannitol inhalation, and this was verified by listening to the audio recording.At the end of the test, we determined the cumulative number of coughs, the maximum cough response evoked by any dose of mannitol (Emax), the dose required to evoke half of the maximum coughs (ED50), the dose of mannitol inducing at least two coughs (C2) and five coughs (C5), and the cough dose ratio (CDR) by calculating (cumulative coughs ÷ cumulative dose of mannitol×100).


Statistical analysis

Analysis on data was performed using SPSS Version 28.0 (IBM Corp., NY, USA).Demographic and baseline cough data are presented as mean±SD or as median and interquartile range (IQR).Generalised Estimating Equations (GEE) modelling was used to estimate the geometric mean coughs at each dose of mannitol.Cough counts at each mannitol dose were log transformed (0.1 was added to zero values) and selected as the dependent variable in the GEE model (exchangeable correlation matrix) including each dose, intervention (salbutamol/placebo), sex, period and sequence as covariates.Main effects and interactions were performed to generate mean±SE at every dose of mannitol inhaled.Data generated from the GEE model were used to generate a nonlinear regression dose-response curve (GraphPad Prism Version 9.5) from which Emax and ED50 (geometric mean and 95% CI) were calculated.Raw data were also used to calculate baseline ED50 and Emax and reported as geometric mean±SD.Additional coughs end-points including CDR, C2 and C5 are presented as the geometric mean±SD at baseline and as median (IQR) after salbutamol compared to placebo and analysed using a paired t-test within group and unpaired t-test between groups.


Results

Participants 20 AAs and 20 HCs and (10 males and 10 females in each group) completed the study.Baseline demographics in table 1 show that age and body mass index were similar in both groups.AAs had numerically lower FEV 1 and FVC than HCs, but this did not reach statistical significance.


The effect of mannitol inhalation challenge on cough outcomes

The cough end-points from Visit 2 baseline mannitol challenge are reported in table 2. The geometric mean±SD CDR was significantly higher in AAs when compared to HCs (7.32±3.67 versus 5.63±1.84)( p=0.023), and this difference was driven by the AA females ( p=0.008) with no difference observed between AA males versus HC males (p=0.588).Emax, ED50, C2 and C5 were not different in AAs compared to HCs overall; however, in females the ED50 and C2 were significantly lower in AAs compared to HCs ( p<0.05) indicating that females with asthma have a higher cough response to mannitol than females without asthma.No cough outcomes measured at Visit 2 mannitol challenge were different between males with versus without asthma.Comparing Emax between sexes, females had a significantly higher Emax than males ( p<0.01).


The effect of salbutamol on bronchoconstriction and cough responses to mannitol

In HCs there was no significant fall in FEV 1 during mannitol challenges.In AAs, mannitol challenge induced 8.11±2.32%fall in FEV 1 from baseline, which was significantly inhibited to 3.19±1.16%by salbutamol treatment ( p=0.03), resulting in a level of bronchoconstriction similar to that observed in the HC group (figure 2a).

GEE modelling demonstrated that salbutamol significantly reduced Emax when compared to placebo in both the AA group (geometric mean 7.07 coughs (95% CI 6. 28   The cough end-points CDR, C2 and C5 were compared by group and intervention and reported in table 3. CDR, C2 and C5 measured in the AA group improved after salbutamol treatment compared to placebo with median differences being −5.83 coughs per 100 mg, 40 mg and 200 mg respectively (all p<0.05).CDR, C2 and C5 measured in the HC group also showed numerical improvements after salbutamol treatment but did not reach statistical significance (table 3).


Sex differences in cough responses to mannitol inhalation cough challenge after salbutamol

Females in the HC group when compared to their male counterparts had significantly higher CDR ( p=0.046) and lower C2 ( p=0.042), and both HC and AA female groups had lower ED50 ( p<0.001) compared to males despite having a similar fall in %FEV 1 (figure 3a), thereby illustrating a different strength of relationship between cough and fall in FEV 1 in males versus females.Asthmatic females demonstrated a higher cough response to mannitol than asthmatic males; after salbutamol treatment the geometric mean Emax was 10.75 coughs (95% CI 7.36-16.77) in females versus 3.55 coughs (95% CI 1.06-6.04) in males ( p<0.001), and after placebo treatment Emax was 12.88 coughs (95% CI 10.30-16.2) in females versus 4.83 coughs (95% CI 3.57-6.68)in males ( p<0.001) (Figure 3a).

Amongst healthy controls, females exhibited the highest Emax to mannitol after treatment with placebo compared to salbutamol (geometric mean 10.28 coughs (95% CI 8.26-12.29)versus 6.29 coughs (5.17-7.40))and also had a higher Emax to mannitol after treatment with placebo compared to salbutamol than males ( p<0.001) (Figure 3b).

It seems that despite females displaying heightened cough responses in contrast to males, the administration of salbutamol elicits a diminished cough response in both sexes.Overall, across both   groups, salbutamol inhibited Emax in males by 52.24% and in females by 29.06% with no significant difference between sexes ( p=0.063).


Discussion

In this study we demonstrated that mannitol inhalation evokes cough and bronchoconstriction in participants with asthma, while evoking only cough in healthy controls.Salbutamol pre-treatment reduced mannitol-evoked coughing and bronchoconstriction in the asthmatic patients, which could suggest that these outcomes are linked.In HCs, however, salbutamol reduced cough without coincident changes in airway calibre, demonstrating that mannitol-evoked coughs may not be related to bronchoconstriction in these individuals, and raising the possibility of alternate mechanisms for mannitol-evoked coughing in asthmatic patients.[11] conducted hypertonic saline cough provocation tests with salbutamol treatment to evaluate sensorineural dysfunction in individuals with asthma.findings demonstrated that variations in saline cough sensitivity between controls and asthmatic patients was not attributed to variances in the extent of induced bronchoconstriction, nor related to airway inflammation or hyperresponsiveness Our study utilising inhaled mannitol challenge also demonstrated that pretreatment with salbutamol diminished both the effect of bronchoconstriction and the increased cough response to mannitol, similar to the observations made with hypertonic saline and aligning with the conclusions drawn in the KOSKELA et al. study.Following salbutamol administration in patients who did not experience bronchoconstriction (HCs), no significant alterations in FEV 1 were observed; nonetheless, it did induce modifications in cough responses to mannitol.Additionally, after administration of salbutamol in AAs, we observed an FEV 1 similar to that seen in HCs, which coincided with a decrease in cough response.However, AAs still exhibited a high cough response to mannitol after treatment with salbutamol suggesting a sensorineural dysfunction in asthmatic patients aligning with KOSKELA et al.'s findings.We cannot dismiss the possibility that airway inflammation contributes to cough responses in asthma.BRANNAN and colleagues (2003) [15] demonstrated that mannitol inhalation increases osmolarity in the airway lumen, causing mast cell activation and cysteinyl leukotriene release leading to bronchoconstriction.Although we did not measure airway inflammation in our study participants, AAs have more mast cells in their airways compared to HCs [15,16].We observed bronchoconstriction coincident with coughing after mannitol inhalation in our asthmatic participants, showing a positive association between the two outcomes.Our findings also corroborate previous studies showing that mannitol leads to airway narrowing in AAs but not in HCs [14].Investigating the effects of combination therapy inhaled corticosteroid/ salmeterol on mannitol-evoked coughs may provide valuable insights into both its anti-inflammatory and bronchodilator properties.While this study measured cough frequency in AAs based on receptor sensitivity, it did not account for cough intensity or the strength of the expulsive effort.Measuring intensity would require more advanced equipment than the cough monitor used in this study.Exploring this aspect in future studies could provide valuable insights into cough dynamics and severity.


KOSKELA et al. (2008)

Cough and bronchoconstriction are critical airway reflexes that protect the lungs from harmful agents.They can be triggered by stimuli in both the larynx and tracheobronchial tree, with cough predominantly initiated at sensitive sites like the larynx and airway branch points, while bronchoconstriction can be activated throughout the airways [17].Different receptors, such as rapidly adapting receptors, slowly adapting receptors, C-fibres and Aδ fibres, play key roles in regulating these responses [17].Although these reflexes share certain receptors, they are mediated through distinct neural pathways, and their interaction remains a promising area for future research, especially in the context of cough challenge studies.

Mannitol is a commonly used indirect bronchial challenge agent that triggers bronchoconstriction in individuals with asthma, and it also causes cough [18,19].In addition to causing inflammation and bronchoconstriction, there are other possible mechanisms by which mannitol could cause cough, such as direct and indirect neuronal stimulation.Mannitol inhaled as a powder can directly stimulate the mechanical Aδ fibres present in the airways, leading to cough reflex activation resulting in cough [20].This is particularly apparent when coughing occurs early at low doses of mannitol even in AAs, without any bronchoconstriction yet developing.Mannitol can also induce degranulation of mast cells, resulting in the release of mediators like prostaglandins and bradykinins that can sensitise airway nerves, leading to cough reflex activation [18,19].The degranulation of mast cells is also a cause of bronchoconstriction [18,19].

Salbutamol is a bronchodilator that is commonly used to treat asthma and other respiratory diseases.Its primary mechanism of action is through the activation of β 2 adrenergic receptors, which results in relaxation of bronchial smooth muscle and the subsequent reversal of bronchoconstriction [3,5].Salbutamol may also have an effect on neuronal processes in the airways.Activation of β 2 receptors can lead to an influx of potassium ions that hyperpolarise the nerve membrane and decrease neuronal excitability [7].Additionally, β 2 receptor activation may interact with intracellular calcium release and secondary messenger pathways, which can then modulate transient receptor potential and sodium channels and inhibit neuronal transmission [7,21].One study showed the possible role of transient receptor potential ankyrin-1 (TRPA1) as a key integrator of interactions between the immune and nervous systems in the airways, driving asthmatic airway inflammation [22].It has been previously demonstrated that salbutamol reduces cough responses to inhaled citric acid, potentially by altering neuroreceptor sensitivity linked to airway dilatation [23].In addition, an earlier study showed that although a bronchodilator did not alter airway sensitivity to capsaicin, it did reduce coughing in patients with asthma [24].These changes in receptor sensitivity may underlie the reduction in cough reflex, suggesting that bronchodilators not only enhance bronchial relaxation but also reduce irritant-triggered coughing by modulating sensory pathways in the airways.It is possible that these neuronal processes may play a role in the regulation of the cough reflex in a subset of patients, but further research is needed to confirm their importance.A current study is evaluating the effects of TRPA1 antagonism on objective cough frequency and mannitol-induced cough in patients with refractory chronic cough, both with and without asthma (ClinicalTrials.govID:NCT05660850) [25].

Sex differences in cough are also prevalent as females were associated with a higher prevalence of nocturnal and non-productive cough [26].Similarly, in Canada, a large population-based postal survey of asthma suggested nocturnal cough was more commonly reported than wheezing, especially in women (37.6% versus 27.6% in males) [2].Sex differences in evoked coughs have not been specifically described in asthma but have been repeatedly shown in both healthy volunteers and chronic cough patients, with females demonstrating heightened capsaicin responses compared with males [27,28].Our study corroborates these studies as females demonstrated a heightened response to mannitol-evoked coughs compared with males in both HCs and AAs.Our study also highlights that if pharmacodynamic modelling approaches are taken and nonlinear dose-response curves are constructed with estimates of Emax and https://doi.org/10.1183/23120541.00815-2024ED50 calculated then these can discriminate between health and disease, and provide more sensitivity to detect effects of intervention.The commonly used C2 and C5 end-points in cough challenges are often more variable with some not achieving C5, hence can be limiting in detecting change in clinical trials.

Importantly, this study does not provide evidence that salbutamol improves objective cough frequency, or that salbutamol monotherapy is a potential treatment option to reduce coughing, especially in patients with asthma.Clinicians should avoid overuse of SABA in keeping with GINA guidelines.Furthermore, patients with refractory chronic cough with asthma or cough variant asthma were not included in this study, and the data do not infer clinical benefit in these patient groups.

In conclusion, the mechanism by which mannitol inhalation causes cough in asthma is likely to be multifactorial, involving a combination of direct neuronal stimulation, indirect osmotic and inflammatory effects and bronchoconstriction.Salbutamol treatment reduced mannitol-evoked coughs to a similar extent in both AAs and HC.In HCs the mannitol-evoked cough and inhibition by salbutamol occurred in the absence of any changes in FEV 1 , suggesting inhibition of cough by salbutamol has a component that is independent of muscle tone.Inhibition of cough responses by salbutamol through mechanisms independent of its smooth muscle function warrants further examination.
