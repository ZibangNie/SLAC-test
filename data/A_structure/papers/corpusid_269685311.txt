INTRODUCTION

Tracheotomy is often performed in Otorhinolaryngology-Head and Neck Surgery (ORL-HNS). 1 Tracheotomy can be done electively or urgently, depending on the degree of upper airway obstruction and underlying disease. An earlier study conducted at the ORL-HNS Department of Dr. Soetomo General Academic Hospital (RSDS) in 1996-1997 and 1990-1992 found laryngeal carcinoma was the most common diagnosis in tracheotomy patients. 1 Indications for tracheotomy can be grouped into four, to overcome upper airway obstruction, remove secretions from the tracheobronchial tract, support mechanical ventilation, and provide airway protection in patients who cannot be intubated under general anesthesia.

Patients with tumors in the airways, vocal cord paralysis, swelling, strictures, or abnormal airway anatomy are indicated for tracheotomy due to airway obstruction. An airway infection, such as acute epiglottitis or laryngotracheobronchitis, can cause upper airway obstruction. Indications for airway protection and secretions are in patients with spinal, cerebrovascular, or brain injuries, so they cannot breathe properly, loss of laryngeal reflex and inability to swallow. Hence, they are at risk of aspiration. Tracheotomy facilitates the suction of secretions from the bronchi in patients who are physiologically unable to clear these secretions. 2, 3 Obstruction in the main airway will cause a lack of oxygen (hypoxia) because inspiration and expiration will be disrupted, thereby increasing the body's carbon dioxide levels. 4 Patients who come to the hospital with airway obstruction will require a tracheotomy to maintain airway patency. [5][6][7] Long-term mechanical ventilation or placement of a respirator is required in patients with chronic respiratory failure. Airway protection can also be performed when the patient cannot be intubated under general anesthesia, for example, surgery for a tumor adjacent to the airway. Tracheotomy also reduces dead space in the upper airway, such as the oral cavity, around the tongue and pharynx. With a stoma, all inhaled oxygen will enter the lungs; nothing will be left in the dead space. This is useful in patients with lung damage whose vital capacity is reduced. 2, 3 According to the time of occurrence, tracheotomy complications are divided into three, complications during surgery, early postoperative, and late postoperative complications. Tracheotomy complications vary from 5% to 40%, and mortality by 2%. Complications increase two to five times in emergencies, critical patients, and young children. Complications to be studied are early postoperative complications, including subcutaneous emphysema, bleeding, pneumothorax, and death. The tracheotomy profile, including the degree of airway obstruction, underlying disease, following procedures, and complications, is important. This is expected to spur motivation to reduce the occurrence of complications. The latest data regarding the profile of tracheotomy patients at ORL-HNS RSDS does not yet exist, so it is necessary to research to complete the data.


METHODS

This research has received approval from the Health Research Ethics Committee of Dr. Soetomo General Academic Hospital Surabaya. This type of research is descriptive retrospective using secondary data in the form of medical records with total sampling. The population in this study was the medical records of patients undergoing tracheotomy with the doctor in charge of ORL-HNS RSDS from January 2015 to December 2019. Meanwhile, the sample for this study was medical records that met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The inclusion criteria were medical records of patients who underwent tracheotomy with the doctor in charge of ORL-HNS at RSDS from January 2015 to December 2019. Exclusion criteria are incomplete medical record data. The variables examined in this study were gender, age, degree of airway obstruction, diagnosis, procedures following tracheotomy, and types of complications. Data were collected and processed descriptively using the IBM SPSS Statistics version 23.0 for Windows.

During a period of five years, from January 2015 to December 2019, patients who underwent tracheotomies with the doctor in charge of ORL-HNS at Dr. Soetomo General Academic Hospital were 590 patients. DISCUSSION

The results of this study indicated that there were more tracheotomies performed on male patients than female patients, with a ratio of 3 It is possible that this could be related to the diagnosis of patients undergoing tracheotomy as laryngeal carcinoma which mostly suffered by men, as in a study in Nigeria in 2018. There are also those who attribute this proportion to the fact that men experience trauma more often, which in their research, is the most common cause of tracheotomies. [10][11][12] This study found that the youngest age of the patient who underwent tracheotomy by ORL-HNS RSDS was 45 days, and the oldest was 86 years, with an average of 52 years. Age was divided every 10 years, as was done in India in 2017, Bangladesh in 2015, Nepal in 2014, and Nigeria in 2004. 6-9 The largest age group was 51-60, with 167 patients (28.31%). The results of this study are almost in accordance with several studies that have been conducted with an average age of 55.3 years, and other studies found an age range of 0-87 years with an average of 55 years, the largest group at the age of 51-70 years. 10, 13 Slightly different results were found in Finland in 2019, with an age range of 19-92 years and an average of 65 years; other studies have found an age range of 1-89 years, an average of 41.9 years and the most at the age of 30-60 years. There were also those who found a range of 1-88 years with an average of 44 years, most in the 21-30 age group.

The tracheotomy is carried out based on the condition of the degree of upper airway obstruction in the patient. The degree of upper airway obstruction was enforced using Jackson's criteria. Patients with grades 1 and 2 underwent elective tracheotomies, while grades 3 and 4 in 1992 found 40 cases of tracheotomy with tumors of the larynx and oral cavity, which was the most cases, 19 patients (47.5%). 1982 there were 249 tracheotomy cases, and diphtheria laryngitis was the most, with 188 patients (75.5%).

Head and neck malignancy as the most common diagnosis was found in a 2019 study in Finland, followed by infection consisting of deep neck abscess and epiglottitis, bilateral paralysis of the vocal cords, and trauma. 5 A study in Portugal in 2016 also obtained similar results; tumors of the larynx and pharynx ranked the most (44.6%), followed by deep neck abscess (19.6%), bilateral paralysis of the vocal cords (10.7%), subglottic stenosis, and trauma. 10 Similarly, a study in Nepal in 2014 had the most tumors of the larynx and hypopharynx, then infection and trauma. 6 Based on research by Romdhoni and Rijal in 2018 stated that out of 162 patients with deep neck abscesses at Dr. Soetomo from 2014-2017, there were complications of upper airway obstruction followed by tracheotomy in 10 patients (6.17%). 15


Procedures following tracheotomy

In this study, a biopsy is the most followed procedure in tracheotomy. Tumor surgery includes tumor excision, cordectomy, tonsillectomy, partial glossectomy, and thyroidectomy, the second most common procedure, followed by incision and abscess drainage. Tumor surgery was the biggest number of procedures following tracheotomy from a study in Finland in 2019, followed by a biopsy, then an incision for drainage of deep neck abscesses. 5 These results are also in accordance with a 2012 American study which stated that oncology procedures were the most performed concurrent with a tracheotomy (51%), followed by surgery for maxillofacial trauma (43%). 14 Tracheotomy is performed surgically to provide airway protection during diagnostic or definitive surgery and reduce the length of stay in the Intensive Care Unit. There are also suggestions that tracheotomy provides better comfort, improved nutrition, reduced risk of laryngeal damage, easier tracheobronchial toileting, and reduced anatomical dead space by 50%. 14-16  underwent urgent or cito tracheotomies. In patients with suspected tumors, tracheotomy is performed earlier without waiting for the patient to become dyspnea. In this study, most patients underwent tracheotomy with Jackson 1 upper airway obstruction (51.86%). The second largest number was tracheotomy patients without signs of upper airway obstruction (35.42%), followed by tracheotomy patients with Jackson 2 (6.44%), then Jackson 3 (5.25%), and finally Jackson 4 (0.51%). Most tracheotomies by ORL-HNS for January 2015 to December 2019 based on the degree of upper airway obstruction were elective, followed by urgent, then cito. In accordance with the results of a study in Jakarta in 2015 which explained that most tracheotomies carried out at ORL-HNS Department of Cipto Mangunkusumo Hospital (RSCM) from January 2011 to December 2013 were mostly elective. Patients with impending upper airway obstruction (54.4%), followed by upper airway obstruction Jackson 1 (25.6%), Jackson 3 (11.2%) and Jackson 2 (8.8%). Diagnostic or definitive measures of the underlying disease usually follow patients undergoing elective tracheotomy.


Diagnosis of the tracheotomy patient

The biggest number of patients diagnosed with underwent tracheotomy by ORL-HNS from January 2015 to December 2019 was laryngeal carcinoma (44.58%), followed by nasopharyngeal carcinoma (9.15%) and LNH (7.46%). Laryngeal tumors (7.12%) that have not been established because the patient hasn't undergone a diagnostic test are the fourth leading diagnosis. After the tumor as a whole (87.29%), a neurological diagnosis consisting of bilateral paralysis of the vocal cords ranked second (3.39%), followed by neck abscess (3.05%) and stenosis of the larynx (2.37%). Fibrostenosis of the trachea is rare and can have a variety of causes, including trauma from intubation and tracheostomy procedures, malignancy, systemic inflammatory disease, and infection. 14 The results of this study are still the same as previous data conducted at the ORL-HNS department RSDS for a period of two years; from 1996 to 1997, there were 101 tracheotomy cases, with most cases being tumors, specifically laryngeal carcinoma in 45 patients (43.5%). RSDS ORL-HNS data from 1990 to 1992, with 138 tracheotomy cases, also found laryngeal carcinoma as the top diagnosis in 63 cases (45.7%). Data from research in the ORL-HNS department of RSCM Jakarta Tracheotomy complications vary from 5% to 40%, and mortality by 2%. Complications increase two to five times in emergencies, critical patients, and young children. 1,4 The complications studied were early postoperative complications, including subcutaneous emphysema, bleeding, pneumothorax, and death. Early complications after tracheotomy were experienced by 11.02% of the total 590 patients who underwent tracheotomy by ORL-HNS. The most common complication is subcutaneous emphysema. Bleeding and pneumothorax accompanied by subcutaneous emphysema occurred in the same number of cases, the second most common complication. In five cases, patients who died after tracheotomy were in the third place of complication, followed by subcutaneous emphysema accompanied by bleeding, and pneumothorax without subcutaneous emphysema as the last ranking complication.

Subcutaneous emphysema as the most common complication of tracheotomy was also found in Jakarta and Bangladesh in 2015. 9,17 A study in Portugal in 2016 found complications occurred in 12.2% of tracheotomy patients; the most was bleeding, then surgical wound infection, and subcutaneous emphysema, no patient died. 10 No tracheotomy patients who died were also found in a 2014 study in Iran, with complications experienced by 32.5% of patients; the most was bleeding (15%). Subcutaneous emphysema was experienced by 2.5% of patients; none had a pneumothorax. 16 Other studies described complications occurring in 13.2% of tracheotomy patients; the most were stoma infection in 4.5% of patients, followed by subcutaneous emphysema in 2.3%, and bleeding, no patient died. 6 In contrast to what was obtained from a study in Nigeria in 2004, the most complications were death (25%) followed by stoma infection (6.7%), laryngeal stenosis (6.7%), subcutaneous emphysema in 4.5% of patients, and bleeding was the least common complication (2.3%). 7 After the tracheotomy procedure, crepitus can be found in the patient's face, neck, chest, abdomen, arms and back, which is a sign of subcutaneous emphysema. Subcutaneous emphysema is the most common complication, generally minimal, limited to the neck, and may resolve within seven days. It may also indicate other underlying complications, such as pneumothorax or tracheal injury. Subcutaneous emphysema occurs due to lacerations to the anterior or posterior tracheal wall that allow air to leak freely into the adjacent subcutaneous space. Sutures that are too tight on the skin or subcutaneous tissue, tracheal incisions that are too large, tracheotomy tube sizes that do not fit, and the patient coughing excessively are factors that cause subcutaneous emphysema. 9,18, 19 Pneumothorax after tracheotomy is one of the complications that often occurs; the incidence is higher in children than adults because the location of the apex of the pleura in children in the neck is in a relatively higher position, so it is easily injured and more vulnerable to lowlying tracheotomies. The mechanism of the pneumothorax may originate from direct pleural injury from contact with a knife during the incision, or air enters through the deep neck fascia towards the mediastinum due to excessive pushing when inserting the tracheotomy cannula so that the tracheotomy cannula enters the wrong way, tracheotomy cannula enters between the anterior tracheal wall and the anterior soft tissue of the mediastinum, or from ruptured pulmonary bleb in severe shortness of breath patients who is straining to breathe, gasping or coughing vigorously during procedures under local anesthesia. 20, 21 Plain chest X-rays do not need to be done routinely after tracheotomy but can be done in the patient's clinical condition. This supports the suspicion of pneumothorax; for example, tightness does not improve after tracheotomy, and PaO2 remains low. Pneumothorax in patients is treated by installing watersealed drainage (WSD); in spontaneous chest tube, pneumothorax can expand the lungs and overcome trapped air. 20, 21 Pneumothorax can be prevented by stabilizing the airway before tracheotomy and limiting the dissection to remain in the midline. Tracheotomy cannula insertion must be done with direct visualization and a good field of view. The suction of secretions from the stoma using a flexible suction catheter should be performed immediately after insertion of the tracheotomy cannula. The anesthesiologist should immediately confirm end-tidal CO2. Oxygenation through the tracheotomy cannula can be delayed until confirmed by the operator to avoid insufflation into the mediastinum. If necessary, the tracheotomy cannula can be removed, and a retractor temporarily placed in the stoma to maintain temporary patency and re-inserted under direct visualization. 22 Bleeding is one of the most common intraoperative complications during tracheotomy. The most common sources of bleeding are from the anterior jugular vein and the thyroid gland. Small venous branches must be controlled because they can be a source of intraoperative and postoperative bleeding. Laceration of the carotid artery or internal jugular vein can also occur if the incision is not in the midline during surgery. 23 Intraoperative mortality is associated with cardiopulmonary arrest or preexisting tracheal injury. 14 Death after tracheotomy can be related to other co-morbidities or can also be due to blockage of the tracheotomy cannula due to accumulation of secretions. Research in the United States in 2018 concerning death 30 days after a tracheotomy explained that death was related to the patient's comorbid diseases, especially liver disease, congestive heart failure, and peripheral vascular disease. 7, 20 The use of fully parametric statistical analysis to support the first in-depth report on Indonesian NPC survival is a strength of this study. The results of this study can be used as a foundation for preventing tracheotomy difficulties. The most recent data on tracheotomy profiles in ORL-HNS can also be completed using this data.

The study also has limitations. Our study is retrospective. Thus, it heavily depends on data available from current medical records. Using routine data may come with risks, such as potential coding mistakes made by people who create and manage clinical records. The retrospective nature of the current study means that there is some inherent baseline bias and variation, which may undermine the results' validity.


CONCLUSION

The results of this study found that the patients who underwent tracheotomy by ORL-HNS doctors during the period January 2015-December 2019 at RSUD Dr. Soetomo Surabaya were 590 people, the number of men more than women, with the largest age group being 51-60 years. Upper airway obstruction Jackson 1 was found the most, followed by tracheotomy patients with impending upper airway obstruction, then Jackson 2, Jackson 3, and Jackson 4. The most common diagnosis in patients undergoing tracheotomy was laryngeal carcinoma, followed by nasopharyngeal carcinoma and LNH. The most procedure following tracheotomy is a biopsy. Tumor surgery includes tumor excision, cordectomy, tonsillectomy, partial glossectomy, and thyroidectomy, the second most common procedure, followed by incision and abscess drainage. The most common complication is subcutaneous emphysema, then bleeding and pneumothorax accompanied by subcutaneous emphysema. Patients who died had the third most complications, followed by subcutaneous emphysema accompanied by bleeding, then pneumothorax without subcutaneous emphysema.

The data of this study can be used as a basis for preventing tracheotomy complications by paying more attention to tracheotomy step by step according to established standards. This data can also be used to complete the latest information regarding tracheotomy profiles in ORL-HNS.
