A promising prognostic model for patients with AIDS-related lymphoma in the combination antiretroviral therapy era

Objective: For patients with AIDS-related lymphoma (ARL), optimizing risk stratification is crucial to creating customized therapy regimens and enhancing their prognosis.This study aims to develop a more precisely predicted prognostic model for ARL patients.Design: A 7-year retrospective cohort study (2016)(2017)(2018)(2019)(2020)(2021)(2022)(2023) of 136 ARL patients at a single institution randomly allocated training (n ¼ 109) and validation (n ¼ 27) cohorts.Methods:We assessed the relationship between HIV, lymphoma, and patient-specific factors and overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) by univariate and multivariate analyses.Results:The median age was 48 (IQR: 40-56) years, 76.5% were men.The overall 2-year OS and PFS were 52.9 and 48.5%, respectively.In the multivariate analysis, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG-PS), central nervous system (CNS) involvement, elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), Hemoglobin (Hb), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and chemotherapy cycles were independently related to OS.A new prognosis score was generated with these variables, including ECOG at least 2, CNS involvement, elevated LDH, Hb less than 130 g/l, NLR more than 5, and not exceeding 5 chemotherapy cycles, with 1 point for each variable, for a maximum of 6.The area under the curve and C-index of the new model were 0.79 and 0.76, respectively.Our model showed better risk stratification in ARL patients than aaIPI, NCCN-IPI, and ARL-IPI.Conclusion:In this study, we created a prognostic model for ARL patients that is clinically straightforward, feasible, and has good predictive power.Compared to the NCCN-IPI and the aaIPI, this model is more discriminative and predictively accurate in risk stratification and high-risk population identification.

Introduction

Infection with the HIV dramatically raises the risk of malignant tumor development.Malignancies account for about 28% of HIV-related mortality, and AIDS-related lymphoma (ARL) is the last diagnosis for over 40% of people with HIV [1][2][3].

The prognosis for ARL patients is still not good, despite significant advancements in the treatment of HIVnegative patients' lymphoma.Patients with ARL are more likely to present with advanced disease and highly malignant pathology, as well as immunodeficiency and a high risk of infection.Before ART was introduced, HIV communities had a 25-150 times higher risk of NHL (non-Hodgkin's lymphoma) than the general population [4,5].Patients in the post-cART era still have a 25-fold increased incidence of NHL, despite significant improvements in the physical status, immune function, and life expectancy of people with HIV.Additionally, the risk of Hodgkin's lymphoma incidence has not even decreased compared to the precARTera [6][7][8][9], which is still one of the leading causes of death in AIDS patients today.

Several prognostic scores have been presented to categorize risk and identify those who have poorer results on normal care.The International Prognostic Index (IPI), which was proposed in 1993 [10], is the most important and commonly used criterion for determining the prognosis of patients with aggressive non-Hodgkin's lymphomas, both HIV-positive and HIV-negative [11].However, the application of cART significantly prolongs the survival of patients with ARL, which is not ideal for identifying high-risk subgroups [12].The IPI must be used in conjunction with the patient's baseline condition, type of pathology, and comorbidity, among other factors, to assess patients' conditions and prognosis [12].A growing body of research is dedicated to refining risk stratification for personalized therapeutic approaches, including tools like the age-adjusted International Prognostic Index (aa-IPI), National Comprehensive Cancer Network International Prognostic Index (NCCN-IPI), Revised International Prognostic Index (R-IPI), International Metabolic Prognostic Index (IMPI), Elderly Prognostic Index (EPI), Relapsed/ Refractory International Prognostic Index (RR-IPI), and AIDS-related lymphoma International Prognostic Index (ARL-IPI) [12][13][14][15][16]. Existing models developed using HIV-negative populations may not fully reflect the unique biological features of HIV-related lymphomas.Although the ARL-IPI was created specifically for these patients, its real-world usefulness requires further testing across different hospitals and patient groups.

The molecular characteristics of the tumor microenvironment are highlighted by emerging prognostic scores [17]; certain immune markers and prognostic molecules, such as cytidine deaminase, BCL-2, MYC, Runt-related transcription factor 3 (RUNX3), RPS6KB1, and CDC2amplified gene, are thought to be biomarkers for determining a patient's prognosis and have been strongly linked to the prognosis of ARL patients [18][19][20][21].Nevertheless, these marker tests are costly, precise, and time-consuming, and they are challenging to implement in underdeveloped nations and areas.To screen high-risk patients as soon as possible, create customized treatment plans, and improve patient survival and prognosis, there is an urgent clinical need to identify more cost-effective and practical prognostic factors and set up a thorough and accurate prognostic assessment system.By assessing independent prognostic risk factors in patients with AIDS-related lymphoma, this study aims to develop a unique, user-friendly, and more precisely predicted prognostic risk stratification model for ARL patients.


Materials and methods


Patients and study design

The Yunnan Provincial Infectious Diseases Hospital/ Yunnan AIDS Care Center, the biggest HIV/AIDS referral hospital in Southwest China, reported 136 new cases of AIDS-related lymphoma between 2016 and 2023.The institutional review board of Yunnan Provincial Infectious Diseases Hospital approved this study, which was carried out by the Declaration of Helsinki.The WHO diagnostic criteria for the classification of hematological lymphomas were used to diagnose ARL, and patients who were lost to brief visits or who lacked crucial diagnostic and treatment data were omitted.

The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics and laboratory parameters were collected, including age at diagnosis, sex, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), pathological subtype, number of extranodal sites, Ann Arbor stage, B symptoms (night sweats, weight loss >10% over 6 months, and recurrent fever >38.38C), bulky mass (>7.5 cm), hemoglobin (Hb), albumin, b2-MG, D-Dimer, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), serum alanine aminotransferase (ALT), prior history of HIV, CD4 þ cell count, CD4/ CD8 ratio, cART condition, immunochemotherapy, cause of death, survival months, and treatment outcomes.

The optimal cut-off values for ALB, Hb, and NLR were obtained from the ROC curves as 32 g/l, 130 g/l, and 5, respectively.Variables deemed significant in the univariate Cox analysis were selected into the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis to determine independent prognostic variables using the Forward Stepwise (conditional LR) approach.These selected factors were then incorporated into the logistic regression model to estimate the predictive probability of outcomes and to construct the receiver operating characteristic curve.The area under the curve (AUC) and Harrell's concordance index (C-index) of the different models were compared to assess the predictive value of the newly developed model.


Outcomes and definitions

Overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) were the study's primary outcomes.August 31, 2024, was the last date for follow-up data.OS was defined as the interval between the diagnosis of lymphoma and the last follow-up or death from all causes.Time from lymphoma diagnosis to disease progression, relapse, or death from any cause was referred to as PFS.


Statistical analysis

Whereas categorical data were displayed as counts and percentages, continuous data were represented as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR).The x 2 test was used to analyze categorical variables, and the Mann-Whitney U test or t-test was used to evaluate continuous variables.The log-rank test was used to assess differences in survival curves, which were shown using Kaplan-Meier estimation.The Cox hazards model was used for both univariate and multivariate analyses, producing hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs).The optimal cutoff thresholds were determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.SPSS 25.0, GraphPad Prism 8, and R software were used for statistical analyses, with a significance level of P less than 0.05.

Development of the new prognostic model

ECOG PS at least 2, CNS involvement, elevated LDH, Hb less than 130 g/l, NLR more than 5, and not exceeding five chemotherapy cycles were used to build a novel prognostic model.Considering each factor's risk ratio and clinical usability, the model received a score of 1 for each feature.In this investigation, the maximum patient score was 6.Based on their scores, each patient in the training cohort was categorized as low-risk (score: 0-1; 12.8%), low-intermediate risk (score: 2-3; 45%), highintermediate risk (score: 4; 29.4%), and high-risk (score: 5-6; 12.8%).Figure 2 presents the Kaplan-Meier OS curves comparing risk stratification performance among the novel model, aaIPI, NCCN-IPI, and ARL-IPI in both training and validation cohorts.The proposed model outperforms aaIPI, NCCN-IPI, and ARL-IPI in terms of predicted accuracy, and is more able to distinguish across OS risk groups, especially highintermediate and high-risk groups.

The novel risk assessment model demonstrated superior predictive performance compared to established prognostic indices across both training and validation cohorts, as shown in Fig. 3.In training cohorts, the model achieved an AUC of 0.79 (95% CI: 0.72-0.87)for OS prediction, significantly surpassing existing models including aaIPI (0.61; 0.52-0.70),NCCN-IPI (0.58; 0.48-0.69),and ARL-IPI (0.56; 0.47-0.66).This performance advantage was further amplified in validation cohorts, where the new model attained an AUC of 0.84 (0.69-0.98) compared to 0.62 (0.44-0.80) for aaIPI, 0.50 (0.28-0.72) for NCCN-IPI, and 0.65 (0.46-0.84) for ARL-IPI.Concordance index analysis confirmed the model's enhanced discriminative ability, with C-index values of 0.76 in training cohorts (vs.0.60 for aaIPI, 0.58 for NCCN-IPI, and 0.57 for ARL-IPI) and


Discussion

In addition to immunodeficiency and a high risk of infection, patients with ARL frequently present with advanced disease and a high degree of malignancy.If treatment is delayed, even with chemotherapy and cART, they may worsen or even pass away in a matter of weeks or months.The prognosis is poor, thus early diagnosis and treatment are crucial [22].Building precise and useful models is therefore essential to direct risk assessment and therapeutic care of ARL patients.ECOG PS at least 2, CNS involvement, elevated LDH, Hb less than 130 g/l, NLR more than 5, and not exceeding five chemotherapy cycles are the six independent prognostic markers that were used in this study to construct a new prognostic model with straightforward and good predictive ability.In terms of differentiation, risk stratification, and predicted accuracy for various risk categories of ARL patients, the novel model performed better than the conventional aaIPI NCCN-IPI and ARL-IPI.The majority of the risk factors in the new model are inexpensive, reproducible, and easy to measure, making it simple to use in clinical settings as well as in less developed economies.It may also prove to be a useful predictive model with strong discriminatory power and clinical utility for ARL patients in the cART era.

Although it is now generally acknowledged that ARL patients have a higher chance of dying than the general population, current prognostic models for HIV-negative lymphoma patients are primarily employed for risk assessment of ARL patients [23,24].To help with diagnosis and treatment choices, it is crucial to optimize   risk assessment for patients with HIV-positive lymphoma.The most popular score for non-Hodgkin's lymphoma is the IPI, although it cannot identify high-risk groups among individuals with ARL [25].Despite improvements in its ability to distinguish between clinically significant risk groups, the NCCN-IPI, which is based on patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL at National Comprehensive Cancer Network centers and has up-to-date data, is still unable to accurately identify subgroups of patients with ARL [26].

1148 AIDS 2025, Vol 39 No 9 In contrast to the ARL-IPI proposed by Barta et al. [16], which incorporates HIV-score (CD4 þ cell count, viral load, prior AIDS history), extranodal involvement, elevated LDH, Ann Arbor stage more than 2, and ECOG more than 1, our prognostic model excludes HIV-related biomarkers.This modification aligns with contemporary evidence demonstrating that HIV-associated indicators lack independent prognostic value in ARL patients receiving combination antiretroviral therapy (cART) [27], a conclusion further validated by our multivariate Cox regression analysis.The enhanced predictive accuracy of our model underscores the growing clinical relevance of nutritional status (Hb), systemic inflammation (NLR), and treatment adherence (chemotherapy cycle) in modern ARL management.These findings emphasize the necessity of incorporating parameters that reflect both tumor biology and host-microenvironment interactions.Subsequent development of prognostic tools and clinical trial protocols should prioritize rigorous assessment of these clinically actionable factors.

A pooled analysis of 1546 patients with ARL across various eras by Barta et al. [27] found that the use of cART not only effectively improves the prognosis of ARL patients, but also attenuated the prognostic impact of HIV-related influences like viral load, CD4 þ T-cell count, and prior history of AIDS.This is in contrast to the period before the widespread use of cART, when characteristics associated with HIV infection, such as HIV viral load, CD4 þ T cell count, etc., were repeatedly shown to be effective prognostic factors in ARL [16].HIV infection is no longer a major risk factor in the modern era, as evidenced by the lack of statistically significant differences in HIVrelated influencing factors in this study.

CNS involvement is common in ARL and is associated with a poor prognosis [28].The incidence of CNS involvement in AR-NHL patients has declined in the cART era [29].Furthermore, the incidence of CNS involvement is higher in AR-DLBCL compared to HIVnegative DLBCL patients [30].CNS involvement was found to be an independent prognostic factor with a greater hazard ratio than other prognostic factors in a study by Zhao et al. [31] on the prognosis of AIDS-related DLBCL.

Neutrophil-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), one of the inflammatory markers, has been used in many studies as a prognostic factor in the assessment of various malignancies.NLR is superior to neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, etc., alone, as it reflects both systemic inflammation (neutrophils) and host immunity (lymphocytes).NLR was initially identified by Porrata et al. [32] as an independent bad prognostic marker for lymphoma patients receiving R-CHOP.NLR was also found to be a predictive factor in lymphoma patients in two Asian patient investigations [33,34].

In the clinical context, Hb is a widely used laboratory biomarker of a patient's nutritional status and anemia that is simple to detect, reasonably priced, and very reproducible.Anemia is a significant prognostic factor for OS in patients with aggressive NHL, according to several studies.[35,36], whereas in other malignant lymphomas, anemia has been formalized as an indicator for their prognostic evaluation [37].There are several theories as to how Hb affects the prognosis of lymphoma patients, including autoimmune hemolysis, bone marrow infiltration, and the effects of cytokines from endocrine and paracrine sources.Tumor necrosis factor-alpha and the interleukins IL-1, IL-6, and IL-10 have been proposed to inhibit erythropoietin synthesis and reduce the erythroid precursors' sensitivity to erythropoietin [38,39].High IL-6 levels were found to be a major cause of anemia in patients with diffuse large Bcell lymphoma (DLBCL) in the study by Tisi et al. [40], Furthermore, it was discovered that individuals with DLBCL who had significantly higher serum levels of IL-6 had a worse prognosis, proving that IL-6 is an independent predictor of outcome [41].

LDH, one of the five IPI indicators, is directly associated with higher indices of tumor growth and proliferation, maintenance, metastasis, and tumor survival and has been found to be a predictor of poor prognosis in many malignancies [42].One of the basic metabolic processes that take place during malignant transformation is the Warburg effect, which occurs in many tumor cells when the metabolism changes to high glucose uptake and enhanced lactate generation regardless of oxygen supply [43].LDH is a crucial enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of pyruvate to lactate, which is intimately linked to tumor growth and proliferation.Tumor cells use 30 times more glucose and generate 40 times more lactate than normal cells [44].In the liver, spleen, and bone marrow, receptor-mediated phagocytosis of macrophages typically removes LDH [45].However, the organism might not be able to sufficiently eliminate LDH from the tumor when it develops malignant lesions or even lymphoma.This would lead to elevated LDH, which would indicate a higher disease burden.

Hb less than 130 g/l, NLR more than 5, and not exceeding five chemotherapy cycles were found to be predictive of worse OS in this study's suggested novel prognostic model, which integrates six independent prognostic markers.With a two-year OS rate of 73.6%, patients who underwent six or more chemotherapy cycles had a significantly longer survival time.The prognosis for patients who received fewer than six cycles of chemotherapy in this study -including those who stopped the treatment and those who were unable to finish chemotherapy because of the rapid progression of their disease -was comparatively unsatisfactory.The 2-year OS rate for patients who were unable to finish chemotherapy because of the rapid progression of their disease was even lower, at less than 20%.In comparison to aaIPI and NCCN-IPI, the new model demonstrated noticeably superior risk discrimination and performed well in predicting patient OS.A particularly high-risk group of patients with a 2-year OS rate of less than 20% was also identified by the new prognostic model; these patients should receive more aggressive treatment in clinical care to increase their chances of survival.

Additionally, this study has limitations.First off, because this study is retrospective in nature, several individuals who lacked crucial clinical information were excluded; nonetheless, given the limited sample size, this had little bearing on the study's findings.Second, the data originated exclusively from a single Chinese clinical center, which may introduce selection bias.The limited sample sizes of both the training and validation cohorts (109 and 27 cases, respectively) constrain the generalizability of our newly developed model to other institutions.While internal validation was implemented, external validation through multicenter studies with larger cohorts remains essential to confirm broader applicability.We are currently establishing collaborative partnerships with other regional clinical centers to enable future validation across expanded and more diverse populations.
