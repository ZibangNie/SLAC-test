Frontline Therapy in Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia
October 27, 20239AC95A626FE87E33F8A65BB9EBA9B90510.1159/000534730Received: June 17, 2023 Accepted: October 19, 2023Frontline therapy • Pathway inhibitors
Background: The treatment landscape of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) has tremendously evolved in the last decades, thanks to the introduction of more effective therapies.Summary: Frontline therapy for patients with CLL includes chemoimmunotherapy (CIT) and pathway inhibitors (PIs) (i.e., bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitors and BCL2 inhibitors); the latter has proved to be more effective than CIT mainly in patients with high-risk features (e.g., TP53 aberrations and unmutated IGHV) with acceptable toxicity.Combinations of PIs are playing the protagonist role as frontline therapy for CLL.Key Messages: In this article, the management of treatment-naïve patients with CLL is discussed.

Introduction

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is the most frequent form of leukemia in Western countries [1].The disease arises from clonal B cells with a distinctive im-munophenotype (i.e., SmIg weak , CD5 + , CD19 + , CD20 + , CD23 + , CD200 + ), which accumulate in peripheral blood (PB), bone marrow (BM), and lymph nodes [2].The clinical spectrum and prognosis of CLL are extremely heterogeneous: some patients with stable disease will never require therapy, while others present an aggressive clinical course and require therapy early after diagnosis [3].IGHV mutational status and TP53 aberrations (del(17p) and/or TP53 mutations) are the backbone of CLL prognosis [4].Patients with TP53 aberrations are considered to have high-risk CLL because of their poor response to chemoimmunotherapy (CIT).In addition, unmutated IGHV (uIGHV) genes convey a short duration of the response to CIT [5].Pathway inhibitors (PIs) that target signaling pathways crucial for the growth and survival of leukemic cells improve the poor prognosis of adverse biomarkers when compared to CIT.Several randomized trials have shown the superiority of PI including BTK inhibitors (BTKis) and venetoclax-based regimens versus CIT [6][7][8][9].Based on these studies, PIs are considered the preferred frontline regimen in most clinical scenarios, although CIT can still be used in certain cases (i.e., young and fit patients with no TP53 aberrations and uIGHV genes) [10,11].Indeed, targeted therapies have displaced CIT as the predominant therapy in the frontline setting in some geographic areas [12,13].

In this manuscript, we present an overview of the frontline therapeutic approaches in CLL, with a focus on the approved therapies and key factors to be considered when advising therapy (Fig. 1).


Selection of Frontline Therapy


BTK Inhibitors

Targeting BTK has become an important strategy to treat B-cell malignancies including CLL [14,15].The development of selective inhibitors of BTK has permitted inhibition of the function of this tyrosine kinase.Depending on how they bind to the BTK, there are two different types of BTKi: covalent and non-covalent [16].

Covalent BTKi bind covalently to cysteine481 (C481) in the activation site of BTK, so the BTK signaling gets irreversibly suppressed.These include ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, and zanubrutinib.These agents are all administered orally but differ in their selectivity to BTK [17].Another group of BTKi is non-covalent BKTi; they are highly selective to BTK, but their effect is mediated by the reversible binding to different loci in BTK.This latter BTKi have the potential of overcoming the main mechanism of resistance known to be associated with covalent BTKi such as the development of mutations at the BTK binding site, the most frequent being at C481S and C481R [18].

Several randomized trials have shown that continuous therapy with covalent BTKi as frontline therapy prolongs progression-free survival (PFS) as compared to several CIT regimens in both younger and elderly CLL populations [19][20][21][22][23][24].Based on these studies, ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, and zanubrutinib have been approved for CLL therapy (Table 1).

Based on the results of the RESONATE-2, ibrutinib was the first-in-class BTKi approved for CLL treatment.The results of the RESONATE-2 trial have recently been updated after 8 years of follow-up (the largest experience with a BTKi), showing that ibrutinib upfront provides long-term disease control; the PFS and overall survival (OS) at 7 years of 59% and 78% were significantly superior to those of the control arm [19].Furthermore, ibrutinib shows benefits independent of the presence of uIGHV genes and del (11q).Other studies with the covalent BTKi ibrutinib have shown a similar benefit in patients with high-risk features including TP53 aberrations [20,21].A pooled analysis including 89 patients with TP53 aberrations has revealed that continuous therapy with covalent BTKi ibrutinib is a reasonable approach with an estimated 4-year PFS of 79% [25,26].

Although updated results have shown that most patients remain free of progression under single-agent ibrutinib, only a few patients achieve complete remission (CR).Although the CR rate improved over time, the CR rate after 8 years of follow-up was only 34% [19]; this data supports the notion that ibrutinib as given as a single agent has to be given continuously.However, many patients treated with ibrutinib will eventually discontinue therapy.At 7 years, 58% of patients in the RESONATE-2 study had to discontinue treatment mainly because of adverse events (AEs).The most frequently reported AEs are diarrhea, bleeding, fatigue, arthralgias, hypertension, and atrial fibrillation.Most of them are not severe, but dose reductions are required in nearly 25% of patients treated within clinical trials [21].Additionally, AEs of clinical relevance, such as atrial fibrillation and hypertension, can appear and increase over time.Atrial fibrillation is reported to be up to 16% and hypertension in 23% of the patients.Of note, the prevalence of these AEs is higher in patients treated outside clinical trials, explaining the higher treatment discontinuation rate in those patients (≈30%) [27,28].Ventricular arrhythmia is not frequent but has been reported under treatment with ibrutinib [29].Although ibrutinib was also approved in combination with anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies based on the results of two studies, the Illuminate, and the ECOG-ACRIN trial, the addition of rituximab has not been shown to improve PFS nor OS compared with ibrutinib as single-agent [30].

The next generation of more selective covalent BTKi, acalabrutinib, and zanubrutinib have also demonstrated to be superior to chemoimmunotherapy regimens but with a more favorable safety profile [7,24].The treatment follow-up with these two agents is shorter compared to ibrutinib.In the phase III ELEVATE-TN study, previously untreated and elderly CLL patients or those with comorbidities were randomized to receive acalabrutinib with or without obinutuzumab versus CIT with bendamustine-rituximab (BR) as a control arm; at the first analysis, acalabrutinib alone or combined with obinutuzumab was superior to CIT.In the last update with 58.2 months as the longest follow-up with acalabrutinib in treatment-naïve patients, the estimated 60-month PFS is 72% and OS is 90%.The rate of discontinuation due to AEs was 16%, and dose reductions were 13.4% [24].

Zanubrutinib is another covalent BTKi approved for CLL last year based on the results of the SEQUOIA study.In the last update with a follow-up of 43.7 months, the estimated PFS and OS at 42 months were 82.4% and 89%, respectively.In this trial, a cohort with 111 patients with TP53 aberrations was included and showed similar results in terms of PFS and OS to patients without these aberrations.The discontinua-tion rate due to toxicity was 14.9%, and dose reductions were required in 14% of patients [23].These results are comparable to those in patients treated with zanubrutinib outside clinical trials, with an estimated 12-month PFS and OS of 92.2% and 94.1%, respectively [31].

The main adverse effects reported with acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib are similar to ibrutinib (e.g., diarrhea, infections, cytopenia, cardiovascular events), but in general, they occur at a lower rate (Table 2).These differences can be attributed to the more selective kinase inhibition of acalabrutinib and zanubrutinib, which decreases off-target effects [17].In the ELEVATE-TN study, with a median follow-up of 46.9 months, acalabrutinib showed rates of diarrhea, arthralgia, hypertension, and atrial fibrillation at 40.2%, 19.6%, 7.3%, and 6.1%, respectively [24].Besides with zanubrutinib, after a median follow-up of 26.2 months, the rate of any-grade atrial fibrillation was particularly low (5%) while hypertension and neutropenia were observed in 17.5% and 16.7% of cases, respectively [7].Serious complications such as ventricular arrhythmia have also been reported in patients treated with either acalabrutinib or zanubrutinib but at a low rate (1%) [32,33].These results with more selective BTKi suggest that some of these cardiovascular AEs may be a class effect of BTKi therapy, and therefore vigilance is needed, particularly outside clinical trials [34,35].

Although patients with CLL achieve durable responses under covalent BTKi, some develop treatment resistance and disease progression.The main mechanism of resistance is the presence of mutations at BTK at Cys481 (e.g., C481S, C481Y, C481R, C481G) that prevent binding of covalent BTKi and also mutations at PLCG2 [36].In contrast, non- C481 BTK mutations have been described, including BTK Leu528Trp mutation (L528W), which has been reported in patients treated with ibrutinib, zanubrutinib, and also the non-covalent BTKi pirtobrutinib [37-39, 76, 77].A major understanding of the mechanism of resistance under covalent BTKi is key to improving CLL therapy.

Several non-covalent BTKi (e.g., pirtobrutinib, vecabrutinib, fenebrutinib) are being investigated in the treatment of CLL.Pirtobrutinib as a single agent is the noncovalent BTKi with the broadest experience in the relapse/ refractory setting.In the phase 1-2 trial, ORR under pirtobrutinib was 73.3%, including patients previously exposed to covalent BTKis and with the presence of C481 BTK mutations.The safety profile was also favorable.There are still no data on pirtobrutinib in the frontline setting [39].Pirtobrutinib is being investigated in front line in several trials.As single-agent pirtobrutinib is being compared with chemoimmunotherapy (i.e., BR) (BRUIN-CLL-313; NCT05023980), versus the combination of pirtobrutinib with venetoclax (MIRACLE, NCT05677919), with ibrutinib (BRUIN-CLL-314, NCT05254743), and with the triple combination, pirtobrutinib plus venetoclax plus obinutuzumab (NCT05536349).


PI3K Inhibitors

PI3K inhibitors (i.e., idelalisib, duvelisib) were approved for patients with refractory/relapsed CLL.In the frontline setting, idelalisib is the only PI3k inhibitor that could be used in certain circumstances.However, due to safety concerns (e.g., infections and immune-related side effects), the use of idelalisib is not recommended, particularly for the treatment-naïve patients [15,40].More recently, umbralisib, a dual inhibitor of phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase-delta (PI3Kδ) and casein kinase-1ε (CK1ε), did initially show promising clinical activity compared to CIT (chlorambucil+obinutuzumab) in treatment-naive and refractory CLL patients.However, an update of the randomized study showed a possible increased risk of death in patients receiving umbralisib.Based on these data, the drug's company voluntarily withdrew it from the market, and the Food and Drug Administration withdrew umbralisib from lymphomaapproved indications [41].


B-Cell Lymphoma-2 (BCL2) Inhibitors

Agents targeting BCL2, an antiapoptotic protein overexpressed in CLL cells, have an important role in the treatment of CLL [42,43].Venetoclax represents the first selective BCL2 inhibitor that has shown great clinical activity in CLL through the induction of apoptosis of leukemic cells [43][44][45].The combination of venetoclax with an anti-CD20 mAb has proved to be more effective than venetoclax alone.

In treatment-naïve CLL patients, venetoclax is available in combination with the anti-CD20 humanized antibody obinutuzumab (VenO) [46] (Table 3).In the CLL14 trial, which included patients with CLL unfit and with comorbidities, the combination of venetoclax and obinutuzumab given for 1 year showed a PFS and OS at 5 years of 62.6% and 81.9%, respectively; these results were significantly better than those obtained with chlorambucil and obinutuzumab, as comparator arm [47].This regimen also provided significantly higher rates of undetectable minimal residual disease (uMRD) below 10 −4 (74% and 57%) in both PB and BM, which importantly translated into a better outcome.In a young and fit CLL population, the randomized GAIA/CLL13 trial compared the efficacy of upfront CIT (fludarabine-cyclophosphamide-rituximab (FCR) or BR) versus three time-limited different regimens: 12 cycles of venetoclax plus rituximab, VenO, or VenO with ibrutinib that could be administered up to 36 months.Patients with TP53 aberrations were excluded from this study.VenO with or without ibrutinib resulted in significantly higher rates of uMRD and longer PFS than with CIT (Table 3) [9,46,48,49].No significant differences in PFS were observed between the double and triple combinations (VenO and VenO plus ibrutinib).

However, uIGHV and TP53 aberrations still have a detrimental impact on PFS in patients treated with VenO.For instance, in 25 patients with TP53 aberrations included in the VenO arm of the CLL14 trial, the PFS was significantly shorter (median PFS: 49 months), and patients showed a faster detectable MRD.Similarly, in patients with uIGHV, the median PFS was 64.8 months.In a multivariate analysis, bulky disease, TP53 aberrations, and uIGHV were independent poor prognostic factors for PFS and achievement of uMRD [47,49,50].Furthermore, patients with TP53 aberrations had a shorter OS (5-year OS: 60% vs. 85.7%;HR: 2.96, 95% CI: 1.44-6.09).

The safety profile of VenO is manageable with a special concern on neutropenia, the most common grade ≥3 AEs, occurring in 52.8% of patients, leading to infections and treatment discontinuation in 17.5% and 16% of patients, respectively.Tumor lysis syndrome (TLS) is rare (<2%), and a 5-week ramp-up phase starting on C1D22 and TLS risk evaluation is mandatory for all patients.


Combinations with BTKi and BCL2i

The combination of two of the most successful treatment categories, BTKi and the BCL2i venetoclax, was a logical step to be pursued.Its rationale is as follows: BTKi preferentially target dividing cell populations in lymph nodes and mobilizes them to the peripheral blood, while venetoclax eradicates resting populations in blood and BM [51].Hence, double combinations have been investigated in several trials.The main aim of these combinations is to improve the quality of the response with the eradication of leukemic cells and to be able to discontinue therapy at some time.

The first combination approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) is ibrutinib combined with venetoclax for 15 cycles (August 2022).The treatment schedule consists of 3 cycles of ibrutinib which permits to reduction of the tumor burden and the risk of TLS, followed by 12 cycles with ibrutinib plus venetoclax.The approval was based on the results of two studies performed on young and elderly CLL patients (see Table 4).The phase III GLOW trial compared upfront ibrutinib plus venetoclax against chlorambucil-obinutuzumab in patients with CLL older than 65 or with comorbidities.The combination of ibrutinib plus venetoclax proved to be superior to CIT.At the last follow-up of 46 months, the PFS continued to be significantly longer in patients who received ibrutinib plus venetoclax (74.6% vs. 24.8%)[52,53].Undetectable MRD rates at the end of therapy were achieved in 54.7% and 51.9% of patients in PB and BM, respectively.Sequential analysis of MRD, however, showed that after 1 year of the combination, the percentage of uMRD rates decreased over time, particularly in patients with uIGHV genes, while they remained stable in the mutated group.Similar results have been observed in the FLAIR study [54].

In patients younger than 70, the phase II study (CAPTIVATE) showed that fixed duration with ibrutinib and venetoclax resulted in high uMRD rates in both compartments, PB and BM (77% and 60%, respectively), and high PFS rates, the 4-year PFS being 79% [55,56].However, in the last update, the 4-year PFS in patients with TP53 aberrations and uIGHV genes was 63% and 73%, respectively [56].Moreover, the 4-year PFS in patients with uMRD at 3 months after stopping treatment was significantly higher than in those who had detectable MRD (90% vs. 66%).These results raise the question of the optimal treatment, particularly in patients with high-risk features.In this regard, the CAPTIVATE study explored treatment discontinuation based on MRD after treatment with ibrutinib plus venetoclax [57].The results after 5 years of follow-up support fixed treatment duration for patients who achieved uMRD, with a similar PFS between fixed-duration versus continuous therapy [58].In patients with measurable MRD, however, further treatment with ibrutinib plus venetoclax has been shown to improve the uMRD rates although there has been no significant improvement in PFS at 41 months.

A phase II study from MD Anderson has investigated the combination of ibrutinib and venetoclax given for 2 years in patients with CLL who had either TP53 aberrations, del(11q), uIGHV genes, or who were at the age of 65 or older.The rate of uMRD in BM at 12 and 24 cycles increased from 56% to 66%.Additionally, PFS at 4 years was 94.5%.Interestingly, TP53 aberrations were not associated with either uMRD rates or PFS [59,60].

While ibrutinib plus venetoclax excels in efficacy and uMRD rates, its trade-off is the added toxicity from the offtarget effects of each drug, resulting in 10.4% and 5% of patients not being able to complete the treatment schedule due to AEs in the GLOW and CAPTIVATE trials, respectively.The most common AEs were diarrhea (51-62%), arthralgia (11-33%), and neutropenia (42%; grade ≥3 33-35%).In addition, atrial fibrillation occurred in up to 14% of patients (6.6% grade ≥3), but grade 3 or higher TLS was very unlikely.On the other hand, due to the fixedduration nature of the combination, most AEs of a continuous BTKi treatment are avoided [53,55].A key question is whether venetoclax-based treatments should be given as fixed-duration regimens or be MRD-guided, which could be of help to estimate the optimal treatment duration.


Triple Combinations

A step forward in the treatment of CLL is the combination of BTKi and BCL2i with the addition of an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody.The CLL2-GIVe trial investigated a triple combination of ibrutinib, venetoclax, and obinutuzumab (GIVe) given upfront to patients with TP53 aberrations.Obinutuzumab was added to ibrutinib and venetoclax for the first 6 cycles, and thereafter patients received up to 12 cycles of ibrutinib plus venetoclax, while ibrutinib could be extended up to 36 months; the 3year PFS was 79.9% in this high-risk population [61].However, triple combinations may result in an unexpected increase in toxicity, as shown in the CLL13 trial and other studies in which the triple combination led to high rates of neutropenia and infections [9,62,63].

Moving further, the more selective second-generation BTKi are being combined with venetoclax as fixed duration or MRD-guided regimens.However, data are much more limited (Table 5).Preliminary results of twophase II studies, one evaluating acalabrutinib plus venetoclax and obinutuzumab (AVO), the other doing so with zanubrutinib (ZVO), look pretty like those achieved with ibrutinib plus venetoclax, either with or without obinutuzumab, with reported OR/CR rates of 98%/48% and 100%/57%, respectively [64][65][66].High rates of uMRD were reported with both combinations, and the estimated 3-year PFS for AVO was 93%, not reported yet with the triple combination with zanubrutinib [64][65][66].Other studies are the AMPLIFY trial, a phase III study testing acalabrutinib plus venetoclax with or without obinutuzumab versus FCR or BR; the MAJIC trial compares acalabrutinib plus venetoclax versus VenO in an MRDguided fashion.

These more selective combinations look effective on high-risk patients as well.After a median follow-up of 9.7 months in the SEQUOIA trial (arm D), the combination zanubrutinib plus venetoclax in 35 patients with TP53 aberrations obtained 96.8% of ORR [67].Similarly, the phase II studies with AVO and ZVO included 45.6% and 13% of patients with TP53 aberrations, respectively.

In terms of safety, 3% discontinued ZVO due to AEs (not reported for AVO).The most frequent hematologic AEs with AVO were neutropenia (75%; 37% grade ≥3) and thrombocytopenia (73%; 28% grade ≥3).Headache (78%), fatigue (76%), bruising (66%), nausea (49%), diarrhea (40%), hypertension (27%), and arthralgia (25%) were reported.Atrial fibrillation occurred in 3% of patients.With ZVO, thrombocytopenia (59%), neutropenia (51%), fatigue (54%), and bruising (51%) were also described.


Chemoimmunotherapy

For many years, CIT has been the standard treatment for patients with CLL.The CIT includes several timelimited therapy combinations that were classically selected according to patients' characteristics: FCR in young and fit patients, BR in older but fit patients, and chlorambucil-obinutuzumab in old and/or unfit patients [10].Some of these regimens have been associated with deep and long responses in patients without high-risk features (mutated IGHV without del (11q) nor TP53 aberrations).For instance, the long-term follow-up of patients treated with FCR in the CLL8 clinical trial (19 years) has shown "virtual" cure rates of >50% of patients with IGHV mutated and without TP53 aberrations disease [5].Thus, CIT namely FCR is still considered useful in certain circumstances, as in patients without TP53 alterations, according to international guidelines (NCCN, ESMO) [10,11].However, CIT has been progressively displaced by targeted therapies, mainly due to its toxicity (cytopenia, infections, secondary malignancies, especially myelodysplastic syndromes and acute myeloid leukemia) [68] and lower efficacy, particularly in a significant group of patients (i.e., TP53 aberrations, del(11q), uIGHV) [7,9,19,21,22,24,48].


Patient-Related Factors to Consider before Starting Frontline Therapy

In routine clinical practice, only patients with CLL with active and symptomatic disease should be treated (Table 6) [4].Pre-emptive treatment of patients with a high risk of progression is not recommended, as in clinical trials, early intervention has not been shown to improve OS under several treatments, even with targeted therapies (i.e., ibrutinib) [69].When choosing frontline therapy, several factors must be taken into consideration (Fig. 2).Acalabrutinib needs an acid gastric environment for optimal absorption; thus, proton-pump inhibitors must be interrupted or substituted with other antacids, such as H2 antagonists.However, there is a formulation of acalabrutinib available in America, Europe, and Australia that can be administered concomitantly with proton pump inhibitors [70].Patients with significant cardiac disorders and elderly patients have a higher risk of cardiovascular events under covalent BTKi, particularly ibrutinib.The ESC/EHA recommends that high-risk Autoimmune complications including anemia or thrombocytopenia poorly responsive to corticosteroids.Symptomatic or functional extranodal involvement (e.g., skin, kidney, lung, and spine).Disease-related symptoms as defined by any of the following: a. unintentional weight loss >10% within the previous 6 months; b. significant fatigue (i.e., ECOG performance scale 2 or worse; cannot work or unable to perform usual activities); c. fever >100.5°F or 38.0°C for 2 or more weeks without evidence of infection; d. night sweats for >1 month without evidence of infection.Progressive lymphocytosis with an increase of >50% over 2 months or lymphocyte doubling time (LDT) <6 months.The absolute lymphocyte count should not be used as the sole indicator for treatment.Achieving response with uMRD is associated with longer PFS and OS [50,74].However, the importance of obtaining uMRD depends on the target population and treatment objectives.For example, achieving uMRD is not critical in very old patients (>80 years), in whom disease palliation is the most reasonable objective.In contrast, in younger patients, it can be of paramount importance.In the allotransplant setting, achieving uMRD is impor-tant because results are better in patients in CR [75].A proposed algorithm for first-line treatment is shown in Figure 3.


Summary

CLL is still considered incurable and will often require chronic/recurrent treatment.In the last 2 decades, the prognosis of patients with CLL has dramatically improved thanks to better therapies.The introduction of CIT (i.e., FCR combination) represented the first regimen that significantly improved OS, particularly in younger patients with mutated IGHV and no high-risk genetics [5,78].Furthermore, over the last decade the introduction of PIs in CLL treatment, BTKis (i.e., ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib), and venetoclax in combination with monoclonal antibodies or BTKi have replaced CIT in most settings due to their higher efficacy (especially in high-risk patients) and tolerability [10].Towards the goal of further improving CLL therapy, current randomized trials are comparing continuous therapy with covalent BTKI versus fixed duration regimens with venetoclax-based regimens.MRD-guided strategies may help us to define optimal treatment duration for some subgroups of patients.Their results will contribute to setting the stage for CLL treatment algorithms for a long while.
