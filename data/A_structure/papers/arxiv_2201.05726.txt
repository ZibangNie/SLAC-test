CATEGORIES OF QUANTUM LIQUIDS III
15 Jan 2022
We continue our study of the categories of quantum liquids started in a previous work. We combine local quantum symmetries with topological skeletons into a single mathematical theory of topological nets and defect nets. In particular, we introduce the notion of a topological net, which is motivated from and generalizes that of a conformal net, and the notion of a defect net which generalizes that of a defect between conformal nets. We give explicit examples of them. Moreover, we construct the category of topological n-nets with k-morphisms defined by defect n-nets of codimension k, and show that the category of nD quantum liquids can be extracted from it and computed explicitly via the condensation theory of topological nets.Contents

In this work, we continue our study of the category of quantum liquids started in [KZ20b]. Quantum liquids are quantum phases that only "softly" depend on the local geometry on spacetime. They include the usual spontaneous symmetry-breaking phases, topological orders, symmetry protected/enriched topological (SPT/SET) orders and CFT-type gapless phases. We have shown in [KZ20b] that the mathematical description of a quantum liquid consists of two parts of data: the local quantum symmetry and the topological skeleton. In [KZ20b], we focused on the topological skeletons and explicitly computed the category QL n sk of the topological skeletons of nD quantum liquids. In this work, we combine local quantum symmetries with topological skeletons into a single mathematical theory of topological nets and defect nets. This theory provides a rather complete mathematical description of quantum liquids in all dimensions. Throughout this work, nD represents the spacetime dimension.

In 2D CFT's, the local quantum symmetries are given by vertex operator algebras (VOA) or their non-chiral analogues. It is not known how to generalize VOA directly to higher dimensions. However, there is an alternative formulation of 2D CFT's in terms of conformal nets (see for examples [BGL93, BMT88, BSM90, GF93, KL04, KLM01, LR95, Reh00a, Reh00b, Was95] and references therein). The idea of conformal net was originated from algebraic quantum field theories (defined in all dimensions), which was based on Haag-Kastler nets or the nets of local observables [HK64] (see [Haa92] for a review). Therefore, it is natural to ask how to generalize the notion of a conformal net to a more general one for the study of quantum liquids in all dimensions. Moreover, from the lattice model realization of topological orders, it seems that the ideas of the net of local observables and the superselection sectors of particles work better in lattice models than in quantum gauge field theories (see for example [Kit03,KK12,KWZ21]). This motivates us to introduce the notion of a topological net, which includes that of a conformal net as a special case. We also generalize Bartels, Douglas and Henriques' theory of defects 2 in conformal nets and their fusion [BDH19a] to the theory of defect nets and their fusion.

In this work, we show that all finite topological n-nets, together with their higher codimensional finite defects, form a symmetric monoidal * -(n + 1)-category Net n . Then we can define a subcategory LQS n of Net n . The category of nD quantum liquids, denoted by QL n , can be obtained from LQS n by a (co)slice construction. The category QL n is equipped with two forgetful functors QL n → LQS n and QL n → QL sk , whose images are precisely the local quantum symmetry and the topological skeleton, respectively. One of the main results of this work says that LQS n is *condensation-complete (see Theorem 3.8). It further implies LQS n ≃ (n + 1)Hilb and QL n ≃ QL n sk , both of which are naturally required by physics thus provide the necessary consistent check of our theory.

We provide the layout of this paper. In Section 2.1, we introduce the notion of a topological net; in Section 2.2, we introduce that of a defect net; in Section 2.3, we construct from every finite group a topological n-net that describe the local quantum symmetry of nD gapped quantum liquids with a finite onsite symmetry; in Section 2.4, we provide an explicit construction of topological nets and defect nets to describe Levin-Wen models and their boundaries.

In Section 3.1, we introduce the category Net n and postpone the complete construction (mainly the composition of higher morphisms) of Net n to Section 4. In Section 3.2, we give the construction of LQS n and QL n , state the main result Theorem 3.8 and discuss its consequences. In Section 3.3, we show how to extract the information of local quantum symmetries from a tower of subcategories of LQS n based on the so-called transparent domain walls.

Section 4 is devoted to defining the fusion of defect nets and completing the construction of Net n . In Section 5, we sketch the condensation theory of topological nets. This theory provides a proof of Theorem 3.8.

In Appendix A, we briefly review Lurie's formulation of Connes fusion and prove the * -condensation completeness of the * -2-category of von Neumann algebras and bimodules.

We assume the reader is familiar with the fundamentals of von Neumann algebras, such as standard form and Connes fusion of bimodules. We say that a bimodule (including a left or right module as special case) over von Neumann algebras is semisimple if it is a finite direct sums of irreducible ones.

We work on the * -settings. Functors between * -n-categories are silently assumed to be * -functors. Recall that Hilb denotes the unitary symmetric monoidal 1category of finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and linear maps. We use Hilb to the denote the symmetric monoidal * -1-category of all (possibly inseparable) Hilbert spaces and bounded linear maps. Given a unitary 1-category C, we useĈ to denote C ⊠ Hilb Hilb and refer to it as the completion of C. By definitionĈ is a finite direct sum of Hilb. 


Topological nets

The notion of a topological net is a generalization of a conformal net. By dropping the continuity of diffeomorphism covariance, we obtain a theory unifying conformal symmetries of gapless phases and onsite symmetries of gapped phases.

2.1. Topological nets. We adopt the standard definition of the n-sphere
S n = {(x 0 , x 1 , . . . , x n ) |
x 2 i = 1}. Let S n ↑ denote the upper half of S n defined by x 0 ≥ 0 and let S n ↓ denote the lower half of S n defined by x 0 ≤ 0.

A disk region of the n-sphere S n is a closed region that is diffeomorphic to the n-disk D n . Let Disk n denote the 1-category of disk regions of S n whose morphisms are inclusions of disk regions. Let VN denote the 1-category of von Neumann algebras whose morphisms are (unital, normal) homomorphisms of von Neumann algebras.

Definition 2.1. An n-dimensional net of von Neumann algebras or an n-net for short is a functor A : Disk n−1 → VN. A partial n-net is a functor A : D → VN where D is a full subcategory of Disk n−1 .

Remark 2.2. Unwinding the definition we see that a partial n-net A : D → VN consists of a family of von Neumann algebras A(I) for I ∈ D and a family of
homomorphisms A(I ⊂ J) : A(I) → A(J) such that A(I ⊂ I) = Id A(I) and A(J ⊂ K) • A(I ⊂ J) = A(I ⊂ K).
The von Neumann algebras A(I) are referred to as local observable algebras.

Remark 2.3. We do not require the isotony of a partial n-net, that is, A(I) may be not a subalgebra of A(J) for I ⊂ J. The isotony of a topological n-net follows from other axioms (see Remark 2.9(2)). This relaxation is necessary for defining a zero defect n-net (see Example 2.18).

The direct sum of two n-nets A, B is an n-net A ⊕ B defined by
(A ⊕ B)(I) = A(I) ⊕ B(I).
The tensor product of A, B is an n-net A ⊠ B defined by
(A ⊠ B)(I) = A(I)⊗B(I).
The diffeomorphism group Diff(S n−1 ) acts on the collection of partial n-nets by the formula:
(h * A)(I) = A(h(I)) if h preserves orientation or (h * A)(I) = A(h(I)) op otherwise.
Definition 2.4. A sector of a partial n-net A : D → VN is a Hilbert space H equipped with a family of homomorphisms ρ I : A(I) → L(H) for I ∈ D such that ρ J • A(I ⊂ J) = ρ I for all inclusions I ⊂ J. We say that H is irreducible if it is neither zero nor the direct sum of two nonzero sectors; semisimple if it is a finite direct sum of irreducible sectors.

A homomorphism f : H → K between two sectors of A is a bounded linear map such that f is a left A(I)-module map for all I ∈ D. We use Sect(A) to denote the * -1-category of sectors of A.

2.3. Nets of onsite symmetries. We define for every finite group G a finite irreducible topological n-net that describes the local quantum symmetry of an nD gapped phase with an onsite symmetry G.

Let G be a finite group and let V be a finite-dimensional G-module which contains all the irreducible G-modules as direct summands. Fix a G-invariant vector of norm one µ ∈ V .

Consider the orthonormal frame bundle E over S n−1 , i.e. the fiber E x over a point x ∈ S n−1 consists of the orthonormal frames at x. For example, E is a double cover of S 1 for n = 2. In general, E has two connected components E ± corresponding to the two orientations of S n−1 (in fact, E ∼ = O(n)).

Assign W s = V * for s ∈ E + and W s = V for s ∈ E − . Define H = s∈E W s to be the completion of the pre-Hilbert space spanned by the following vectors {⊗w s | w s = µ or µ * except for finitely many s ∈ E}.

Define H A to be the subspace of G-invariants in H
H A = H G = {w ∈ H | Gw = w}.
It carries an obvious action by the diffeomorphism group Diff(S n−1 ).

Let I ⊂ S n−1 be a disk region. Note that I and I ′ divide E into two disjoint parts E(I) and E(I ′ ) such that an orthonormal frame (e 1 , . . . , e n−1 ) at x ∈ ∂I belongs to E(I) if the first e i not tangent to ∂I points towards I. Define A(I) to be the von Neumann algebra on H A generated by End G ( s∈P W s ) where P runs over all finite subsets of E(I).

Proposition 2.20. The pair (A, H A ), together with the obvious action of Diff(S n−1 ) on A, defines a finite irreducible topological n-net.

Proof. The axioms of locality, additivity, covariance and split property are all clear. To see the vacuum property we note that
H = H ↑ ⊗H * ↑ where H ↑ = s∈E(S n−1 ↑ ) W s hence H = L 2 (L(H ↑ )). Moreover, L(H ↑ ) is generated by End C ( s∈P W s ) for finite P ⊂ E(S n−1 ↑ ) hence L G (H ↑ ) = A(S n−1 ↑ ). Passing to the G-invariants yields H A = L 2 (A(S n−1 ↑ )
). For the duality condition, note that for any disjoint I, J ∈ Disk n−1 ,
the O(H A , I ∪ J)-O(H A , I ′ ∩ J ′ ) op -bimodule H A is semisimple hence dualizable. It remains to show that O(H A ) = L(H A ). Indeed, the operators from O(H A ) supported on the point (0, . . . , 0, 1) ∈ S n−1 intertwine H ↑ and H * ↑ . Together with these operators, L G (H ↑ ) and L G (H * ↑ ) generate L(H G ).
Remark 2.21. The topological n-net A constructed above (except the trivial case) differs from a conformal net on several aspects. First, from the proof above we notice that all of A(I) are type I von Neumann algebras on an inseparable Hilbert space while the local observable algebras of a conformal net are usually type III von Neumann algebras on a separable Hilbert space. Second, A does not satisfy the strong additivity axiom. Thirdly and most importantly, the whole diffeomorphism group Diff(S n−1 ) acts genuinely on H A , however, the action is not continuous in any reasonable sense because every vector of H A lies over countably many points of S n−1 . In particular, Diff(S n−1 ) acts strongly continuously only on the onedimensional subspace spanned by the vacuum vector s∈E + (µ⊗µ * ). This suggests that the topological n-net A describes the local quantum symmetry of a gapped phase.

The above remark motivates the following definition. Typical examples include that a conformal net in the sense of [BDH15] is a conformal 2-net and the topological n-net A constructed above is gapped.

Definition 2.22. We say that a defect n-net A of codimension k is gapless if Diff(S n−1 ) k acts strongly continuously on an infinite-dimensional subspace of H A . Otherwise, we say that A is gapped. We say that a topological n-net A is a conformal n-net if Diff(S n−1 ) acts strongly continuously on H A .

3.3. Transparent domain walls. As we have seen from the previous subsection, QL n is equivalent to QL n sk . That is, local quantum symmetries can not be recovered from the equivalence type of QL n at all.

To detect local quantum symmetries, one needs to distinguish a class of transparent domain walls from invertible ones in certain categorical structures. Roughly speaking, a domain wall F between two quantum liquids or defects A and B is transparent if A and B can be identified in such a way that F is a trivial domain wall.

We say that two defect n-nets A and B of codimension k are isomorphic if there exists a natural isomorphism ξ : A → B intertwining the action of Diff(S n−1 ) k such that ξ I is the identity for I ∩ S n−1−k = ∅.

Then we say that a k-morphism F : A → B of Net n where 1 ≤ k ≤ n is completely transparent if the defect n-nets A and B can be identified by an isomorphism in such a way that the defect n-net F is isomorphic to the identity k-morphism. An (n + 1)-morphism of Net n is completely transparent if it is an isometry of sectors.

Let T be the minimal class of morphisms of Net n that contains all the completely transparent morphisms and is closed under composition, tensor product and adjunction. The members of T are called transparent. We say that a morphism of QL n is transparent if it is defined in terms of transparent morphisms of LQS n . We say that two objects or morphisms of QL n are unitarily equivalent if there is a transparent morphism between them.

Generalizing the above construction, we see that all * -condensation bimodules and bimodule maps, etc. are induced by morphisms of LQS n . This completes the proof of Theorem 3.8.
