Hydrogen induced bending deformation was studied by using elecroless Pd plated pure aluminum. Thin plate specimens were hydrogen charged by cathodic polarization on a single side. It was observed that an increase in charge time resulted in the bending deformation of the specimens. It was also shown that bending is nearly relieved following hydrogen gas release from the specimens by heating above 300°C. Repetitious bending behavior was not observed due to a combination of hydrogen absorption and desorption resulting from the damage of the plated surface layer. Thermal desorption analysis revealed that the hydrogen induced bending deformation of the Pd plated aluminum was brought about by the volume expansion of the Pd layer as a result of hydrogen absorption.
