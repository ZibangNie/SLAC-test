Electrochemical Signal-Off Competitive Immunoassay of Chromogranin A toward a Sandwiched Graphene Oxide Structure for Neuroendocrine Tumor Detection

Neuroendocrine tumors (NETs), often misdiagnosed and mistreated, require early detection for precise therapeutic interventions.This study presents a newly developed competitive electrochemical immunosensor for sensitive and selective detection of chromogranin A (CgA), a key biomarker for diagnosing and monitoring NETs.The sensor, featuring a sandwiched structure with versatile and multifunctional graphene oxide (GO), utilizes polyethylenimine-capped gold nanoparticles (PEI-AuNPs) to enhance the electroreactivity and biocompatibility of a screen-printed electrode (SPE).The immunosensor operates by immobilizing standard CgA antigens on the PEI-AuNPs/GOmodified SPE surface and employing GO nanotags loaded with anti-CgA antibodies (Ab) and ferrocene monocarboxylic acid (Fc) redox probes to capture target CgA.As the CgA concentration increases, the current response of the immunosensor decreases due to a reduction in the amount of Fc/Ab/GO tags on the electrode surface.This reduction occurs because the nanotags bind to the free CgA in the sample rather than the CgA immobilized on the electrode.The immunosensor demonstrates a good linearity (0.10−50 ng mL −1 ), a low detection limit of 90 pg mL −1 , and high accuracy in detecting CgA levels in human serum samples.With its high specificity, long-term stability, and excellent reproducibility, our cost-effective and user-friendly platform holds promise for clinical screening and point-of-care diagnosis of NETs.Further optimization of the immunosensor's design and exploration of its use for additional biomarkers could enhance NETs' diagnosis and provide advancements in managing other related health conditions.

■ INTRODUCTION

Neuroendocrine tumors (NETs) are a heterogeneous group of rare malignancies originating from neuroendocrine cells distributed throughout the body. 1,2Unfortunately, misdiagnosis and mistreatment are common among NET patients, thus causing delayed treatment and poor outcomes. 3,460−80% of NET cases are diagnosed at advanced stages, often with metastatic progression, leading to a poor prognosis. 5,6Early diagnosis through the identification of effective biomarkers is critical for improving treatment efficacy and patient survival.Chromogranin A (CgA) is a 48 kDa hydrophilic glycoprotein from the granin family, costored with biogenic amines and peptide hormones in the secretory granules of neuroendocrine cells, which are part of the diffuse endocrine system spread throughout the body. 7,9−12 Its measurement in the blood can indicate elevated levels in patients with neuroendocrine system tumors, signaling increased risk. 7,10imely diagnosis and treatment are crucial for reducing NETassociated mortality, and CgA measurements help evaluate treatment effectiveness and prognosis while aiding in NET diagnosis. 11,13,14CgA is also valuable in therapeutic monitoring, especially for patients with neuroendocrine-differentiated prostate cancer and resistance to androgen deprivation therapy [8].Clinically, detecting pancreatic NETs is crucial for effective treatment, with serum CgA as a sensitive, noninvasive test for detection and management. 15Plasma CgA levels correlate with tumor burden and are recommended for patient follow-up after a NET diagnosis. 10Additionally, circulating CgA is an efficient biomarker for NET diagnosis, offering high sensitivity and specificity, which enhances its utility in clinical practice. 16CgA levels tend to be lower in patients with widespread metastatic disease compared to those with liver metastases only, while higher levels are associated with metastatic gastroenteropancreatic NETs, demonstrating its usefulness as a clinical biomarker, albeit with limited sensitivity and specificity. 8Elevated CgA levels (above 156.5 ng mL −1 ) can indicate pancreatic neuroendocrine neoplasm metastasis, with a correlation between plasma CgA levels, the Ki67 index, and mitotic count informing prognosis. 17Patients with metastatic pancreatic NETs exhibit significantly higher median CgA levels (169 ng mL −1 ) compared to those with localized disease (66 ng mL −1 ), and CgA levels are predictive of distant metastasis and overall survival. 18In pancreatic NETs, median CgA levels (126.62 ng mL −1 ) exceed those in other tumor types (69.82 ng mL −1 ), making CgA measurement valuable for distinguishing pancreatic NETs from other tumors in the pancreas, especially in larger tumors. 13The reliability of CgA as a serum diagnostic biomarker for pancreatic NETs is wellestablished, with a cutoff of 18 U L −1 showing strong diagnostic accuracy. 7,19CgA also serves as a predictive marker for tumor burden, overall survival, and progression in gastroenteropancreatic NETs. 11Elevated CgA levels may indicate tumor progression or recurrence and are more effective for assessing NET prognosis in pancreatic and gastroenteropancreatic NETs than in other types.Therefore, there is an urgent need for more sensitive and reliable diagnostic techniques for NETs, especially to enhance CgA detection specificity and sensitivity in pancreatic and gastroenteropancreatic cases. 10,20,21arious analytical techniques, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), 22 surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS), 23 surface plasmon resonance (SPR), 24 fluorescence, 25 and field-effect transistors (FETs), 26 have been used for biomarker detection.−28 This has driven the demand for rapid, cost-effective alternatives.Electrochemical immunosensors have emerged as promising diagnostic tools due to their fast detection, simplicity, high sensitivity, minimal sample requirements, and low cost.They leverage specific immune interactions combined with electrochemical detection to monitor antigen−antibody binding events.−33 Moreover, the device fabrication and development focus on minimizing nonspecific protein adsorption causing lower analytical accuracy. 29,34To address these limitations, competitive assays offer a streamlined approach by eliminating the need for costly primary antibody immobilization on the electrode.Instead, the target is immobilized directly, with immunoprobes capturing the target in the sample before binding to electrode-bound targets.The signal generated by the immunoprobes on the electrode is then allowed to quantify the target in the sample, offering a simplified and efficient alternative for biomarker detection.

−37 However, there is still a challenge in developing novel functional nanomaterials combined with the merits of high surface area, good conductivity, easy modification, and facile preparation, which can effectively adsorb antibodies and redox probes to simply construct sensors. 30,38,39−42 The GO is one of these outstanding materials due to many merits, e.g., high specific surface area, structural stability, large volume, electroreactivity, and biocompatibility. 43,44In addition, integrating gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) into an electrode composite can enhance the electrode's surface area and reactivity, and facilitate the charge transfer kinetics, which promotes efficient device sensitivity. 45,46AuNPs were deposited on the electrode in advance to improve the immobilization of proteins. 45,47,48Moreover, biocompatible GO with sufficient conductivity can be designed to create sensing surfaces and construct signaling particles for dual purposes.

This study aims to develop a competitive electrochemical immunosensor based on a sandwich-structured GO platform for the highly sensitive detection of CgA, utilizing a screenprinted electrode (SPE) modified with GO and (polyethylenimine-capped gold nanoparticles) PEI-AuNPs.Multifunctional GO was employed both as an electroactive support and a nanotag, while PEI-AuNPs were comodified on the electrode surface owing to their high surface area, excellent conductivity, and effective CgA immobilization capacity.The combination of GO and PEI-AuNPs enhances electrode reactivity and forms the basis for constructing the sensing platform, with GO also serving as a labeling particle by loading a redox probe and anti-CgA antibodies.Ferrocene monocarboxylic acid (Fc) and anti-CgA antibodies were sequentially adsorbed onto GO particles to form Fc/GO-tagged anti-CgA antibodies (anti-CgA/Fc/GO nanotags) functioning as current response generators.For surface modification, CgA was immobilized on the PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified working area of the SPE, followed by the binding of anti-CgA/Fc/GO nanotags via the formation of a GO-sandwiched structure after competitive capture of CgA from the sample.The competitive detection strategy was optimized by transferring the remaining nanotag solution onto the electrode surface, minimizing the current response as tag deposition decreased.This approach achieved high reactivity, selectivity, and sensitivity toward CgA detection.The developed biosensor demonstrated desirable performance in serum samples, providing a practical and reliable tool for accurate CgA analysis in medical diagnostics.


■ EXPERIMENTAL SECTION


Materials and Reagents

All chemicals and reagents are of analytical grade and used as received without further purification.Chromogranin A (CgA, lot: SCP0116) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Germany.Antichromogranin A antibodies (anti-CgA, lot: 10R-C126A) were ordered from Fitzgerald, USA.Immunoglobulin G (IgG, lot: 082M4772 V, ≥95%) was bought from Sigma-Aldrich, Israel.Bovine serum albumin (BSA, lot: D00155527, 98%) was purchased from Calbiochem, USA.Phosphate buffered saline tablets and powder (pH 7.4), and 1-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP, 99.0%) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, USA.Nitric acid (HNO 3 , 65.0%) was purchased from RCI Labscan, Thailand.Potassium nitrate (KNO 3 , 99.0%) was bought from Rankem, India.Potassium ferricyanide (K 3 [Fe(CN) 6 ], 99%) and potassium ferrocyanide (K 4 [Fe(CN) 6 ], 98.5%) were obtained from Merck, Germany.L-ascorbic acid (AA, 99%) was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Singapore.D-(+)-glucose (Glu, ≥99.5%), interleukin-6 (IL-6, lot: 0409AFC16), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA, lot: SLBM6069 V), uric acid (UA, 99.0%), gold(III) chloride hydrate (HAuCl 4 •xH 2 O, 99.995%), and human serum from human male AB plasma (lot: SLBS6544) were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, USA.Monocarboxylic ferrocene (Fc, ≥96%) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, China.Polyethylenimine (PEI, branched, M w ≈ 800) was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich, Germany.Dopamine hydrochloride (DA, 98.0%) was bought from Sigma-Aldrich, Germany.Milli-Q water (18 MΩ) was employed to prepare all aqueous solutions for all experiments.PBS (pH 7.4) was used for the preparation of all protein solutions and as a washing solution.Three-electrode configuration screen-printed electrodes (SPEs) were ordered from Zensor, Taiwan.


Instrumentation

Scanning electron microscopic (SEM) and transmission electron microscopic (TEM) images measured using a JEOL JSM-IT800, Japan, and a JEOL JEM-2100Plus, Japan, respectively, were used to investigate the surface morphologies of the modified electrodes and particle characteristics of the prepared GO and PEI-AuNPs.Cyclic voltammograms (CVs) and electrochemical impedance spectra (EIS) were used to study the electrochemical properties of electrodes and sensors, and recorded by a PalmSens4 potentiostat (Netherlands).Together with CVs, differential pulse voltammograms (DPVs) were employed to study the device fabrication, and DPV responses of the immunosensor are analytical signals for the quantitative assay of CgA target.


Immunosensor Fabrication

GO powder was synthesized through a triple-adjusted exfoliation process based on the modified Hummers' method, involving iterative oxidation and exfoliation of graphite. 40The PEI-AuNPs were synthesized following the previous protocol with a few modifications. 27,28PEI (1.0%, 0.90 mL) served as both a reducing and stabilizing agent for the formation of AuNPs.A 25 mL solution of 1.40 mM HAuCl 4 •xH 2 O with the presence of PEI was reduced under continuous magnetic stirring at 800 rpm for 16 h.During this process, the solution's color gradually changed from yellow, indicative of Au 3+ ions, to a characteristic wine-red solution, forming PEI-stabilized AuNPs. 27The resulting colloidal suspension was centrifuged at 6000 rpm for 10 min to collect the PEI-AuNPs sediment.The precipitate was then redispersed and adjusted with DI water to prepare a stable PEI-AuNPs solution, which was subsequently stored at 4 °C for future use.To form a binary composite, GO powder (3.0 mg mL −1 ) was dispersed in PEI-AuNPs solution (4.0 mM) and sonicated for 1 h.Before constructing the immunosensor, the working electrode of the SPE was treated with O 2 plasma at 400 mbar for 1 min using a plasma cleaner (PDC-32G, Harrick Plasma, United States).

Scheme 1 shows the fabrication of a competitive immunosensor by forming a sandwich-structured GO platform.Briefly, a 2.5-μL highquality PEI-AuNPs (4.0 mM)/GO (3.0 mg mL −1 ) dispersion solution was coated onto a plasma-treated SPE's working electrode (3 mm in diameter) and dried in the air. 30,47,49Incorporating GO with PEI-AuNPs offers the favorable uptake of proteins on the modified SPE and the improved electrochemical reactivity of SPE.The PEI-AuNPs/ GO-modified SPE was incubated with 2.5 μL of 50 ng mL −1 CgA solution in a humidity chamber for 30 min to obtain the sensing electrodes (CgA/PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPEs).After washing with deionized (DI) water three times, the electrodes were incubated with a 2.5-μL drop of 0.50% BSA solution for 30 min to prevent nonspecific adsorption by other substances and then rinsed with DI water three times.To fabricate the signaling GO (anti-CgA/Fc/GO), 25 μL of 3.0 mg mL −1 GO dispersion was mixed with 25 μL of 3.0 mg mL −1 Fc solution under stirring for 1 h, resulting in an orange-brown mixture.The Fc/GO was separated by centrifuge (Digicen 21 (Orto alresa), Spain) at 5000 rpm for 10 min to obtain a black-brown precipitate, washed several times, and then redispersed into a 50-μL volume with DI water to yield a Fc/GO dispersion.Subsequently, 5.0 μL of 50 ng mL −1 anti-CgA solution was added into 50.0μL of the resulting Fc/GO dispersion under gentle mixing in a thermomixer (ThermoMixer C (Eppendorf), USA) at 400 rpm for 1 h.The anti-CgA/Fc/GO tag solution was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 10 min twice, and the precipitate of the signaling anti-CgA/Fc/GO tag particles was redispersed in 50.0 μL of ultrapure water.The resultant solution is ready to be used and stored at 4 °C.For the competitively qualitative detection of CgA, 2.0-μL samples containing various CgA concentrations were mixed with 2.0 μL of the signaling GO solution and incubated in a thermomixer at 400 rpm and 25 °C for 1 h.After that, the solution was coated onto the CgA/PEI-AuNPs/GOmodified SPEs and left in a humidity chamber at 4 °C for 30 min to form a sandwich structure, GO/Fc/anti-CgA/CgA/PEI-AuNPs/ GO-modified SPEs.The DPV responses of the structure compared to a blank solution were recorded.With increasing CgA concentration in the sample, the response decreases due to lower binding of the signaling anti-CgA/Fc/GO on the sensing electrode.In this case, the detection of CgA in a blank solution gives the highest electrochemical response.


■ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION


Characterization of Materials and Modified Electrodes

TEM images confirm the characteristics of GO and PEI-AuNPs prepared for this study as seen in Figure S1A,B, respectively.GO presents a blanket-like structure while PEI-AuNPs form a spherical shape with an average diameter of 20.58 ± 5.81 nm. Figure S1C illustrates a PEI-AuNPs/GO sample in which PEI-AuNPs (23.55 ± 6.27 nm) sit on GO sheets.Figure 1 shows morphologies of the electrode surface before and after modification with functional nanomaterials.After coating with GO (Figure 1C), the surface was changed by covering a blanket-like structure compared to unmodified SPE, possessing small particles.The surface with PEI-AuNPs presents dispersion of PEI-AuNPs on the surface as seen in Figure 1B while when SPE is modified with a PEI-AuNPs/GO composite, the surface shows dispersed PEI-AuNPs sitting on the GO blankets (Figures 1D and S2A).C and Au elemental mappings and EDX spectrum confirm the distribution of PEI-AuNPs on the surface of a PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPE as shown in Figure S1B−D, respectively.with all materials, as shown in Figure S3. Figure S3A shows the CVs of all electrodes.It is found that anodic peak currents are increased from 75 μA to 89, 97, and 101 μA by GO, PEI-AuNPs, and PEI-AuNPs/GO, respectively, and peak-to-peak separation (ΔE P ) is reduced from 190 mV to 110 mV by all materials, indicating better electroreactivity and faster electron transfer of [Fe(CN) 6 ] 4−/3− process.In addition, EIS spectra (Figure S3B) assess the electron transfer properties of the electrodes.With a large semicircle, SPE has a large electron transfer resistance (R ct , 124 Ω) while SPEs modified with GO, PEI-AuNPs, and PEI-AuNPs/GO reveal reduced resistances to ca. 36, 17, and 16 Ω, respectively.The EIS result agrees well with the CV result.Based on electrode kinetics and the highest current, PEI-AuNPs/GO would offer the best sensitivity of the fabricated sensor.


Optimization of Immunosensor Construction

The device fabrication and detection parameters are crucial in constructing the immunosensor with a competitive approach.The assay is based on sequential immobilizations of CgA, blocking agent BSA, and antibodies/redox probes/GO tags after competitively capturing CgA in sample solutions to form a GO/Fc/anti-CgA-CgA-PEI-AuNPs/GO sandwich structure.For this study, we use a blank solution instead of sample  solutions, which is expected to maximize the number of anti-CgA/Fc/GO nanotags on CgA and get the baseline current (maximum current).The optimizations involve PEI-AuNPs content, CgA content, immobilization time of CgA, immunoreaction time of anti-CgA on Fc/GO tags and CgA on the modified electrode surface after competitive capture, adsorption period of anti-CgA on Fc/GO tags to form anti-CgA/Fc/ GO tags, and immunoreaction time between anti-CgA on Fc/ GO tags and CgA in 1.0 ng mL −1 CgA solution during competitive capture.When each parameter is varied, 3.0 μL of 50 ng mL −1 CgA (40 min) and 3.0 μL of 0.50%w/v BSA (30 min), along with 3.0 μL of anti-CgA/Fc/GO tag solution (30 min) were employed for immunosensor construction.Figure 2A shows the DPV current responses of immunosensors in 0.20 M acetate buffer solution (pH 4.5) prepared using 3.0 mg mL −1 GO mixed with different PEI-AuNPs concentrations (1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 mM).With increasing PEI-AuNPs concentration, the current increases to a maximum of 4 mM and then decreases afterward.Therefore, to prepare the modified electrode, a solution containing 3.0 mg mL −1 GO and 4 mM PEI-AuNPs is chosen.To obtain the saturation of CgA on the PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPE's surface, various CgA solutions (25, 50, 75, 100, and 125 ng mL −1 ) are used for incubation.The DPV current response of the immunosensor is increased at the CgA concentration of 50 ng mL −1 and then remains constant, as shown in Figure 2B.So, the CgA concentration of 50 ng mL −1 is employed in the next experimental study.In addition, the immobilization time of CgA on the PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPE by incubation of 50 ng mL −1 CgA solution over 10−60 min is examined as depicted in Figure 2C.The immunosensor shows an increase in peak current to the plateau at a 30 min incubation, indicating complete binding of CgA to the electrode surface.Consequently, an incubation period of 30 min was selected for CgA immobilization.Figure 2D reveals the sensor's responses after incubation of the anti-CgA/Fc/GO tag solution on CgA/ PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPE over a range of 10−60 min.The peak current reaches a maximum value at an incubation time of 30 min and remains constant thereafter.This period indicates complete immunoreaction between the electrodebound CgA and the Fc/GO-bound anti-CgA.Additionally, anti-CgA content on the tag particles is important due to favorable amplification and detection sensitivity.The time for adsorption of anti-CgA on Fc/GO tags is also studied.5.0 μL of 50.0 μg mL −1 anti-CgA solution and 50.0 μL of the Fc/GO dispersion were employed for this study.Figure 2E shows the responses of the immunosensor when the time is varied in a range of 10−60 min.The current increases with extended adsorption time, reaching a plateau after 30 min, beyond which no significant variation is observed, indicating maximum adsorption of anti-CgA onto Fc/GO or a complete binding reaction between anti-CgA and Fc/GO tags.To prepare anti-CgA/Fc/GO tags, 30 min is required as a minimum time for adsorption of anti-CgA.Furthermore, the time for the competitive capture of CgA in 1.0 ng mL −1 CgA solution by the tags is examined, ranging from 10 to 60 min, to form the corresponding tag-bound immunocomplex.After these periods, the left anti-CgA/Fc/GO tags further bind to CgA/PEI-AuNPs/GO-modified SPE for 30 min.The current of the immunosensor decreases with the incubation duration, reaching the level-off point from 30 min as exhibited in Figure 2F, thus suggesting the completion of the immunoreaction between CgA in the sample solution and anti-CgA on the tags during the competitive binding.After a 30 min incubation time, this implies no further immunoreaction.This period is required and suitable for capturing CgA on the tags.For the construction of the developed competitive immunosensor, the optimized conditions for next study are as follows: 4 mM PEI-AuNPs, 3.0 mg mL −1 GO, 50 ng mL −1 CgA, a 30 min CgA immobilization time on the electrode surface, a 30 min binding time for Fc/GO-bound anti-CgA to electrode-bound CgA, a 30 min anti-CgA incubation on Fc/GO tags, and a 30 min reaction time between sample CgA and anti-CgA on Fc/GO nanotags.

■ CONCLUSIONS

In summary, a new competitive electrochemical immunosensor has been successfully designed for the CgA assay related to NETs diagnosis.The device detects CgA through the formation of a sandwich architecture using bifunctional GO nanosheets, which serve both as electrode comodifiers with PEI-AuNPs and as signaling particles when loaded with Fc and anti-CgA.The immunosensor exhibits a dynamic range of 0.10−50 ng mL −1 and a LOD of 90 pg mL −1 .Acceptable accuracy in detecting CgA levels in human serum samples is demonstrated by satisfactory recovery results.Compared to earlier sensors, this sensor offers significant advantages, including high specificity, long-term stability, and excellent reproducibility.Moreover, the low-cost fabrication process, based on inexpensive and readily available GO material, makes it suitable for large-scale production.This platform holds strong potential for clinical screening and point-of-care diagnosis of NETs.Additionally, integrating the GO platform with other biomolecules opens new possibilities for developing versatile biosensors.The current strategy provides a promising alternative for detecting various biomarkers.
