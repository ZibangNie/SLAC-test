This study examined the distributions of radon and its parent radionuclides 226 Ra and 238 U in groundwater samples from the Singhbhum region, Jharkhand, India.Radon showed a heterogeneous spatial distribution pattern, with a lognormal distribution.A good correlation between radon bubbler-Lucas cells and liquid scintillation counting techniques was observed for radon monitoring. 222Rn and 226 Ra were negatively correlated with pH, suggesting the mobilization of radionuclides at low pH.Uranium was positively correlated with common ions (e.g., Ca, Mg, and SO 4 2− ).The activity ratios among the radionuclides indicated disequilibrium, with 222 Rn origin controlled by parent radionuclide activity in bedrock.The 226 Ra and 238 U concentrations in groundwater were lower than the limits recommended by the WHO.

Introduction

Groundwater contains radionuclides of natural origin in trace amounts depending on the geological (nature of rock and soil) and environmental conditions (temperature and rainfall) [1,2].Uranium is naturally present in soil and rock in different quantities and is released into groundwater by natural weathering and anthropogenic activities such as industrial mining, milling, and poor management of mining waste.The abundance of 238 U is greater (99.27%) than that of two other uranium isotopes: 234 U and 235 U [3]. Uranium mainly exist in two different oxidation states such as U 4+ and UO 2 2+ in aqueous environments.U(IV) produces polymeric mixed hydroxides under anoxic conditions which are insoluble, whereas carbonate complexes are formed in groundwater under natural conditions.In an oxidizing atmosphere, U(IV) oxidizes to U(VI), which can readily form many soluble species and become more vulnerable to migration during weathering [4].pH, redox status, partial pressure of carbon dioxide, and the presence of complexing anions such as CO 3 2− , PO 4 3− , F − , and SO 4 2− are the primary factors influencing the distribution pattern of uranium in groundwater [5].Among the daughter products of 238 U, the most important radionuclides are 226  Ra and 222 Rn because of their radiological significance. 226Ra is an alphaemitting radionuclide with a 1622-year half-life, possesses only Ra(II) as its oxidation state in the environment, and has chemical characteristics similar to those of other alkaline earth metals [6].If radium is ingested, approximately 20% is absorbed and transported into the blood circulation, where it primarily builds up in the bones [7]. 222Rn is an immediate decay product of 226 Ra, and its concentration is governed by the 226 Ra content in different rocks, soils, and associated aquifers.Radon is a unique natural radionuclide in drinking water because it is a gas that does not have chemical affinities.Short-lived noble gas 222 Rn (t 1/2 = 3.82 days) has the potential to either escape before decay or decay inside the matrix to create additional daughter products such as 218 Po, 214 Pb, 214 Bi, 214 Po, 210 Pb, 210 Bi, and 210 Po. 222 Rn is not transported over long distances because of its short halflife, and its level in groundwater is regulated primarily by the rock lithology and soil around the borehole.In a mineralized region, higher 222 Rn and 226 Ra concentrations may be expected in groundwater, and sometimes higher concentrations of these radionuclides can help identify uranium mineralization in a particular zone and predict the nature of the rocks associated with the aquifers. 222Rn can enter the human body via the ingestion of water and/or inhalation of air.Fifty-two percent of the natural radiation dose to the worldwide population is contributed by 222 Rn and its daughter products [8].Indoor 222 Rn is caused mainly by exhalation from adjoining soil and building materials.Drinking water contributes approximately 1-2% of the total exposure to radon through the inhalation of radon gas [9].Dissolved 222 Rn has also been highlighted as a public health concern when it is present in excessive quantities in potable water.A maximum contaminant level (MCL) of 11 Bql −1 and an alternate maximum contaminant level (AMCL) of 148 Bql −1 combined with requirements for multimedia mitigation (MMM) programs for community water systems serving up to 10,000 people have been recommended by the USEPA (United States Environmental Protection Agency) for dissolved radon in water [9].Several studies have been carried out in other parts of the country and worldwide to assess radon levels in groundwater; however, few studies have explored the relationships of radon with its parent radionuclides and other physicochemical properties [10][11][12][13].The current study area bears special significance because of the large deposit of uranium and other commercially significant minerals.A brief study on the distributions of 222 Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U activity and physicochemical parameters in groundwater was conducted around the mineralized Singhbhum region of Jharkhand, India.This study explores the influence of groundwater chemistry on the distribution of these radionuclides; the relationships among the occurrence of 222 Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U; and background databases for the assessment of alterations in radioactivity levels resulting from natural or anthropogenic activities in the future.


Materials & methods


The study area

Groundwater sampling was conducted in the southeastern part of Jharkhand state (Saraikela-Kharsawan and East Singbhum districts) in eastern India, which is a part of the highly mineralized Singhbhum shear zone (SSZ).The Singhbhum Shear Zone hosts a large quantity of established mineral deposits (copper, uranium, etc.) of significant commercial importance.The major copper deposits are located around Rakha, Surda, and Musaboni.The country's second-largest uranium deposits are located in the SSZ, where Jaduguda, Narwapahar, Turamdih, and Banduhurang are the major uranium mineralized deposits.Approximately 53% of the study area is occupied by residual mountains and hills composed of granite, quartzite, polymictic conglomerate, and various schists [14].The main types of soil found in the research region include lateritic soil, red gravelly soil, red sandy soil, and red loamy soil [15].The region's climate is described as extreme in nature, with scorching summers and chilly winters.The rainy season runs from June to September.In the studied region, rainfall is the key regulating factor for groundwater recharge, which ranged from 945 to 1498 mm and 1602 to 2147 mm, respectively, in Saraikela-Kharsawan and East Singhbhum in the last five years.Unconfined as well as semiconfined to confined aquifer conditions are found in the study areas.The unconfined state occurs in the weathered part of the mantle portion of the rock and generally ranges in depth from 15 to 34 m.Fractures occur at depths of 30-200 m below ground (mbgl) in hard rock terrains [16].


Sampling and processing

Groundwater samples (108 nos.) were collected from borewells and handpumps in precleaned plastic bottles for analysis of different radiochemical constituents during the postmonsoon season.Five liters of water from each location were collected, and the pH, temperature, oxidationreduction potential (ORP), total dissolved solids (TDS), and electrical conductivity (EC) of the samples were measured immediately after sampling.The groundwater sampling locations are presented in Fig. 1.


Assessment of 222 Rn

222 Rn in groundwater was estimated via a radon bubblerscintillation cell technique and liquid scintillation techniques.The water samples were sampled, handled, and stored as outlined in ISO 13164-4 [17].

Analysis of Uranium

An LED fluorimeter (Make: M/s.Quantalase Enterprises, Model: LF2a) was used to measure the amount of uranium in the groundwater.In a clean, dry cuvette made of ultrapure fused silica, a 5 ml water sample was taken for analysis.A total of 0.5 ml of sodium pyrophosphate-phosphoric acid buffer solution was added to the cuvettes to increase the fluorescence yield by forming stable uranyl phosphate complexes.The buffer preparation method is described elsewhere [22].After the fluorescence of the solution was recorded, 0.1 ml of a 500 mgl −1 standard U solution was added twice to each water sample.After each addition, the corresponding fluorescence counts were recorded.To determine the uranium concentration in the sample, least squares fitting was used with the fluorescence data and the uranium content of the solution following the addition of standards.Every batch of analyses included counts for reagent blanks, and samples were analyzed in duplicate.M/s.Accu Standard, USA-made NIST traceable certified reference material (CRM), was used to evaluate the performance.The activity of 238 U was evaluated from the total uranium concentration by using the natural abundance ratio (99.27%), as it was (4)

Analysis of physicochemical parameters

Physicochemical parameters such as pH, temperature, ORP, EC, and TDS were measured instantly after the samples were collected via a multiparameter water quality analyzer (Make: M/s.Thermo Scientific, Model: PCD 650).The instrument was calibrated via NIST traceable CRM (Make: Eutech Instruments, Singapore) for pH, ORP, and EC.The anionic concentrations of HCO 3 − , Cl − , and SO 4 2− were estimated by following the Bureau of Indian Standard (BIS) methods [25][26][27].The Na + , K + , Ca 2+ , and Mg 2+ concentrations were estimated by an atomic absorption spectrometer (Make: M/s.GBC Scientific Equipment, Model: SavantAA Sigma).The calibration of the spectrometer was performed using NIST traceable CRMs (Make: Inorganic Ventures, USA).The quality control of the data was ensured by analysis of samples in duplicate and blank runs.


Results & discussion


Physicochemical characteristics of groundwater

pH, temperature, ORP, EC, TDS, HCO 3 − , Cl − , SO 4 2− , K + , Na + , Mg 2+ , and Ca 2+ were estimated in groundwater samples collected around the Singhbhum region.A summary of the results, including the mean, standard deviation (SD), geometric mean (GM), geometric standard deviation (GSD), range, median, first quartile (Q1), third quartile (Q3), skewness (S), and kurtosis (K), is presented in Table 1.The pH of the water samples varied from 5.3 to 7.7, with a mean of 6.5 ± 0.4, indicating the neutral to slightly acidic nature of the water.The ORP of the samples varied from 28 to 138 mV, with a mean of 85 ± 19.The temperature of the groundwater samples varied from 25 to 32 °C due to the wide variation in borehole depth.

The ORP values were in positive ranges, which indicated that the groundwater originated from an oxic environment.− concentrations ranged from 2 to 927, 4 to 848, and 66 to 797 mgl −1 , with means of 88 ± 118, 125 ± 142, and 332 ± 144 mgl −1 , respectively.The high standard deviation observed in the concentrations of cations and anions in the groundwater samples indicates significant variability within the dataset.This variability may be attributed to several factors, including geological heterogeneity, diverse rock formations, aquifer variability, hydrological processes, and anthropogenic influences.Geological factors such as the presence of different rock types with varying mineral compositions and the characteristics of the aquifer system can significantly impact ion concentrations.Hydrological processes like groundwater flow paths, recharge, and discharge rates also play a crucial role in determining ion distribution.Additionally, anthropogenic activities like agriculture, industry, and urbanization can introduce various ions into groundwater, further contributing to the observed variability.Similar findings have been reported in coastal aquifer system in India and northwest part in India, which highlight the influence of geological, hydrological, and anthropogenic factors on groundwater ion concentrations and their variability [28,29].According to the Piper diagram, the studied groundwater samples were mostly composed of calciummagnesium-chloride-sulfate ions in high-TDS areas and calcium-magnesium-bicarbonate ions in low-TDS areas [30].


Dissolved 222 Rn concentration and spatial distribution

The dissolved 222 Rn concentration in the groundwater samples ranged from 11 ± 0.4 to 883 ± 3 Bql −1 , with mean and median values of 190 ± 191 and 125 Bql −1 , respectively (Table 1).The high standard deviation in the concentrations of dissolved radon may be attributed to geological heterogeneity and variataion in hydrological process.The distributions of 222 Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U levels in terms of the median, mean, outliers, third quartile (Q3), first quartile (Q1), and interquartile range (IQR) are shown in the box-whisker plot (Fig. 2).

A box-whisker plot of the dissolved radon data indicated that the variation over a wide range and high standard deviation is due to the presence of significant numbers of outliers.The minimum detectable activity for this method was estimated to be 0.3 Bql −1 .The contour plot of the 222 Rn distribution revealed relatively high 222 Rn levels in the central region of the study area.This heterogeneous spatial pattern of radon concentrations in groundwater signifies the preferentially localized presence of its precursors, such as 226 Ra and 238 U.The dissolved radon data sets are positively skewed, as shown by the analyzed skewness (S) and kurtosis (K) values, which indicates the presence of asymmetric trails toward the right side of the mean and deviation from the normal distribution (Table 1).The distribution pattern of dissolved radon was evaluated statistically via Origin Pro 2020 software via Shapiro-Wilk, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Lilliefors, D'Agostino-K square, Chen-Shapiro, and Anderson-Darling hypothesis tests, which revealed the lognormal distribution of dissolved 222 Rn in the study area at the 95% confidence level.

Presently, there are no guidelines available for radon in drinking water in the country.On the basis of the national reference level for 222 Rn in the air and the distribution of 222 Rn in the housing area, the WHO suggests establishing a screening level for dissolved radon.The WHO also recommends carrying out measurements where high radon levels are expected in groundwater.A reference level above 100 Bql −1 in water used for commercial and public activities to consider any remedial actions to protect human health has been recommended by the European Union (EU) [31].The EU also recommends using the reference level of 1000 BqL −1 in water used for individual activities [31].In 46% of the studied samples, the dissolved radon concentration was less than 100 Bql −1 , and 100% of the samples presented dissolved radon concentrations less than 1000 Bql −1 .However, 56% of the groundwater samples presented dissolved radon concentrations lower than the USEPA-recommended AMCL (148 Bql −1 ) [9].In the study area, groundwater is used as the primary source of drinking water, which is collected from hand pumps (47%), overhead tanks (37%), and open wells (14%), and a minor portion of the public (2%) uses nearby river water for drinking.The drawn water is treated by a common method of cloth filtering (48%) and boiling (10%) [32].However, 42% of the population does not use any water treatment process, where they collect the groundwater into steel/aluminum pots or buckets and keep it inside the room using a lightweight cover; thus, it is expected that most of the groundwater is released into the air before consumption.The same water is generally consumed within one or two days.The higher levels of dissolved radon may be attributed to the presence of its precursor radionuclides in the adjoining rocks of the groundwater aquifer.The presence of uranium-bearing minerals in rocks in addition to copper minerals has already been reported in the SSZ, which extends over a 160 km belt that passes through the study region [33].Groundwater is in contact with uranium-rich geological formations and often has a closed or near-closed structure where 222 Rn may accumulate at a concentration of activity of several kBql −1 .However, whenever water is left in an open container for an extended period and degassing during the filtration process, the soluble radon rapidly escapes from the water.

The interrelationships between uranium, radium, radon, and other hydrochemical parameters at the 95% confidence level were evaluated via the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) via Origin Pro 2020 software (Table 3).The PCC establishes the degree and direction of a linear relationship between two variables [34].The Pearson correlation coefficient of dissolved radon with pH was -0.45 at the 95% confidence level, which indicates greater occurrence of radon in low-pH water samples.The positive correlation (0.38) of dissolved 222 Rn with 226 Ra suggests the cooccurrence of radon with its parent radionuclides, which may be attributed to (a) the production of radon from its parent radionuclide, which is already dissolved in groundwater; (b) the recoil of radon from uranium-and radium-rich rocks and soil in contact with the groundwater aquifer; and (c) the high rate of percolation of radon from the matrix to the groundwater aquifer [35,36].The co-occurrence of Fe with dissolved radon indicates that it is primarily controlled by low pH and oxic conditions.Iron and manganese, although often observed as cooccurring, exhibit complex behavior that does not necessarily result in co-occurrence in the analyzed samples.

The quality of the generated 222 Rn data was assured by simultaneous measurement via a liquid scintillation counter (LSC) and the radon bubbler-scintillation cell technique for eight randomly chosen samples, and the corresponding relative error was varied from 1 to 15% (Table 2), with a Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.99.The variation in radon concentrations analyzed by the two different techniques is presented graphically in Fig. 3.

The dissolved radon concentrations in the liquid scintillation counter and radon bubbler-scintillation cell technique varied from 30 ± 5 to 738 ± 23 and 34 ± 1 to 746 ± 6 Bql −1 , with means of 423 ± 261 and 412 ± 268 Bql −1 , respectively.

oxides present in rocks and soil and their dependence on pH [6].Increasing pH favors the adsorption of radium, whereas adsorption is negligible at acidic pH.The relative affinity of radium for ion exchange is greater than that of other alkaline earth elements [38]. 226Ra did not show any significant correlation with either alkaline earth metals (Mg and Ca) or anions, which may be attributed to its very low molar concentration compared with its other cationic and anionic counterparts.Radium mainly exists as uncomplexed Ra(II) in lowsalinity water [6].In groundwater, radium forms the most stable compound with sulfate because of its high stability complex (log K = 2.75) compared with bicarbonate and chloride; for 70 mgl −1 sulfate, approximately half of the radium remains as radium sulfate [6].In the present study, 41% of the analyzed groundwater samples showed a sulfate concentrations of 70 mgl −1 or more.The total alkalinity of the water samples ranged from 54 to 653 mgl −1 , with a mean of 272 ± 118 mgl −1 .The main component of alkalinity is the HCO 3 − ion, with a modest amount of CO 3 2− contribution at high pH values, along with a small contribution from weak acids such as borate, phosphate, and dissolved organic matter.As the pH of the samples is less than 8.3, it may be assumed that the total alkalinity predominantly arises from bicarbonates.The samples with alkalinity values less than the average alkalinity, including all the samples (272 ± 118 mgl −1 ), had higher 226  Ra and 222 Rn concentrations than did the samples with alkalinity values greater than the average alkalinity (Figs. 4 and 5).The 226 Ra and 222 Rn concentrations were negatively correlated with the bicarbonate concentration, and the same was confirmed by the significant Pearson correlation coefficients of -0.18 and -0.45, respectively, at the 95% confidence level.

Although the PCC analysis did not reveal any significant correlation with the ORP, the detailed analysis revealed relatively high levels of radium and dissolved radon at relatively high ORP values (Table 3).

The spatial distribution of 226 Ra concentrations indicates variations in location, and comparatively, higher levels were observed in the northwest direction of the study area.The radon concentrations are 3-4 orders of magnitude higher than the activity levels of its parent 226 Ra (Table 2).The  large excess 222 Rn may be attributed to the high percolation rate of radon from the matrix to the groundwater aquifer.


U concentration and spatial distribution

The 238 U concentration in the water samples ranged from 0.5 to 14.7 μgl −1 (0.012-0.368Bql −1 ), with mean and median values of 2.1 ± 2.8 μgl −1 (0.05 ± 0.070 Bql −1 ) and 0.8 μgl −1 (0.02 Bql −1 ), respectively.The mass concentration to activity concentration conversion was carried out by assuming natural equilibrium among uranium isotopes with abundances of 0.0055, 0.72, and 99.27% for 234 U, 235 U, and 238 U, respectively.The evaluated skewness and kurtosis values indicate that the uranium concentration distribution is positively skewed and deviates from the normal distribution (Table 1).Statistical analysis via Shapiro-Wilk, Lilliefors, Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Chen-Shapiro, D'Agostino-K square, and Anderson-Darling hypothesis tests revealed neither a normal nor log-normal distribution pattern of uranium in the water samples at the 95% confidence level.All of the water samples presented natural uranium concentrations below the drinking water limit (60 μgl −1 ) of the Atomic Energy Regulatory Board [39].The WHO issued a guidance level of 10 Bql −1 in terms of radioactivity for 238 U in drinking water [37].However, the WHO guideline for total uranium concentration is 30 μgl −1 in terms of chemical toxicity [37].In the present study, all of the samples presented 238 U activity below the WHO guidance level.A contour plot of the distribution of 238 U indicates that the uranium levels in the water samples varied widely from place to place.Comparatively higher 238 U concentrations were observed in the northwestern region than in the central region of the study area; however, there was significant variation even in these locations.

The localized high uranium levels may be attributed to groundwater contact with uranium-rich rocks and soil and the dissolution of uranium in an oxic environment, which is supported by the positive correlation (0.42) between EC and TDS with the uranium levels in groundwater.Uranium was also positively correlated with Ca, Mg, Na, SO 4 2− , total alkalinity, and Cl − (Table 4).Elevated uranium concentrations are the result of the formation of solid solutions with carbonated minerals, including calcite, aragonite, and vaterite.In calcic horizons, UO 2 2+ ions easily form complexes with carbonates that migrate alongside calcium and crystallize as calcium carbonate (CaCO 3 ).The study area is composed of Singhbhum granites, and alkaline and alkaline earth metal-rich granites typically contain more uranium than typical granites do [11].The presence of sulfide minerals such as pyrite generated during the late stage of magma or hydrothermal alteration in granitic rocks may be the cause of the positive connection with SO 4

2− [40].There was no correlation between uranium and pH or the ORP.

The aqueous uranium concentration is lower under reducing conditions because of the creation of U(IV) through the reduction of U(VI), as are minerals with a poor solubility of U(IV).Under reducing conditions at a neutral pH, uranium is immobilized, but under oxidizing conditions, it is easily mobilized.The positive correlation with HCO 3  − shows that uranium species exist in aquifers as uranyl-carbonate complexes [41].At high pH values, uranium desorption from aquifer materials may be accelerated by the production of uranium-carbonate complexes, leading to an increase in the uranium concentration in groundwater.This explains why uranium concentrations are higher in close-to-neutral pH ranges than in slightly acidic pH ranges.

Activity ratios of 222 Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U

The 222 Rn/ 226  Ra, 222 Rn/ 238 U, and 226 Ra/ 238 U activity ratios ranged from 246 to 2.3 × 10 5 , 286 to 1.3 × 10 5 , and 0.04 to 29, respectively.The high 222 Rn/ 226 Ra and 222 Rn/ 238 U activity ratios indicate that the dissolved radionuclides are not in secular equilibrium. 222Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U are transported in groundwater through various pathways. 226Ra and 238 U are solutes, whereas 222 Rn is an inert gas.The hydrogeochemical processes occurring beneath the subsurface may cause 238 U and its daughters, including 226 Ra, to be enriched on the surface of the fissures, which may significantly increase the 222 Rn exhalation efficiency of the bedrock [55,56].As a result, 222 Rn concentrations in groundwater in uranium-rich crystalline rocks may be higher than those in groundwater in other rock types. 222Rn migration is influenced by several variables, such as rock permeability, diffusion, rock moisture content, and emanation.Most recoiling 222 Rn remains within the mineral grain lattice since the recoil range in solids is negligible (< 0.05 μm) unless 226 Ra atoms are present within the recoil range [57]. 222Rn can migrate to groundwater if the recoiled 222 Rn ends up outside a particular grain or in an open pore space.Furthermore, the radon diffusion coefficient is very low (10 −25 -10 −27 m 2 s−1 ), suggesting a minimal contribution of radon by the diffusion process [57].Therefore, a significant amount of 226 Ra is present in the recoil range of radon, which may be attributed to the mineralization of uranium being present or adsorbed 226 Ra on the silicates and metal oxides [58,59].The low activity ratio of 226 Ra/ 238 U suggests disequilibrium among them.A  significant negative correlation coefficient (− 0.32) between 226 Ra/ 238 U and pH was observed.The above phenomenon suggests that the dissolution of 226 Ra decreases and/or uranium mobility increases with increasing pH.This differential radionuclide migration causes disequilibrium of radionuclides in the groundwater aquifer and a different activity ratio than that of the adjacent matrix.


Conclusions

This study provides a thorough understanding of the availability and mobility of dissolved 222 Rn, 226 Ra, and 238 U in groundwater by examining radionuclide concentrations and corresponding hydrochemistry.Analysis of groundwater samples from the Singhbhum region, India, revealed a heterogeneous distribution of 222 Rn (11-883 Bql −1 ).The 222 Rn exhibited a log-normal distribution and a strong positive correlation with its parent radionuclide 226 Ra (3.5-185 mBql −1 ), while both 222 Rn and 226 Ra were negatively correlated with pH. 238U concentrations (0.5-14.7 µgl −1 ) were positively correlated with common ions (Ca 2+ , Mg 2+ , Na + , SO 4 2−

) and total alkalinity, indicating an influence of water-rock interactions and the formation of soluble uranyl carbonate complexes on uranium mobility.There were significant differences in the radon and uranium concentrations of their parent components in the groundwater.The study results indicate that the source of radon in water is its parent radionuclides dissolved in water and other sources located on the bedrock of the aquifer, which is supported by the very high concentration of 222 Rn coupled with low amounts of uranium and radium.The radioactive uranium/radium deposit present at the fracture surface is one of the most plausible causes.The majority of the analyzed wells have a 226 Ra/ 238 U ratio that is greater than unity, indicating that 226 Ra is more transportable than uranium is.Statistical analysis revealed a log-normal distribution pattern of 222 Rn in groundwater. 222Rn shows a significant positive Pearson correlation coefficient with its parent 226 Ra, which suggests that it cooccurs.The 222 Rn/ 226 Ra, 222 Rn/ 238 U, and 226 Ra/ 238 U activity ratios indicate disequilibrium among radionuclides.The origin of radon is predominantly the adjoining bedrock of the groundwater aquifer, and the contribution of 222 Rn from the dissolved 226 Ra is minor.
