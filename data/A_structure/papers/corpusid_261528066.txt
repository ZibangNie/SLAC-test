Background: Desmoplastic fibroblastoma (collagenous fibroma) is a rare soft tissue tumor that usually arises in the subcutis or skeletal muscle.Cases superficial to fascia are unusual and can cause diagnostic difficulty.We present 11 cases of superficial desmoplastic fibroblastoma involving a wide anatomic distribution.Methods: Archives were searched using the term "desmoplastic fibroblastoma" over a 10-year period (2012-2022).Cases superficial to fascia were retrieved, and available clinicopathologic features were recorded.Only cases involving the dermis were included.Results: Eleven cases were identified, all of which were received in consultation.Tumors involved the head and neck (2), lower extremity (2), back (2), foot (1), shoulder (1), axilla (1), hand (1), and breast (1).Each consisted of a hypocellular proliferation of bland stellate to spindled fibroblasts set in a collagenous to focally myxoid stroma.The immunohistochemical stains available for review demonstrated SMA positivity (4/7) and negative immunoreactivity for CD34 (0/6), EMA (0/3), desmin (0/3), and S100 (0/7).Conclusions: Desmoplastic fibroblastoma may present superficially in the dermis to subcutis, posing a potential source of diagnostic difficulty.Recognition of the characteristic histopathologic features of desmoplastic fibroblastoma with judicial use of immunohistochemical stains should allow for accurate diagnosis.

| INTRODUCTION

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma (also known as collagenous fibroma) was first described in a seven-case series by Dr. Harry Evans in 1995. 1 It is a benign fibroblastic/myofibroblastic neoplasm, typically composed of medium-sized to large "reactive-appearing" spindled to stellate fibroblasts sparsely distributed in a fibromyxoid to densely fibrous background. 1 In his series, three cases were intramuscular and four were subcutaneous. 1In 1998, Miettinen et al. reported a large series of 63 cases of desmoplastic fibroblastoma.In their series, the lesions also had a wide anatomic distribution, similar to the initial description.The lesions they observed were predominantly in the subcutis, though Ahmed Bakhshwin and Gabriel Oaxaca should be considered joint first author.[8] Desmoplastic fibroblastoma, given its propensity to arise within the subcutis or deeper soft tissues, may pose diagnostic difficulty when encountered involving the dermis, as it is not typically at the forefront of the practicing dermatopathologists' differential diagnoses.

We present a case series of 11 desmoplastic fibroblastomas that involved the dermis to heighten awareness of this benign fibroblastic/ myofibroblastic lesion.


| METHODS

After obtaining the appropriate Institutional Review Board, our pathology archives were searched using the term "desmoplastic fibroblastoma" over a 10-year period (2012-2022).The clinicopathologic features of desmoplastic fibroblastoma were recorded.Cases arising within the dermis to subcutis were retrieved.Of these, only the patients with dermal involvement were included.We excluded the patients where the tumor was exclusively located in the subcutis with no evidence of dermal extension and where the relationship to the dermis was not clear.For all cases included, hematoxylin and eosin-stained slides were reviewed, and the diagnosis was confirmed.

For the cases that have immunohistochemical stains, we recorded their results as positive or negative (if less than 5% of the lesional cells demonstrated immunoreactivity).


| RESULTS

Of 110 cases diagnosed over the time period, 20 cases of desmoplastic fibroblastoma arising within the dermis to subcutis were identified.

All cases were received in consultation.Of the 20 cases, 13 showed dermal involvement, 11 of which had material available for review.

They demonstrated no clear gender predilection (six female: five male; Table 1) and ranged in age from 12 to 76 years (median 49 years).The cases arose in the head and neck (n = 2; 18%), lower extremities (n = 2; 18%), back (n = 2; 18%), feet, shoulder, axilla, hand, and breast (n = 1 for each; 9%).Of the 11 cases, 10 had gross tumor measurements available, which ranged from 0.7 to 3 cm in greatest dimension (median 1 cm).Each had typical features of desmoplastic fibroblastoma: a hypocellular proliferation of bland stellate to spindled fibroblasts set in a collagenous to focally myxoid and sparsely vascular, stroma (Figures 1-3).More than half (6 out of 11 cases) were well-circumscribed, while approximately 45% (5 out of 11 cases) have ill-defined boarders, including some with pushing boarders against subcutaneous adipose tissue and peripheral collagen trapping (Figure 3).

The dermal distribution occupied by the tumors was noted and categorized as dermal only (n = 5; 45%) and dermal/subcutaneous (n = 6; 55%).Immunohistochemical stains were performed on 10 of the 11 tumors.Immunoreactivity for SMA was seen in four of seven.

None were positive for S100 protein (0/7), CD34 (0/6), EMA (0/3), desmin (0/3), SOX10 (0/2), ERG (0/1), or Melan-A (0/1).Clinically, most of these lesions were thought to be lipomas, benign cysts, or scars.Suggested diagnoses from referring pathologists were: nodular fasciitis (2), fibromatosis (1), chondromyxoid fibroma (1), spindle cell lipoma (1), low-grade sarcoma (1), and myxofibrosarcoma (1).

In none of the cases was the diagnosis of desmoplastic fibroblastoma suggested.


| DISCUSSION

Desmoplastic fibroblastoma is a rare, benign soft tissue tumor that typically occurs in the subcutis or deep soft tissue of the extremities, although it can occur in other anatomic locations as well.As it is seldom encountered in the dermis, it represents a potential source of diagnostic difficulty for practicing general pathologists and dermatopathologists who are not accustomed to dealing with such cases in their routine practice.Herein, we present the largest series of desmoplastic fibroblastoma affecting the dermis with the intention of raising awareness of their existence and describing their clinicopathologic characteristics.In general, diagnostic considerations include neurofibroma, perineurioma, low-grade fibromyxoid sarcoma (LGFMS), fibromatosis, nodular fasciitis, and fibroma of tendon sheath (Table 2).

Desmoplastic fibroblastomas were typically present as wellcircumscribed, firm, non-encapsulated lesions that are composed of bland, stellate fibroblasts with elongated cytoplasmic processes set in a paucicellular and sclerotic to focally myxoid stroma with few if any, small caliber vessels.Atypia or increased mitotic activity should not be observed. 1All of the lesions in the differential diagnosis are, on average, more cellular than desmoplastic fibroblastoma (Figures 1-3).
