Parameter-adaptive variational autoencoder for linear/nonlinear blind source separation

Blind source separation (BSS) serves as an important technique in the field of structural health monitoring (SHM), particularly for solving modal decomposition tasks.This study proposes a novel approach to both linear and nonlinear BSS problems in the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework, where the encoding and decoding processes of VAE are interpreted as procedures for inferring sources from observations and remixing these sources, respectively.In this way, the distribution of latent variables inferred by VAE is equivalent to the distribution of sources.We make improvements to the vanilla VAE to augment its effectiveness for BSS.First, we substitute standard normal distributions with trainable Gaussian processes (GP) as priors for latent variables and implement an exponential function as the activation function for adaptive parameters in the GP kernel functions.While the form of the priors is set as GP, the parameters of their kernel functions are not fixed but automatically converge to suitable values during the model training process.Additionally, a hyperparameter is introduced to balance the terms in the loss function.The proposed method is referred to as parameter-adaptive VAE (PAVAE).Then, upon different assumptions of the variances of sources, the proposed PAVAE is branched into two types: homoscedastic PAVAE (Ho-PAVAE) and heteroscedastic PAVAE (He-PAVAE).Through numerical and laboratory experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in solving BSS problems and their potential to underpin future research in SHM.

Introduction

Blind source separation (BSS) is a crucial computational problem, serving as a foundation for numerous applications, including structural health monitoring (SHM) [1][2][3][4].BSS methods do not require additional information other than observations to separate independent source signals.Consequently, BSS methods facilitate the extraction of modal vibrations and the corresponding modal matrix solely based on the structural responses, eliminating the need for additional information.This is particularly applicable to low-damping structures, where a linear relationship exists between modal vibrations and structural responses.Given the above characteristics, BSS methods can assist in model updating [5,6] and damage detection [7] of structures.In recent years, neural networks have been increasingly adopted to solve BSS problems due to their ability to extract features and represent complex functions.Variational Bayesian methods have been explored to solve source separation problems [8,9].Further combining variational Bayesian into an autoencoder (AE) [10] architecture, the variational autoencoder (VAE) comes out [11].

Although VAE was initially proposed for unsupervised learning [12,13], it is effective for supervised or semisupervised learning as well [14,15].Several source separation methods grounded on VAE that need supervision were proposed [16][17][18][19].Through the training of a conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) using labeled spectrograms, Kameoka et al. [16] developed a method capable of iteratively estimating source power spectrograms, thereby enabling an algorithm for supervised source separation for determined problems.By building a VAE for the spectral information of each source signal, Pandey et al. [17] proposed a supervised source separation method which can solve underdetermined problems.Seki et al. [18] proposed a multi-channel VAE that differentiates source signals using label information, thereby surmounting the inefficiency of needing to establish an independent VAE model for each source signal.Murashima et al. [19] utilized a set of speaker-labeled training samples to train the parameters of the encoder and decoder in the VAE model.Through the trained VAE model and supplemented by the Wiener filter [20], the spectrograms of each speaker in the mixed audio can be separated.The above VAE-based supervised source separation methods share two key traits.First, they employ spectral signal information rather than pure timedomain data.Second, they consider the encoder as a means to extract low-dimensional features from the mixed input observations, while the decoder is tasked with generating independent source signals.

For domains supported by sufficient training datasets, supervised source separation methods are mature and practical.However, when the knowledge of the sources is lacking, or the dataset of clean sources is insufficient, it is necessary to solve source separation problems in an unsupervised manner [21,22].Compared with the supervised methods, unsupervised source BSS methods with VAE are less studied and more challenging.Neri et al. (2021) [23] presented a way to separate several images that are directly overlapped by adopting Laplace likelihood in the objective function of VAE.In this work, an overlapping image is compressed into low-dimensional latent variables by the encoder of VAE.This process can be regarded as the low-dimensional feature extraction of the input image, similar to the aforementioned supervised methods.Then, these latent variables are, respectively, fed into the decoder, and the corresponding output is each independent image.It should be noted that this work focuses on separating two directly superimposed images, and does not address other mixed scenarios.

Distinct from the aforementioned supervised or unsupervised learning methods, Brakel and Bengio's Anica [24] method adopts a different model to derive the sources.This model adheres to the classic assumption in the BSS problem, which states that the observations ∈ R C×T (C signals each with T data points) is a static mix of the source signals ∈ R D×T (D signals each with T data points), i.e., = M , and the mixing mode M does not change over time.The Anica framework comprises an autoencoder (AE) and a discriminator [25].The role of the AE's encoder is to map the observations directly to the source signals .In this sense, Anica's encoder does not compress or feature the observations but directly inverses the mixing process, i.e., = M −1 .Anica's decoder, on the other hand, recon- structs the observations from the sources , i.e., = M .

In simple terms, Anica's encoder and decoder represent M −1 and M , respectively.In the Anica architecture, a dis- criminator is also included along with the AE.Brakel and Bengio created a sampling strategy that enables obtaining samples from the joint and marginal distribution product of the latent variables .These samples are then fed into the discriminator, which uses adversarial training [25] to enforce the joint distribution and the product of the marginal distributions to approach each other, thereby achieving p 1 , 2 , … , D = p 1 p 2 … p( D ) .In this way, the encoder of the AE in Anica is forced to work as a separator to obtain latent variables that are individual to each other.Therefore, the latent variables are equivalent to sources in Anica.

Due to the ingenious use of adversarial learning, Anica can directly separate sources from observations in the time domain alone without any supervised training process, which is more in line with the meaning of the word "blind" in BSS.Drawing inspiration from Anica, we present a novel BSS approach within the VAE framework.Given that VAE encompasses an encoder and decoder, we can also assign them the functions of M −1 and M , respectively, just like in Anica.However, since VAE is essentially a variational inference framework, our interpretation of using VAE to obtain sources is different from that of Anica.In the VAE framework, the inversion process is actually performed by the inference process.As observations are generated through sources , there is a causal relationship between and .If we, like Anica, treat the latent variables as sources , the process of extracting from known would amount to determining the posterior distribution p( | ) of these latent variables.Leveraging variational inference in the VAE framework, this posterior can be approximated.Therefore, when the sources are regarded as latent variables, it is theoretically possible to directly use VAE to infer the sources from the observation without any supervised training.At this juncture, the sources are expressed in the form of distribution p( | ).

However, we found that following VAE with its original setting [11] (vanilla VAE), the latent variables obtained are not actually the sources.Given this, a series of improvements are proposed.In the framework of vanilla VAE, the untraceable true posterior p( | ) is approximated by q( | ) [11].To make q( | ) close to p( | ) by variational infer- ence, a reasonable prior p( ) is needed.It is known that the prior distribution of the latent variables in the vanilla VAE is the standard normal distribution [11].On the one hand, the standard normal distribution is too naive to adapt to complex conditions; on the other hand, this prior only focuses on the statistical characteristics of the data while ignoring the possible temporal correlations of sources.Most of the aforementioned methods assume the data points of each source are independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and ignore the temporal correlation between them.However, this setting does not sufficiently concur to the real world [26][27][28].In this connection, we introduce zero-mean Gaussian processes (GP) [29,30] as the priors of sources to depict the potential temporal correlation in each source signal.However, it is difficult to directly determine the length scales of the GP's kernel functions without other information.Therefore, we make these parameters to be adaptive variables, so that their values can be automatically determined during the process of minimizing the loss function in the proposed method, which will be detailed in the next section.

Although the parameters are set to be trainable, the range of their values must be positive.This study uses activation functions to control the final output values of these parameters.Among commonly used activation functions, the Softplus activation function [31] can limit the values of these parameters to positive.However, we find that the exponential function, which also meets our requirements, can make converge faster than Softplus.Therefore, this study utilizes the exponential function as the activation function of .The performances of the exponential and Softplus functions will be detailed in the next section, as well as the analysis of why the exponential function is not commonly used.

In addition to the above improvements, a hyperparameter H is also introduced to balance the weights of the terms in the loss function.It is found that the value of H affects both the BSS performance and the reconstruction quality of the observation signals.We propose a method to determine an appropriate value of H based only on the reconstruction quality of observation signals, which does not change the unsupervised nature of the proposed method.

Within the framework of VAE, the posterior q( | ) is defined by a mean matrix ∈ R D×T and a variance matrix 2 ∈ R D×T .That is to say, the distribution of a single source signal q i | is represented by a mean vector i ∈ R 1×T and a variance vector 2 i ∈ R 1×T , which also indicates that q i | is assumed to be heteroscedastic.However, it is worth considering whether making such a complex assumption on the variance of the estimated sources is necessary, as introducing more unknown parameters may increase the difficulty of variational inference.Given this, we propose a modification to reduce the size of the variance of q( | ) to 2 ∈ R D×1 and make the distribution of each source sig- nal q i | homoscedastic.Thus, the variance parameters 2 ∈ R D×1 are no longer output by the encoder, but rather are set as additional adaptive parameters like the length scales , which automatically converge during model training.

With the above improvements, we name the proposed method parameter-adaptive VAE (PAVAE).For a more comprehensive analysis, the heteroscedastic assumption that 2 ∈ R D×T and the homoscedastic assumption 2 ∈ R D×1 for PAVAE in solving BSS problems are both investigated.

For distinction, these two settings of PAVAE are referred to as He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE, respectively.

Numerical and experimental investigations are conducted to verify the performance of the proposed He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE.And the performances of the vanilla VAE [11], traditional FastICA [32], and the state-of-the-art Anica [25] are also presented for comparison.The main contributions of this stuy can be summarized as follows:

(1) A novel approach named PAVAE for solving BSS problems is presented.The proposed PAVAE can be further divided into He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE according to different assumptions on the variances of distribution of the source signals.To model the temporal structure of the source signals, GPs with adaptive length scales are introduced as the priors for He/Ho-PAVAE.The introduction of the adaptive length scales allows the prior of He/Ho-PAVAE not to be fixed and can converge automatically to an appropriate form.

(2) The exponential function is used as the activation function of the additional adaptive parameters in He/Ho-PAVAE; it helps the adaptive parameters to converge quickly.(3) A strategy for determining the value of the hyperparameter H is proposed.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.The "Methodology" section provides a detailed derivation of the loss functions for He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE, as well as an explanation of the principle behind the adaptive parameters and their use in conjunction with the exponential function as their activation function.The "Numerical Verification" and "Experimental Verification" sections illustrate in detail the performance of the proposed methods to solve BSS problems in different scenarios, including the comparison with several commonly used BSS methods.Finally, the section "Conclusion" summarizes the strengths and weaknesses of the proposed method.

Determination of hyperparameter H

The hyperparameter H has a great influence on the source separation performance of the proposed He/Ho-PAVAE.If the hyperparameter H is too large, it means that the propor- tion of MSE in the loss function ( 20) is reduced, which may cause the decoder to be unable to reconstruct the observations.Note that in He/Ho-PAVAE, the role of the decoder is to ensure that the inferred sources can be remixed into observations.That is, failing to reconstruct the observations by the He/Ho-PAVAE model also implies that the sources are not successfully inferred.This reasoning will be justified in the following example.On the other hand, if the hyperparameter H is too small, it means that the proportion of MSE in the loss function is too large, then the influence of other variational inference terms becomes negligible.Although, in this way, the observation signals can be accurately reconstructed, He/Ho-PAVAE will degenerate into a general AE without the impact of variational inference.Therefore, an appropriate H is important in solving BSS problems.Since the true source signals are theoretically unknown, we intend to find a way to get an appropriate value of H only by observing the reconstruction performance of the observation signals.

The reconstruction performance under various H of Ho- PAVAE is shown in Fig. 8 as an example.For ease of comparison, the observations and the corresponding reconstructed signals ̂ are both normalized to [− 1, 1], where the solid blue line represents the normalized and the dashed red line represents the normalized ̂ .The mean value of root-mean-squared errors (RMSEs) between the three original and reconstructed observation signals is used to quantify the reconstruction quality.

Unlike FastICA and Anica, the sources q ( | ) inferred by He/Ho-PAVAE are distributions rather than deterministic values.For the convenience of comparison, we simply use the means of q ( | ) to represent the estimated sources from He/Ho-PAVAE and from vanilla VAE.The estimated sources are normalized by the standard score and the ground truth, that is The solid blue line in Fig. 9 represents the normalized ground truth, and the red dashed line represents the normalized estimated sources.The difference between them is also quantified by the mean value of RMSEs of the three normalized estimated sources and the ground truth.Figure 10 depicts the variations of reconstruction quality and source separation performance of He/Ho-PAVAE as H varies.The reconstruction quality of He/Ho-PAVAE changes from underfitting to overfitting, which is reasonable, because the weight of the MSE term in the loss function increases as H decreases.While, the source separation per- formance of He/Ho-PAVAE shows a trend of improvement first followed by degradation.This is because the weight of the prior and posterior terms of the source signals in the loss function decreases as H decreases, causing He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE to lose their ability of variational inference and gradually degenerate into AE.

More details can be demonstrated by combining Figs. 8 and 9 for analysis.It is found that source 1 is successfully inferred when H = 6 and it seems that the reconstructed observation signals ̂ are only remixed by source 1 along with some noise.With the decrease of H , source 2 is obtained.Meanwhile, the reconstructed signals ̂ capture more details and are closer to .And then, when three sources are all successfully obtained, the gap between ̂ and is further reduced.This implies that a necessary condition for obtaining source signals is that the observations are successfully reconstructed.

With the above analysis, a suitable H can be determined by evaluating the reconstruction performance of the known observation signals .Qualitatively speaking, when the reconstructed ̂ fits the features of observations accept- ably but not overfitting, the inferred sources will be close to the ground truth.It is reasonable to allow some gap between and ̂ , since the reconstructed ̂ is generated by a sample ̃ from q ( | ) with some randomness.Hence, under the premise of ensuring a certain level of reconstruction quality, the value of H should be as large as possible to ensure that the KL divergence gets sufficient weight in the loss function.However, we still need a quantitative threshold to avoid the above criterion being too subjective.Therefore, we choose the value of H when the mean values of RMSEs between and ̂ is below 0.15, and regard the corresponding inferred results as the estimated sources.All cases in this study abide by the above criterion.It is almost impossible to obtain the optimal value of H . Fortunately, the range of acceptable values of H is not fleeting, as can be seen in Fig. 10(b), so finding a suitable H value is not very difficult.

OLM scenario

In addressing BSS problems, the number of observations may not equal the number of sources.This OLM scenario explores the performance of the proposed methods when the number of observations exceeds that of sources.Because the actual number of sources is unknown, we simply assume that the number of sources is equal to the number of observations.Figure 14 illustrates the performance of He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE in addressing the BSS problem with an OLM setting, where the blue solid line represents the mean of the estimated source distributions, and the gray area represents the 95% confidence interval.The H values for He- PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE are both taken as 0.1.Considering that there are five observed signals, both He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE set the source count to five as well.Notably, He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE successfully infer the distributions of three actual source signals, while the distributions of redundant, spurious sources show characteristics akin to a zero-mean Gaussian distribution.Consequently, even though He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE do not explicitly output the quantity of source signals, they enable the identification of redundant sources through inferred distributions.Should any of the signal distributions closely resemble a zero-mean Gaussian, it would be deemed redundant.This the number of source signals to be indirectly ascertained when using the proposed method.

The performance of other methods is shown in Fig. 15.It is seen that these methods are unable to obtain the source signals accurately.After normalization, the three source separation results closest to the ground truth in each method are presented for comparison.Figure 16 provides a comparison of the normalized estimated results obtained by all the methods and the ground truth, which indicates that the performance of He/Ho-PAVAE is superior to that of the vanilla VAE, FastICA, and Anica.

In the framework of He/Ho-GPVAE, the encoding process is viewed as an inverse process of obtaining sources (t) ∈ R D from observations (t) ∈ R C .However, this process cannot be solved when D > C .As such, He/Ho-PAVAE cannot solve the BSS problem under the underdetermined mixing condition, and the limitation is also valid for the vanilla VAE and Anica.


MLPM scenario

As shown in Fig. 7, hidden layers are added to the encoder and decoder of He/Ho-PAVAE for solving the source separation task in the MLPM scenario.We configure hidden layers with different depths and lengths, ranging from 1 to 4 layers with the number of nodes in each layer being 5 to 30.The rectified linear unit (ReLU) is adopted as the activation function between the hidden layers.

Using the mean value of RMSEs between the normalized source separation results and the ground truth as the evaluation criterion, Fig. 18 shows the performance of He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE with different levels of complexity in their hidden layers.According to the previous criterion, the values of H for He/Ho-PAVAE under these conditions are mostly between 0.01 and 0.6.The blue line in Fig. 18(a) and (b) represents the mean values of RMSEs when no hidden layers are added in the encoder or decoder of He/Ho-PAVAE.In general, the more complex the hidden layers of the encoder/ decoder in He/Ho-PAVAE, the stronger its nonlinear mapping ability.However, it is found that an excessive number of parameters may increase the difficulty of variational inference and may not necessarily lead to better performance.It is observed that after adding one hidden layer, the performance of He-PAVAE improves significantly.However, when the hidden layers of its encoder and decoder are set to be more complex, its performance begins to decline.Due to the simpler setting of the variance of the latent variables and fewer parameters, Ho-PAVAE outperforms He-PAVAE.It is also seen that within the range of 1-3 layers, the performance  of Ho-PAVAE improves with an increasing number of hidden layers.However, this trend is reversed when the number of hidden layers increases to 4. A similar phenomenon occurs in the setting of the number of nodes per layer, where the BSS performance of He/Ho-PAVAE does not always improve with an increase in the number of nodes.

The above observations indicate that setting the complexity of hidden layers may lead to a dilemma.On the one hand, the encoder and decoder of He/Ho-PAVAE require sufficiently complex hidden layers to have enough nonlinear mapping capability, but setting the hidden layers too complicated will increase the difficulty of variational inference.Besides, the performance of Ho-PAVAE is better than that of He-PAVAE, which further illustrates that unnecessary complexity can increase the difficulty of solving BSS problems.

In addition to the nonlinear mixing scenario, we also investigate the impact of hidden layers on solving the BSS problem under linear mixing conditions.Figure 19 presents the performance of He/Ho-PAVAE in solving the DLM scenario under various complexity settings of the hidden layers.The blue line illustrates the mean values of RMSEs of He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE when no hidden layers exist.It is found that increasing the complexity of the encoder/ decoder in He/Ho-PAVAE does not improve its ability to solve the BSS problem under linear mixing conditions.This demonstrates once again that unnecessary parameters may increase the difficulty of variational inference and thus generate adverse effects when addressing BSS problems.When solving BSS problems, we do not know a priori whether the source signals are mixed linearly or nonlinearly.In view of this, a sensible way is to first assume that the sources are linearly mixed and then gradually increase the complexity of the hidden layers.We also examine the impact of the complexity of hidden layers on the performance of the vanilla VAE and Anica in handling the MLPM scenario.Figure 20(a) shows that, compared to the linear setting, the performance of the vanilla VAEs in handling the MLPM scenario is improved when the depth of the hidden layers is no greater than 2. Further increasing the complexity of the hidden layers does not enhance the performance of the vanilla VAEs, but instead increases the difficulty of inferring accurate results.A similar phenomenon is present in the case of Anica, as demonstrated in Fig. 20(b).An increase in the complexity of Anica's encoder/decoder does not appear to enhance its ability to handle nonlinear problems significantly but instead has a detrimental effect on its performance.The performance of the vanilla VAE and Anica indicates that, without reasonable assumptions and configurations, merely increasing network complexity offers limited assistance in addressing nonlinear BSS problems.

It is observed that He-PAVAE achieves its best performance when adding a hidden layer with 25 nodes to both its encoder and decoder, while Ho-PAVAE achieves the optimal performance with three hidden layers, each containing 15 nodes, in both its encoder and decoder.The best-performing results of the vanilla VAE occur when its encoder/decoder has two hidden layers with 20 nodes.For Anica, this happens when four hidden layers with 25 nodes per layer are equipped.The detailed source separation results in the above scenarios are illustrated in Fig. 21.

Conclusions

This study proposes a series of improvements which enable the BSS problems to be solved within the VAE framework.

According to different assumptions about the complexity of the estimated source signals, He-PAVAE and Ho-PAVAE are put forward.The proposed method treats the encoding process as an inverse process of obtaining source signals from the observation signals and the decoding process as a process of remixing the estimated source signals into the observation signals.In both simulation and experimental studies, the proposed method using appropriate priors and adaptive parameters shows better performance than the vanilla VAE, FastICA, and Anica.

In comparison with He-PAVAE, Ho-PAVAE performs better, especially in handling nonlinear BSS problems.This is because Ho-PAVAE assumes simpler settings of variance in the distributions of latent variables.It is also found that the hidden layers lead to adverse effects when solving linear mixing BSS problems.Therefore, unnecessary complexity settings of neural networks may negatively impact the source separation performance.
