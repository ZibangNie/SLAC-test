Development of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial based dispersive solid phase microextraction method for cadmium determination in thyme samples using flame atomic absorption spectrometry

This study presents an efficient and straightforward approach for the easy synthesis and innovative analytical application of an industrial nanomaterial of BaSrTiO 3 (BST).Characterization techniques including XRD (X-ray Diffraction), FTIR (Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy), and SEM (scanning electron microscopy) were utilized for characterizing the synthesized nanomaterial.Subsequently, the incorporation of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial-based DSPME (dispersive solid phase microextraction) and FAAS (flame atomic absorption spectrophotometry) was implemented for the preconcentration of Cd with enhanced precision and accuracy.Each variable influencing the outcome of extraction efficiency has been defined in the optimization studies.The limit of quantification (LOQ) and limit of detection (LOD) values for the BaSrTiO 3 (BST)-DSPME-FAAS system were determined to be 1.1 µg/L and 0.33 µg/L, respectively.Recovery examinations were conducted on two different thyme tea samples utilizing multiple calibration methodologies.The calculated percent recovery values from the spiked samples were in the range of 89.2-123%.

Studies in the literature have shown that cadmium binds to organic matter and is absorbed by plants, including cereal crops 7,8 .In regions that are marked by both anthropogenic and geogenic elevated concentrations of cadmium in soil and groundwater, food sources have been observed to accumulate cadmium, resulting in higher daily intake levels, even across various continents across the world 3,5,9 .An excessive intake of products contaminated with cadmium can result in significant harm to vital organs comprising the lungs and liver.Numerous studies have revealed associations among cadmium exposure and various health conditions comprising the development of cancer and other life-threatening health conditions.The occurrence of "Itai-Itai disease" in Japan during the 1950s, caused by the long-term consumption of cadmium-contaminated rice drew significant global attention for cadmium pollution in food industry 10,11 .The continued utilization of Cd in industrial applications and its subsequent release into the environment have led to the persistent contamination of food products, indicating that the general population continues to face exposure through various pathways.Furthermore, the limited capacity of the human body to eliminate this element may only become apparent years or even decades after exposure begins, highlighting the adverse public health effects of Cd pollution and its preventable, yet persistent, presence 6 .Hence, developing reliable and efficient methods for determining cadmium in plant-derived beverages and foods is essential to identify excessive cadmium levels in products and in the environments where they are cultivated.

The literature presents analyses of heavy metal concentration levels in various biological samples, utilizing both cost-effective, user-friendly devices as well as advanced, high-sensitivity instruments.Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS), Inductively Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy (ICP-OES) are analytical techniques that offer high levels of sensitivity and accuracy for the precise measurement of heavy metals but incur of significant costs [12][13][14] .In contrast, Flame Atomic Absorption Spectrometry (FAAS) presents a cost-effective, rapid, and user-friendly alternative, although it has reduced sensitivity and accuracy, and is significantly influenced by the sample matrix being analyzed 15 .Based on its affordability, simplicity, and selectivity, FAAS is frequently used to monitor metal ions in biological specimens and food compositions, particularly utilized in conjunction with sample preparation techniques 16 .

The complex structure of a biological matrix can cause interference within both the matrix and spectrum components if samples are not properly treated prior to FAAS analysis 17,18 .Liquid-liquid extraction (LLE) and solid-phase extraction (SPE) are effective and widely recognized methods for the extraction of contaminants from biological matrices, continuing to serve as fundamental sample preparation techniques.However, current trends in sample handling emphasize the advancement of extraction techniques that are simpler, less hazardous, and more environmentally sustainable, comprising DSPME and dispersive liquid-liquid microextraction (DLLME) techniques [19][20][21] .In DSPME, analyte extraction is accomplished through the insertion of solid adsorbents to the sample environment.Following the extraction procedure, the adsorbents with adsorbed analytes are separated via centrifugation, filtration, subsequently washed with a desorption solvent to facilitate analyte recovery.The DSPME technique promotes enhanced interaction between target analytes and adsorbents 22 .DSPME has gained significant attention as a promising sample pretreatment technique, allowing the utilization of numerous adsorbent variations that can be further functionalized and characterized in accordance with the matrix and analyte of interest [23][24][25] .

Nanomaterials have been extensively explored as nano-adsorbents in dispersive solid phase micro extraction for their utility in extracting and preconcentrating pollutants and analytes from complex biological and food matrices.In addition to carbon-based nanomaterials 26,27 , metal oxide composites 28 , metal-organic frameworks (MOFs), covalent organic frameworks (COFs) 29 , and functionalized magnetic nanoparticles 30 , which have been synthesized specifically for heavy metal extraction and sample treatment, the efficacy of materials not originally designed for microextraction techniques has also been evaluated to promote the circular bioeconomy 31 .

Barium strontium titanates (BST) have previously been investigated for applications in configurable microwave devices, including filters, antennas, and phase shifters.This is attributed to BST's high tenability, high permittivity, low loss, and adaptable ferroelectric properties 32 .The utilization of this material as an adsorbent has not been thoroughly explored, regardless of its ferroelectric properties, ease of surface modification, and crystalline structure, which are conducive to adsorption applications.Furthermore, the material's potential for industrial-scale use and associated waste generation is significant, alongside its various synthesis methods and the availability of polymeric and metallic blended versions integrated into industrial processes [32][33][34][35][36] .

A comprehensive review of the literature reveals that BST powder has not been thoroughly examined as a nano-adsorbent in the DSPME method utilized for thyme tea matrices.The morphological formation of BST powder was precisely and readily adjusted based on the barium and strontium ratio, leading to improved adsorption efficiency.This enhancement is attributed to its modified multilayered porous surface and ferroelectric properties, resulting in increased affinity for metal ions through possible variations in adsorption energies and mechanisms, particularly relevant to cadmium.The BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial structure has both Ba and Sr ions present at the same time, along with a strong catalytic presence of titanium.In this study, a novel adsorbent nanomaterial, BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial, has been utilized in an advanced DSPME method for the preconcentration and determination of cadmium in thyme tea from different brands and flavors.


Materials and method


Chemicals and standards

The Cd 2+ stock standard solution was prepared from the slat of cadmium as CdSO 4 •3H 2 O salt procured from Sigma-Aldrich (Steinheim, Germany).The standard solutions were prepared using deionized water (Elga Flex 3, High Wycombe, England) Water Purification System (resistance of 18.2 MΩ.cm).Barium nitrate (Ba(NO₃)₂), strontium nitrate (Sr(NO 3 ) 2 ) and titanium isopropoxide (Ti{OCH(CH₃)₂}₄) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (Steinheim, Germany).


Instrumentation

A flame atomic absorption spectrometer (FAAS, ATI UNICAM 929 AA, UNICAM, England) was used for the determination of Cd.The system had a burner head, a cadmium hollow cathode lamp (PHOTRON) operating at a current of 8.0 mA, a spectral bandwidth of 0.50 nm, and an analytical wavelength of 228.8 nm.A deuterium lamp was used for background correction.The primary objective of using FAAS was to determine analyte concentrations in samples both prior to and following the implementation of the developed microextraction method.A stoichiometric flame was produced using an acetylene/air combination for the atomization process.

The characterization of nanomaterial was performed via Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy, X-Ray Diffraction (XRD) analysis, and Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) imaging.The FTIR spectrum of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial was obtained in the wavelength range of 500-4000 cm −1 using a Bruker Tensor 27 model Attenuated Total Reflection (ATR) spectrometer, including a wavenumber precision of ± 0.01 cm −1 .XRD analysis was conducted with the PANanalytical X'Pert Pro XRD system, which is equipped with a Cu-Kα radiation source.The XRD spectrum was acquired with a scanning angle of 2θ ranging from 10° to 90°, at a scanning rate of 2°/min, under conditions of 40 mA and 45 kV.SEM pictures were acquired with a Zeiss EVO LS10 type SEM in beam mode at 20 kV.The nanomaterial was coated with gold-palladium under argon plasma using a Quorum SC7620 Sputter Coater.


Preparation of real samples

To evaluate the applicability of the developed procedure on real samples, thyme tea was brewed using dried thyme leaves from two distinct brands sourced from unique regions of Türkiye.The tea samples were prepared optimally for commercial consumption.Approximately, 3.0 g of dried thyme leaves, equivalent to about 2 dessert spoons, were weighed and infused in 100 mL of distilled water at 90 °C for 30 min.Following the brewing process, the tea extract, filtered from the leaves using standardized filter paper, was diluted 50-fold with distilled water, equilibrated at room temperature, and kept in a light-protected environment for further recovery tests.Following the simulation of the requisite matrix environment, thyme tea samples spiked with cadmium at specified concentrations were analyzed using FAAS instrument in accordance with the developed method.


Synthesis procedure of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial

The synthesis of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial was achieved by hydrothermal method.Initially, 5.0 mmol of Ba(NO₃)₂ and 5.0 mmol of Sr(NO₃)₂ were dissolved in a solution of 25 mL of acetic acid and water (in a 5:95 v/v ratio), with vigorous stirring at 65 °C.Subsequently, 5 mmol of titanium isopropoxide (Ti (OCH(CH₃)₂)₄) were dissolved in 5.0 mL of ethanol, and the mixture was stirred with a magnetic stirrer until a homogeneous solution was achieved.The titanium isopropoxide/ethanol solution was slowly introduced to the uniformly heated solution in a dropwise manner.The pH of the solution was subsequently adjusted to 3.5.Following around 30 min of agitation, the solution was put into a 50 mL stainless steel autoclave.The autoclave was inserted in a laboratory oven and heated at 100 °C for 12 h.The resulting gelatinous white mass was then rinsed twice with distilled water and twice with ethanol, followed by drying at 65 °C for 24 h.The gelatinous solid was calcined at 800 °C for 4.0 h, yielding BST powder.


Procedure for BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial based solid phase microextraction (BaSrTiO 3 -DSPME)

Initially, 20 mg of BaSrTiO 3 was weighed into a 15 mL centrifuge tube.8.0 mL of a Cd 2+ standard solution was injected into the tube.Subsequently, 1.0 mL of a pH 7.0 buffer solution was added to the sample.The solution was vortexed for 30 s to ensure homogeneous mixing of the adsorbent material with the volume of the sample, followed by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 2.0 min to facilitate the separation of nanomaterial.The upper liquid phase was discarded, and 100 µL of 3.0 M nitric acid was utilized as the desorption solvent.The solution was vortexed for 15 s afterward.Centrifugation was used as the last step to isolate the desorption solvent from the nanomaterial.After separation, the analyte-rich nitric acid phase was analyzed using the FAAS system.


Results and discussion


Characterization studies of sorbent


FTIR analysis of BST powder

The formation of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial has been verified employing SEM, XRD, and FTIR analyses.Figure 1 illustrates the FTIR spectrum of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial across the range of 4000-500 cm −137 .The band recognized at 635 cm −1 indicates the formation of Ti-O bonding.The distinct band at 1415 cm −1 confirms the presence of Ba-Ti-O bonds, while the wide band around 3100 cm −1 and the sharp band at 1339 cm −1 further validate the formation of O-H groups in the material prior to calcination 38 .


XRD analysis of BST powder

The crystal structure and phase composition of BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial were confirmed by X-ray diffraction analysis.Figure 2 illustrates the XRD patterns of the powders calcined in air at 800 °C for 4 h.The peaks detected at 31.79°, 38.02°, and 75.19° indicate the crystallization of BST powders (ICSD file no.00-034-0411) 38,39 .

The crystallite size (D) of 40.22 nm is obtained utilizing Modified Scherrer's equation:
D = Kλ/β cos θ,
where K represents the Scherer constant, specifically K = 0.9 in this instance, λ indicates the wavelength, and β represents the full width at half maximum (FWHM) expressed in radians.

The obtained theoretical crystallite size aligns with the SEM images, substantiating the synthesis of the nanomaterial and the phase formations.


SEM analysis of BST powder

The morphology of the BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial was analyzed using SEM images (Fig. 3).The synthesized BaSrTiO 3 nanomaterial exhibited a heterogeneous shape resembling both spheres and cubes, featuring a rough surface.Each BaSrTiO 3 particle is an aggregate of several grains.The distinctive form of BST was believed to emerge during the calcination process.The particle size of BST composites demonstrated in 200 nm scale further supports the theoretical crystallite size found in modified Scherrer equation.The findings indicate that the BST particles synthesized by the current approach are presumed to possess favorable sintering activity, attributed to their sub-micron particle size and high surface area of the nano-powder.Comparable findings were also documented in the literature 33,40,41 .


Optimization of extraction procedure

Variables influencing the extraction process were subjected to a single-point variable optimization method to enhance the extraction efficiency of cadmium onto the adsorbent material.The parameters included the type and volume of buffer solution, the amount of nanomaterial selected, the procedure and duration of mixing, and eventually the type and volume of the desorption solvent.A 10 µg/L Cd standard has been continuously used  throughout the optimization experiments.Each variable was examined with three replicates, and the parameter exhibiting the highest average absorbance value among the variables being studied was selected as the optimum experimental condition for the procedure to be developed.The remaining experimental parameters were held constant for each variable while assessing the optimal value of each.


Optimization of buffer solution and volume

The adsorption process of heavy metals is significantly influenced by the medium's pH and the material's point of zero charge (PZC) value [42][43][44] .The pH optimization of materials like BST, often referenced in literature as metal-insulator-semiconductor (MIS), must be conducted to provide insight into the mechanism of adsorption and concept of ionization that takes place within the environment 45 .Buffer solutions with pH values in the range of 5.0 to 9.0 (5.0-6.0 phthalate buffer, 7.0 tris buffer, 8.0-9.0 borate buffer) were prepared and added to a sample volume.In Fig. 4a, the extraction efficiency enhanced as the environment approaches the alkaline region, indicating a higher interaction between the metal ions in the aqueous medium and the adsorbent.The upward trend may be elucidated by various reactions that may occur among BST and cadmium in alkaline conditions 46 :

Surface adsorption:
BST − OH + Cd 2+ → BST − O − Cd + + H +
Precipitation as cadmium hydroxide:
Cd 2+ + 2OH − → Cd(OH) 2 (s)
Ion-exchange with BST surface:  www.nature.com/scientificreports/
{Ba|Sr} + Cd 2+ → Cd − BST + { Ba 2+ |Sr 2+ }
Considering the possible formation of cadmium precipitation as hydroxides at pH 8.0 and pH 9.0, a pH 7.0 buffer solution was selected as the optimal condition for subsequent stages of the study.Following buffer type optimization, four various volumes of buffer solution were evaluated to ascertain the optimal buffer volume.Figure 4b demonstrates each of the buffer volumes that yielded almost identical analytical signals, accompanied by varying standard deviations.Moreover, each volume of buffer solution maintained the pH of the environment at the appropriate level prior to and following the execution of the method.Consequently, 1.0 mL of buffer solution was selected as the optimal amount, considering standard deviations and the necessity to maintain adequate buffer capacity in real samples.


Optimization of adsorbent amount

The quantity of nanomaterial used in adsorption and microextraction methods depends on the type of adsorption and the physical and chemical characteristics of the nanomaterial 47 .Therefore, the influence of varying amounts of BaSrTiO 3 nano on the efficacy of cadmium extraction was assessed by evaluating 5.0, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50 mg of the adsorbent in constant sample volume.Figure 5 illustrates a linear increase in absorbance values as the adsorbent amount rises from 0 to 20 mg, followed by a decline with the use of larger quantities.It has been observed that 5 and 10 mg of BaSrTiO 3 are insufficient for optimal extraction of cadmium from the sample volume.The linear decline in absorbance may result from increased nanoparticle quantity causing matrix effect on the elution solvent, or from inadequate desorption solvent volume for desorption.Therefore, 20 mg of BaSrTiO 3 was chosen to be the optimum amount to be utilized in developed method.


Optimization of mixing period

The optimization of mixing period is essential for improving the interaction between a specific nanomaterial and the target analyte 48 .Insufficient mixing duration of the sample volume may lead to reduced extraction efficiency, depending on the adsorption kinetics influenced by the nanomaterial's structure and the analyte of interest.Figure 6 illustrates that the absorbance values for the unmixed sample and those mixed by vortex for varying durations of 15 to 60 s yielded nearly comparable findings.The significant standard deviations in the results from both the unmixed samples and those mixed for 15 s indicated that a vortex duration of 30 s is the optimal condition.


Optimization of desorption solvent concentration and volume

The concentration and total volume of desorption solvent are critical for the recovery of target analytes adsorbed on the nanomaterial's surface are critical as they directly influence the preconcentration factor, the complete analyte recovery, and the final sample volume 49 .The optimization step involved evaluating 1.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, and 14.44 M concentrations of nitric acid as desorption solvents at constant volume.Figure 7a illustrates that no increase in absorbance values occurs with the use of increasing concentrations of nitric acid.Therefore, considering real sample experiments and numerous analytes that may interact with adsorbent in a complex matrix, 3.0 M nitric acid has been selected as the optimal condition.

Following the selection of the concentration of nitric acid, volumes ranging from 100 to 250 µL were assessed to determine the optimal value of 3.0 M nitric acid volume for the desorption step.Figure 7b shows that the dilution factor increased with the increase of final volume, accompanied by a linear decline in absorbance values.It has been observed that 100 µL of desorption solvent was sufficient for the recovery of the analyte from the adsorbent surface.Due to inadequate phase separation, lower volumes were not tested and 100 µL was chosen as the optimal condition.The optimal conditions of DSPME procedure are given in Table 1.The analytical figures of merit for the system were identified under optimal instrumental and experimental conditions.To assess the analytical performance of developed method, aqueous standard solutions of cadmium at increasing concentrations were prepared and analyzed to construct separate calibration graphs for the direct FAAS and BST-DSPME-FAAS methods.The developed BST-DSPME-FAAS exhibited highly linear behavior throughout the concentration range of 0.5 to 15 µg/L, with a determination coefficient of 0.9938.The limit of detection (LOD) was determined to be 0.33 µg/L, and the percent relative standard deviation (%RSD) calculated from the lowest concentration in calibration plot was 15.22%, indicating acceptable repeatability of the method.

In calculating the LOD and LOQ values, the formulas LOD = 3 • s m and LOQ = 10 • s m have been used, where s represents the standard deviation of the lowest concentration in the calibration plot (n = 6 for the FAAS system and n = 6 for the DSPME-FAAS method), and m indicates the slope of the corresponding calibration plot.The enhancement in detection power (EDP) was determined to be 59.9-fold, respectively, using the formula:     2.

Table 3 compares the performance metrics of the developed method with various alternatives reported in the literature.The EDP value of 59.9 underscores the effectiveness of this method in enhancing analyte concentration, while the detection limit of 0.33 µg/L for cadmium is comparable to those achieved in previous studies.Furthermore, the low requirement for adsorbent material not only improves the method's effectiveness but also enhances its economic viability and sustainability.


Recovery experiments in thyme tea samples

The experiments were performed to validate the applicability and accuracy of the proposed method in thyme tea samples.Two independent dry thyme tea specimens obtained from distinct regions of Türkiye were used for the preparation of thyme tea to determine Cd concentration by utilization of the described BST-DSPME method.Under the specified optimal conditions and within the defined dynamic working range, cadmium was not detected in the thyme tea samples.To assess analyte recovery from the tea matrix, varying concentrations of Cd were spiked into the diluted thyme tea solutions.Percent recoveries were obtained using both external calibration and matrix-matched calibration techniques, and these results were compared with the theoretical spiked concentrations.Table 4 presents the percent recovery results obtained using matrix-matched and external calibration techniques.The matrix-matched calibration technique yielded recovery percentages ranging from 89.2 to 103.1% for Brand A and from 93.6 to 108.6% for Brand B, indicating reliable performance within acceptable limits.In the external calibration method, which was constructed with distilled water, recovery percentages were obtained ranging from 109.4 to 118.8% for Brand A and from 105.9 to 123.0% for Brand B. The findings demonstrate that both calibration techniques are applicable for accurately determining Cd concentrations in thyme tea samples.


Conclusion

Solid phase microextraction procedures have demonstrated great success in analyzing heavy metals in various matrices, possessing distinct benefits and allowing the application of various adsorbent types that can be modified without any limit, resulting in enhanced analytical uses.Nonetheless, the ongoing manufacturing of these materials leads to increased consumption and neglects the incorporation of analyte-or matrix-specific substances, hence disregarding the principles of green chemistry and reusability.This study developed a method for the accurate measurement of trace amounts of cadmium, employing both external calibration and matrix matching calibration methodologies that improve accuracy by mitigating matrix influences on the analyte signal.

An industrial nanomaterial has been used beyond its conventional application, yielding a large surface area and effective extraction properties.The detection limit for cadmium was determined to be 0.33 µg/L, with an enhancement factor of 59.9 based on the comparison of limits of detection.The findings evidently indicate that trace amounts of cadmium in thyme tea samples can be precisely determined with the BaSrTiO 3 -based DSPME method, thereby illustrating the feasibility of employing industrially manufactured materials for analytical applications.
