This article mentions that Smith ideal theory generalizes the adic completion theory of commutative rings to monoid objects of locally presentable symmetric monoidal abelian categories.As an application, we provide an almost mathematics version of completion theory, and we prove that the project limit of the residue algebras by powers of almost finitely generated ideals of almost algebras are complete as well-known results of commutative algebra theory.Furthermore, we introduce a naive construction of the completion theory of E ∞ -rings, which is a homotopy theory analogue of the adic completion theory of commutative algebra by Hovey's Smith ideal theory.We prove that the completion is homotopically complete for any weakly compact Smith ideal as the ordinal commutative algebra theory.Furthermore, by using the completion theory of Smith ideals of the symmetric monoidal category of motivic spectra due to Voevodsky, we formulate an approximation of algebraic cobordism by algebraic K-theory, and we prove that the approximation inherits properties of algebraic K-theory, like the Bott periodicity and the Gabber rigidity.

Introduction

Let A be a commutative ring.Then an ideal I of A determines a topological ring structure on A by the filtration {I n } n≥1 as a system of neighborhoods of zero of A, which is called the I-adic topology.The projective limit Â = lim ← − − A/I n is called the completion of A for the I-adic topology.Write I m = lim ← − −n I m /I m+n for each m ≥ 1.Note that, in general, Â is complete for the topology determined by the induced filtration { I n } n≥1 , but not the Î-adic topology { Î n } n≥1 .We can find an example in Yekutieli [Yek11,Example 1.8].

For this reason, in this paper, we use the terminology "nilpotent approximation" instead of "completion".

In the case that I is a finitely generated A-module, due to Yekutieli [Yek11, Corollary 3.6.],all of the induced homomorphism I n Â → În → I n are isomorphisms, for each n ≥ 1, giving us that Â is Î-adic complete.

A homotopical version of completion theory is defined by Goodwillie calculus [Goo90], [Goo92], and [Goo03], which is Taylor approximation theory of functors.Lurie [Lur17] gave a brief review of Goodwillie calculus of functors between ∞-categories: Let C be an ∞-category.For any n ≥ 0, an n-cube is a functor from the nerve N(P ([n])) of the powerset P([n]) of the total ordered n + 1-points set [n] = {0, 1, . . ., n} to C, where morphisms of P([n]) is determined by inclusions of subsets of [n].

Definition 1.1 ( [Lur17] p.1016, Definition 6.1.1.6).Let C be an ∞-category.We say that C is differentiable if C satisfies the following conditions:

(1) The ∞-category C admits finite limits.

(2) The ∞-category C admits all sequential colimits.That is, for any diagram X : N(Z ≥0 ) → C, the colimit of X exists in C.Moreover, the left adjoint P n is left exact.

Let F : C → D be a functor between ∞-categories.Suppose that C and D satisfy the assumption of Theorem 1.3.Then we have the tower of functors
• • • → P n (F) → P n−1 (F) → • • • → P 1 (F) → P 0 (F)
form C to D. We call the tower {P n (F)} n≥0 of functors the Taylor tower of F. Goodwillie calculus is the approximation theory of functors by n-excisive functors.Preygel [Pre15] gave completion of E ∞ -rings as follows: For any E ∞ -ring R, the completion R of a morphism f : R → R ′ of E ∞ -ring is the homotopy
limit R = lim ← − − P n (id CAlg /R ′ )( f ) ∈ CAlg /R ′ .
However, it is somewhat complicated to explicitly construct an n-excisive approximation by following the definition of Goodwillie calculus.Inspired by Heuts's work [Heu21] defining Goodwillie calculus of ∞-categories and applying Hovey's Smith ideal theory [Hov14] to Preygel's completion theory, by nilpotent Smith ideals of degree n + 1 instead of n-excisive approximation, we introduce a more naive approximation theory of E ∞ -algebra objects of stable symmetric monoidal model categories.This is a generalization of the completion of commutative algebra on the adic topology by ideals.As an application, we establish an almost mathematics version of the adic completion theory of commutative algebra, calling it the "almost completion" theory, without the assumption the tight condition of the defining ideal [GR03, p. 32, Definition 5.1.5].Furthermore, as another result, we introduce nilpotent approximation in homotopy theory.Namely, we construct an approximation of algebraic cobordism by algebraic K-theory, inheriting some good properties of algebraic K-theory, like the Bott periodicity and the Gabber rigidity [Gab92].

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we recall finitely generated objects of categories.In Section 3, we define the nilpotent approximation theory of Smith ideals, being a generalization of the completion theory of commutative algebras.The main result of Section 3 is the completeness of nilpotent approximation of finitely generated objects of the symmetric monoidal categories of Smith ideals.As an application of Section 3, in the final part, we introduce almost nilpotent approximation (completion) theory of almost modules, being distinct from Gabber-Rameros's completion theory by tight ideals.We provide an almost analogue of Gabber-Ramero [GR03, p.147, Theorem 5.3.24].In Section 4, we generalize the nilpotent approximation theory to homotopy theory: we define homotopically nilpotent approximation of Smith ideals of stable symmetric monoidal model categories and prove that, for weakly compact Smith ideals, homotopically analogue of completeness of homotopically nilpotent approximation.The final part of this paper Section 5 establishes the approximation of algebraic cobordism of algebraic K-theory.We show that the approximation of algebraic cobordism inherits the Bott periodicity and the Gabber rigidity.

Conventionally, we fix an uncountable regular cardinal κ in this paper.Small sets and categories mean κ-small sets and categories, respectively.We consider the case categories to be locally κ-presentable, model categories are proper combinatorial, and symmetric monoidal structures are closed (that is, they have inner-mapping objects).
