Introduction

Salidroside, the major bioactive ingredient in various Rhodiola plants, has attracted extensive research interest because of its powerful anti-hypoxic, anti-fatigue, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals (Biswal et al. 2018;Darbinyan et al. 2000).

The long growth periods of Rhodiola and the complex extraction process have initiated research into artificially synthesis of salidroside (Grech-Baran et al. 2015).However, chemical approaches for synthesizing salidroside face enormous challenges, such as by-products, low yield, laborious protection and deprotection steps.Recently, synthetic biology has enabled microbial cells to produce salidroside at a high titer (9.48 g/L) (Liu et al. 2022).However, this process is still in its early stages and purifying salidroside from a complex fermentation broth is generally difficult and expensive.

Compared to the above methods, biocatalytic synthesis provides an alternative way to realize a greater market value for salidroside because of the simplicity, convenience and low cost of product recovery (Li et al. 2020(Li et al. , 2022)).UDP-glucosyltransferases (UGT; EC 2.4.x.y), which can catalyze the glucosylation of tyrosol at its alcoholic hydroxyl group, are the preferred biocatalysts for the production of salidroside (Fan et al. 2017;Xue et al. 2016).In recent years, remarkable advancements have been made, particularly in the realms of UDP-glucosyltransferase screening and overexpression, which have significantly impacted industrial production (Fan et al. 2017;Torrens-Spence et al. 2018).However, low glucosylation activity of UDP-glucosyltransferases and the supply of large amounts of the costly glucosyl donor UDP-glucose (UDPG) were still the major bottlenecks for the production of salidroside (Fan et al. 2017;Xue et al. 2016).

Here, UGTs from different species were cloned and investigated for their efficiency in salidroside synthesis and a cascade reaction by coupling UGT85A1 from Arabidopsis thaliana to sucrose synthase from Glycine max (GmSuSy) was constructed to regenerate UDP-glucose.Under such optimal conditions, different substrate-feeding modes were investigated and a final titer of 6.0 g/L was obtained with a minor addition of UDPG, proposing a potential industrial alternative for salidroside production.


Materials and methods


Strains, plasmids, and chemicals

Escherichia coli strains DH5α and BL21 (DE3) were used for plasmid construction and recombinant enzyme production.pET28a was purchased from Novagen (Darmstadt, Germany) and used as the expression vector.UDP and UDP-glucose were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Co.(St.Louis, MO, USA).Icariside D2 and salidroside were purchased from Energy Chemical (Shanghai, China) and used as the standards.Restriction enzymes and PrimeSTAR @ HS DNA Polymerase were purchased from Takara Bio Inc. (Dalian, China).The one step cloning kit was purchased from Vazyme Biotech (Nanjing, China).


Gene cloning and construction of recombinants

The BlYjiC gene (NCBI accession: WP_003182014.1)was amplified from the genome of Bacillus licheniformis ATCC14580.UGT85A1 (NCBI accession: Q9SK82.1),RrUGT33 (NCBI accession: AUI41147.1),RrUGT17 (NCBI accession: AUI41131.1),UGT73B6 (NCBI accession: AAS55083.1),UGT72B14 (NCBI accession: AQD20582.1),and GmSuSy (NCBI accession: NP_001237525.1)genes were synthesized to incorporate E. coli codons by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China).All genes were subcloned into the expression vector pET28a at the Noc I and Hind III sites according to the instructions of the one step cloning kit to create plasmids pET28a-BlYjiC, pET28a-UGT85A1, pET28a-RrUGT33, pET28a-RrUGT17, pET28a-UGT73B6, pET28a-UGT72B14 and pET28a-GmSuSy, respectively.All the recombinant plasmids were transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3) cells.The plasmids, recombinant E. coli strains and primers used in this study are listed in Tables S1 and S2.


Preparation of recombinant enzymes

The E. coli was cultured in low salt Luria-Bertani (LB) medium (pH 7.5) with 50 μg/mL kanamycin at 37 °C.When the absorbance (OD 600 ) of the cultures reached 0.6-0.8,0.1 mM IPTG was added and the recombinant E. coli strains were further incubated at 18 °C, and 200 rpm for 24 h.The cells were then harvested by centrifugation at 10,000 rpm, 4 °C for 10 min.The cells were disrupted by ultrasonication (50% amplitude, 3 s pulse and 3 s pause cycles for 15 min) and the insoluble pellets were discarded after centrifugation at 10,000 rpm for 20 min at 4 °C.

Screening UGTs for salidroside production

The screening was carried out in a 200 μL reaction mixture containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 1 mM tyrosol, 2 mM UDP-glucose, and 50 μL crude enzyme.Reactions were conducted at 30 °C for 6 h.The reaction mixture was assayed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).The conversion was calculated according to the following equation: conversion = (salidroside concentration/initial substrate concentration) × 100%.

Enzyme activity assay UDP-glycosyltransferase activity measured in a 200 μL reaction mixture containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 1 mM tyrosol as a substrate, 2 mM UDP-glucose, and appropriate amounts of UDP-glycosyltransferase.The reaction mixture was incubated for 30 min at 30 °C and terminated by the addition of 400 μL methanol.One unit of enzyme activity was defined as the amount of enzyme required to synthesize 1 μmol of salidroside per min at 30 °C and pH 8.0 under standard assay conditions.

The GmSuSy activity was measured in a reaction mixture containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.0), 10 mM UDP, appropriate amounts of GmSuSy, and 50 mM sucrose,.(Ma et al. 2014).The mixture was incubated for 30 min at 30 °C.The resultant reducing sugar content was determined by adding DNS reagent (3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid) and immediately boilling for 10 min.The reduced product (3-amino-5-nitrosalicylic acid) was detected at 540 nm.One unit of enzyme activity was defined as the amount of GmSuSy required to release1 μmol of fructose per min at 30 °C and pH 7.0 under standard assay conditions.


Optimizing the conditions for the cascade reaction

The standard cascade reaction mixture (200 μL) contained 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 1 mM tyrosol, 0.2 mM UDP, 50 mM sucrose, 50 mU/mL UGT85A1, and 50 mU/mL GmSuSy.The reaction was performed at 30 °C and 1000 rpm in a constant temperature metal bath.The effects of different ratios of UGT85A1 to GmSuSy on the coupled reaction were investigated at different addition amounts (mU/mL): 50:0, 50:25, 50:50, 50:100, and 50:150.The optimal pH for the cascade reaction was determined using 50 mM Na 2 HPO 4 -NaH 2 PO 4 (pH 6.5-7.5) and 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.5-8.5).The effect of the concentrations of UDP (0-0.5 mM), sucrose (0-400 mM), and tyrosol (1-9 mM) on the cascade reaction was also investigated.The reaction was terminated by adding methanol to twice the reaction volume.The reaction mixture was assayed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).


Synthesis of salidroside via the UGT85A1-GmSuSy cascade reaction employing fed-batch and continuous modes

Under the fed-batch mode, the reaction mixture (100 mL) contained 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5), 3 mM tyrosol, 0.3 mM UDP, 100 mM sucrose, 50 mU/mL UGT85A1, and 100 mU/mL GmSuSy.The reaction was stirred at 30 °C.Tyrosol (3 mM) was periodically added to the reaction mixture based on the consumption of this substrate.Under the continuous mode, the substrate concentration was maintained below 1 mM throughout the reaction by controlling the substrate flow speed.During the reaction, 50 μL of the reactant was periodically collected, terminated, and analyzed using HPLC.


HPLC and NMR analysis

The column was maintained at 30 °C with a 20% methanol flow rate of 1.0 mL/min, and absorbance was measured at 275 nm.All assays were performed in triplicates.Salidroside and icariside D2 were purified using semipreparative HPLC. 1 H-and 13 C-NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker AVANCE IIII 600 spectrometer using DMSO-d6 as the solvent (Figs.S4, S5).


Statistical analysis

All measurements were made in triplicate, and experiments were repeated three times to calculate the means and standard deviations.


Results


Screening UGTs for salidroside production

The glycosylation of tyrosol at the alcoholic hydroxyl group requires a highly efficient UDP-glucosyltransferase (Fig. 1a).It has been reported that BlYjiC from B. licheniformis (Liu et al. 2018), UGT85A1 from A. thaliana (Liu et al. 2018), UGT73B6 and UGT72B14 from R. sachalinensis (He et al. 2017;Xue et al. 2016), and RrUGT33 and RrUGT17 from R. rosea (Torrens-Spence et al. 2018) can catalyze the glycosylation of tyrosol.These UDP-glucosyltransferases were then codon-optimized and cloned into E. coli.According to SDS-PAGE analysis, four out of six UGTs (BlYjiC, UGT85A1, UGT73B6, and RrUGT17) were well expressed, while UGT72B14 and RrUGT33 were mostly distributed in the precipitates (Fig. S1).The glucosylation activity of the supernatant from each UGT sample was then analyzed (Fig. 1b).Comparatively, BlYjiC, UGT85A1 and RrUGT17 showed higher activities than the other UGTs, with conversion of 43.9%, 28.8% and 22.6%, respectively.Notably, the six UGTs demonstrated distinct regioselectivity towards tyrosol.Specifically, UGT85A1 and RrUGT33 glucosylated the alcoholic hydroxyl group of tyrosol generating salidroside as the sole product.In contrast, other UGTs showed low regioselectivity, producing significant amounts of icariside D2 (Fig. 1b).In view of its high activity and regioselectivity, UGT85A1 was further used for the production of salidroside.


Construction of a UGT85A1-GmSuSy cascade reaction

In general, tyrosol glycosylation requires UDPglucose (UDPG), an expensive nucleotide sugar, as donor molecule.However, GmSuSy is a glycosyltransferase that can efficiently convert inexpensive sucrose into UDPG and fructose in the presence of.

UDP (Gutmann et al. 2014).Therefore, the coupling of GmSuSy with UGT in one-pot could lead to in situ regeneration of UDPG in the presence of catalytic amounts of UDP.Consequently, a cascade biocatalysis for salidroside production was designed using UGT85A1 and GmSuSy (Fig. 2a).The enzyme ratio on salidroside synthesis by the reconstituted cascade reaction was confirmed by directly detecting the conversion of tyrosol (Fig. 2b).First, a 0.5-fold increase in GmSuSy activity relative to that of UGT85A1 was included.The cascade biocatalytic system could convert tyrosol into salidroside, with a significantly improved conversion of 28.7%.The amount of GmSuSy was then increased to further facilitate the cascade reaction.With twofold GmSuSy activity, the conversion reached 36.7%, which was 4.8-fold higher than in the absence of GmSuSy (7.6%).Further increases in GmSuSy activity of over twofold were not beneficial.These results indicated that the presence of GmSuSy was necessary for recycling UDPG, and that the optimal enzyme activity ratio of UGT85A1:GmSuSy should be 1:2 in this cascade biocatalytic system.


Optimizing the parameters of the cascade reaction

Although the optimal pH for UGT85A1 was 8.0, it is worth noting that GmSuSy activity was inhibited under alkaline conditions (Fig. S2).Therefore, to investigate the optimal pH for the cascade system, reactions were conducted at different pH values, and the highest conversion of tyrosol was obtained at a pH of 7.5.

Given that UDPG was the key factor in the UGT85A1-GmSuSy reaction mixture, the effects of different concentrations of UDP on salidroside production were investigated (Fig. 3b).The conversion of tyrosol was rapidly increased as the concentration of UDP increased from 0.1 to 0.3 mM and a slight decrease was observed at UDP concentrations above 0.3 mM.Therefore, it was determined that 0.3 mM UDP was the optimal concentration for the coupled reaction system.In addition, the effect of different concentrations of sucrose, the cosubstrate, on salidroside production was also investigated (Fig. 3c).The conversion of tyrosol significantly increased when the sucrose concentration was increased from 0 to 100 mM.In contrast, when the sucrose concentration was above 200 mM, the conversion decreased rapidly.This may have been caused by enzyme activity inhibition in the reaction mixture at a relatively high concentration of sucrose.

The effect of tyrosol concentration on the UGT85A1-GmSuSy cascade reaction was determined after optimizing the above parameters.As shown in Fig. 3d, the conversion reached > 99% with 1-5 mM tyrosol as the substrate, while significantly decreased with tyrosol concentrations above 7 mM, even with extended reaction times.It appeared that the cascade was inhibited by high concentrations of the substrate.The enzymatic activities of UGT85A1 and GmSuSy at different tyrosol concentrations were then investigated (Fig. 4).The results showed that the activity of both enzymes, especially that of UGT85A1, decreased significantly with an increase in tyrosol concentration, indicating substrate inhibition, which can be addressed if the substrate is constantly consumed by the cascade reaction and fed in either a fedbatch or continuous mode.One-pot synthesis of salidroside by the substrate fed-batch reaction To improve the final concentration of salidroside and avoid serious substrate inhibition, a fed-batch cascade reaction was conducted by the periodic addition of 3 mM tyrosol.As shown in Fig. 5a, kinetic analyses of salidroside production and tyrosol consumption over time were performed.The specific productivity during the first batch cultivation period was 220.5 mg/L / h.Productivity gradually decreased, as the reaction proceeded, to 147.6 mg/L / h, 102.9 mg/L / h, and 55.9 mg/L / h during the second, third, and fourth batch cultivation periods, respectively.Finally, 3.6 g/L (11.9 mM) salidroside was produced, with a corresponding molar conversion of 99.2% and a specific productivity of 93.9 mg/L / h.To explore the possibility of further improving the performance of the UGT85A1-GmSuSy cascade reaction, the substrate was fed into a continuous model and maintained at concentrations below 1 mM by controlling the flow rate of the pump.As shown in Fig. 5b, the production of salidroside reached 6.0 g (19.9 mM) with a corresponding molar conversion of 99.6% and a specific productivity of 199.1 mg/L / h.Compared with the fed-batch mode, the continuous mode provided 66.7% higher concentration and 112.0%higher productivity of salidroside.The salidroside was then purified by medium-pressure preparative column chromatography with 20% methanol as a mobile phase.A total recovery of 92.5% and purity of > 99% were obtained after removal of methanol and water by evaporation and freeze-drying.This data laid the foundation for mass production of salidroside.

Conclusion

The UDP-glycosyltransferase UGT85A1 from A. thaliana was identified as an efficient biocatalyst for the salidroside production.A UDPG recycling system was established by coupling UGT85A1 with the sucrose synthase GmSuSy from G. max.With bioprocess engineering efforts, salidroside was produced at a titer of 3.6 g/L with a corresponding molar conversion of 99.2% and specific productivity of 93.9 mg/L/h in a fed-batch mode.To further increase the titer of salidroside, a continuous feeding mode was also conducted and a final titer of 6.0 g/L with a corresponding molar conversion of 99.6% and specific productivity of 199.1 mg/L / h was obtained.This study suggests that the in vitro UGT85A1-GmSuSy biocatalytic cascade reaction could be exploited as an efficient approach for potential industrial-scale production of salidroside in an economically effective manner.
