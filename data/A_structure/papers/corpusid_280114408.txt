PNPLA3 I148M variant is the main driver of weight gain after hepatitis C cure
51C678DA4A8BA47923DB67B6297309F310.1038/s41598-025-11085-9
BMI increase is commonly described in patients after chronic hepatitis C (HCV) cure with direct-acting antivirals.The aims of our study were to confirm the BMI increase compared with a population control group and to search for the allelic association between BMI increase and PNPLA3 gene I148M variant (G allele) carriage.We compared the BMI increase in 230 patients (HCV group) 3 years after the end of anti-HCV treatment with a group of 640 healthy subjects (controls) within the same period.The median BMI increase in the HCV group was 0.39 (range − 6.5-11.8),0.70 in controls (-6.3 − 14.0); the difference was not significant (p = 0.95).However, the HCV subgroups showed significant difference according to PNPLA3 genotype: CC 0.0 (-6.5-8.7) vs. CG + GG 0.94 (-4.2-11.8),p = 0.0045, in contrast to the subgroups of controls: CC 0.7 (-5.1-13.7) vs. CG + GG 0.7 (-0.3-14.90),p = 0.86.BMI increase in the HCV subgroup CC + CG was significantly higher than in the entire control group (p = 0.035), whereas the HCV subgroup CC did not differ from controls (p = 0.07).The BMI increase was associated with PNPLA3 G allele carriage in the allelic model and the multivariate analysis.Keywords Hepatitis C virus, Direct-acting antivirals, Weight gain, PNPLA3Since 2014, direct-acting antiviral (DAA) regimens have represented the standard of care treatment of chronic hepatitis C. Currently used DAA regimens are associated with an almost universal sustained virologic response (SVR) rate 1 .The term SVR achievement means the virus eradication from the patient's body.The prognosis of the DAA-treated patients who achieved SVR has improved substantially 2 and HCV cure is associated with minimal risk of liver-related and extrahepatic complications of chronic HCV infection 3 .However, certain DAAtreated patients develop Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD) resulting from body weight gain and obesity, and some of them also suffer from metabolic complications such as hypertension, hyperlipidaemia and type 2 diabetes mellitus, which can worsen their life expectancy despite HCV infection clearance.Several studies monitoring the body weight in patients after HCV treatment with DAAs have been conducted over the last decade[4][5][6][7].Their results are consistent in terms of weight gain: the patients treated for chronic HCV infection had significantly higher mean body weight one or more years after the treatment than at the time of the DAA administration.The mean body weight was significantly higher when compared by a paired test, considering a higher proportion of patients who had gained weight.The mean weight gain was 1.7 kg three years after the end of DAA treatment 4 .Almost one-half of the studied patients had gained weight, whereas the remaining patients had not changed or even lost weight.The results of these studies are also consistent in factors predicting weight gain after the DAAs treatment, which were the age under sixty and the absence of liver cirrhosis.Unfortunately, none of the mentioned studies compared the weight gain after HCV cure with the body weight increase associated with ageing in a population control group4,8.As the patients treated with a DAA regimen for chronic HCV infection usually do not change their eating and/or exercise habits, they could switch to a positive energy balance after HCV treatment because the immune active cells typically disappear from the liver after the virus clearance and there is no need to maintain the virus immune control in the liver anymore.Since only a particular proportion of patients are prone to converting this excess energy into body mass, we suspected genetic predisposition mediated by the PNPLA3 rs738409 C > G

(p.I148M) variant -a well-known driver of liver fat accumulation 9 .The direct effect of PNPLA3 I148M allele on lipid accumulation in adipocytes is controversial: chronic overexpression of PNPLA3 I148M caused liver steatosis in the mouse model but no increased lipid accumulation in the adipose tissue 10 ; however, the double knock-out mice for PNPLA3 and monoacylglycerol lipase (MGL) developed liver steatosis and gained weight 11 .Fat accumulation in brown adipocytes carrying the PNPLA3 I148M variant was found in mice and cells cultured in high-glucose medium 12 .There was only one human study describing the association between PNPLA3 GG genotype and weight gain in patients with metabolic-associated liver steatosis, who had to abandon all their physical activities during the lockdown owing to the COVID-19 pandemic 13 .

The aim of our study was to prove that the BMI increase in a group of patients three years after HCV cure was significantly higher than in the population control group during the same period.The second aim was to confirm the allelic association between BMI increase and the carriage of the PNPLA3 I148M variant allele (G allele further in the text) in the group of patients cured from chronic HCV infection with DAAs.


Results


Body mass index change in the HCV group and controls

The three-year median BMI (Body Mass Index) gain was 0.39 kg/m 2 (range − 6.5-11.8) in the HCV cohort and 0.70 kg/m 2 (range − 6.3-14.0) in the control cohort.The difference between both cohorts was not statistically significant (p = 0.95).However, when comparing the BMI gain in the HCV cohort subgroups divided according to the PNPLA3 genotype, there was a significant difference: the genotype CC subgroup gained 0.0 kg/m 2 (range − 6.5-8.7)whereas the subgroup of allele G carriers (genotypes CG and GG) gained 0.94 kg/m 2 (range − 4.2-11.8,p = 0.0045).The BMI difference between similar subgroups of the control cohort was not significant: the genotype CC subgroup gained 0.7 kg/m 2 (range − 5.1-13.7),and the gain in subgroup of the allele G carriers was about the same − 0.7 kg/m 2 (range − 0.3-14.90,p = 0.86).The BMI gain in the HCV subgroup of G carriers was significantly higher in comparison with the control cohort (p = 0.035), whereas the BMI change in the HCV CC homozygotes did not differ from the entire control cohort (p = 0.07) (Fig. 1).

Table 1 shows the distribution of parameters and events with potential impact on the weight gain among the HCV subgroups with different PNPLA3 genotypes.The baseline and end of the study values are displayed when relevant.All the parameters and events were distributed equally among both HCV subgroups (PNPLA3 CC homozygotes and G allele carriers) except for baseline liver stiffness, which was higher in the G allele carriers subgroup.


Association between PNPLA3 genotype and BMI gain

The frequency of the PNPLA3 variant allele G was significantly higher in the subgroup of patients in whom BMI increased by ≥ 0.75 kg/m 2 (genotypes CC 49 of 136, CG 45 of 85, GG 6 of 9) with p = 0.007 and OR 2.1 (CI 1.2-3.59.) in the allelic model (CC vs. CG + GG).The allele G dose-dependent increase of BMI was observed in the HCV subgroup: the median BMI gain was + 0.0 kg/m 2 in CC homozygotes, + 0.87 kg/m 2 in CG heterozygotes, and + 2.42 kg/m 2 in GG homozygotes (CC vs. CG p = 0.01, CC vs. GG p = 0.03).No difference in BMI increase was found in control cohort subgroups with different PNPLA3 genotypes (CC 0.70, CG 0.76 and GG 0.45; CC vs. CG p = 0.68, CC vs. CG p = 0.50), Fig. 2, Table 2.


Predictors of BMI increase (multivariate logistic regression)

Analysis of potential predictors of BMI increase was performed based on the data from univariate analysis for BMI increase ≥ 0.75 kg/m 2 .The following factors were further analysed: age, sex, BMI at the end of therapy, PNPLA3 G allele carriage, type 2 diabetes status before treatment, fibrosis stage before therapy (mild and moderate fibrosis vs. severe fibrosis and cirrhosis, LSM ≥ 9.5 kPa, ≥ F3), HCV genotype 3 and intravenous drug use as the mode of transmission of HCV.PNPLA3 allele G carriage is the only significant predictor of BMI increase (OR 2.16, 95% CI 1.23-3.81,p = 0.007, Fig. 3).


Discussion

The patients in our HCV cohort showed a significant BMI increase after the treatment of chronic hepatitis C. Consistently with the data of other authors 4,5,8 the BMI increase was significant already one year after the antiviral treatment.It rose with time after antiviral treatment, two and three years after (Supplementary Figs. 1 and 2).

In contrast to previously published data, we compared the BMI increase in patients after HCV cure with a group of population controls without HCV infection, but followed up for the same period.Therefore, we proved that the observed BMI increase (weight gain) was not the natural course associated with ageing, but it was directly associated with HCV cure and in the presence of the PNPLA3 G allele, which turned out to be the only significant predictor of weight gain in the multivariate analysis.Even though the number of GG homozygotes was relatively low in our cohort, we proved an allele dose-dependent impact on BMI increase: the  BMI increase in PNPLA3 GG homozygotes was nearly three times higher than in CG heterozygotes.The carriage of the PNPLA3 variant allele G is a well-known risk factor for liver steatosis, fibrosis and cirrhosis in patients with MASLD and alcoholic liver disease.The carriage of the PNPLA3 variant allele as the risk factor of weight gain was previously described only in the study analysing the development of obesity in Italians with a known diagnosis of MASLD during the COVID-19 pandemic lockdown 13 .The weight gain occurred although they had been recommended to maintain a Mediterranean diet and exercise regularly.We think that both cohorts were similar in the point that they experienced a rapid change in energy spending.The Italians had to stay at home and stop all outdoor exercise and sports activities, but they probably did not change their eating habits substantially.Similarly, our patients    could have had an energy excess after the HCV infection clearance and a decline in the immune control of the virus.We suppose that the impact of the PNPLA3 variant allele on weight gain can be apparent only when the energy balance change is relevant and happens in a very short time.PNPLA3 has been reported [14][15][16][17] to influence the accumulation of fat not only in the liver but also in adipose tissue, and dietary changes, significantly increased carbohydrate consumption 18 may modulate its expression, possibly explaining the role of PNPLA3 in weight gain.The precise mechanism by which the PNPLA3 variant allele G contributes to weight gain remains unknown.We can speculate that the impaired conversion of triglycerides in liver lipid droplets and lower lipid trafficking is resulting in the accumulation of triglycerides in the liver and adipocytes, and their clinical consequence would be weight gain.However, this mechanism does not work in the slow weight increase associated with ageing 19 .It seems that this mechanism plays a role only in the case of a fast change in the energy balance.The Italian patients abruptly stopped their physical activities 13 and in HCV, they suddenly stopped the HCV immune controls.However, the role of the PNPLA3 variant is complex and difficult to be easily explained by faster fat accumulation owing to its lower transmembrane transport; PNPLA3 GG homozygotes exhibited a more significant weight loss and, as a consequence, a greater reduction in liver steatosis than wild-type allele carriers after a dietary intervention 20 .Interestingly, our patients with weight gain after the HCV treatment showed no change in liver steatosis expressed as the CAP value.The Italian patients with weight gain showed an increase in liver steatosis, but the method used was less precise; they were evaluated by a standard abdominal ultrasound, with the assessment of the steatosis grade.Furthermore, the Italian patients were all patients with MASLD, which means patients prone to accumulating fat in the liver.Our patients did not suffer from MASLD, but chronic hepatitis healing and fibrosis regression occurred over the same period in their liver, therefore, it could have a beneficial impact on liver fat accumulation.

In our cohort, individuals with a history of drug use represented a third of the HCV group.Weight gain after the cessation of drug use is a well-known fact associated with the change of lifestyle in these individuals, and sometimes results in switching to overeating and craving for sweets and junk food, which often serve as a replacement for drugs.Weight gain and obesity, therefore, contribute to fibrosis progression in HCV infection if not treated early 21 .Despite this, the PNPLA3 G allele carriage still represented the only significant risk factor for weight gain in the studied group.However, the allelic association between PNPLA3 variant allele G and BMI increase after HCV cure was confirmed only in our study group.We were not able to find a similar group of treated HCV patients to validate the allelic association.Until this association is validated, our study should be interpreted as a pilot study.

We conclude that the carriage of the PNPLA3 variant allele G is the crucial risk factor for weight gain after the HCV cure.PNPLA3 genotyping in patients treated for HCV infection could help identify those at risk of weight gain and offer them nutritional counselling.


Methods


Study design and eligibility of patients

Adult patients (> 18 years of age) who had been treated for chronic hepatitis C by all-oral DAA regimens 3 years ago and achieved SVR12 were enrolled in the study.Patients were treated with different combinations of DAAs (Supplementary Table 1).Antiviral regimens were chosen according to the availability of the drugs on the market in the Czech Republic at the time of treatment, HCV genotype, stage of liver fibrosis and renal function.

Exclusion criteria were: decompensated liver cirrhosis, hepatocellular carcinoma, organ transplant, renal replacement therapy, type 1 diabetes mellitus, HCV/HIV coinfection, HCV/HBV coinfection (HBsAg positivity) and pregnancy.

Eight hundred and six patients with chronic hepatitis C, treated between 2016 and 2019 at our centre, met the inclusion criteria; 256 patients met at least one exclusion criterion.All other patients (550) were contacted by telephone or e-mail, and 230 patients consented to participate in the study.All the patients signed informed consent with participation in the study and with blood sampling for DNA analysis.The study protocol included one additional visit 3 years after the end of DAA treatment.The end of DAA treatment was defined as the baseline for the current study.The study visit at the centre included taking a medical history, physical examination with measurement of blood pressure and heart rate, body weight and height, and blood sample draw.Liver stiffness and CAP measurement were performed with Vibration Controlled Transient Elastography (FibroScan ® ).A questionnaire was filled out during the discussion with the patient.The questions included patients' history data regarding the presence and treatment of arterial hypertension, thyroid gland disease, cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus, other diseases diagnosed after HCV treatment and change of lifestyle and eating habits, and past and present medication.

The study protocol included one additional visit 3 years after the end of DAA treatment.The end of DAA treatment was defined as the baseline for the current study.


Patients' characteristics

The study group (HCV group) consisted of 230 subjects, of which 126 (55%) were males and 104 (45%) were females.Patients' characteristics are shown in Tables 1 and 3. Sixty-three patients (27%) had liver cirrhosis at the initiation of the antiviral therapy.Seventy-one patients (31%) had received previous unsuccessful interferon treatment.Five patients had isolated anti-HBc total positivity while all other serologic markers for hepatitis B were negative.Two women were pregnant after treatment and gave birth at least 6 months before the visit at FU3Y.The most frequent HCV genotype in our group was genotype 1 (77%).One hundred forty-nine patients (64%) were infected with HCV genotype 1b, and 29 patients (13%) were infected with HCV genotype 1a.One person had HCV genotype 1, and the subtype was not identified by the laboratory.Three patients were infected with HCV genotype 2, 45 patients (20%) were infected with HCV genotype 3, 2 patients were infected with HCV genotype 4, and 1 patient was infected with HCV genotype 6.


Control group characteristics

The control group consisted of 640 individuals from the Czech cross-sectional population study MONICA 19 , 369 (57.7%) females and 271 (42.3%) males.Inclusion criteria were age between 25 and 65 years and permanent residence in one of the 6 country districts in the Czech Republic where the study was conducted.Individuals were selected through a stratified random sampling method to ensure representativeness based on age and sex.Participation required the ability to complete questionnaires and undergo physical examinations, and all participants had to provide informed consent.The group was followed up for 8 years, and individuals' weight was assessed at time points 0, 3 years and 8 years.The median weight at time points 0 and 3 years were used for comparisons with the HCV group.The median age of the control group was 46.5 and 49.5 years at time point 0 and 3 years, respectively.Control group characteristics are displayed in Table 3.All control group individuals were genotyped for the PNPLA3.The genotype frequencies in both, HCV and control group, complied with Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.


Body weight measurement and BMI calculation

All patients had their body height and weight measured by a certified scale at baseline (BL) and 3 years after treatment.Weight change was defined as a difference in weight at 3-year follow-up compared to the weight at baseline.BMI was defined as a person's weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters.


Liver stiffness measurement and steatosis assessment

Liver stiffness measurement and steatosis assessment were performed noninvasively before treatment initiation and 3 years after the end of treatment.Liver stiffness was assessed by Vibration-Controlled Transient Elastography (VCTE, FibroScan 502 Touch ® , Echosens, France) to evaluate the stage of liver fibrosis, together with Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP, FibroScan 502 Touch ® , Echosens, France) to assess the grade of liver steatosis, both following the manufacturer´s instructions 22 .


HCV RNA, HCV genotype and gDNA genotyping

Serum HCV RNA levels were determined in blood samples taken from all patients before initiation of therapy as well as HCV genotype was examined.Assessment of HCV RNA was also done at other time points of treatment according to the health care practice in our centre and 12 weeks after the end of treatment to state SVR (in some patients, also 24 weeks after the end of treatment).HCV RNA levels were assessed by the Roche COBAS AmpliPrep/TaqMan (Roche Molecular Systems, Branchburg, NJ, USA).HCV genotype was assessed using the SIEMENS Versant ® HCV Genotype 2.0 Assay (LiPA) (Siemens Healthcare Diagnostics Inc., Tarrytown, NY, USA).

All patients had their blood collected in EDTA 2 ml or 9 ml tubes for genetic testing.DNA was isolated from the peripheral blood using the Qiagen QIAamp kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany).DNA purity and concentration were assessed by spectrophotometry.All patients' DNA was examined for the PNPLA3 rs738409 C > G polymorphism by the TaqMan predesigned SNP genotyping assay No. C_7241_10 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).


Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism version 10.3.1 for macOS, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA.Clinical characteristics were analysed descriptively and reported as medians and ranges.For pairwise comparison, the chi-square test was used for binary outcome variables, and the Fisher exact test was used for small samples.

The paired and unpaired t-test was used for continuous numeric outcome variable data.Mann-Whitney test and Wilcoxon rank-sum test were used for data analysis with the non-parametric distribution.The compliance with the parametric distribution of the continuous variables was analysed by Shapiro-Wilk test and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test.Statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05 throughout the study.
