Ecological assessment of pit lakes in Raniganj, India: Application of different indices and multivariate statistics

Coal mine generated open pit lakes are well distributed in Raniganj coal field area, West Bengal, India.This paper screened out ecological state macrophyte index (ESMI), water quality index (WQI) and multivariate statistical methods to assess the ecological status of four pit lakes, Nagrakonda, Joyalbhanga, Dalurbandh, and Kumardihi.Seven water quality parameters (pH, total dissolved solids, dissolved oxygen, Secchi depth nitrate, chloride, and phosphate) and six sediment quality parameters (pH, organic carbon, bulk density, particle density, available nitrogen and phosphorus) were analyzed for one year on a monthly basis from four pit lakes.The abundance and diversity of aquatic macrophytes were also analyzed.In the studied pit lakes, water and sediment quality variables showed significant spatial variation.WQI showed that the water quality in all pit lakes was good and according to ESMI these lakes were categorized into good ecological status.The results of this study indicated the values of water quality and macrophyte based indices highlighted the ecological condition of the pit lakes of the study sites thus should be considered it as reliable tool for assessing of the ecological status of any wetlands.

Introduction

Pit lakes are originated after cessation of open mining operation (Castro & Moore, 2000;Ghosh, 2012;Ghosh, Pal, & Mukherjee, 2005;Schultze, Vandenberg, McCullough, & Castendyk, 2022;Tiwary & Dhar, 1994;Vandenberg, Schultze, McCullough, & Castendyk, 2022).There has been a boom in open-pit coal mining, giving up to construct of multiple pit lakes.Because of the vast number of pit lakes that will grow across the world over the next half-century and a large amount of water they have, water quality conditions in these pit lakes will be critical, particularly in locations where water is limited.Weathering of the wall rocks and other exposed rocks in the surrounding drainage releases solutes into the lake water.Pit lakes are distinguished from natural lakes by their much greater relative depths.Pit lakes may be expensive to restore when the mine is closed.However, depending on specific attributes, such as water quality, pit lakes may often provide considerable regional benefit, resolve residual closure concerns associated with both their own and overall project closure, and even offset mining-related environmental costs by establishing new end uses.Water quality, slope stability, and safety considerations all play a role in these prospects (McCullough, Schultze, & Vandenberg, 2020;Schultze, Vandenberg, McCullough, & Castendyk, 2022;Vandenberg, Schultze, McCullough, & Castendyk, 2022).However, some of pit lakes are broadly available and have been utilized by the residents for taking a bath, agribusiness, and fishery.The Raniganj coal field (RCF) has a high proportion of pit lake clusters in West Bengal.Most of them have remained unchanged by anthropogenic activity and preserving their unique character.At present there is no organized strategy for monitoring and management of the pit lakes (Palit & Kar, 2019).As a result, numerous pit lakes have seen water quality degradation and significant water loss.Even though it is strictly illegal, liming, and fertilizing lakes to stock them with fish (mostly Indian major carps) is rather common in these pit lakes.These are some of the major factors that lead to negative and irreversible changes in such ecosystems.Therefore, it is necessary to understand the ecological assessment of these pit lakes to conserve their unique characteristic and biodiversity.Because of their steep slope and the fact that most pit lakes are inaccessible to people, ecological evaluation of pit lakes is a difficult task.Given the study programming aims and objectives, monitoring these systems necessitates balancing sample efforts with available resources.Those macrophytes are commonly utilized for ecosystem monitoring as well as water quality detection.For this reason, indexes have been devised in several countries (Bytyçi et al., 2022;Demars & Harper, 1998;Toso, Marzani, Siligardi, Negri, & Fabris, 2005).Relationship between macrophyte diversity and limnological parameters has been investigated for decades (Bubíková & Richard Hrivnák, 2018;Parray, Koul, & Shah, 2021;Stendera et al., 2012).This knowledge is essential for sustainable freshwater management and protection, which contributes to the world's vulnerable wetlands (Dudgeon et al., 2006).Aquatic plants are frequently involved in a variety of life and development processes.Multiple environmental variables impact such groupings of species, which can make analyzing and generalizing the link between diversity and environment much more difficult (Alahuhta et al., 2014;Edvardsen & Økland, 2006).Macrophytes are effective to maintain ecological balance and stability and contribute to primary productivity of aquatic bodies (Berthold & Paar, 2021;Bhute & Harney, 2017).Multiple types of research on aquatic macrophytes from various waterbodies have been conducted by many researchers (Devi & Sharma, 2007;Kumar & Pandit, 2005;Rameshkumar, Radhakrishnan, Aanand, & Rajaram, 2019;Sarkar, Ghosh, & Mondal, 2020).They also develop a water quality index (WQI) and an Ecological State Macrophyte Index (ESMI), as well as their initial reactions to water and sediment quality parameters.Various water quality evaluation methods are in the literature to analyze water quality in recent times (Adimalla & Taloor, 2020;Chabukdhara, Gupta, Kotecha, & Nema, 2017;Sotomayor, Hampel, & Vázquez, 2018).WQI may be used as an analytical model that can indicate overall water quality (Adimalla, Li, & Venkatayogi, 2018;Alver, 2019;Hurley, Sadiq, & Mazumder, 2012).Since Horton (1965) introduced the WQI, it has been frequently utilized in aquatic ecosystem evaluation.Ecological State Macrophyte Index approach was used to investigate the ecological state of small lakes and wetland ecosystems in the European Union by several researcher (Ciecierska & Kolada, 2013;Søndergaard et al., 2010;Willby, Pitt, & Phillips, 2009).However, applications of ESMI in other parts of the world, as well as in India, are quite rare and in the coal mine generated pit lakes it has been not performed yet.In this regard, this study is unique.ESMI uses macrophyte abundance to assess the ecological condition of wetlands.Furthermore, only a few studies targeting assessing the water quality status using a WQI has been performed in these pit lakes.Consequently, we still lack a thorough and comprehensive understanding of the water quality and ecological status of the pit lakes.The water quality, the application of WQI method and the development of the ESMI consisting of the macrophytes need further investigation.To bridge these gaps, we examined some water quality parameters and macrophytes collected from four pit lakes during 2021-2022 for investigation of the ecological status of the pit lakes.The objectives of this study are as follows: (1) to analyze spatial changes in water and sediment quality characteristics employing multivariate statistics, (2) to assess water quality using the WQI method, (3) to look at the composition of aquatic macrophytes and establish ESMI that may be utilized to assess the ecological health of pit lakes (4) to assess the ecological situation of selected pit lakes using WQI, ESMI, and multivariate statistics.


Methodology


Study area

The sampling was done between February 2021 and January 2022, using four coal mine generated pit lakes viz., Nagrakonda Pit Lake, Joyalbhanga Pit Lake, Dalurbandh Pit Lake, and Kumardihi Pit Lake.Their main characteristics and locations are highlighted in Table 1 and Figure 1.The size of the pit lakes ranged from 17.19 to 42.97 acres, with average depths ranging from 15.24 to 31.48 meters (Table 1).All of the pit lakes were sandy with mud and had hydro-periods that were permanently waterlogged.All of the sampling stations had previously been used for coal mining.


Collection of samples

Water and sediments samples (0-0.5 m depth) were collected in duplicates.Sampling was carried out in 2020-2021.Some parameters were analyzed in situ, such as Water pH, total dissolved solids (TDS), and dissolved oxygen (DO).Secchi disk was used to measure visibility.Other parameters were analyzed in the laboratory following the standard protocol (APHA, 2017).The apparatus was calibrated before analysis.Table 2 displays the techniques utilized for water quality parameter analysis, as well as the equipment and units employed in this investigation.

The bulk density of sediment samples was calculated using a specific gravity bottle with a 50 ml volume that was weighed without the stopper.The bottle was then filled with sediment samples up to the edge of the neck, tapped 20 times, and weighed.By dividing the weight of the sediment samples by the volume of the sediment, the bulk or apparent density was calculated (Baver, 1956).The particle density was calculated using a pycnometer and the Baver technique (1956).As indicated by Jackson, the pH of the sediment was determined using Oakton PCSTestr 35 in a 1:10 sediment-water suspension.A volumetric approach for estimating organic carbon  , 1954), while the Kjeldahl method was used to estimate available nitrogen in sediment samples, as described by Subbiah and Asija (1956).

Macrophyte abundance

The macrophytes were sampled following Southwood and Henderson (2000).A nylon thread about 100 was used to indicate a five meter (m) from the bank to the pit lake's center.Colored tags were used to indicate off intervals of 0.5 m.Each interval was divided into 5 m line transects and handled as a single unit.Individual macrophytes were counted at each location along a line transect that stretched from the embankment to the middle of the pit lake at 0.5 m spacing.For macrophyte sampling, four replicate transects were performed at each site.The standard literature was used to identify macrophytes to the lowest taxonomic level attainable (Cook, 1996;Prain, 1963).At each site, the abundance of each species was reported.


Determination of ecological state macrophyte index (ESMI)

To calculate ESMI, different other indices were established such as phytocoenotic diversification index (H), maximum phytocoenotic diversification index (Hmax) and vegetation under anthropogenic pressure (Panek, 2001; Wijeyaratne & Bellanthudawa, 2020).H max was determined using the equation Hmax = ln(s), where S is the total number of species according to Ciecierska and Dynowska (2013).Vegetation under anthropogenic pressure J = H/Hmax (Pielou's, 1966).The settlement rate of macrophytes (Z) were calculated by determination of phytolitoral surface (Ciecierska & Dynowska, 2013).

(m 2 ).The following equation is used to compute the ESMI for each location using the values derived in the aforementioned formulae (Ciecierska, Kolada, Soszka, & Gołub, 2010):

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics (mean and standard deviation) were computed to explain the dataset.ANOVA was used to see whether there were any significant differences in the specified parameters between pit lakes (at a 95% confidence level).The Kolmogorov-Smirnov normality test was used to check the normality of the dataset.When the data were not normally distributed, a logarithmic transformation was performed.The links between biological and environmental factors were elucidated using redundancy analysis.To evaluate physicochemical heterogeneity among the lakes investigated, PCA was used.Regression analysis between environmental parameters and different indices (ESMI and WQI) was also performed.XLSTAT Software was used for all the statistical analysis.

Discussion

Two indices (WQI and ESMI) and two multivariate analysis (PCA and RDA) were implemented in this study to evaluate the ecological status of the studied pit lakes.The results showed a good relationship between the indices and limnological variables.By using of ESMI analysis, it was quite evident that aquatic plants are good indicator of ecological health of waterbodies.In any aquatic ecosystem, limnological variables play a crucial role and often exhibit spatial and temporal changes.The pH has a role in various biochemical pathways in waterbodies (Jalal & Sanalkumar, 2013).pH varied between 7.36 and 8.78.The Kumardihi Pit Lake showed the lowest pH content, whereas the Dalurbandh Pit Lake had the highest value.In the present study, TDS represented different salts in the water.TDS ranged between 207 and 263 mg/L with the lowest value in Dalurbandh Pit Lake and the highest value was recorded in Nagrakonda Pit Lake and this results mainly due to the presence of human society development, runoff of domestic and farming by-products.This finding is quite similar with the study of Bala and Mukherjee (2010).DO content in water can be from the air or through photosynthetic activities (Kotadiya & Acharya, 2014).Do is the potential indicator of pollution (Gopalkrushna, 2011).The present result showed that DO ranged between 4.8 and 5.7 mg/ L and phosphate in different pit lakes varied from 0.81 to 1.1 mg/L.Also, the nitrate content ranged from 0.40 to 1.16 mg/L.The high values of both nitrate and phosphate content indicates organic pollution (Sadat-Noori, Ebrahimi, & Liaghat, 2014).Also, the present study showed that the value of chloride ranged from 45.10 to 66.81 mg/L which is under permissible limit (Bureau of Indian Standards BIS, 2012).WQI is a good indicator of water health.Different values of WQI in the studied pit lakes were as follows: 82.105 (Nagrakonda), 82.406 (Joyalbhanga), 106.044 (Dalurbandh), and 85.622 (Kumardihi) (Tables 9,10,11,and 12).However, WQI pattern in the present study did not follow the same pattern of WQI performed by different researchers in different waterbodies (Akumtoshi, Singh, Puro, & Puro, 2020;Bora & Goswami, 2017).This might be due to the unique characteristics of pit lakes, presence of various aquatic macrophytes and might relate to the lack of proper management strategies.Additionally, the residents in the pit lake area put some impacts on overall ecological health (Bouslah, Djemili, & Houichi, 2017;Schultze, Vandenberg, McCullough, & Castendyk, 2022;Shah & Joshi, 2017).Macrophytes play a crucial role in wetland ecosystems in various ways (Gopal, 2016;Khan et al., 2022;Schaumburg et al., 2004).In the pit lakes of Dalurbandh and Joyalbhanga, there was a high abundance of invasive alien species Eichhornia crassipes (Mart.)Solms.In Dalurbandh Pit Lake, high levels of nitrate, phosphate, and DO were found.The presence of significant density of phytoplankton might explain the enhanced water quality parameters in the Dalurbandh Pit Lake.Macrophytes in abundant state absorbing most of the phosphate and nitrates available (Hilt, 2015;Preiner et al., 2020;Zimmer, Hanson, & Butler, 2011).As a result of the reduced nutrient availability for phytoplankton in macrophyte-rich habitats, the possibility the density and the community structure of phytoplankton become less (Hilt, 2015;Zimmer, Hanson, & Butler, 2011).The strong connections between macrophyte species and limnological characteristics may have an impact on aquatic habitats (Naiman, Decamps, & Pollock, 1993;Zhang, Liu, & Wang, 2015).A sum of 32 different species of macrophytes was evaluated in the present study; these species belong to 25 families (Table 5).It is clear that both the submerged (S) and free-floating (FF) hydrophytes eliminate a large number of nutrients in the water (Ali et al., 2020;Sooknah & Wilkie, 2004).Eichhornia crassipes (Mart.)Solms influences the decreased water supply, deteriorate the water and significantly decrease the DO (De Laet, Matringe, Petit, & Grison, 2019;Penfound & Earle, 1948).This species also decreases biodiversity, eradicates the submerged community structure through obstructive sunlight, and reduces fish species compassion (Gowanloch, 1944;Pegg et al., 2022).However, this perennial species can be used to eliminate high concentration of nitrogen and it is considered the fastest-growing  wastewater plant species (Rakocy & Allison, 1981;Ting, Tan, Salleh, & Wahab, 2020).Many scholars have demonstrated the connection of macrophytes distribution with the limnological features in the different aquatic ecosystems (Rameshkumar, Radhakrishnan, Aanand, & Rajaram, 2019;Sarkar, Ghosh, & Mondal, 2020) though very few studies have been conducted in coalmine generated pit lakes.Several studies have confirmed that trophic conditions and illumination situations affect the production and settlement of aquatic macrophytes (Kocic, Hengl, & Horvatic, 2008;Lv, He, Hong, Liu, & Yu, 2019).Other findings have shown that water quality parameters like pH can significantly affect the density of the aquatic macrophytes and their distribution,  particularly the submerged ecotypes (Song et al., 2018;Vestergaard & Sand-Jensen, 2000).Our results also indicated quite similar findings.Multivariate statistical techniques in comparison to univariate provide much meaningful and accurate outputs related to biotic and abiotic relationships with the community structure (Buckley, Day, Case, & Lear, 2021;Warwick & Clarke, 1993).Macrophyte-based ecological quality evaluation methodologies give crucial information about the wetlands' ecological state (Ciecierska & Kolada, 2013;Khan et al., 2022).interrelationships between biotic and abiotic factors that then can be used in effective and sustainable wetland management programs.


Conclusion

In this study, we documented distinct macrophytes assemblages, estimated Water Quality Index and Ecological State Macrophyte Index (ESMI) and how they responded to water and sediment quality parameters.The most of the analyzed water quality parameters showed significant spatial patterns.The findings of this study demonstrated the appropriateness of RDA and WQI as tools for the analysis and interpretation of large datasets used to assess water quality as per regression analysis.There was a significant positive relationship of WQI and ESMI with water and sediment quality parameters.We also realize that, although the small sample size and regional type of data, the approach used was sound and appropriate for WQI and ESMIbased ecological assessments of pit lakes.The results of the study can be considered as initial findings aimed at identifying macrophyte composition in pit lakes via some environmental parameters.In the studied pit lakes, a good water quality situation was noticed, as exemplified by WQI and all the pit lakes were categorized into the good ecological status as per ESMI.These macrophyte and water quality indices provide crucial information on the correlations between water quality and macrophyte indices, which can then be applied in long-term wetland restoration and wetland management initiatives.
