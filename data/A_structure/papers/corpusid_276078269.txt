Introduction 1.The application of eSRT in CI fitting

The programming of cochlear implant (CI) devices may be sometimes challenging particularly when some patients such as young pre-linguistically deaf children are unable to provide verbal feedback.Objective methods especially for suprathreshold judgments can significantly be helpful in CI device programming.The stapedius response (SR) as a protective mechanism against high-intensity sounds is used as an objective measure to assess and optimize the electrical stimulation levels required for effective auditory nerve stimulation.Electrically evoked Stapedius Reflex Threshold (eSRT) provides valuable insights into personalized responses to electrical stimuli, ultimately contributing to improved auditory perception performance.Notably, in the study conducted by (Wolfe and Kasulis, 2008), speech recognition tests showed better scores with programs created using eSRTs compared to programs using conventional behavioral measures of Most Comfortable Loudness levels (MCL or C-level).In recent times, advancements in surgical techniques have even allowed for the direct and permanent measurement of evoked Stapedius Reflex (eSR) using implanted needle electromyogram electrodes (Guntinas-Lichius et al., 2022).This innovative approach positions eSRT as a more promising objective measure of loudness than ever before.


Relation between eSRT and MCL in different CI devices

Utilizing the eSRT as an objective measure for the MCL necessitates fully characterizing the relationship between the two for all CI devices, featuring with different stimulation paradigms and current propagation patterns.Typically, these characterizations involve correlation coefficient, linear regression slope and offset, as well as eSR detection rate.Our study aims to further elucidate eSR mechanisms by employing specific stimulation paradigms commonly utilized in Oticon Medical CI devices due to their specific features in terms of stimulation current propagation and loudness coding (Gärtner et al., 2023).

Correlation coefficient: Many studies have demonstrated a noteworthy correlation between the eSRT and other behaviorally measured loudness levels like the MCL in CI patients (Bergeron and Hotton, 2015;Brickley et al., 2005;Carranco Hernandez et al., 2019;Hodges et al., 1997;Hodges et al., 1999;Wolfe and Kasulis, 2008).The correlation coefficient value reported in these studies is often greater than 0.6, as demonstrated by (Bergeron and Hotton, 2015), and in certain cases, it may even exceed values surpassing 0.9, as observed in the studies conducted by (Hodges et al., 1997) and (Raghunandhan et al., 2015).A few studies have shown that the correlation strength may vary with electrode location.(Wolfe and Kasulis, 2008) measured correlation coefficients of r = 0.87, 0.84, 0.80, and 0.58 for electrodes 1-4 (most apical), 5-8, 9-12, and 13-16 (most basal), respectively, on adult patients with Advanced Bionics CI.This study in which speech bursts were being delivered to four electrodes, indicated the lowest correlation value for basal electrodes.In a separate investigation (Charroó et al., 2021) showed that when obtaining the eSRT using a single electrode, correlation coefficients were 0.89, 0.95, 0.77, and 0.9 for electrodes 3 (most apical), 7, 11, and 14 (most basal), respectively.However, when utilizing a group of four electrodes, the correlation coefficients changed to 0.94, 0.93, 0.79, and 0.77, respectively, supporting the findings of (Wolfe and Kasulis, 2008).(Mishra and Nandurkar, 2019) however obtained different results in pediatric patients with Cochlear Nucleus® implants, revealing a moderate correlation (r = 0.68) in middle electrodes and stronger correlations (r = 0.85 and r = 0.93) for basal and apical electrodes, respectively.

Linear offset (intercept): Despite the high correlation values, various difference values (i.e.offset or intercept) have been reported between eSRT and MCL.The differences may depend on the cochlear implant device and/or the stimulation approach.In a study by (De Andrade et al., 2018), involving 24 Cochlear® implant-wearing CI patients, it was demonstrated that eSRT consistently exceeded MCL for all electrodes.Fitting each electrode required different correction factors.In another study by (Brickley et al., 2005), which involved 22 adult patients with MED-EL COMBI 40+ cochlear implants, eSRT profiles along the electrode array were quite uniform, with thresholds ranging from approximately 10-30 nanocoulombs (nC) for different patients.The mean charge levels for eSRT and MCL500 (i.e.MCL using a 500 msec burst) were almost identical, but MCL50 (i.e.MCL using a 50 msec burst) was 2 dB higher.

It's worth noting that the value of eSRT can undergo changes over time.(Gordon et al., 2004) illustrated that intraoperative eSRT tends to be higher than postoperative measurements.Postoperative measurements, however, showed an increase over time, in contrast to MCL measurements, which tend to decrease with time.In contrast, (Raghunandhan et al., 2015) demonstrated that eSRTs were greater than MCLs, and both values increased over time.However, the difference between them decreases with time, emphasizing the need for caution to avoid overstimulation in the initial months after implantation based on eSRT fitting.

eSR detection rate: Concerning the detection rate of eSR, quite different rate values have been reported.(Bergeron and Hotton, 2015) found eSR in 73% of Oticon Medical Digisonic SP CI patients.Among those with an eSR, it appeared in 69% of the electrodes, with a more noticeable presence in basal electrodes (82%) compared to medial (62%) and apical (64%) ones.Hodges et al. (1999), Charroó et al. (2021), Wolfe and Kasulis (2008) reported 65%, 75% and 79% detection rates on Advanced Bionics CI patients.Again, on Advanced Bionics CI patients, (Carranco Hernandez et al., 2019) showed that eSR detection rate is lower in ipsilateral (16%) than contralateral (58%) ears, and the increase of probe tone frequency from the conventional value of 226 Hz to 1000 Hz could increase these detection rates to 37% and 68%, respectively.It was suggested that the middle ear stiffness modification after cochlear implantation would lead to a higher incidence of contra-lateral detection compared to ipsi-lateral eSRTs thresholds.Noteworthy that the value of contra-lateral aSRT (i.e.acoustic) is about 5-10 dB higher than ipsilateral side (VW.Rawool, 2011).On Med-El CI patients (Brickley et al., 2005) reported 87% for the eSR detection rate.Lastly, (Hodges et al., 1997) have evaluated this rate to be 68% in Cochlear® CI patients.


Our study focus

While numerous studies have studied eSR characteristics with various cochlear implants from Advanced Bionics, Med-El, and Cochlear®, there is not enough information regarding eSR features in Oticon Medical (OM) Neuro Zti implants.Investigating this holds dual advantages.Firstly, it aids in a deeper understanding of eSR functioning as in OM CI devices, loudness is encoded through pulse-width modulation rather than pulse-amplitude modulation.Additionally, these implants employ a unique stimulation approach known as Distributed All-Polar (DAP), which combines common-ground and monopolar modes.It delivers pseudo-monophasic electrical pulses with a fixed amplitude and modulated pulse duration to encode loudness.The pulse consists of an anodic stimulation followed by a passive cathodic recovery stage (Franco-Vidal et al., 2020;Schramm et al., 2020;Stahl et al., 2022;Zhu et al., 2012).

Consequently, the activation mechanism of the stapedius muscle could vary in OM implants.Secondly, unofficial field investigations indicate lower eSR detection rates in OM Neuro Zti implants compared to other implants (e.g.Advanced Bionics, Med-El, Cochlear®, and OM Digisonic SP implants).As a result, some audiologists resort to stimulations with multi-electrodes (typically 3-5 electrodes) to facilitate the elicitation of eSR.

In this study, our primary objective is to determine if the constant stimulation amplitude in OM is responsible for low eSR detection rate.We conduct our investigations at two distinct low and high stimulating current amplitudes.The inclusion of these two amplitudes allows us to study the effect of pulse shape on eSRT and to test the hypothesis that the lower eSRT detection rate in OM Neuro Zti implants is linked to the low-amplitude pulse-width modulation loudness coding.For each current amplitude, we also examine the characteristics of ipsi-and contra-lateral eSRT as a function of number of stimulating electrodes (G1: single electrode, G3: a group of 3 electrodes, G5: a group of 5 electrodes).

To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first study that specifically investigates the influence of current amplitude (i.e.waveshape) on eSRT.In terms of multi-electrode stimulation, there is a recent study conducted by (Charroó et al., 2021) that investigates the effects of multi-electrode stimulation in Advanced Bionics implants.In our study, we measure both ipsi-and contra-lateral eSRTs for two different stimulation amplitudes in 1-, 3-, and 5-electrode stimulation modes, like the utilized method in the field by some expert audiologists, to facilitate the stapedius muscle stimulation.


Methods


Subjects and selection criteria

This study was conducted at the Otorhinolaryngology Department of 'North Lisbon University Hospital Centre' in Portugal on patients who utilized OM Neuro Zti cochlear implants.The submission to the Hospital's Ethics Committee received approval (Ethical committee reference number: 84/23), ensuring compliance with ethical standards for this research.A total of 18 adults were shortlisted for the study.One patient was excluded because only 7 electrodes out of 20 were activated.The 17 CI patients who met all selection criteria participated in the study after providing informed consent.These patients ranged in age from 34 to 75 years, with an average age of 55.3 years.Nine patients underwent bimodal auditory rehabilitation with both a CI and a hearing aid (HA), six had unilateral CIs, including one patient with single-sided deafness (SSD), and two had bilateral sequential CIs.In total, 19 ears were evaluated in the study.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: participants had to be aged 18 years or older, have a minimum of one year of experience using Oticon Medical cochlear implants, and be current users of the Neuro Zti cochlear implant system.Participants who met any of the following criteria were excluded from the study: those with inactivated electrodes, preventing the formation of G5 stimulations; those with psychological or linguistic limitations, preventing understanding of provided information; individuals with a medical condition causing middle ear modifications as detected through otoscopic examination and tympanometry; those under legal protection or deprived of liberty.


Test setup

The Oticon Medical GMCI fitting software was utilized to determine the MCL threshold.An Interacoustics A/S Titan device (IMP440) was employed to conduct tympanometry and continuous eSR recordings in ipsi-lateral and contra-lateral ears.Conventional tympanometry was performed before the eSR recordings to ensure normal middle ear function.Continuous eSR recordings were carried out using a 1000 Hz probe tone due to its lower eSRT levels compared to those obtained with a 226 Hz probe tone (Palani et al., 2022;Wolfe et al., 2017).An eSR response was considered valid when admittance values exhibited a minimum variation of 0.02 mmho synchronized with the input stimuli (Wolfe et al., 2017;Wolfe et al., 2018).


Test procedure and data analysis

Each participant was provided with detailed information about the experimental procedure during the informed consent process.They were instructed to promptly notify the audiologist conducting the experiment if they experienced any discomfort from the stimuli.In such cases, the stimulation would either be stopped, or its intensity reduced.

For each participant, the MCL was measured on all available electrodes (out of 20 electrodes) using GMCI fitting software.We employed the loudness scale suggested by OM, which is a categorical loudness scale with 11 response levels (Fultz et al., 2020).The response levels 6 and 7 (starting from level 0) correspond to comfortably loud and most comfortable level categories.Notably, loudness is coded using pulse width modulation in OM implants.For each participant, MCL thresholds were measured on all electrodes using GMCI.Subsequently, ipsi-and contra-lateral eSR measurements were separately conducted in five different cochlear regions spanning from the apical to the basal areas.These regions are centered at electrode numbers {18, 16, 12, 8, 3} and are expressed as {AP1, AP2, MID1, MID2, BAS} regions, respectively (Figure 1).Each of these five regions was stimulated using a group of 5, 3, or 1 electrode, referred to as G5, G3, and G1 stimulation mode, respectively.The multi stimulating electrodes were positioned in the center of each region.For example, electrodes {10, 9, 8, 7, 6} were stimulated in G5 mode for the MID2 region.In the presence of deactivated electrodes, the audiologist could move the center of the region maximally 2 electrodes toward the apical or basal direction.

The stimulations were conducted at two different current amplitudes: LowAmp and HighAmp.LowAmp corresponds to the current amplitude that the patient typically uses on a daily basis.HighAmp corresponds to a current amplitude that is 0.22 mA higher than LowAmp.This value is equivalent to 10 current steps (0.22 × 45) in OM GMCI fitting software.

For each given cochlear region, eSR recording started using the G5 stimulation mode, initially for LowAmp and then for HighAmp current amplitudes.The stimulations consisted of two to three manually generated pulsatile stimulations, each lasting approximately 0.5 s with 0.5-second intervals.Initially, the stimulation charge level was set to the MCL charge level.It is noteworthy that loudness is coded using pulse-width modulation in OM implants, and the delivered charge for a given electrode is equal to q = I • D nanocoulomb (nC), where I is the current amplitude in milliamperes, and D is the pulse width in microseconds.In other words, keeping the MCL charge level fixed at the beginning of the process requires a shorter stimulation duration for HighAmp compared to LowAmp.

If eSR was observed in response to the pulsatile stimulation at the MCL charge level, the loudness was decreased until no eSR was detected.Conversely, if no eSR was observed, the loudness was increased until the eSR could be observed or the loudness became too intense for the patient, or the implant entered the out-of-compliance area.The stimulation charge level at which the eSR was observed was recorded as the eSRT level.This measurement process was repeated similarly for G3 and G1 stimulation modes only if an eSRT level could be assigned to the higher-numbered group.

The eSRT levels were then tabulated for all the interactions between the three stimulation modes (G5, G3, and G1), the two current amplitudes (LowAmp, HighAmp), and for both ipsi-and contra-lateral measurements.This facilitated the reporting of the eSR detection rate and the expression of the linear relationship between eSRT and MCL.We utilized the 'fitlm' function from the Matlab statistics toolbox to derive this linear relationship and calculate the corresponding correlation coefficient r.For multielectrode stimulations, we employed the average MCL values calculated over the same electrodes used in the respective G3 or G5 multi-electrode stimulation modes.


Results

In this section we provide an in-depth analysis of eSR characteristics in relationship with stimulation side (ipsi and contra), stimulation current levels (LowAmp, HighAmp), and stimulation modes (G1, G3 and G5) in Oticon Medical NeuroZti cochlear implants.


eSR detection rate

Figure 2 displays average eSR detection rates for low and high current amplitudes (LowAmp, HighAmp) in the ipsi-and contra-lateral ears for 3 stimulation modes G1, G3 and G5.The detection rates are higher in contra-lateral than in ipsi-lateral side.The rates increase with both current amplitude and number of stimulating electrodes.In the G1-Ipsi-LowAmp condition, the detection rate is notably low at 23%.Shifting to Contra or HighAmp, the rate increases significantly to 41% or 48%, respectively.Combining both in G1-Contra-HighAmp raises the detection rate to 61%.

Transitioning from G1 to G3 in LowAmp importantly enhances the detection rates to 45% and 54% in Ipsi and Contra conditions, respectively.This effect is less pronounced for HighAmp condition.Transitioning from G3 to G5 slightly increases all rates but not as much as the difference between G3 and G1.The highest detection rate of 68% is achieved for G5-Contra-HighAmp.

Within each stacked bar in Figure 2, there are four smaller bars indicating eSR detection rates across four partitioned averaged MCL values ranging from low to high charge levels corresponding to Lo: q < 18, Med-Lo: q = [18, 22), Med-Hi: q = [22, 28), and Hi: q ≥ 28 nanocoulombs (nC).These levels were selected to ensure approximately equal number of MCL data samples in each partition.Stacked bars in all conditions suggest a higher possibility of obtaining eSR for low MCL values compared to high values.When the overall eSRT detection rate is low (e.g. in G1-Ipsi-LowAmp), the eSR detection rate drastically drops for high MCL values.The difference in detection rates between ipsi-and contra-lateral conditions is primarily associated with medium (Med-Lo, Med-Hi) and high (Hi) MCL values, rather than low (Lo) MCL values.

We aimed to examine eSR detection patterns across various cochlear regions.In Figure 3, we present profiles for G1, G3, and G5 stimulation modes, considering low and high current amplitudes as well as ipsiand contra-lateral recordings.The detection rate profiles are generally flat, with slightly lower rates observed in the basilar region under certain conditions.However, one profile stands out.The G1-Ipsi-LowAmp condition shows very low detection rates (∼10%) in the middle electrodes (AP2 and MID1).This can be attributed to higher MCL values in the middle electrodes compared to apical and basal electrodes (Gärtner et al., 2022; see also Figure 5 top row).As shown in Figure 2, in the G1-Ipsi-LowAmp condition, no eSR is triggered when MCL is high, suggesting that 1-electrode low-amplitude stimulation mode does not provide sufficient charge to evoke eSR.


Relation between MCL and eSRT

In this part, we explore the relation between MCL and the eSR recordings for which a detection threshold could be identified.Figure 4 illustrates the eSRT levels across all cochlear regions plotted against their corresponding MCL values.Each panel represents the interplay of low and high stimulation currents (LowAmp, HighAmp) with ipsi-and contralateral recordings for three stimulation modes G1, G3, and G5.

All correlation coefficients (0.64 ≤ r ≤ 0.87) indicate that there are medium to strong linear relations between MCL and eSRT in different conditions.The coefficients have the tendency to increase with the number of stimulating electrodes.In other words, G1 is the stimulation mode that is mostly assigned with the lowest correlation coefficients.This is mostly explained by the increase of standard deviation of eSRT values with the increase of MCL.For example, in G1-Contra-HighAmp condition the correlation coefficient is 0.64.The Contra and HighAmp factors in this condition provide the best factors to achieve the biggest detection rate amongst all the G1-based conditions.However, the increase of detection rate also means that there are some patients for whom the eSRT is hardly detectable in G1-Contra-HighAmp condition, but not detectable in other G1-based conditions.Being, hardly detectable is tightly linked to have more variant eSRT levels in different patients and thus more standard deviation, and ultimately lower correlation coefficient.Shifting from G1 to G3 and G5 facilitates the induction of the stapedius response.As a result, the standard deviation of eSRTs at high MCLs slightly decreases so that the correlation coefficient increases ultimately to 0.85 in G5-Contra-HighAmp.

Fitting the eSRT data with first-order linear equations (depicted by the lines in Figure 4) indicates that in G1, G3 and G5 conditions, the slopes in the Ipsi condition (LowAmp: {1.4,1.6, 1.5}; HighAmp: {1.8, 1.6, 1.2}) are larger than the slopes in the Contra conditions (LowAmp: {1.1, 1.1, 0.9}; HighAmp: {1.3, 1.0, 0.8}).Interestingly, this occurs even though the offsets (intercepts) of the linear fits are not necessarily greater in the Ipsi condition; in fact, they are even slightly smaller.This suggests that for low MCL values, the eSRTs are comparable in both Ipsi and Contra conditions for both LowAmp and HighAmp conditions.However, for high MCL values, a higher charge level is required to invoke eSRT in the Ipsi condition compared to the Contra condition.

When comparing slopes between LowAmp and HighAmp conditions, it becomes evident that the slopes for G3 and G5 under LowAmp (Ipsi: {1.6, 1.5}, Contra: {1.1, 0.9}) are slightly greater than the slopes observed under the HighAmp condition (Ipsi: {1.6, 1.2}, Contra: {1.0, 0.8}).This suggests that stimulation with a higher current amplitude facilitates the stapedius response and lowers its threshold for activation.In the G1 condition, however, we could not observe this reduction in slope, mostly due to the significant difference between detection rates in LowAmp and HighAmp (>20% difference).Indeed, in the G1 stimulation mode, HighAmp increases the detection rate compared to LowAmp, however at the cost of elevated eSRT levels for high MCL values.


Regional eSRT trends

In this section, we explore the average offset between eSRT and MCL levels across various regions in the cochlea.This analysis adds depth to the insights provided by the linear slopes and correlation coefficients presented in the previous section, helping to mitigate the risk of under or over stimulations in CI patients when determining MCL based on eSRT.

Figure 5 illustrates the MCL values across distinct cochlear regions, with error bars indicating that MCL is assigned a higher charge level in the middle region (∼30 nC) compared to the basal and apical regions (∼20 nC).No significant difference is observed between MCLs in G1, G3, and G5 stimulation modes.In the same figure, the differences between eSRTs and MCLs for various interactions involving Ipsi, Contra, LowAmp, and HighAmp suggest that the eSRT consistently surpasses MCL in the G1 stimulation mode.The difference plateau remains relatively flat (except for AP2 region in HighAmp condition), indicating that eSRT follows the pattern of MCL with a positive offset around 20 nC across different regions in the cochlea.The AP2 region exhibits the highest MCL values.As previously mentioned, the HighAmp condition can elicit eSR in this region, but it comes with the elevated eSRT levels for high MCL values.

Transitioning from the G1 to G3 reduces the mean and standard deviation of difference values (offsets) between eSRTs and MCLs in all conditions.The differences consistently present as positive values, around 10 nC in LowAmp and 5 nC in HighAmp conditions.Under the G5 stimulation mode, the mean difference values approach zero, and the standard deviation decreases even further.Notably, in the HighAmp-Contra condition, the mean difference values dip into negative levels, around −3 nC.Since the linear fit slope for the G5-Contra-HighAmp condition is less than 1.0 (refer to the slope of 0.8 in Figure 4), this negative offset suggests that many CI patients will experience under-stimulation if MCL is predicted from eSRT without any correction.This under-stimulation effect will be more pronounced for patients with high MCL values.


Conditions interactions

In this section, we conduct statistical comparisons of eSRT levels across different conditions, considering varying stimulation amplitudes (LowAmp, HighAmp) and stimulation modes (G1, G3, G5).These comparisons are performed for both ipsi-and contra-lateral recordings, as depicted in Figure 6.

In section 3.1, it was demonstrated that an increase in stimulation amplitude facilitates eSR invocation.Here we analyze whether this facilitation requires a higher stimulation charge to be delivered to the electrodes (see Figure 6).Paired t-tests between eSRTs in HighAmp and LowAmp conditions for G3 and G5 modes reveal that the HighAmp condition even requires slightly less charge than the LowAmp condition to invoke eSR (G3-Ipsi: t(42) = −8.6,p < 1e-10; G5-Ipsi: t(44) = −5.9,p < 1e-6; G3-Contra: t(56) = −5.6,p < 1e-6; G5-Ipsi: t(63) = −7.1,p < 1e-8).Regarding the G1 mode, no significant difference is observed between HighAmp and LowAmp conditions (refer to the 2nd row in Figure 6).

Another important aspect that should be statistically analyzed is the impact of stimulation mode (i.e.G1, G3, G5) on eSRT charge levels.Figure 6 presents these charge values when expressed per electrode (refer to the 3rd row) and in total charge value delivered to all stimulating electrodes (refer to the 4th row).All paired t-test analyses between stimulation modes demonstrate statistically significant differences in eSRT charge levels ( p < 1e-5) both in ipsi-and contra-lateral ears.When the charge level is expressed per electrode, the eSRT level decreases with shifting the stimulation mode from G1 to G3 and G5.These values, indicated by the grey bars in Figure 6, are equal to {37, 23, 19}, {45, 26, 21}, {39, 27, 22}, and {43, 25, 20} nC for Ipsi-LowAmp, Ipsi-HighAmp, Contra-LowAmp, and Contra-HighAmp conditions respectively.Expressing the abovementioned eSRT values using the total charge indicates that eSRT increases quasi linearly from about 40 nC to 75 nC and then to 100 nC when transitioning from the G1 to G3 and G5 stimulation modes, respectively.


Discussions

In this paper, we first investigated the eSRTs in ipsilateral and contralateral recordings for various interactions involving stimulation amplitude (LowAmp and HighAmp) and stimulation modes (G1, G3, and G5).We analyzed the eSR detection rates, correlation coefficient between MCL and eSRT, and the linear fit between these two.Additionally, we explored the impact of cochlear regions on the eSR detection rates and the charge offsets between MCL and eSRT.The eSR detection rates were higher in all contralateral conditions than in ipsi-lateral conditions.The plateaus of the detection rates were relatively flat across cochlear regions.The only exception corresponded to G1-Ipsi-LowAmp, for which the electrodes in the middle of the cochlea had the lowest detection rates.Transitioning from G1 to G3 significantly increased the eSR detection rate in all conditions.By partitioning the MCL into four charge categories, we could reveal that the higher detection rate in G3 compared to G1 is mostly due to observing more eSRs at high MCL values.In other words, G1 cannot provide the necessary charge to invoke eSRTs for patients with high MCL values.In the G1 mode, the charge needed to invoke eSRs is approximately 20 nC higher than MCL.This extra charge level is either too loud for the patients to tolerate or cannot be delivered to the electrodes by the device due to safety or compliance issues.As a result, the detection rate is generally low in the G1 stimulation mode, especially for G1-Ipsi-LowAmp, which requires very high stimulation charges in the middle of the cochlea.Transitioning from G3 to G5 provided more facilities for the detection of eSR but not as pronouncedly as those obtained by transitioning from G1 to G3. Increasing the stimulation current (i.e.shifting from LowAmp to HighAmp) predominantly increased the eSR detection rates for G1 and to a lesser extent for G3 and G5 stimulation modes.This increase was more noticeable in ipsilateral recordings than in contralateral ones.Analysis of eSRT charge levels between LowAmp and HighAmp conditions revealed that the increase in eSR detection rate in the HighAmp condition is not necessarily accompanied by the delivery of larger charge levels to the electrodes.Conversely, the total delivered charge levels were slightly lower in the HighAmp condition.It can be inferred from the literature that increasing the stimulation current amplitude provides a louder perception to patients when the total charge is kept constant (Chatterjee et al., 2000;Zhou et al., 2020).These findings are also consistent with auditory nerve responses to electrical stimulations in animal studies (Shepherd et al., 2001).Our data suggests that the underlying neuronal mechanism of the stapedius response has a similar characteristic to that of loudness perception: an increase in current level facilitates eSR invocation at a lower charge level.This observation is supported by neurophysiological characteristics of some types of neurons present in the stapedius contraction neural circuitry.It is known that the stapedius motoneuron pool receives a diversity of inhibitory and excitatory inputs from the left and right cochlear nucleus and superior olivary complex (Mukerji et al., 2010;Venet et al., 2011).Spike patterns of neurons located in the lateral superior olive in response to ipsilateral tones indicate that they are more sensitive to the onset of input stimuli rather than its duration, and in particular, the bushy principal neurons act as differentiators rather than integrators (Franken et al., 2018).Considering these factors, we hypothesize that some neurons involved in the stapedius reflex respond more prominently to the stronger pulses provided in the HighAmp condition than to the pulses of the LowAmp (but longer duration) condition.

The analysis of correlation coefficients indicated that both LowAmp and HighAmp conditions produce strong correlations between MCL and eSRT.This analysis suggests that eSRTs that are measured based on HighAmp condition can be also used to determine the MCL levels that are based on the LowAmp stimulation current.Doing so has the advantage of the higher eSRT detection rate especially for the patients who have high MCL levels.Indeed, the slopes of the linear fits in the HighAmp condition are basically lower than those in the LowAmp condition; therefore, the eSRT is observed more securely and at a lower charge for high-amplitude MCLs.

Similarly, to the HighAmp, increasing the number of stimulating electrodes can be a practical option to determine MCL.The offset between eSRT and MCL reduces by moving G1 to G5.This movement also improves the correlation coefficient and reduces the offset between eSRT and MCL.In addition, the slope of the linear fit approaches one.However, cautious should be taken if two extreme factors HighAmp and G5 come together.In such a condition, the linear slope decreases to 0.8, so it may have under-stimulation effects if a proper correction factor is not applied when using eSRT to predict MCL.

Our findings align to a good extent with the outcomes of the study conducted by (Charroó et al., 2021).In their study, the eSRT levels obtained for 1-electrode stimulation were significantly higher than those for other stimulation modes.Similarly, eSRTs for 4-electrode stimulation were significantly higher than those for 15-electrode stimulation.However, no significant differences were observed between eSRTs for 15-electrode stimulation and MCL.(Charroó et al., 2021) suggested that the 15-electrode stimulation mode could be utilized to determine the maximum value of MCL.In our study, eSRTs in G1 and G3 stimulation modes were significantly higher than MCLs (for both LowAmp and HighAmp).In the G5 mode, the eSRT reached the MCL level, and in the HighAmp condition, it could even be found below the level of high MCL values because of the linear slope of 0.8 (i.e.smaller than 1.0).

Putting all of this together, our data suggests that for MCL fitting purposes, contra-lateral recording is a better option than ipsi-lateral recording.It provides a higher eSRT detection rate and linear slopes that are closer to one.If LowAmp is used to determine eSRT, utilizing G5 may offer more advantages than utilizing G3 and G1.These advantages include a higher eSRT detection rate, the least offset between eSRT and MCL, and a linear slope close to one.If the eSRT cannot be detected, then transitioning to HighAmp is a good choice.In this case, the G3 mode may be preferable as it corresponds to a linear slope very close to one.Finally, the G5-HighAmp stimulation mode provides the last chance to detect the eSRT; however, fitting will require a correction factor to prevent under-stimulation.

Our statistical data analysis on eSRT charge levels revealed that less charge is required per electrode when transitioning from G1 stimulation mode to G3 and G5.However, interestingly, the total charge needed to invoke eSRT increased approximately from about 40-75 and then to 100 nC, respectively.This suggests that there is an imperfect charge integration effect mechanism to invoke eSR.For a charge integration factor of 0 between electrodes (i.e.no summation effect) we should have observed 40, 3 × 40 = 120, and 5 × 40 = 200 nC in G1, G3, and G5 stimulation modes, respectively.In other words, if there is no loudness summation effect in multi-electrode stimulation, each electrode should provide exactly the same charge as in a singlechannel stimulation paradigm.Therefore, the ratio of total delivered charges will be q n /q 1 = n, where n > 1 is the number of stimulating electrodes, q n is the total charge delivered by all the n electrodes, and q 1 is the delivered charge in G1 condition (i.e.delivered by 1 electrode).On the other hand, for an integration factor of 1 (i.e.perfect summation) a similar total charge of 40 nC should have been observed in all stimulation modes.In other words, the sum of the charge delivered by all the n electrodes is equal to the charge delivered by a single electrode in the G1 condition, and therefore q n /q 1 = 1.Heuristically, we may express mathematically this interaction factor as γ = 1 − (q n /q 1 ) − 1 n − 1 , where γ will be a value between 0 and 1 (i.e.γ = 1 if q n /q 1 = 1, and γ = 0 if q n /q 1 = n).The calculation is based on the division of the relative increase in the total charge to the increase in the number of stimulating electrodes with respect to the reference condition where n = 1 (i.e.G1 condition).Our data suggests that the charge summation factor γ was around 0.6 for both G3 and G5 stimulation modes [γ G3 = 0.56 (n = 3, q 3 ≅ 75, q 1 ≅ 40); γ G5 = 0.62 (n = 5, q 5 ≅ 100, q 1 ≅ 40)] indicating that around 60% of the charges delivered to each extra electrode in multi-electrode stimulations contribute to eSRT invocation.In other words, about 40% of the extra charges delivered by extra n − 1 electrodes do not participate in loudness growth and/or eSRT mechanism.

Our clinical protocol was complex, and the participants had to be very patient to receive loud stimulations corresponding to different conditions (ipsilateral/contralateral, multi-electrode, multiamplitude).We conducted this complex clinical study on 17 eligible patients, which aligns well with other similar studies e.g.(Hodges et al., 1999): 25 adults, (Carranco Hernandez et al., 2019): 19 children, (Bergeron andHotton, 2015): 11 adults, (Raghunandhan et al., 2015): 10 children, (Mishra and Nandurkar, 2019): 40 children, (De Andrade et al., 2018): 24 adults, (Gordon et al., 2004): 20 children, and(Palani et al., 2022): 14 children.However, the generalizability of our findings should be studied in a multi-center study.

Our CI recipients belonged to one of the following groups of adult recipients: unilateral, bilateral, bimodal, and SSD.To the best of our knowledge, the MCL fitting method using eSRT measurement does not change across groups if they meet the inclusion/exclusion criteria presented in this study ( particularly the intactness of the middle ear as tested by the tympanogram).However, it should be noted that SSD patients hear the continuous probe sound of the tympanometer, making it more difficult for them to tolerate long-lasting clinical research trials.

Reprogramming the recipient's sound processor using values provided by eSRT in multi-electrode and/or multi-amplitude stimulation configurations was beyond the scope of our study.This requires a chronic, preferably multi-centric study to examine speech performance over time for different eSRTbased fitting configurations.


Conclusion

The analysis of eSR in Oticon Medical CI devices confirmed observations in the audiology field regarding the low detection rate of eSR when only one stimulating electrode is used.

Our study showed that in Oticon Medical implants, single-electrode stimulation at nominal stimulation amplitude does not invoke eSRT very well.However, increasing the stimulation amplitude and using multi-electrode stimulation can facilitate the eSRT response.Consequently, our study suggests that for Oticon Medical patients, we can increase stimulation amplitude and/or use multiple-electrode stimulation for eSRT-based fitting purposes.In other words, our data indicated that audiologists may increase the current amplitude to a higher value than that clinically used for the patient to enhance eSR detection rates.This increase in amplitude does not modify importantly the correlation coefficient between eSRT and MCL.

This study demonstrated that multi-electrode stimulation in groups of 3 and 5 (i.e.G3, G5) increased the eSRT detection rate.In addition, it generally reduced the slope of the linear regression fits between MCL and eSRT in all conditions (i.e.low and high amplitudes, ipsi-and contra-lateral ears).Our data suggests that the slope between MCL and eSRT is around 1.0 for low-amplitude contra-lateral G5 and high-amplitude contra-lateral G3 modes.

Taking these elements into account, we suggest using low-amplitude or high-amplitude contralateral G3 to obtain a more precise and localized MCL curve around each electrode.Additionally, using low-amplitude contra-lateral G5 can help achieve a more global MCL curve and increase the likelihood of detecting eSR.
