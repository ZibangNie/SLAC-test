In this paper, we will introduce the notion of (u, v)-absorbing hyperideals in multiplicative hyperrings and we will show some properties of them.Then we extend this concept to the notion of (u, v)-absorbing prime hyperideals and then we will give some results about them.2010 Mathematics Subject Classification.Primary 20N20; Secondary 16Y99.Key words and phrases.(u, v)-absorbing hyperideal, AB-(u, v)-absorbing hyperideal, (u, v)absorbing prime hyperideal.

Introduction

In 1934 [25], the French mathematician F. Marty presented the notion of hyperstructures or multioperations in which an operation yields a set of values rather than a single value.Many authors have contributed to the advancement of this novel area of modern algebra [12,13,14,15,16,26,30,36].An importan class of the algebraic hyperstructures called the multiplicative hyperring was defined by Rota in 1982 [32].In this hyperstructure, the multiplication is a hyperoperation however the addition is an operation.Multiplicative hyperrings are deeply demonstrated and characterized in [3,4,5,22,28,29,31,35].Dasgupta studied the prime and primary hyperideals in multiplicative hyperrings in [17].The notions of 1-absorbing and v-absorbing hyperideals as generalizations of the prime hyperideals were studied in [29] and [5].The idea of (u, v)-closed hyperideals in a multiplicative hyperring was proposed in [6].Our purpose of this research work is to introduce and study the notion of (u, v)-absorbing hyperideals as an extension of the n-absorbing hyperideals in a commutative multiplicative hyperring.Several specific properties are given to illustrate the structures of the new notion.Furthermore, we extend this concept to (u, v)-absorbing prime hyperideals.

In this section, we recall some background materials.A hyperoperation " • " on non-empty set X is a mapping from X × X into P * (X) such that P * (X) is the family of all non-empty subsets of X.In this case, (X, •) is called hypergroupoid.Let X 1 , X 2 be two subsets of X and x ∈ X, then X 1 • X 2 = ∪ x1∈X1,x2∈X2 x 1 • x 2 , and X 1 • x = X 1 • {x}.This means that the hyperoperation " • " on X can be extended to subsets of X.A hypergroupoid (X, •) is called a semihypergroup if
∪ a∈y•z x • a = ∪ b∈x•y b • z for all x, y, z ∈ X which means • is associative. A semihypergroup X is called a hypergroup if x • X = X = X • x for each x ∈ X. A non-empty subset Y of a semihypergroup (X, •) refers to a subhypergroup if x • Y = Y = Y • x for each x ∈ Y [19].
Definition 2.1.[19] An algebraic structure (A, +, •) refers to a commutative multiplicative hyperring if

(1) (A, +) is a commutative group;

(2) (A, •) is a semihypergroup;

(3) x • (y + z) ⊆ x • y + x • z and (y + z) • x ⊆ y • x + z • x for every x, y, z ∈ A;

(4) x • (−y) = (−x) • y = −(x • y) for every x, y ∈ A;

(5) x • y = y • x for every x, y ∈ A.

If in (3), the equality holds then the multiplicative hyperring A is called strongly distributive.
