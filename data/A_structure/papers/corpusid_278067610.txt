Objective: Immunocompromised patients are at increased risk of bacterial and viral respiratory infections and related complications.Available vaccines against these infections are the most effective tools in preventing complications.Community pharmacist vaccination service is evolving in many countries, and they could present an opportunity to improve vaccination coverage in this population.This study aims to describe how community pharmacists are involved in vaccinating high-risk (immunocompromised) populations.Methods: This retrospective study included vaccination information of patients who received chronic immunosuppressive therapy among those who were taking selected chronic medications from community pharmacies in Montreal, Canada, from January 2020 to September 2023.Immunosuppressive therapy was categorized as either corticosteroids or immunosuppressive drugs or the combination of corticosteroids with one or two immunosuppressive drugs.Data were available from all private pharmacies through the Quebec Association of Pharmacists Owners database.Descriptive statistics are used for data analysis.Findings: Of the total number of 4,114,528 patients who were taking selected chronic medications from community pharmacies, 611,789 (14.8%) were taking immunosuppressives.Prednisone/prednisolone was the most commonly administered drug in this population (345,744 [8.4%]).Most of these patients are aged over 50 years (484,827 [79.2%]).A total of 409,171 (66.8%) co-administered at least one other drug.The vaccination rate was highest for influenza (142,877 [23.3%]) and lowest (23,532 [3.8%]) with the pneumococcus vaccine.Patients younger than 25 had the lowest vaccination rate with three respiratory vaccines.Vaccination rate decreased after the pandemic with all three vaccines in our study population.Conclusion: Vaccination rates of patients taking chronic immunosuppressive drugs in community pharmacies are low.These rates are worse than rates for other high-risk groups from the same database.This represents a missed opportunity for pharmacists to encourage this high-risk population to vaccinate proactively.

Introduction

R espiratory tract infections (RTI) are among the most frequent infections worldwide, burdening public health. [1]Influenza viruses (IV), pneumatiques, parainfluenza viruses (PIV), coronaviruses (CoV), and rhinoviruses (HRV) are considered by the World Health Organization (WHO) as global health concerns. [2]treptococcus pneumoniae is another common pathogen of RTIs that is currently among a limited number of respiratory pathogens that may be prevented by vaccination.It is also a significant cause of respiratory infections and related complications in high-risk populations such as immunocompromised patients. [3]veral conditions, including various underlying diseases, including malignancy, organ or stem cell transplantation, chronic kidney diseases, connective tissue disorders, diabetes mellitus, etc., or drugs such as chemotherapeutic agents or other immunosuppressive agents, can compromise the function of the immune system. [4]In general, suppression of T-cell function is associated with a higher susceptibility to infection or reactivation of various pathogens. [5]Therefore, immunocompromised patients are at increased risk of bacterial, fungal, and viral respiratory infections and related complications. [6]Some studies report that the incidence of diseases in immunocompromised individuals is more than 10-fold higher than in immunocompetent individuals. [7]Multi-pathogenic infections are also more common in this population.For example, in neutropenic cancer patients, bacterial infections cause early febrile disease along with late-onset fungal infections, leading to a high rate of morbidity and mortality. [8]Unfortunately, the number of immunocompromised patients is growing worldwide, and this problem is becoming a global concern. [9]The respiratory tract is the organ system most commonly affected by pathogens in patients with compromised immune systems. [10]Pathogens that cause acute respiratory illness in the general population are also responsible for diseases and hospitalizations in patients with underlying medical conditions, [11] but these patients comprise a more significant portion of invasive diseases that put them in the category for special medical attention. [12]ophylactic use of antiviral agents and administration of immunoglobulin or monoclonal antibody products have been utilized to limit the spread of infection within this population. [4]So far, available vaccines against selected respiratory pathogens are the safest and most effective tools in decreasing infection rates and preventing complications. [13]neumococcal, influenza, and COVID-19 vaccinations are not only advocated in most immunization guidelines for high-risk populations but also strongly recommended for this high-risk population's family members and household contacts. [14,15]Despite demonstrated efficacy and safety, vaccine coverage is still below targets in almost all societies.Treating patients without routine and recommended vaccinations costs a lot of money. [15]Therefore, all healthcare systems must fund and implement vaccination recommendations to address the magnitude of the disease burden, especially among targeted populations. [16]mmunity pharmacists are the most frequently consulted healthcare providers, especially for patients with chronic diseases.Community pharmacy-based immunization services have evolved in many countries during the past few decades.Their role is educator, facilitator, and immunizer. [17]These services have increased the number of immunization providers and the number of sites.It also extends access hours for patients to get required immunizations and decreases the workload of other healthcare providers, especially in global outbreaks like the recent pandemic.Besides, patients often reported that they prefer to get vaccinated in community pharmacies because it is more convenient than traditional vaccination settings such as doctor's offices or hospitals. [18]Therefore, community pharmacies could present an opportunity to improve vaccination coverage rates, especially in high-risk populations like immunocompromised patients.Despite the high enthusiasm of community pharmacists to vaccinate, several barriers limit their engagement in vaccination services. [19]It is thus essential to investigate the current role of community pharmacy-based immunization in each healthcare system to identify opportunities and challenges for improving the public health infrastructure to deliver vaccinations, especially to high-risk populations.This study aims to evaluate community pharmacists' contribution to the vaccination of high-risk (immunocompromised) populations against RTI to assess the potential recommendations to improve vaccination coverage in immunocompromised persons in our population.


Methods

This retrospective study included vaccination information (all forms of commercialized COVID-19, pneumococcus, and influenza vaccines) of patients who received chronic immunosuppressive therapy.These patients were among those taking selected chronic medications from community pharmacies.This information was collected from 16 administrative regions throughout Quebec, Canada, from January 2020 to September 2023.Data were available from all private pharmacies through the Quebec Association of Pharmacists Owners database.Patients who were on immunosuppressive medications were selected from this database.We used some prescription refills as an indicator of chronic drug administration; a higher average number of refills represents the longer duration of the treatment course.Because each prescription refill is usually enough for 1 month of treatment, we included all patients who collected two or more prescription refills from community pharmacies representing at least 2 months of treatment period.Immunosuppressive therapy was categorized as either corticosteroid (cortisone acetate, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, methylprednisolone, prednisolone or prednisone) or immunosuppressive drugs (azathioprine, cyclosporine, daclizumab, methotrexate (MTX), mycophenolate mofetil, sirolimus, tacrolimus) as well as combination of one corticosteroid with one or two immunosuppressive drugs.Other data extracted from that database includes patient age and chronic co-administered drugs indicating comorbidities.Antidiabetics, cardiovascular, and respiratory co-administered drugs were recorded, representing patients' comorbidities.Approval from the local research and ethics board has been obtained at each participating institution.

The results are presented as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables with normal distribution, while frequency (percentage) was used for categorical variables.Data entry and analysis were conducted using SPSS, version 27 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).


Results

Discussion

We conducted a 4-year analysis of the vaccination rate with respiratory vaccines in community pharmacies among patients who co-administered selected chronic medications with immunosuppressive drugs in 16 administrative regions throughout Quebec, Canada.Our  2) Data are presented as n (%) of patients in each year receiving either steroids, immunosuppressives, or combinations of both results showed a low rate of vaccination in community pharmacies, with a declining pattern from 2020 to 2023.Younger patients had a lower vaccination rate.

Vaccination is the most established and cost-effective intervention for preventing infections, especially respiratory infectious diseases.Therefore, all measures to promote vaccination rates are important goals of all health systems and can reduce the burden of infectious diseases.However, apart from all efforts, certain vaccine-preventable diseases are still among the leading causes of hospitalization and death. [20]accination is a traditional activity in all public health settings.It used to be more common in health settings other than community pharmacies, such as hospitals or doctor offices.However, it is still a common practice in many countries, such as underdeveloped or developing countries.Although community pharmacy-based immunization has been a common practice in many countries during the past few decades, it still faces various barriers, such as regional laws, collaboration protocols, and health plan restrictions. [21]Among the Western countries, the U.S. is a pioneer in vaccination training for pharmacists.Nowadays, pharmacists are authorized to administer vaccinations all over the U.S. For example, pharmacies have become the second most common places, just behind physician offices, for vaccination against seasonal influenza. [22]In Canada, legislation for pharmacists as immunizers (PAIs) has expanded since 2007, when BC became the first province to allow pharmacists to administer vaccination.PAIs in Canada have expanded since then, with differences across jurisdictions regarding which vaccines a pharmacist can administer, to whom, and under what funding model. [23]Nowadays, Pharmacists have access to and administer a comprehensive list of publicly-funded vaccines in almost all jurisdictions in Canada.Most of these vaccines, such as influenza and COVID-19, are part of a routine immunization program, and a doctor's prescription is not required for vaccination. [24]Pharmacist's role in vaccination is well-studied in Canada.The public widely accepts that community pharmacy vaccination services and vaccination rates have improved. [25]It is now a common practice for immunization against seasonal influenza, and more studies have focused on this vaccine.For example, a survey of 3000 Canadian residents aged ≥18 used a cross-sectional, self-reported, online structured questionnaire during the 2022-2023 season.The results showed that 56.6% of respondents received an influenza vaccine at a pharmacy, including 57.5% of respondents considered to be at high risk of complications from influenza. [26]This rate is about twice as high as the rate of vaccination against influenza in our study population.

[29] Considering our results, we can conclude that most influenza vaccines are administered in community pharmacies, but about 1/4 of individuals got vaccinated in other healthcare facilities.The WHO has suggested that IVRs should reach 75% among older adults and individuals with chronic diseases. [30]A total of 351 studies from 41 countries/regions reported IVRs of 41.65% in individuals with chronic diseases. [31]Our results indicate that the IVR for the group of immunocompromised patients in community pharmacies of Quebec is far below the desired level of the WHO.It is also less than reported in other studies, reflecting the small contribution of community pharmacies in vaccinating this target population.According to our results, the overall vaccination rate was higher in older people, which was similar to previous studies that showed the influenza vaccine was mostly used in people over 50 years. [31]In one study in the U.S., people who used the out-of-hours service provided by the community pharmacies (evenings and weekends) were mostly younger people and those without comorbidities.This may reflect the fact that we may be able to improve our community pharmacies' vaccination rate by motivating younger patients to use this service, focusing on these advantages. [32]Furthermore, per the results of many studies that reported the public acceptance of the influenza vaccine improved during the COVID-19 pandemic, [33,34] we observed that the IVRs were highest in 2020.Although we did not have whole data for 2023, overall findings demonstrate a significant reduction in IVR across all age groups from 2020 to 2022, when the severity and lethality of COVID-19 declined over 2 years of the pandemic.Vaccine fatigue can be an explanation for this declining pattern.The overall vaccination rate decreased from 2020 to 2023 with all three vaccines.This was particularly observed with the COVID-19 vaccination rate, which is possibly related to the unrealistic public perception of negligible risk of COVID-19 infection after the pandemic ended.Removing practical barriers to vaccination by improving availability and access to immunization services in all pharmacies at patient convenience, sharing evidence-based information and patient counseling to manage vaccine hesitancy, for example, by identifying and reminding target groups and including government-funded/supported pharmacy-based vaccination programs as part of the overall health promotion national plans are recognized as potential solutions. [35,36]e vaccination rate for COVID-19 in different countries varies significantly.Based on the WHO report in 2023, 87% of Canadians received at least one dose of the vaccine, 83% were vaccinated with a complete primary series, and only 53% received one booster dose. [37]Our result shows that most patients, including the target population, have been vaccinated against COVID-19 in centers other than community pharmacies.The WHO report also indicated the drop-in rate of booster doses that can be consistent with a sharp decline in the COVID-19 vaccination rate observed from 2020 to 2023.Only limited studies evaluated vaccination rates in immunocompromised patients.For example, a study conducted in a University's Department of Family and Community Medicine assessed COVID-19 vaccination trends among immunocompromised patients.They observed that 62.7% of patients received ≥3 mRNA vaccine doses, and 37.3% received ≤2 mRNA vaccine doses. [38]As our overall data about the vaccination rate in this target group is unavailable, we cannot estimate community pharmacies' contribution rate.The vaccination rate in our study population is relatively lower than that of others, indicating that our overall vaccination rate in this target group is less than that of others or that they mostly get vaccinated in centers other than community pharmacies.Most of our study population aged over 50 and were taking steroids.Among steroids, prednisone/prednisolone was the most common drug used in our patients.These demographic data can be used to identify which subgroup of the high-risk population could have the most significant impact on increasing vaccination rates in community pharmacies.They should be identified and educated using different strategies.Overall, the lower rate of vaccination with COVID-19 and pneumococcus vaccine compared to influenza in community pharmacies and sharper decline, especially with COVID-19 vaccine after the pandemic, indicates the fact that the influenza vaccine is the most accepted respiratory vaccine as a routine prophylactic measure against respiratory infections in our community.Implementing vaccination against other respiratory illnesses like COVID-19 as a routine public health measure may need more careful attention and resources to invest.Comparing the vaccination rate in different regions of Quebec can be a guide to identifying which part needs more attention to improve pharmacy-based immunization service to deliver vaccinations, especially to high-risk populations.

We can conclude that the rate of community pharmacy vaccination among patients taking immunosuppressive medications, who are recognized as a high-risk population against RTIs, is below the national target ranges and those of other developed countries.These rates were even worse than those of other high-risk groups from the same database.This represents a missed opportunity by pharmacists to proactively encourage this high-risk population to vaccination, mainly since patients visit this category of professionals most frequently.This study has some limitations.First, information about the dosage of drugs was not included in our database, and the exact duration of drug administration could not be interpreted from several refills.The second number of refills was significantly different with different drugs.Therefore, some patients may have taken corticosteroids or immunosuppressants for shorter durations than others.For example, the average number of refills for patients receiving dexamethasone was about 3, indicating a brief duration of treatment compared to others such as tacrolimus or sirolimus, with almost more than 30 refills in all regions.Third, other demographic data, such as sex, socioeconomic determinants, and comorbid disease, could not be extracted from the database.Fourth, data about the year 2023 are limited to September.Considering that vaccination against seasonal influenza is routinely performed after that, data about influenza vaccination cannot be interpreted for 2023.Some conclusions must, therefore, be interpreted with caution.
