Normal objects appear surprisingly unchanged as we move around them, explained by perceptual scaling giving visual constancy, and we correctly attribute the motion to oneself. The more realistic the Other World of the picture, the more vividly we see it rotating with us as we move. This is opposite to normal motion parallax. Especially dramatic are the weird distortions of a huge, flat, but apparently concave dome, of a trompe l'oeil cathedral, as we walk beneath it. Trompe l'oeil is impressive when it is working as intendedöand is even more amazing when creating its own dramatic illusions in pictures.


Exhibitions

Exhibitions of trompe l'oeil pictures are quite frequent, and their catalogues are generally more instructive than books on the subject, which seldom recognise their scientific or perceptual interest.
