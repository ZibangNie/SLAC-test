Observational research shows a link between celiac disease (CeD) and sarcoidosis, but the causal link between CeD and sarcoidosis is still unknown.A two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) study was conducted to ascertain the causal connection between the 2 disorders.In our two-sample MR analysis, we identified independent genetic variants associated with CeD using publicly accessible GWAS data from people of European ancestry.Summary data for sarcoidosis were obtained from the FinnGen Consortium, the UK-Biobank, and a large GWAS dataset.To assess the association between CeD and sarcoidosis, our MR analysis used inverse variance weighted (IVW) as the primary method, incorporating the MR-Egger, weighted median (WM), and MR-PRESSO (outliers test) as a complementary method.In order to ensure that the findings were reliable, several sensitivity analyses were performed.Our study indicated that CeD had a significant causal relationship with sarcoidosis (IVW odds ratio (OR) = 1.13, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.07-1.20,P = 5.58E-05; WM OR = 1.12, 95% CI: 1.03-1.23,P = 1.03E-02;MR-Egger OR = 1.07, 95% CI: 0.96-1.19,P = 2.20E-01).Additionally, we obtain the same results in the duplicated datasets as well, which makes our results even more reliable.The results of this investigation did not reveal any evidence of horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity.Our MR analysis showed a causal effect between CeD and an elevated risk of sarcoidosis.Further study is still needed to confirm the findings and look into the processes underlying these relationships.

Introduction

Sarcoidosis is a chronic idiopathic granulomatous disorder that causes granulomas to grow in multiple organs. [1][9] CeD is a chronic autoimmune condition with a frequency in most countries ranging from 1:70 to 1:300. [10][13] Current research shows that the worldwide prevalence of CeD is about 1%, [14] and it is expected to increase in the future. [15]According to a recent study, people with CeD had an even greater chance of getting sarcoidosis. [16]oth CeD and sarcoidosis are autoimmune disorders that are concerned with genetic and immunological factors.A genetic study confirmed that sarcoidosis and CeD are familial in European populations. [17]While autoimmunity is linked to sarcoidosis, no link has been demonstrated between sarcoidosis genetic factors and CeD.One study discovered a significant genetic pleiotropy between sarcoidosis and CeD. [18]An increased chance of getting CeD has been linked to sarcoidosis, according to earlier research. [19,20]Despite decades of active research, conclusive findings about the relationship between sarcoidosis and CeD are still elusive.Previous studies have found a connection between CeD and sarcoidosis, but some studies have shown that no such association has been observed. [21,22]It is possible that shared genetic susceptibility, specifically immune cross-reactivity between HLA-DR3 and gluten proteins and autoantigens, is the biological connection between the 2 diseases. [8,23,24]Although prior research has shown a link between CeD and sarcoidosis, there is no definitive evidence to report whether there is a causal link between the 2 disorders.Confounding variables and reverse causality limit traditional observational research.Due to the possible bias of confounding factors, these previous observational data may be limited in terms of association inference, and even the conclusions may be disputed.Mendelian randomization (MR) examines if there is a link between risk factors and outcomes, and it can explain and test this hypothesis using causal effects. [25]Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and other forms of MR through genetic variants are employed as instrumental variables (IVs) that can affect disease risk factors or exposures. [26]To clarify the causal relationship between CeD and sarcoidosis and to assess the directionality of this association, we used the latest GWAS data to perform a two-sample MR study.


Materials and methods


Study design

To determine whether CeD and sarcoidosis are causally related, we conducted a two-sample MR investigation in the European population.[29] The study involved the major procedures below to validate each implication: Multiple MR approaches, pleiotropy evaluation, heterogeneity, and sensitivity analysis, as well as other techniques, were used to choose genetic IVs associated with the right exposures.The most recent recommendations (STROBE-MR) [30] were followed in this investigation.We did not need to perform any additional ethical reviews because informed permission and ethical approval had already been obtained for each of the initial studies.


Data sources

All SNPs and summary data were generated from studies that solely included individuals of European ancestry in order to avoid the impacts of population stratification.First, summary data for CeD, [31] comprising 11,812 cases and 11,837 controls, was retrieved from the IEU OpenGWAS database (https:// gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/datasets/).CeD was diagnosed using clinical criteria, matched serology, and a small intestinal sample.

The original study has more information on the features of CeD cases.We got sarcoidosis summary statistics from the FinnGen consortium, [32] the UK Biobank (UKB) study, and Rivera et al [33] Data from the FinnGen consortium was used as the primary analysis.Diagnostic criteria for sarcoidosis are defined by ICD-8, ICD-9, and ICD-10, and GWAS statistics for sarcoidosis contain 16,380,463 variant loci from 2046 cases and 215,712 healthy controls.Data from the UKB and Rivera NV et al were used as validation (Table 1).All summary datasets are available from the PheWeb (https://pheweb.org/UKB-SAIGE/) [34]and the IEU GWAS database.The age and gender of the participants were among the demographic details provided by the FinnGen project as well as the primary literature.Table 1 listed the GWAS data source details in our study.


Instrumental variables selection

As a first step, SNPs significantly associated (P < 5.00E-08) with CeD were extracted in this study.Second, to remove SNPs linked to linkage disequilibrium (LD), we employed a clumping approach with R 2 < 0.001 and a window size of 10 Mb.We used F-statistics to assess the IVs' strength to prevent weak-tool bias (F > 10): F = R 2 (N-K-1)/K(1-R 2 ). [35]


Mendelian randomization analysis

The inverse-variance weighted (IVW), [36] MR-Egger, [37] and weighted median [28] techniques were used in this investigation to establish the causal link between CeD and sarcoidosis, with the IVW technique predominating.Fixed-effects IVW was employed unless there was evidence of heterogeneity among the IVs, in which case random-effects IVW was used.Additionally, to check for pleiotropy, we used the MR-Egger intercept.Moreover, the MR-PRESSO test is used to test for horizontal pleiotropy as well as to detect outliers and recalculate results by removing them if necessary. [38]With the use of Cochran's Q test, the heterogeneity of IVW estimations was measured.Furthermore, we used the ivw_radial method to identify the IVs that contributed the most to heterogeneity, then removed them and reran the analysis. [9]Additionally, we removed certain SNPs that showed significant independent effects on the MR approach using the "leave-one-out" technique.Finally, to determine whether our causal direction is accurate, we run the MR-Steiger test.The whole analysis was carried out in RStudio (version 4.2.1) with the "TwoSampleMR" (version 0.5.6), "Radial MR" (version 1.0), and "MRPRESSO" (version 1.0) packages.

Discussion

We used MR analysis in this work to demonstrate a positive correlation between CeD and sarcoidosis.This study is the first that we are aware of to evaluate the causal link between CeD and sarcoidosis using MR technology.There is sufficient evidence from our study to suggest that CeD increases the risk of sarcoidosis.Firstly, both CeD and sarcoidosis are chronic immunological disorders that affect numerous organs. [39][42][43] Consistent with our study, a recent retrospective study found a clear link between CeD and sarcoidosis, which significantly made CeD patients more susceptible to developing sarcoidosis, with a standardized incidence rate of 36.8 (95% CI: 26.7-50.9),while also providing a reference to the mechanisms of occurrence of both diseases. [44]As a multisystem disorder, sarcoidosis is associated with immune-mediated chronic inflammatory diseases, among which CeD is associated (OR 3.1; 95% CI: 1.01-7.3). [45]he most frequent causes of CeD and sarcoidosis, which include genetic predisposition and immunological factors, are now widely acknowledged to exist.It has been suggested that both sarcoidosis and CeD are caused by improper antigen processing.As a systemic illness with an unclear cause, sarcoidosis is genetically linked to several human white cell antigen (HLA) variations and genetic polymorphisms, mostly in the HLA region. [46,47][50][51] HLA-DR3 is surprisingly linked to a higher risk of CeD, according to studies. [52,53]In addition, variants in the HLA-DQB1*02 allele are also critical for the susceptibility and development of CeD. [54]So, this could be one explanation for why the 2 diseases might co-occur more frequently than expected.In addition, anti-gluten immunoreactivity in CeD patients may also be a factor in this explanation. [55,56] and T lymphocytes that recognize the epitopes on gluten proteins might occasionally become reactive to their own antigens.Therefore, autoimmune illnesses like sarcoidosis are more likely to occur in people with CeD.

Furthermore, autoimmunity may be yet another crucial shared mechanism.[4][5][6] And sarcoidosis may be caused by a significant association with CeD, thus further supporting autoimmune The MR method has the benefit of being able to deduce the causative axis between CeD and sarcoidosis.The MR is built using the publicly available GWAS summary statistics.It is feasible to mine trustworthy genetic data without incurring additional experimental expenses.Secondly, we confirmed our findings with other data sets, which increased the credibility of our conclusions.According to the findings of this investigation, CeD has been connected with an increased risk of sarcoidosis.

There are also more limitations to our study.To begin, because of the small sample size of CeD and sarcoidosis, some false positives and false negatives may have occurred.A second shortcoming was that the causal link between CeD and sarcoidosis was not stratified by age or gender.Third, exposure and outcome analyses included only Europeans in the study population.It must be determined whether the results are repeated in other populations.Other ethnic groups also need MR studies, which must be supported by extensive GWAS data.Finally, we weren't able to perform a reverse MR analysis due to data source constraints.However, we performed a directional test, suggesting that our results are indeed correct.Larger and more comprehensive datasets may be needed for further analysis in the future.


Conclusion

Finally, this investigation discovered a causality between CeD and a significant risk of sarcoidosis.The results obtained offer fresh perspectives on the genetic pathogenesis of CeD and sarcoidosis.There are currently few investigations into CeD and sarcoidosis, and the mechanism behind the two-way relationship is unknown.Future research should concentrate on the mechanism that connects the two.
