Exploring the digital mirror: Problematic social media usage and its influence on body shape among medical students in a South Indian Tertiary Care Setting
19FDD802D9EF82A875ABD04B4419F9A710.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_60_25Received: 08-01-2025 Revised: 01-05-2025 Accepted: 07-05-2025Addictionbody imageobesity
Background: Social media usage addiction has become very rampant in this AI era.In India, the idea of body image has received less attention, particularly among young females.Environmental and cultural factors have a big impact on how body shape concerns develop.Objectives: 1.To determine the pattern of social media usage among medical students.2. To assess the body shape satisfaction among medical students.3. To determine factors associated with body shape concerns among medical students.Materials and Methods: This analytical cross-sectional study was conducted among 430 medical students of a Private Medical College in Tamil Nadu using a semi-structured questionnaire.Social media addiction was assessed using Bergen scale (six-item) and body shape satisfaction using a validated BSQ 34-item scale.Data was analyzed using SPSS version 22. Variables significantly associated with body shape concerns were identified using univariate and multivariate analysis.Results: The mean age was 21.43 ± 3.40 years and 67.1% were females.Among the study participants, 56.5% were using social media for an average of 1-3 hours in a day and 76.3% of them had problematic social media usage (PSMU).About 36.2% had various degrees of body shape concerns and it was significantly associated with PSMU and body mass index in regression analysis.Conclusion: Nearly 76.3% had problematic social media usage, and 36.2% had body shape concerns.Awareness programs should be conducted among medical students to identify early cases of social media addiction and body shape and image-related concerns.

Introduction

Since COVID, smartphones have become inevitable due to online learning, and as a result, the screen time of youth has escalated significantly. [1]The emergence of "influencers" on social media platforms has greatly influenced current trends and behaviors among young adults, who are more likely to trust the information these influencers supply than to look for scientific proof for it. [2]ecause of this, they hold themselves to an unattainable standard, which lowers their self-esteem and produces emotional and body image problems. [3]dical students are more vulnerable and many medical students use their mobile devices to access online lectures, videos, and other learning resources. [4]Through digital food marketing in social media platforms, teenagers are exposed to sugary drinks, candy, and fast-food restaurants on daily basis.The majority of studies in India has focused on social media use, gaming addiction, and mental health issues rather than the impact of social media on eating behaviors and body image. [5,6]Researchers have reported that behaviors of those exposed to online and social media usage were associated with higher unhealthy food and beverage consumption. [7]dy image is a complex concept that includes the thoughts, feelings, and assessment of individuals own body. [8]It is a complicated interaction between physical attractiveness, self-worth, and self-esteem. [9]Research has repeatedly linked obesity and inactivity to negative body image and it is a major contributing factor to the development of eating disorders in teenagers.A positive body image will influence self-esteem, self-acceptance, and a healthy outlook and behavior. [10]ven that India is still a developing nation, many beliefs around body image are often stigmatized.In India, moderate-to-severe body dissatisfaction was reported by 28% of young women. [11]oor body image affects mental health in ways that go beyond the occasional bad thought. [12]Poor general well-being in both sexes and a worsening in women's mental health are caused by body dissatisfaction.Numerous aspects, such as socioeconomic position, weight, age, sex, and weight control, have been linked to how people see their bodies, behavior as well as peer and familial pressure. [13] is well established that environmental and cultural factors have a big impact on how body image dissatisfaction (BID) develops.The issues of body image perception, which are rarely acknowledged, have been brought to light by the increased use of social media and decreased physical activity. [13]Paucity of studies in the Indian context to explore the influence of social media on the body image-related concerns among medical students was observed.This current study was planned to estimate the prevalence of problematic social media usage and its relationship to body image concerns among medical students.


Materials and Methods


Study design and study setting

It is an analytical cross-sectional study conducted among undergraduates and postgraduates medical students of a tertiary care hospital in Salem district of Tamil Nadu.


Sample size and sampling method

The sample size was calculated based on a previous study by Kapoor et al. [13] in Delhi during 2022.The prevalence of body shape concerns was taken as 30.6%, and using the formula N = Z 2 άpq/[L] 2 (where Z = 1.96, p-30.6 q-69.4%,L-5, 20% non-response rate), the final sample size included was 430.A simple random sampling method using computer-generated random number was used to select the study samples.


Study period

This study was carried out for a period of six months (March 2024 to August 2024).


Study tool

Data was collected using a semi-structured questionnaire.The questionnaire consisted of baseline details, body mass index (BMI).Social media addiction was assessed using the Bergen social media scale.A body shape questionnaire (34-item) was used to assess the body image and dissatisfaction among the study participants.


Data analysis

Excel and SPSS version 22 were used for data analysis.Frequency, percentage, mean, and standard deviation were used to represent study variables.Chi-square test was used to test the association for univariate analysis, and a P value < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.Multivariate binomial logistic regression analysis was done using the enter method.


Ethical clearance and informed consent

Study was carried out after approval from Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref ID: VMKVMC and H/IEC/24/012), and written informed consent was obtained from all the study participants.


Operational definitions

1. Problematic social media usage [14] :

The Bergen social media addiction scale (BSMAS) was used to assess the problematic social media usage.It is a six-item scale rated on a five-point Likert scale (1 = very rarely to 5 = very often).If the total score is ≥19, it indicates problematic social media usage.2. Body Shape Concerns [13] :

The body shape questionnaire was used to assess the body shape concerns among the study population.It is a 34-item scale rated on a six-point Likert scale (1 = never to 6 = always).The scoring is as follows:

• <80-No concern with body shape • 80 to 110-Mild concerns with body shape • 111 to 140-Moderate concern with body shape • >140-Marked concern with body shape


Results

The mean age of the study participants was 21.43 ± 3.40 years and 67.1% were females.About 21.6% were first-year MBBS students, 15.6% were interns, and 9.3% were postgraduates.The majority, 60% of them, had normal BMI, and 31.9% were either obese or overweight in this study [Table 1].

Among the study participants, 56.5% were using social media for an average of 1-3 hours in a day and 41.6% were using 3-5 social media handles daily.Nearly 61.9% preferred Instagram, 55.3% preferred WhatsApp, 13.5% preferred Snapchat as the social media handle for regular use, and only 9.3% had a public social media account [Table 2].As per the Bergen social media addiction scale, more than 3/4 th (76.3%) of them were having problematic social media usage [Figure 1].

In this study, 63.8% of them had no concerns regarding the body shape, 23% had mild concerns, 8.4% had moderate concerns, and 4.9% had marked concerns regarding their body shape as per the BSQ scale [Figure 2].

Body shape-related concerns were significantly associated with female gender (P value-0.0006,OR-2.18), overweight/obese individuals (P value-0.0001,OR-2.42), and problematic social media usage (P value-<0.0001,OR-6.55) as per the univariate analysis [Table 3].

The variables significantly associated with body shape concerns in univariate analysis were included in the model for bivariate regression analysis.Overweight/obese individuals (P value-0.013,AOR-1.45) and problematic social media usage (P value-0.001,OR-2.69) were found to be significant in regression analysis [Table 4].

Conclusion

From the present study, it can be concluded that 76.3% had problematic social media usage and 36.2% were having body shape concerns.The prevalence of body shape concerns was found to be comparable to other Indian studies.Problematic social media usage was found to significantly influence body shape satisfaction.

Health programs should be conducted to increase the awareness among medical students, including health professionals, teachers, and parents who are the first point of contact for them, and to identify early cases of addiction and body shape and image-related concerns.It would be beneficial to offer counseling services and IEC materials about body image and associated taboos.They ought to be advised to refrain from talking about dieting, weight loss, body form, and even attractiveness in general.
