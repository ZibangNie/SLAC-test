Introduction

RecQ helicases belong to superfamily 2 (SF2) helicases that can unwind a wide spectrum of DNA structures with a 3 to 5 polarity [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].These helicases play a crucial role in resolving complex recombination and repair intermediates, thereby maintaining genome stability [ 2 , 8 , 9 ].These helicases can unwind diverse DNA substrates like duplex, triplex, and quadruplex as well as branched structures like Holliday Junctions.Almost all members of the RecQ family helicase have an identical catalytic core comprising two characteristic RecA folds, a zinc binding domain and a winged helix (WH) domain [ 1 , 2 ].Some members of the family like Esc heric hia coli RecQ, BLM, and WRN also harbor the HRDC domain [ 1 , 2 , 10 ].Although the members of this family share a common structural architecture, they widely vary in their substrate specificity and unwinding activities.A comparison of available crystal structures of different RecQ helicases from human and E. coli indicates strong structural similarities in their catalytic core, but there is a remarkable difference in the relative orientation of the domains [ 10 , 11 ].The WH domain of human RECQ1 is linearly positioned beneath the helical hairpin of the zinc domain, while in E. coli RecQ, the WH domain is perpendicular to the helicase core [ 11 ].A similar open conformation is observable in the nanobody bound BLM structure where the WH domain is projected away from the helicase domains [ 12 ].Moreover, the WH domain of these RecQ helicases is characterized by the presence of a β-hairpin that acts as a wedge element in DNA strand separation [ 11 ].However, there is a striking variation in the precise structure of the β-hairpin and amino acid composition at the tip of the β-hairpin [ 11 , 13 , 14 ].These apparent structural differences might play a critical role in determining substrate selectivity and unwinding processivity of the respective enzymes.

Among the five human RecQ helicases, RECQ1 is the smallest member of the family.Human RECQ1 shares considerable structural similarity with the E. coli RecQ helicase except for the presence of the HRDC domain in the later [ 11 ].In spite of structural similarity, the enzymes exhibit differences in substrate selectivity and processivity.While E. coli RecQ helicase can unwind blunt end duplex DNA, forked DNA, single strand-tailed duplex DNA, G-quadruplex DNA, and Holliday junction, human RECQ1 unwinds forked DNA, tailed duplex DNA, Holliday junction, and weakly D-loop substrates [15][16][17][18].Human RECQ1 structure in complex with DNA or with ADP (Adenosine diphosphate) reveals that in both cases, the enzyme remains in a closed state with the WH domain placed in line with the rest of the helicase core [ 11 , 19 ].In contrast, the bacterial RecQ can assume two different conformations in DNA unbound and bound states.While in the DNA unbound state, the WH domain is projected perpendicularly away from the helicase domains, the DNA bound state exhibits a closed conformation, as evident from Cronobacter sakazakii RecQ helicase structure [ 10 , 20 ].A similar two-state structural rearrangement is found in the nanobody bound and DNA-bound BLM helicase structures [ 12 ].Interestingly, upon DNA binding, both these helicases assume the closed state representing the human RECQ1 structure.The relative movement of the domains in bacterial RecQ or BLM [ 12 , 20 ] provides a reasonable two-state model for the unwinding mechanism but limits our understanding to explain the difference in substrate selectivity.Although RECQ1 has a similar structural architecture, it appears to be an exception, having a more rigid structure with no major conformational change upon DNA binding.This difference in conformational dynamics and relative domain flexibility among different RecQ helicases gives rise to a couple of fundamental questions regarding the substrate selectivity and unwinding mechanisms of these helicases.First, how different RecQ helicases with the similar structural organization can recognize and accommodate so diverse substrates with different complexity and different size?Second, is there a common basis for DNA binding and unwinding shared by all RecQ helicases, or they can vary in their unwinding mechanisms depending on their domain flexibility and different conformational requirements?

The DNA unwinding activity of helicases require an efficient coupling of DNA binding to ATP binding and hydrolysis.Both superfamily 1 (SF1) and superfamily 2 (SF2) helicases share conserved helicase motifs (I, Ia, II, III, IV , V , and VI) in their RecA-like domains [ 1 , 2 ].In SF1 helicases, the motif III is directly involved in DNA and ATP binding, while in SF2 helicases, the motif III is involved only in ATP hydrolysis [ 21 , 22 ].Some members of the SF2 family, such as RecQ helicases, are characterized by the presence of an aromatic-rich loop (ARL) immediately downstream of the motif II that has been implicated in coupling the ATP hydrolysis and DNA binding [ 23 , 24 ].Previous studies with E. coli RecQ and PriA DNA helicases demonstrate that the ARL acts as a coupling element that coordinates DNA binding and ATP hydrolysis to mediate unwinding [ 23 , 24 ].Although this coupling model establishes a direct connection between DNA binding and subsequent remodeling of the ATPase site, there is still a missing link as to how the ATPase domain communicates with the WH domain and drives strand separation.Measurement of the distance of the ARL from the tip of the β-hairpin in DNA bound or unbound structures of different RecQ helicases can provide significant insight into the modes of inter domain communication.There is considerable variation in the distance between the ARL and the tip of the β-hairpin in DNA unbound states of E. coli RecQ, BLM, and human RECQ1 [10][11][12].In E. coli RecQ, the distance between the ARL and β-hairpin tip is 21.8 Å, while in BLM, it is 63.7 Å, and in human RECQ1, the distance is 33.2 Å (Fig. 1 A, Supplementary Fig. S1 ).Interestingly, upon DNA binding, this distance changes to 34.8 Å in bacterial RecQ and 37.1 Å in BLM ( Supplementary Fig. S1A and  B ).On the contrary, there is no significant change in the distance in the case of human RECQ1 (Fig. 1 A).Notably, all the DNA bound states exhibit similar distances ranging from 33 to 37 Å and closely represent the DNA bound state of human RECQ1 (Fig. 1 A).We, therefore, hypothesize that the distance between the ARL and the tip of the β-hairpin might be a crucial determinant for inter-domain communication and unwinding activity of RecQ helicases.It is possible that since in bacterial RecQ or BLM, this distance can vary, both enzymes can accommodate a more diverse range of substrates than human RECQ1.Moreover, this distance might be important to determine the optimal trajectory of the ssDNA that extends from the tip of the β-hairpin to the D1 domain and, in turn, establish efficient coordination between the ATPase domain and the WH domain to facilitate unwinding.A structural rearrangement of the RQC domain in bacterial RecQ or BLM helps to achieve this optimal distance conformation, while in human RECQ1, no such rearrangement is possible, and hence, the distance remains fixed restricting its substrate selectivity.

Since no apparent structural rearrangement is observed for human RECQ1, we hypothesize that imparting some degree of structural flexibility might alter the substrate binding and selectivity of the enzyme.To test our hypothesis, we have engineered human RECQ1 by incorporating a glycine-serinerich flexible linker between the zinc binding domain and the WH domain.Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation predicted a reduction of the distance between the β-hairpin tip and the ARL by almost 7 Å.Interestingly, the engineered RECQ1 exhibited more efficient DNA unwinding activity and improved coupling of nucleotide to DNA binding.Crystal structure of the protein in complex with a tailed duplex DNA revealed a more compact structure with reduced distance between the β-hairpin and the ARL, thus providing direct evidence in support of our hypothesis.Mutational analysis of DNA binding residues in the ARL confirmed that efficient coordination between the ARL and WH domain is essential for driving the unwinding activity of RECQ1.Overall, this study provides significant insight into the functional mechanism of RECQ1 and establishes a common basis for DNA transaction by RecQ helicases in general.


Materials and methods


Cloning and site-directed mutagenesis

The sequence corresponding to RECQ1 T1 (amino acid 49-616) was amplified from the cDNA clone of human RECQ1 (Origene Technologies) using gene-specific primers and cloned in pET28a as described earlier [ 25 ].For the construction of RECQ1 T1Lk , the sequence of WH (aa 481-616) and D1-D2-Zn (aa 49-480) were separately amplified using gene specific primers.The sequence corresponding to the linker (GGGGSGGGGS) was split into two halves.The sequence encoding the first half (GGGGS) was incorporated in the re-verse primer at position aa 480, such that the last two amino acids are coded by a Bam H1 site.The sequence for the other half (GGGGS) was included in the forward primer at position aa 481 with the Bam H1 site.The WH was initially cloned into pET28a vector at Bam HI and Xho I sites.This clone was further digested with Nde 1 and Bam H1 and used as a vector for D1-D2-Zn to reconstitute the linker.RECQ1 T1Lk , therefore, contains the sequence (GGGGSGGGGS) in between the Zn and the WH domains (aa 481-490).RECQ1 FLLk encompassing aa 1-649 was constructed adopting similar strategy using gene specific primers and RECQ1 FL as the template.

The long linker containing construct RECQ1 T1Lk4 (containing four repeats of GGGGS) was generated using RECQ1 T1Lk as the template, following a similar strategy with minor modification.The sequence of WH (aa 491-626) and D1-D2-Zn (aa 49-480) was separately amplified using genespecific primers from within the linker region.A sequence encoding the linker (GGGGSGGGGS) was incorporated in the reverse primer at position aa 488, such that the last two amino acids are coded by a Bam H1 site.The same sequence (GGGGSGGGGS) was also included in the forward primer at position aa 481 with a Bam H1 site.The primers were designed so as to incorporate a silent mutation at the internal Bam H1 site coding for GS (aa 484) in RECQ1 T1Lk .The final construct RECQ1 T1Lk4 contains the sequence (GGGGSGGGGSGGGGSGGGGS) in between the Zn and the WH domains (aa 481-500).The presence of linker and long linker were confirmed by sequencing.

The constructs, RECQ1 T12A and RECQ1 T1Lk2A (containing two mutations, D230A and R232A in the ARL), were generated by polymerase chain reaction-based mutagenesis using RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk (in pET28a) as template and mutagenic primers.The mutations were confirmed by sequencing.


Overexpression and purification of RECQ1 constructs

The construct RECQ1 T1 was purified by the method described in Mukhopadhyay et al., 2021 [ 25 ].The other constructs, RECQ1 T1Lk , RECQ1 T1Lk4 , RECQ1 FLLk , RECQ1 T12A , and RECQ1 T1Lk2A , were also purified following the same protocol with slight modifications.Briefly, E. coli Rosetta (DE3) cells expressing the constructs were grown in terrific broth (TB) containing 50 μg / ml kanamycin and 34 μg / ml chloramphenicol and were induced with 0.5 mM IPTG for 6 h at 22 • C and 120 rpm.Cells harvested after induction were resuspended in lysis buffer (50 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 500 mM NaCl, 10 mM MgCl 2 , 10 mM imidazole, 1 mM β-mercaptoethanol, and 1 mM phenylmethanesulfonylfluoride) and subjected to ultrasonication followed by centrifugation at 15 000 x g for 30 min at 4 • C. Nucleic acids and cell debris were removed by adding 0.15% polyethyleneimine (PEI) from a 5%, pH 7.5 stock solution, followed by centrifugation at 15 000 x g for 30 min at 4 • C. The supernatant was loaded onto HisTrap TM FF (GE Healthcare USA).The final elution of the protein was done with a buffer containing 300 mM imidazole.Only the cleanest fractions were further concentrated using Amicon Ultra (10kDa MWCO) and loaded onto HiLoad 16 / 600 Superdex 200 pg (GE Healthcare) pre-equilibrated with buffer A (50 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 500 mM NaCl,10 mM MgCl 2 , and 1 mM β-mercaptoethanol) The entire purification was done in GE ÄKTA Prime Plus FPLC system at 1 ml / min flow rate and at 4 • C. Fractions containing the protein were identified by 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS PAGE), combined and concentrated using centrifugal ultrafiltration (10kDa MWCO, Amicon Ultra), flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 • C for further use.RECQ1 FL was purified as described in Mukhopadhyay et al., 2021 [ 25 ].For crystallization, RECQ1 T1Lk was buffer exchanged to Buffer B (10 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 100 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl 2 , and 2 mM DTT).The proteins were con-centrated and used for crystallization on the same day without freezing.

Protein crystallization, structure determination, and refinement RECQ1 T1Lk crystallized in the presence of a DNA substrate with both 3 and 5 tails (27 / 13 mer) (Top strand: 5 -CGGT A TTGGA TCTCGACGCTCTCCCTT-3 , Bottom strand: 5 -A GCGTCGA GATCC-3 ) (sequence of oligonucleotide from 19 ) and non-hydrolysable ATP analog, AMPPNP.Initial crystallization hits were obtained by sitting drop vapor diffusion method at 25 • C from the Index screen (Hampton Research, USA).The two DNA strands were annealed by heating to 95 • C and then slowly cooling it down to 4 • C in a buffer containing 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.5) and 50 mM NaCl.Protein eluted from size-exclusion chromatography was concentrated to 5 μM and incubated with the annealed DNA at 1: 1.2 (protein: DNA) molar ratio and AMPPNP at two-fold molar ratio for 5 h.The protein was further concentrated to 8 mg ml -1 and was mixed 1:1 (v / v) with reservoir solution containing 0.2 M sodium malonate pH 7.0 and 20% w / v polyethylene glycol 3350.Diffraction quality crystals were grown using the hanging drop vapor diffusion method at 25 • C, by mixing 1.2 μl of protein solution with 1.2 μl of reservoir solution containing 0.2 M sodium malonate pH 7.0 and 22% w / v polyethylene glycol 3350.Plate-like crystals appeared after 72 h of incubation.The crystals were mounted in a nylon loop and cryoprotected with 20% (v / v) glycerol in the mother solution and subsequently flash-cooled in liquid nitrogen.Diffraction data were collected at the synchrotron beamline facility PX-BL21 at RR CA T (Indore, India) and recorded with a MarCCD detector.A total of 180 frames of data with an oscillation range of 1 • , an exposure time of 45 s per frame and a crystal-to-detector distance of 220 mm were collected at a wavelength of 0.979 Å. Crystals diffracted to a maximum resolution of 2.43 Å.The data were processed with XDS [ 26 ].The space group was determined using POINTLESS [ 27 ].The data were further processed using autoPROC (Global phasing) [ 28 , 29 ] and STARANISO [ 29 ] pipeline.The diffraction data were isotropically and anisotropically scaled using AIMLESS [ 30 ] and STARANISO, respectively, both within the autoPROC data processing pipeline.STARANISO produced a best resolution limit of 2.43 Å for an I / σ(I) cut off of 1.2.RECQ1 T1Lk crystallized in P2 1 space group, with two proteins and one DNA molecule per asymmetric unit.The structure was solved by molecular replacement using PHASER [ 31 ], with the structure of RECQ1 T1 (PDB accession code: 2WWY) as the search model.Further refinement and chain fitting were done in PHENIX [ 32 ] and COOT [ 33 ].Prior to refinement, 5% of the reflections were set aside.The refinement strategy involved the refinement of atomic coordinates, occupancy refinement, TLS (Translation / Libration / Screw) refinement and simulated annealing.The RECQ1 T1Lk / DNA complex crystallized in two distinct orientations, resulting in a symmetry operator aligning the DNA onto itself within the asymmetric unit.To avoid clashes between symmetry-related molecules, refinement was performed by setting the occupancy of the overlapping DNA atoms to 0.5.Validation of the stereochemistry was done using the program PROCHECK [ 34 ] and MOLPROBITY [ 35 ].The atomic coordinates and structure factors have been deposited in the Protein Data Bank with a PDB ID of 8YRS (Table 1 ).


Preparation of the DNA substrates

All oligonucleotides used in assays and crystallization were obtained from Integrated DNA Technologies, USA (listed in Supplementary Table S1 ).For radioactive gel-based unwinding assay, the substrates were 5 end labeled with γ 32 P-ATP using T4 polynucleotide kinase (NEB) at 37 • C for 1 h and were further purified using QIAquick nucleotide removal kit (QIAGEN).The labeled oligonucleotide was annealed to a 3fold molar excess of the respective unlabeled complementary oligonucleotide in an annealing buffer (20 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5 and 50 mM NaCl) by heating at 95 • C for 5 min and then cooling slowly to room temperature.For the fluorescence anisotropy titrations, oligonucleotide labeled at 5 end with 6-FAM was used.For preparation of the double-stranded DNA substrates, unlabeled fully or partially complementary oligonucleotide was added to the labeled oligonucleotide in equimolar concentrations and heated at 95 • C for 5 min and then cooled slowly to room temperature.Substrates for ATP hydrolysis experiment were prepared using equimolar concentration of both DNA strands and annealed using a protocol same as above.


DNA unwinding assays

Unwinding assays were performed in a reaction mixture containing unwinding buffer (20 mM Tris HCl pH 7.5, 5 mM MgCl 2 , 1 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 25 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, and 80 μg / ml bovine serum albumin) and 32 P-labeled duplex DNA (1nM).The reaction was initiated by the addition of 5 mM ATP (RECQ1 T1 , RECQ1 T1Lk , RECQ1 T1Lk4 , RECQ1 FL , and RECQ1 FLLk ) or 10 mM ATP (RECQ1 T12A , RECQ1 T1Lk2A ).Proteins were added in increasing concentrations, and the mixture was incubated at 37 • C for 30 min.The reaction was terminated by the addition of a stop solution (100 mM EDTA pH 8, 0.5% SDS, 5% glycerol, and 0.1% bromophenol blue).Reaction products were resolved using 15% or 20% native PAGE in 0.5X Tris Borate EDTA buffer.The gel was run at 4 • C and visualized by phosphorimaging using Typhoon FLA7000 [ 25 , 36 ].Quantification of gel images for density estimation were done using the Image J 1.52 [ 37 ], and the percentage of DNA unwound was calculated for each lane.The data shown represent the mean of three independent experiments, with error bars indicating standard deviation.

The binding of different DNA substrates to RECQ1 T1 , RECQ1 T1Lk , RECQ1 T12A , and RECQ1 T1Lk2A was studied by equilibrium fluorescence anisotropy titrations [ 38 ].All the anisotropy titration experiments were carried out in a binding buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH = 7.5), 20 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl 2 , and 0.1 mM β-ME at 37 • C in the presence or absence of 2 mM ADPNP. 10 nM of 5 6-FAM labeled substrates were titrated using varying enzyme concentrations in a Fluoromax-4 fluorimeter (Horiba).The excitation and emission wavelengths were fixed at 490 and 518 nm, respectively.The fluorescence anisotropy values were fitted to quadratic function describing 1:1 binding (equation 1) using Origin 8.5 and dissociation constants (K d ) of the enzyme / DNA complex were determined.

Steady-state ATPase assay

The steady state ATP hydrolysis activity was determined using a coupled ATPase assay and measured as a decrease in A 340 corresponding to the oxidation of NADH to NAD + [ 36 , 38 ].The reaction was carried out in the presence of saturating concentration of substrates in a buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 10 mM MgCl 2 , 0.1 mM β-ME, 0.4 mM phosphoenol pyruvate, 0.2 mM NADH, 23 mg / ml lactate dehydrogenase and 37 mg / ml pyruvate kinase, and 20 mM NaCl at increasing ATP concentrations.The initial velocity, v (in mM ATP s -1 ), was calculated from a change in absorbance A 340 / t with ε 340 , NADH = 6220 M -1 cm -1 and was converted to k cat .The values obtained were fitted to Michaelis-Menten equation using Origin 8.5, and K M for ATP was determined.Each data point represent mean of three independent experiments with standard deviation indicated.


MD simulation

MD simulations were carried out for 0.5 μs in the presence of DNA for both RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk .The model for RECQ1 T1Lk was built using the structure of RECQ1 T1 (PDB ID: 2WWY) as the template in Modeller 10.1 software [ 39 ].The topology and the coordinate files for the systems were built using the LEaP module of Ambertools21 [ 40 ].The interaction parameters were described by the force fields ff14SB for protein [ 41 ], ParmBSC1 for DNA [ 42 ] and ZAFF for the zinc metal ion [ 43 ].The topology files were generated in AMBER and then converted to the GROMACS format using external utilities like acpype tools [ 44 ].TIP3P waters were used for the solvation [ 45 ] in cubic box.The systems were subjected to energy minimization using the steepest descent algorithm [ 46 ].All systems were equilibrated at a constant temperature, 300 K and constant pressure, 1 bar by utilizing the two-step ensemble process (NVT and NPT) for 100 ps.The simulations were performed using GROMACS 2020 [ 47 ].The time step employed was 2 fs, and coordinates were saved every 10 ps for analysis.Analysis was done using GROMACS suit of packages and local scripts.


Results


MD simulation predicts domain motions upon insertion of a flexible linker between zinc and WH domains of human RECQ1

A careful inspection of the crystal structures of bacterial RecQ, BLM, and human RECQ1 reveals a common closed conformation in the DNA bound state shared by all the three helicases where the WH domain is placed linearly beneath the zinc binding domain.Interestingly, the DNA unbound states of the E. coli RecQ and BLM strikingly vary from that of RECQ1 in the relative positioning of the WH domain [10][11][12].As a result of domain rearrangement, the distance between the ARL and the tip of the β-hairpin changes significantly in DNA-bound and -unbound states of bacterial RecQ and BLM ( Supplemantary Fig. S1A and B ).This distance is nearly fixed in both DNA bound (33.4 Å) and unbound (33.2 Å) states of RECQ1, indicating a structural rigidity of the enzyme (Fig. 1 A).We hypothesize that this distance might be a critical determinant for coordination between the ATPase and WH domains and for efficient unwinding activity.To test this hypothesis, we took a unique approach of incorporating a flexible linker between the zinc and WH domains of RECQ1 that might impart some degree of flexibility in the RQC domain, which is otherwise rigid.We first tested our idea in silico by inserting artificial linkers of varying lengths [ 48 , 49 ] that comprise of serine / glycine residues (repeats of GGGGS).Using the available crystal structure of human RECQ1 T1 (PDB ID: 2WWY), we generated the model for the engineered protein RECQ1 T1Lk with the artificial linker (GGGGSGGGGS) inserted between the residues K480 and D481.It may be noted that the incorporation of the 10 amino acids long linker resulted in a change in residue numbering starting from residue D481 (D491 in RECQ1 T1Lk ).We then performed MD simulation of RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk for 0.5 μs in the presence of a tailed duplex DNA (sequence from 19 ).All the simulations were performed using GROMACS 2020.Clustering algorithm was used to identify and categorize distinct conformational states sampled during the simulation.Clustering analysis of RECQ1 T1Lk simulation trajectory identified a predominant conformation characterized by an overall compaction of the structure due to inward drift of the D1 and WH domains.Interestingly, the structural compaction resulted in a decrease in the distance between the tip of the β-hairpin and the ARL residues (Fig. 1 B), reducing it from 33.4 to 27.1 Å (between Y574 and W227).To understand the domain motions, we used PC analysis and extracted the extreme conformations along the first eigenvectors for both RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk .A comparison of the extreme structures for RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk reveals significant domain movements for the latter, as indicated by the integrated mode vectors (Fig. 1 C and D).Interestingly, the WH domain exhibits a drift of 5.9 Å in the case of RECQ1 T1Lk , whereas the WH of RECQ1 T1 was restricted to the initial position, showing no drift (Fig. 1 C).The results from the MD simulation studies provide the initial proof of concept and further supports our hypothesis that insertion of a flexible linker might change domain motions resulting in a reduction of the distance between the β-hairpin and the ARL.To test the effect of linker length on domain motions, we also constructed a model with a longer linker containing four repeats of GGGGS incorporated between residues K480 and D481 (RECQ1 T1Lk4 ).Similar results were obtained from clustering analysis, and no significant reduction of the distance (for RECQ1 T1Lk4 ) was observed as compared to RECQ1 T1Lk ( Supplementary Fig. S2 ).We, therefore, chose to construct RECQ1 T1Lk and characterize the engineered protein structurally and functionally to validate our hypothesis.


Insertion of flexible linker alters substrate selectivity and unwinding activity of human RECQ1

DNA-induced rearrangement of the WH domain might be the key reason for the wide range of substrate processing by E. coli RecQ and BLM, while the structural rigidity of RECQ1 restricts it to a narrow spectrum of substrates.How this domain repositioning can actually improve substrate selectivity and processivity in E. coli RecQ or BLM and why would RECQ1 have limited substrate selectivity in spite of having a similar domain organization remains unclear.We, therefore, inserted a flexible linker between the Zn and WH domains of RECQ1 (Fig. 2 A) so as to impart local flexibility in the RQC domain [ 48 , 49 ].We expect that this local flexibility might impart some domain motions, leading to an alteration of the distance between ARL and β-hairpin that might affect enzyme functioning.The RECQ1 T1 construct containing the (GGGGS) 2 linker between Zn and WH domains was constructed as described in "Materials and methods."RECQ1 T1Lk was purified using Ni 2+ -NTA affinity and gel filtration chromatography, and the protein was purified as a dimer similar to RECQ1 T1 (Fig. 2 B and C).We tested the unwinding activity of RECQ1 T1Lk on different DNA substrates like tailed duplex and blunt duplex DNA.Interestingly, RECQ1 T1Lk was more efficient in unwinding a 3 tailed duplex with 11 base ssDNA overhang.While 50% unwinding of the duplex was observed at 4.5 nM RECQ1 T1 concentration, the RECQ1 T1Lk can unwind almost 70% of the duplex at 1.25 nM (Fig. 2 D  and H).Surprisingly, RECQ1 T1Lk was able to unwind a 20 mer blunt duplex, which is not a preferred substrate for human RECQ1.As evident from Fig. 2 E, the RECQ1 T1Lk can unwind more than 80% of the blunt duplex substrate at 80 nM concentration whereas RECQ1 T1 exhibits a maximum of 50% unwinding even at 100 nM concentration (Fig. 2 E and  G).This result is particularly important because blunt duplex is a preferred substrate for E. coli RecQ helicase and not for human RECQ1.We hypothesize that local domain motions in the RQC due to linker incorporation might be responsible for duplex fraying and initiating strand separation in RECQ1 T1Lk , a phenomenon similar to E. coli RecQ.If the distance between the tip of the β-hairpin and ARL is crucial for driving unwinding, shortening of the single stranded region of the tailed duplex might affect the unwinding activity of RECQ1 assuming that the enzyme structure is rigid and does not allow any domain rearrangement.It is easy to imagine that if the ssDNA / dsDNA junction is contacted by the tyrosine residue at the tip of the β-hairpin [ 19 ], then the ssDNA tail must be long enough to cover a distance of 33 Å and reach the ARL.On the contrary, if the predicted motions for RECQ1 T1Lk are true, a shorter ss tail should be able to contact the ARL and facilitate efficient coordination between the domains to initiate strand separation.We therefore used a 3 tailed duplex with a 4-base single-stranded overhang.As expected, the wild type RECQ1 T1 exhibits very feeble or no unwinding of the shortened duplex, even at 200 nM enzyme concentration.Interestingly, RECQ1 T1Lk could unwind more than 50% of the shortened tailed substrate at 150 nM concentration (Fig. 2 F and G).This result provides the first direct evidence in support of our hypothesis that the distance between the ARL and the β-hairpin is a critical determinant for inter-domain communication.

Our MD simulation studies suggested that increasing the length of the linker (four repeats of GGGGS) can also induce similar conformational change leading to reduction in the distance between the β-hairpin and the ARL.In order to test the effect of linker length variation on unwinding activity we repeated all the unwinding experiments with the longer linker construct RECQ1 T1Lk4 .Our results suggest that RECQ1 T1Lk4 can also unwind all the three substrates with similar efficiency as the RECQ1 T1Lk ( Supplementary Fig. S3 ).Similar to RECQ1 T1Lk , the RECQ1 T1Lk4 was highly active on 3 tailed duplex and exhibit more than 70% unwinding at 2nM enzyme concentration ( Supplementary Fig. S3B  and C ).The long linker containing construct RECQ1 T1Lk4 can also efficiently unwind the short tail containing substrate showing almost 80% unwinding at 200 nM concentration ( Supplementary Fig. S3E and F ).This further strengthens our hypothesis and validates our prediction from the MD simulation studies with the construct containing longer linker.To further validate our hypothesis, we inserted the linker (GGGGSGGGGS) between the Zn and WH domains of the full length RECQ1 (RECQ1 FLLk ).Interestingly, the purified RECQ1 FLLk also exhibit efficient unwinding of all the three DNA substrates ( Supplementary Fig. S4 ).While RECQ1 FL (wild type) can moderately unwind the 3 tailed duplex, the RECQ1 FLLk exhibit very efficient unwinding with almost 80% unwinding completed at 1.5 nM enzyme concentration ( Supplementary Fig. S4C and D ).The RECQ1 FLLk can also reasonably unwind the blunt duplex and the shortened tailed substrate while the wild type enzyme RECQ1 FL cannot unwind these substrates ( Supplementary Fig. S4E -H ).Our results clearly indicate that the effect of the linker insertion on altered unwinding activities is consistent among all variants of the enzyme and hence our hypothesis can be extrapolated for the full length RECQ1 as well.


Insertion of flexible linker improves DNA binding by RECQ1 in presence of nucleotide

Previous works from the Keck group with E. coli RecQ and PriA helicases demonstrated that the ARL acts as a coupling element that couples ATP hydrolysis and DNA binding, which is essential for driving unwinding [ 23 , 24 ].This would mean that the pre-hydrolytic conformation of the ATP bound state of the enzyme is likely to favor more cooperative binding of the DNA substrate.For enzymes like RECQ1, where domain rearrangement is minimal, substrates with short 3 ss tail or no overhangs will not be able to contact the ARL efficiently, and hence, they are likely to exhibit less or poor cooperativity in DNA and nucleotide binding.This might explain why the wild type RECQ1 T1 exhibits poor or no unwinding of the blunt duplex and the short-tailed duplex.On the contrary, RECQ1 T1Lk , which can unwind blunt duplex and short tailed duplexes, might have an improved nucleotide coupled DNA binding.MD simulation of the RECQ1 T1Lk suggested a drift in the D1 and WH domains, leading to a reduction of the overall inter-domain distance by almost 7 Å.It is possible that this re-duced inter-domain distance helps the DNA to reach the ARL, and hence, there is better coupling of ATP and DNA binding (Fig. 3 A and B).MD simulation studies with RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk in complex with DNA indicate that the 25 th base (dC25) at the ssDNA end can comfortably make polar contact with the Asp 230 on the ARL of RECQ1 T1Lk while in RECQ1 T1 the dC25 cannot contact the ARL residues (Fig. 3 A and B).Hence, the distance between ARL and β-hairpin might act as a regulatory factor for ssDNA binding trajectory.If this argument stands correct, RECQ1 T1Lk should exhibit better cooperativity in DNA and ATP binding as compared to RECQ1 T1 .We therefore performed DNA binding experiments using fluorescence anisotropy in the presence and absence of non-hydrolysable ATP analog, ADPNP.RECQ1 T1Lk indeed exhibits better cooperative DNA binding in the presence of ADPNP for all three substrate types.There is a 4-fold reduction in the K d for DNA binding for RECQ1 T1Lk to the 3 tailed duplex (with 10 base ssDNA overhang) in the presence of ADPNP (92 to 23 nM), indicating a cooperative binding (Fig. 3 C right).For the same substrate, there is not even a 2-fold reduction of the K d for RECQ1 T1 in the presence of ADPNP (131 to 73 nM, Fig. 3 C left).More interesting results were obtained for the short-tailed duplex and blunt duplex (Fig. 3 D and E).The wild-type RECQ1 T1 binds to the blunt duplex with the same affinity (0.57 μM) in the absence or presence of ADPNP, indicating no cooperativity (Fig. 3 E left).RECQ1 T1Lk binds to the blunt duplex with a 3-fold higher affinity in the presence of ADPNP (0.26 μM) than in the absence of nucleotide (0.76 μM, Fig. 3 E right).A similar cooperative behavior is observed for the substrate with four base ssDNA tail.While no cooperativity is observed in the case of RECQ1 T1 (Fig. 3 D left), there is more than a 3-fold reduction in the K d for RECQ1 T1Lk (Fig. 3 D right) binding to the shorttailed duplex (0.34 μM in absence and 97 nM in presence of ADPNP).All the dissociation constants are listed in Table 2 .These results clearly indicate that insertion of the linker leads to improvement of nucleotide coupled DNA binding, which is possible only if the DNA can contact the ARL properly and remodels the ATP binding cleft, which in turn improves DNA binding.This again strengthens our argument that efficient DNA mediated coordination between the ARL and WH is essential for the unwinding activity of RECQ1, and hence the distance between the ARL and the β-hairpin is a crucial factor for substrate selectivity and binding.

contribute to better coupling of DNA to ADPNP binding.Moreover, the ARL in RECQ1 T1Lk structure reorients and moves by 3.2 Å toward the D2 domain.Reorientation of the ARL and its stabilization by extra interactions at the interface might account for better DNA binding and facilitate its coordination with WH domain.


Rearrangement of the ATPase domain and the DNA-binding trajectory

The DNA binding and unwinding results demonstrate that the engineered construct RECQ1 T1Lk has a better coupling of DNA and ATP binding, which, in turn, led to improved strand separation.MD simulation and crystal structure data indicate that there is drift in the WH domain due to the insertion of the linker, leading to a reduction of distance between ARL and the β-hairpin.It is unclear that if the domain motions in the RQC domain can actually help in better coordination with the D1 domain and, if so, whether that leads to the rearrangement of the ATPase domain.A more careful inspection of the crystal structure of RECQ1 T1Lk can provide feasible explanations.Surface representation and comparison of the structures of RECQ1 T1Lk and RECQ1 T1 indicate remarkable changes in the dimer interface and a significant reduction in the volume of the ATP binding cleft ( Supplementary Fig. S6 ).Superimposition of the structures of RECQ1 T1Lk and RECQ1 T1 in complex with DNA indicates a prominent rearrangement of the D1 domain (Fig. 5 A) that can provide significant insight with respect to altered DNA binding and unwinding activities.The ATP binding cleft is narrowed in RECQ1 T1Lk due to an inward movement of a loop containing the conserved Leu 89 in motif Q by 1.9 Å.The α-helix3 (residues 95-105) located next to the motif Q is also drifted inward by 2.1 Å ( Supplementary Fig. S7 A and B).These movements in the ATP binding cleft result in a significant reduction of the cleft volume from 2383.7 Å 3 (in RECQ1 T1 bound to DNA) to 1181.5 Å 3 (in RECQ1 T1Lk ).This raises the possibility that the ADPNP might be actually bound to RECQ1 T1Lk , although the electron density for the same could not be traced.The inward movement of the α-helix3 induces rearrangement of the glycine rich loop immediately downstream and thereby pulls the catalytic lysine (K119) inward more toward motif II.The catalytic Lys 119 has a backbone drift of 1.7 Å and moves more toward Glu 220 in motif II, reducing the distance from 6.5 Å in RECQ1 T1 to 4.1 Å in RECQ1 T1Lk structure (Fig. 5 B).The motif II residues and the catalytic Lys 119 is known to make water mediated contact with a Mg 2+ ion and helps in ATP hydrolysis.Another significant remodeling of this domain involves inward movement of the motif III residues more toward the motif VI located on the D2 domain.As a result, the distance between the Thr 256 on motif III and Gln 400 on motif VI reduces to 4.6 Å in RECQ1 T1Lk as compared to 7 Å in RECQ1 T1 structure (Fig. 5 C).A motif III / motif VI interaction may be implicated in RECQ1 / DNA complex bringing the two lobes of ATPase domain closer resulting in narrowing of the cleft.A local rearrangement of the ATPase domain is suggestive of a catalytically active conformation that is probably stabilized by DNA contacts to the ARL, similar to as observed in the case of C. sakazakii RecQ helicase.

Although the electron density of the DNA chain could be traced only until the 25 th base, we modeled the last two bases (dT-p-dT) in RECQ1 T1Lk to get an idea as to how the ss-DNA end contacts the ARL ( Supplementary Fig. S7C ).The 3 end of the ssDNA in the RECQ1 T1Lk structure makes extensive contacts with the ARL (Fig. 5 D).While in RECQ1 T1 , the last base (dT 27 was modeled) reaches the Asp 230, in the case of RECQ1 T1Lk the last base can comfortably reach the His 229.In the RECQ1 T1Lk structure, the phosphate between dT26 and dT27 makes H bond contact with the backbone carboxyl of His 229 and also interacts with Asp 230.The phosphate between dT25 and dT26 makes extensive contacts with Arg 232 and Asp 230 on the ARL (Fig. 5 D and Supplementary Fig. S7D ).These extra interactions with the ARL definitely provide increased stability to the ssDNA 3 end in RECQ1 T1Lk and hence directly support our findings from the DNA binding experiments.Interestingly, the Trp 227 flips inward by 4.1 Å toward the 3 hydroxyl end of the ssDNA, raising a possibility that there can be salt bridge formation, leading to further stabilization of the DNA end.This is clearly not the case with RECQ1 T1 , where the Trp 227 is flipped away from the DNA although the interactions with D230 and R232 are conserved (Fig. 5 E).When we traced the entire ssDNA trajectory, we found the interactions in D1 and D2 domains are fairly conserved with a few minor changes.One interesting finding is that the phosphate between dC19 and dT20 makes a hydrogen bond with the Lys 325 on the D2 domain ( Supplementary Fig. S7 D).This interaction is absent in the RECQ1 T1 structure in complex with same DNA duplex.The ssDNA trajectory shows a clear bump on the WH side as if the DNA is pushed from the RQC end ( Supplementary Fig. S7C ).The extra contact with Lys 325 is probably due to this compression and helps in stabilizing the ssDNA bubble immediately next to the branch point.The domain motions induced in D1 and WH domains lead to reduced distance between the ARL and β-hairpin, thereby pushing it from the RQC end and allowing its better interaction with the ARL.A shorter ssDNA tail would be expected to follow a more stretched trajectory along the D2 and D1 domains, with the 3 end reaching the ARL comfortably.The structural rearrangement of the ATP binding cleft in the D1 domain and altered modes of DNA binding in RECQ1 T1Lk clearly argue in favor of the hypothesis that the distance between the tip of β-hairpin and the ARL is a critical determinant for DNA recognition and unwinding.Any change in this distance resulting from domain movements is likely to affect interdomain communication, which in turn can positively or negatively regulate the coupling of DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis and subsequent unwinding activity of RECQ1.


Coordination between the ARL and the WH domain is essential for driving the unwinding activity of RECQ1

The crystal structure of RECQ1 T1Lk proposes an altered conformation of the ATP binding cleft that might be a result of efficient DNA binding and rearrangement of the ARL.The DNA trajectory in RECQ1 T1Lk remains fairly conserved with the 3 end of the ssDNA having extensive interactions with His 229, Asp 230 and Arg 232.Interestingly, a Trp at position 227 flips by 4.1 Å toward the 3 hydroxyl end of the DNA.It is possible that the 3 hydroxyl of the ss-DNA end can contact this Trp 227 via salt bridge interaction, thereby further stabilizing the ARL-DNA interaction.These interactions of the ssDNA end with His 229 or Trp 227 are not present in the case of wild-type RECQ1 T1 [ 19 ].We hypothesize that the domain motions in RQC and subsequent reduction of distance between the ARL and β-hairpin are primarily responsible for extended interaction of the ss-DNA end with ARL.If that is correct, then mutation of the conserved ARL residues, Asp 230 and Arg 232, might have varying effects on unwinding activities of RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk .We, therefore, mutated Asp 230 and Arg 232 to alanine (D230A, R232A) in both RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk .The mutants are named RECQ1 T12A and RECQ1 T1Lk2A , respectively, indicating the mutation of two amino acids to alanine (Fig. 6 A).Both the mutants were purified to homogeneity (Fig. 6 B) and tested for their unwinding activity on all three substrates.

The mutant RECQ1 T1Lk2A exhibits almost 35% unwinding of 3 tailed duplex at 100 nM enzyme concentration, while very feeble or no unwinding was observed for RECQ1 T12A even at 500 nM concentration (Fig. 6 C).More striking results were observed for the short tailed (4 base ssDNA overhang) and blunt duplex substrates.Unwinding was abolished for both the substrates in the case of RECQ1 T12A , while the mutant RECQ1 T1Lk2A containing the linker still retains unwinding activity.The linker containing protein RECQ1 T1Lk2A bearing mutations on ARL is capable of unwinding the shorttailed duplex starting at 100 nM and 35% of the duplex is unwound at 500 nM concentration.No unwinding could be detected for the ARL mutant of RECQ T1 even at 500 nM (Fig. 6 D).The ARL mutant containing the linker (RECQ1 T1Lk2A ) is also capable of unwinding the blunt duplex at high concentrations (500 nM) while no unwinding is detectable for RECQ1 T12A (Fig. 6 E).These differences in unwinding activity prompted us to check the DNA stimulated steady state ATP hydrolysis activity for both the mutants in presence of the respective DNA substrates.Interestingly, both RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk have similar K M, ATP in the presence of all three substrates ( Supplementary Table S3 ).Only in the presence of the blunt duplex there is a 2-fold difference in the k cat / K M ratio, indicating more efficient ATP hydrolysis by RECQ1 T1Lk , although there is no difference in affinity for ATP.The ARL mutants for both RECQ1 T1Lk and RECQ1 T1 turned out to be catalytically less efficient compared to the respective wildtype enzymes, having k cat / K M values in the same range.The Das et al. k cat / K M values reduce to 11.2 and 17.6 mM -1 s -1 for ARL mutants (RECQ1 T12A and RECQ1 T1Lk2A ) from initial values of 60 and 68 mM -1 s -1 for RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk , respectively, in the presence of 3´tail duplex (Fig. 6 F and G).Similar reductions in k cat / K M values were observed for the short tail and blunt duplex stimulated ATPase in case of both the ARL mutants (Fig. 6 F and G).Similarity in the K M, ATP values for both RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk indicate that there is no significant change in ATP binding, although the enzymes vary in their DNA binding and unwinding properties ( Supplementary Table S3 ).An equivalent reduction of k cat / K M values for both the ARL mutants demonstrate that the mutations D230A and R232A affect ATP binding and hydrolysis to the same extent in both mutants.Since the ARL is known to couple DNA and ATP binding in RecQ helicases, mutation of the conserved DNA binding residues affects ATP hydrolysis for both mutants to the same extent.But the difference in their unwinding activities indicates that ATP hydrolysis is not linearly correlated to the unwinding activity of RECQ1.Hence, we hypothesize that efficient ATP hydrolysis is not the only criterion for DNA unwinding activity; rather, efficient DNA binding and proper coordination between the ARL and WH domain is required for the helicase activity.

To assess the effect of the mutations on DNA binding ability, we measured the DNA binding of the mutant proteins in the presence of ADPNP.Both RECQ1 T1Lk2A and RECQ1 T12A bind to the 3 tailed duplex (with 10 base overhang) in the presence of ADPNP with K d of 97 and 92 nM, respectively (Fig. 6 H and I).This is expected since the 10-base ssDNA overhang can still contact the other residues of ARL in both mutants.Interestingly, no DNA binding was detectable for RECQ1 T12A (ARL mutant of RECQ1 T1 ) with the short tail and blunt duplex substrates, and K d could not be determined.The mutant RECQ1 T1Lk2A binds to the short-tailed duplex with a K d of 0.92 μM and to the blunt duplex with a K d of 0.57 μM (Table 2 , Fig. 6 H and I).Mutation of both the conserved DNA binding residues results in loss of interaction of the ssDNA end with the ARL in the case of RECQ1 T12A , affecting the DNA binding drastically for shorter duplex.In contrast, the ARL of RECQ1 T1Lk2A can still contact the ssDNA end probably via His 229 and Trp 227, resulting in weakened but significant DNA binding with the short-tailed duplex as well as blunt duplex.These results indicate that efficient DNA binding to the ARL is essential to communicate with the WH domain and couple the ATP hydrolysis to unwinding activity.Mutational analysis of the ARL therefore confirms that the extra interactions at the 3 end of the ssDNA are significant in contacting the ARL efficiently.Results from ATPase assay, DNA binding and unwinding assay collectively indicate that ATP hydrolysis, as well as efficient DNA binding to the ARL and the WH domains, is essential to drive unwinding activity.


Discussion

RecQ helicases are superfamily 2 (SF2) helicases ubiquitously present in prokaryotes to higher eukaryotes and share a common helicase core comprising of two RecA domains, a zinc binding domain and a WH domain.Among the five human RecQ helicases, RECQ1 is the smallest, and its helicase core shares considerable structural similarity with that of E. coli RecQ.In spite of their structural similarities, the E. coli and human enzymes vary in their substrate selectivity and processivity.This difference in substrate selectivity can be partly explained on the basis of relative domain positioning observed in the crystal structures of E. coli and C. sakazakii RecQ helicases.Comparison of the DNA unbound and DNA bound structures of E. coli and C. sakazakii RecQ helicases indicated a rearrangement of the WH domain, achieving a closed conformation.This rearrangement of the catalytic core requires a 90 • pivot-like movement of the WH, thereby securing the DNA.A similar rearrangement of the WH domain was also demonstrated in human BLM enzyme, where the WH domain is restructured upon DNA binding.Crystal structure of D. radiodurans RecQ also indicates a similar motion in the WH domain that is implicated in inter-domain communication and unwinding activity [ 51 , 52 ].Such conformational rearrangement and domain motion are absent in the human RECQ1, as evident from its DNA bound and unbound structures [ 11 , 19 ].Since RECQ1 does not undergo conformational rearrangements upon DNA binding, this domain remodeling mechanism cannot completely explain the substrate selectivity and unwinding mechanism of the enzyme.Moreover, the unwinding of DNA duplex essentially requires efficient coupling of DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis, which is possible only if the domains can communicate efficiently.An ARL in the D1 domain has been shown to couple the DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis in bacterial RecQ helicases [ 23 , 24 ].Significant allosteric remodeling of the ATPase domain has been shown to occur upon DNA binding in the bacterial helicases, which is thought to promote DNA induced ATP hydrolysis and initiate unwinding.Nucleic acid dependent remodeling of ATPase active site has also been reported for both SF1 and SF2 helicases like PcrA and NS3 helicases [ 53 , 54 ].Unfortunately, available crystal structures of RECQ1 do not exhibit similar restructuring of the ATPase domain upon DNA binding.Hence, the question remains how the human RECQ1 manage to perform unwinding without such conformational rearrangements that prominently exists in other members of the family.Is there a different mechanism altogether that helps in coordinating DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis by RECQ1, or a common mechanism of DNA binding and unwinding is shared by all RecQ helicases and is also operative in RECQ1 that is not fully understood yet?

A careful observation of the available crystal structures indicates that the distance between the ARL residues and the β-hairpin varies in different members of the RecQ family due to the varying positions of their WH domains [ 10 , 12 , 20 ].Interestingly, a DNA induced conformational change, repositions the WH domain of bacterial RecQ or BLM [ 12 , 20 ] such that the distance between the ARL and the β-hairpin is in the range of 33-37 Å, which is very similar to that in case of human RECQ1.We therefore hypothesize that the distance between the ARL and the tip of the β-hairpin is important for inter-domain communication and is a crucial determinant of substrate recognition, ATP hydrolysis and unwinding activities of RecQ helicases.To prove this hypothesis, we attempted to engineer the human RECQ1 protein to impart more flexibility in the RQC domain so as to alter the distance between the ARL and the β-hairpin and investigate its effect on substrate recognition, unwinding activity and ATPase domain remodeling.

In this study we have engineered a minimal functional human RECQ1 (RECQ1 T1 ) by inserting a flexible linker between the zinc binding and WH domains so as to impart more flexibility in the RQC domain [ 48 , 49 ].MD simulations with the engineered construct indicated increased domain motions in the RQC as well as the helicase domain, leading to a reduction of the distance between the tip of the β-hairpin and the ARL by almost 7Å.In vitro unwinding experiments indicate that the engineered construct RECQ1 T1Lk exhibits more efficient unwinding activity when compared to the wild type RECQ1 T1 .Interestingly, RECQ1 T1Lk can also unwind a blunt duplex, which is not a preferred substrate for human RECQ1.We hypothesize that domain motion induced due to insertion of the linker induces local fraying of the duplex, and a short stretch of ssDNA is sufficient to reach the ARL, thereby initiating unwinding of the duplex.Since the ss / ds DNA branch point is always bound to the tip of the β-hairpin, the length of the ssDNA overhang must be sufficiently long to cover the distance and contact the ARL efficiently.In wild type enzyme, a 6-7 base overhang is required to complete the trajectory and reach the ARL [ 19 ].If insertion of the linker can indeed reduce the distance between the β-hairpin and the ARL, a shorter ssDNA overhang must be sufficient to reach the ARL.RECQ1 T1Lk efficiently unwinds the short-tailed duplex, while the wild type RECQ1 T1 fails to unwind the substrate even at very high concentrations thereby supporting our hypothesis.A comparison of the unwinding activities reveal that RECQ1 T1Lk exhibits distinct preference for the blunt or the tailed duplex substrates that are weakly or not unwound by RECQ1 (Table S4).To test whether similar effects can be observed in case of full length RECQ1 we also engineered the full length protein by inserting the same flexible linker.The engineered full length enzyme RECQ1 FLLk also exhibit altered substrate preference and improved unwinding of all the substrates similar to RECQ1 T1Lk .These results demonstrate that our hypothesis holds true for all RECQ1 variants including the full length protein.A comparison of unwinding activities of different RECQ1 variants and other RecQ helicases is summarized in Table S4.A better picture emerges from the results of DNA binding experiments with different substrates in presence of ADPNP.Our results demonstrate that the engineered protein RECQ1 T1Lk binds DNA more efficiently in the presence of ADPNP as compared to RECQ1 T1 .A 3-to-4-fold reduction in the dissociation constants in the presence of ADPNP clearly indicates a cooperative binding of DNA and nucleotide to RECQ1 T1Lk .Similar cooperative binding is not observed in the case of RECQ1 T1 .ARL residues have been implicated in coupling DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis in E. coli RecQ and PriA helicases [ 23 , 24 ].In bacterial RecQ helicases, extensive domain movements restructure the nucleotide-binding pocket upon DNA binding, as evident from C. sakazakii RecQ structure [ 20 ].It is, therefore, logical to assume that insertion of the linker induces domain rearrangements in the helicase core, leading to better coordination between the β-hairpin and the ARL, which in turn improves the DNA binding in RECQ1 T1Lk .

To obtain a clear picture of the domain rearrangement that might have occurred due to linker insertion, we crystallized the RECQ1 T1Lk in the presence of ADPNP and a partial duplex with 3 and 5 ssDNA overhangs.RECQ1 T1Lk crystallized as a dimer with a head-to-tail arrangement of the two protein chains bound to the partial duplex.Interestingly, an alternative elongated structure was also observed with two adjacent protein chains arranged in an array bound to a sin-gle DNA duplex.Such an arrangement can be justified if the protein monomers assume a more compact structure, and as a result, two WH domains of adjacent RECQ1 molecules can fit on the 13 bp duplex region without any steric clash.Superimposition with RECQ1 monomer structure indicates that RECQ1 T1Lk monomers are more compact with an inward drift of the WH domain by 2.6 Å and the D1 domain by 2.2 Å.As a result, there is reduction in the distance between the β-hairpin and the ARL by 3 Å, in line with the prediction from our MD simulation studies.This finding strengthens our hypothesis that the insertion of the linker led to a reduction in the distance between the β-hairpin tip and the ARL, which in turn results in improved DNA binding and efficient strand separation.The structural compaction of RECQ1 T1Lk also led to an alteration in the dimer interface.The RECQ1 T1Lk dimer is stabilized by extra interactions at the interface in contrast to the RECQ1 T1 dimer.The ARL of RECQ1  [ 11 , 50 ].It is possible that improved interface interactions might have been helpful in stabilizing the ARL, allowing its better interaction with the ssDNA end.Apart from overall structural compaction, we observed that there is a drastic reduction of the ATP binding cleft volume, indicating the possibility of ADPNP binding, although we could not trace the electron density for the same.Analysis of the relative positioning of the D1 and D2 domains across the RecQ family proteins based on the distance between two pairs of invariant points revealed a strong correlation among the different conformational states bound to DNA and nucleotides [ 55 ].A similar analysis with available RECQ1 structures reveals that the distance between Q96 (on motif 0) and F462 (on Zn domain) reduces from 22.3Å in DNA bound state (2WWY) to 20.8Å in ADP bound state (2V1X) and 20.2Å in RECQ1 T1Lk (8YRS).While the 2V1X structure represents an intermediate or partly closed state with only ADP bound to it, RECQ1 T1Lk structure (8YRS) resembles a further closed conformation with both the DNA and nucleotide bound.The same can be evidenced from the analysis of the ATP binding cleft volume in all RECQ1 structures.The cleft volume reduces from 2382.7 Å 3 in 2WWY to 1502 Å 3 in 2V1X and 1181.5 Å 3 in 8YRS clearly indicating the three distinct conformational states.

In the crystal structure of RECQ1 T1Lk , we were able to observe significant remodeling of the D1 domain, particularly in the ATP binding cleft upon DNA binding to the ARL.An inward drift of the loop containing motif Q residues by 1.9 Å and the α-helix 3 being drifted by 2.1 Å pulls the catalytic lysine, K119 more toward motif II.This movement results in a significant reduction of the distance between Lys 119 and Glu 220 as compared to the RECQ1 T1 structure.This structural remodeling brings the catalytic Lys 119 and the general base Glu 220 in close proximity, facilitating their contact with the ATP.A very similar allosteric remodeling of the ATPase domain upon ssDNA binding to ARL was demonstrated in C. sakazakii RecQ in complex with DNA, where, the carboxylic group of Glu 147 on motif II moved closer to the binding site for the gamma phosphate of ATP, thereby representing an ATP-hydrolysis competent structure [ 20 ].Another significant observation from our structure is the reduction of the Das et al. distance between Thr 256 on motif III and Gln 400 on motif VI from 7 Å in RECQ1 T1 to 4.6 Å in RECQ1 T1Lk .This is because the motif III moves as a whole toward the D2 domain, bringing the two lobes of the ATPase domain closer to each other, leading to the narrowing of the cleft.In C. sakazakii RecQ helicase structure, an equivalent glutamine residue (Gln 322) located on motif VI has been shown to move toward and interact with His 149 of motif II, resulting in a similar closure of the ATP binding cleft [ 20 ].The data presented here help to establish a common basis for DNA binding coupled ATPase domain remodeling in RecQ helicases that was only reported for bacterial RecQ helicases.There was no major restructuring of the ATPase domain observed in the DNA or ADP bound RECQ1 T1 structures reported earlier.Pike et al. 2009 suggested a possibility of D1 domain dynamics from an overall comparison of three different crystal forms (P2 1 vs. C222 1 and P2 1 vs. P6 3 ) obtained for RECQ1, but no structural remodeling was shown [ 11 ].Our structure, for the first time, demonstrates that the ATPase domain of RECQ1 undergoes significant alterations upon DNA binding and also indicates that our structure might represent the catalytically active and hydrolysis competent state of the enzyme.Insertion of the linker might have facilitated the stabilization of the catalytic state by inducing structural compaction and stabilizing DNA-ARL interactions.A closer look at the ssDNA trajectory reveals that the last two bases make extensive contacts with the ARL residues Asp 230 and Arg 232.Moreover, the last base in the RECQ1 T1Lk structure can comfortably reach the His 229 which is not possible in the RECQ1 T1 structure (2WWY).The Trp 227 on the ARL flips toward the 3 hydroxyl end of the ssDNA offering extra contact with the DNA.These observations further strengthen our hypothesis that domain motions in the RQC result in a reduction of the distance between the β-hairpin tip and the ARL, thereby allowing the ssDNA end to reach the ARL comfortably and helps remodeling of the ATPase domain.Mutation of conserved residues on the ARL have been shown to drastically affect ATP hydrolysis and unwinding activities in E. coli RecQ and PriA DNA helicase [ 23 , 24 ].In our study, mutation of conserved ARL residues Asp 230 and Arg 232 had varying effects on unwinding activities of RECQ1 T1 and RECQ1 T1Lk , although the effect on ATP hydrolysis activity was comparable.DNA stimulated ATP hydrolysis was reduced to a similar extent in both the mutants RECQ1 T12A and RECQ1 T1Lk2A .While unwinding is almost abolished for RECQ1 T12A , the mutant RECQ1 T1Lk2A can still unwind all the substrates (3 tail duplex, blunt duplex, short tailed duplex) to a reasonable extent at high concentrations.This clearly indicates that reduced ATP hydrolysis due to ARL mutations cannot be directly linked to unwinding activity in the case of the RECQ1 T1Lk2A .This is probably because of better inter domain communication between the WH and the D1 domains resulting from reduced distance between ARL and the β-hairpin.In RECQ1 T1Lk2A , the residual ATPase activity is sufficient to initiate strand separation because it can efficiently coordinate with the WH domain.The DNA binding data of the ARL mutants in presence of ADPNP further strengthen the results from unwinding experiments.From our crystal structure data and mutational analysis, it may be inferred that due to structural compaction and reduction of the distance, the 3 end of the DNA can still contact the farthest residues of ARL in RECQ1 T1Lk2A in the presence of nucleotide and initiate strand separation.

The crystal structure and biochemical evidences presented in this paper help to propose a general mechanistic model for RecQ helicases that holds true for eukaryotic RecQ helicases like human RECQ1.Previous structural and biochemical works on E. coli and C. sakazakii RecQ [ 10 , 20 , 23 ] already demonstrated a mechanism involving domain rearrangement and allosteric coupling of DNA binding to ATP hydrolysis.The open questions that remained unanswered was how the structurally rigid RECQ1 can couple DNA binding to ATPase activity and perform unwinding without domain remodeling and what is the structural requirement for substrate recognition.Our work helps to answer these questions by providing new structural insight into ATPase domain remodeling of RECQ1 upon inducing domain motions in the RQC.Using our engineered construct, we successfully establish that reduction of the inter domain distance can favorably restructure the ATPase domain, which otherwise was thought to be rigid compared to other RecQ helicases.Further biochemical experiments and structural studies will be required to validate the hypothesis in the context of other members of the family and build a more general mechanistic model that can be extrapolated for all eukaryotic RecQ helicases.
