An ionic liquid system based on two amide anions; bis(fluorosulfonyl)amide anion (FSA − ) and bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)amide anion (TFSA − ), with a quaternary ammonium cation; N,N,N-trimethyl-N-propylammonium cation (TMPA + ), was investigated over entire composition range. The system showed the binary phase diagram and the eutectic point was 5.5°C around the anion ratio of 1:2 (FSA − : TFSA − ). Polymorphic behavior of solidified ionic liquid was observed by X-ray diffraction (XRD) and discussed comparing with ionic liquids composed of other anion; (fluorosulfonyl) (trifluoromethylslufonyl)amide anion (FTA − ) or tetrafluoroborate anion (BF 4 − ). In addition, the phase diagram of the ionic liquid system containing lithium cation with binary anion was confirmed. Raman spectroscopy suggested that lithium cation interacts more preferentially with TFSA − than FSA − .

Introduction

Ionic liquids are expected as next-generation liquid material for various kinds of utilization and have become an expanding topic of chemical research because of their attractive and unique properties; a negligible vapor pressure, nonflammability, electrical conductivity, good solubility, and so on. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][9] Ionic liquids are generally recognized as salts which have melting point below 100°C, and their physical properties depend on the choice of ionic species. Aliphatic quaternary ammonium is known as one of the cationic species and their good electrochemical stability was reported. [10][11][12] In the past decade, ionic liquids including bis(fluorosulfonyl)amide anion (FSA ¹ ) have attracted much attention due to its higher fluidity and conductivity compared with bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)amide anion (TFSA ¹ ) which is famous as a constituent of hydrophobic, low viscosity, and low melting temperature ionic liquids. 13,14 The flexibilities by internal rotations with respect to the C-S-N-S dihedral angle are key factor for lowering melting temperature or viscosity as well as delocalization of charge. However, with N,N,N-trimethyl-N-propylammonium cation (TMPA + ), there is an obstacle for operation in room temperature because the melting point of N,N,N-trimethyl-N-propylammonium bis(trifluorosulfonyl)amide (TMPAFSA) is 43.2°C. In the past, authors found that mixture of TMPAFSA and N,N,N-trimethyl-Npropylammonium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)amide (TMPATFSA) showed phase transition at lower temperature than each pure substance, and reported the phase diagram. 15 From the viewpoint of practical applications, mixing several materials is beneficial to design desired performances. 16,17 Although many studies have been investigated focusing on binary or ternary ionic liquid systems these are mainly limited in transport properties, and there are few studies about basic properties overlooking the whole composition. [18][19][20][21][22][23][24][25][26] Because both FSA ¹ and TFSA ¹ possess isomerization by intramolecular rotation, it is important to understand that how anionic composition affect the properties. 27,28 Hence, in this study, we investigated anionic binary ionic liquid; TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x (x means mole fraction of FSA ¹ ) focusing on the relationship between anionic composition and basic properties. For reference purposes, other ionic liquids which have different shape of anion were also studied.

In addition, binary amide anion-based ionic liquid system containing lithium cation was investigated. The lithium secondary batteries have to be treated carefully for temperature management to ensure the credibility and safety because the conventional electrolytes contain organic solvents prone to decompose. Therefore, a possible alternative for high temperature operation might be ionic liquid based electrolytes. 29 In this study, how the coexistence of two anion affect phase transition temperature and coordination around lithium cation are discussed.


Experimental


Materials

TMPAFSA and TMPATFSA were synthesized by an anionexchange method. 30 LiFSA and LiTFSA were purchased from Kishida Chemical Co., Ltd. and Morita Chemical Industries Co., Ltd., respectively. The recovered organic phase was dried under vacuum for 12 h at 95°C and then TMPAFSA and TMPATFSA were gained. They were stored in an argon filled glove box. Appropriate amounts of the ionic liquids were mixed and stirred for 3 h at 50°C in the glove box to prepare the anionic binary ionic liquid; TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x . Ionic liquid system containing lithium cation; Li y TMPA 1¹y TFSA and Li y TMPA 1¹y FSA 0.33 TFSA 0.67 (here, y means mole fraction of Li + against cation composition) were also prepared in a similar way keeping the anion ratio constant. N,Ndiethyl-N-methyl-N-(2-methoxyethyl) ammonium tetrafluoroborate (DEMEBF 4 ) was purchased from Kanto Kagaku Co., LTD., and N,N,N-trimethyl-N-propylammonium (fluorosulfonyl) (trifluoromethylslufonyl)amide (TMPAFTA) was provided as product. All chemical structures are shown in Fig. 1.


Thermal analysis

Phase transition of ionic liquids were investigated by DSC measurements with a Thermo Plus DSC 8230L (Rigaku Denki Co., Japan) with liquid nitrogen cooling. Sealed aluminum pans were prepared in the glove box. The employed scanning temperature range was between ¹50 and 50°C and the scan rate was 5°C min ¹1 . Cooling and heating processes were repeated more than three times in dry nitrogen atmosphere. In case that small endothermic peak (prepeak) was observed in the lower temperature side, the reproducibility was confirmed for comparison with the results of XRD patterns for each composition.


X-ray diffraction analysis

XRD patterns were measured using X-ray generator RINT-TTR (Rigaku Co., Ltd., Japan) equipped with semiconductor detector and low temperature control unit (PTC30). The sample was investigated by focusing method (CuKA, 50 kV, and 300 mA) with a Ni filter. A scanning speed of 20°min ¹1 was used within the range of 10°< 2ª < 60°. We measured XRD patterns at every 20°C as stepwise procedure between ¹100°C and 50°C and heating and cooling rates were 1°C min ¹1 . All measurements were carried out under vacuum condition to avoid dew condensation. At each measurement temperature, the sample was retained for 10 min before measurement. The stepwise procedure was repeated 10 times to confirm the effect of the heating and cooling cycles and reproducibility. We analyzed the degree of variation using a simple statistical method to estimate the reproducibility in the obtained XRD patterns. We calculated the sample standard deviations for the root mean square of the d-value (D k ) derived from diffraction angles of each repeated XRD measurements for each ionic liquid as shown below:

where n and N are the number of diffraction peaks in each cycle and the number of measurement repetitions, respectively. Repeated XRD measurements with higher reproducibility in diffraction angles tend to result in relatively lower sample standard deviation value. When no diffraction peaks appeared due to the glassy state, we applied zero value instead of the root mean square to calculate the sample standard deviation. This represents there was big difference between crystal and glassy state. The calculation was carried out within the range of 10°< 2ª < 40°.


Raman spectroscopy

Raman vibrational spectra were recorded by a T-64000 Raman spectrometer (Horiba Ltd., Japan) with a charge coupled device detector cooled using liquid N 2 . The neodymium-doped yttrium aluminium garnet (i.e., Nd: YAG) laser with a wavelength of 532 nm was used for excitation. Samples were loaded into 5 mm diameter glass tubes and set into the Raman cell (#2000-A of AABSPEC) in the 180°scattering geometry. The laser power and integration time were 20 mW and 10 s, respectively. The wavenumber of monochrometer was calibrated by F 2g mode of Si at 520 cm ¹1 using silicon powder.


Ionic conductivity and viscosity measurements

The ionic conductivities were measured by AC impedance measurement using a precision LCR meter (4284A and 4285A of Agilent Technology, USA) with a 16452A test fixture. The electrode area was 38 mm and the distance was 0.5 mm. The cell was calibrated by using a 0.01 mol L ¹1 KCl aqueous solution at 25°C. The viscosities were measured by a cone-plate digital viscometer (TVE-22L of Toki Sangyo Co., Ltd., Japan). A cone rotor 1°34B © R24 was used and the rotating speed was 5 rpm. The fluidities were gained as the reciprocal of viscosities. The nonlinear temperature dependences of the electrical conductivities and fluidities showed were analyzed using Vogel-Tamman-Fulcher (VTF) equation given as following:

where W is conductivity or fluidity and A, B and T 0 are constants. The VTF parameters were determined with a nonlinear Gauss-Newton inverse matrix optimizing method similarly to our previous study. 31 


Results and Discussion

3.1 Thermal properties and phase diagram of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1−x Excepting the single or eutectic salts, two endothermic peaks were observed in the heating process of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x from the DSC measurement. Peaks appeared at lower temperature were assigned to the liquidus line on the phase diagram, and higher ones were linked with the solidus line. From the all results of DSC spectra, the phase diagram of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x was obtained as Fig. 2. The binary anion system showed the binary phase diagram composed of two kinds of immiscible crystals and completely   18 The eutectic composition of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x had much TFSA ¹ content compared with their report. Slight difference in cation structure; two methyl groups or pyrrolidine, made a difference for interaction among the constituent ions. It could be due to the difference of randomness between TMPA + and N-methyl-N-propylpyrrolidinium cation because TMPA + based system showed higher phase transition points, and TMPA + might need much TFSA ¹ which exhibits more randomness than FSA ¹ to get the eutectic mixture. Here, the endothermic prepeak at the lower temperature of eutectic points should be mentioned. Possibility of polymorphism phenomena might be related to the composition. Figure 3 represents two endothermic curves of TMPAFSA 0.5 TFSA 0.5 . Whereas Fig. 3(a) shows only endothermic peak at the single temperature for fusion of the sample, Fig. 3(b) shows small endothermic prepeak. The peak suggested that there is minor phase transition below the phase transition temperature, and more than one metastable crystal structure in solid state. Such melting behavior is particularly common in materials which exhibit polymorphism, and it has been recently reported that ionic liquid exhibits polymorphic behavior. [32][33][34][35][36][37][38][39] Also in this work, there are several solid phases which have different segmental motion due to polymorphism, such solid phase exhibited melting behavior below the temperature corresponding to the solidus line. Additionally, we got composition dependence of heat of fusion as Fig. 4 from integration of endothermic peaks. The ionic liquids at the eutectic composition shows the lowest heat of fusion. It suggests that eutectic composition can solidify more easily than other composition. The small peak was hardly found near the eutectic composition. This results will be discussed for the polymorphism of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x in a later section with XRD patterns.

3.2 Phase transition behavior of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1−x XRD patterns at every 20°C as stepwise procedure between ¹100°C and 50°C of TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x under 6th cooling process are shown in Fig. 5. The XRD patterns after the 6th repetition measurement was almost changeless. TMPATFSA got glassy state until ¹100°C despite its melting point at 19.8°C. On the other hand, TMPAFSA which has a melting point at 43.2°C also got glassy state but to a lesser extent. Although phase behavior of ionic liquids is affected by pressure, the difference between TFSA ¹ and FSA ¹ is due to the intramolecular rotation of each anion. 40 Effect by intramolecular rotation of TFSA ¹ is supposed to be more drastic than FSA ¹ because of its intrinsic bulkiness, therefore it makes the solidification of TMPATFSA unsettled. Interestingly, the eutectic composition hardly got glassy state. This composition seems to be relatively stable for crystallization, and that corresponded with the composition dependence of heat of fusion.

The separated solid phases of TMPATFSA and TMPAFSA should melt above the solidus temperature because the system showed binary phase diagram as Fig. 2. Consequently, we carried out XRD observation in heating processes. Some diffraction peaks of mixed composition disappeared along with the temperature increase, while TMPAFSA did not show such behavior until 40°C. This suggested that different solid phases exist in mixed composition. Figure 6 represents cycle-dependent XRD patterns observed at ¹100°C in each cycle. It is obvious that TMPATFSA showed different XRD patterns depending on cycles, while TMPAFSA showed XRD patterns at relatively same angles shown as Fig. 6(c). We confirmed that there is an influence from sample orientation for X-ray. However, the cycle dependences of the XRD patterns are unexplained by just the sample orientation. Various solid phases existed and the phase transition was not fixed clearly by the varieties of the molecular conformation. Nishikawa et al. reported unique thermal phenomena of 1-isopropyl-3-methylimidazolium bromide using a laboratory-made DSC with nanowatt stability and sensitivity. [41][42][43] They pointed out that domains of different conformers existed and could be closely related with phase transition behavior. Their indication would be more remarkable in TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x than 1-isopropyl-3-methylimidazolium bromide because amide anions have more complicated structure. It was reported that TMPAFSA and TMPATFSA exhibit isomerization equilibria displacement depending on temperature, and alter its liquid structure depending on the isomerization equilibria displacement. 15 It was supposed that isomerization equilibria occurred during cooling processes until just before solidification and resulted in polymorphic behavior. We found difficulty to determine the crystal structure due to the polymorphism. On the other hand, XRD patterns of eutectic mixture represented in Fig. 6(b) showed relatively good reproducibility for each cycle. Hence, the polymorphic tendency of FSA ¹ and TFSA ¹ might be suppressed as above discussed in the interpretation of Fig. 3. Cycle-dependent XRD patterns of TMPAFTA, which has asymmetric anion structure and melting point at ¹27.3°C, were shown as Fig. 7(a). Solidification did not occur even in heating process from ¹100°C except for first two cycles and kept glassy state until end of measurement. Matsumoto et al. pointed out that introducing asymmetry into an  amide anion is one of effective strategies to reduce the melting point of ionic liquid. 44 The solidification difficulty of TMPAFTA was due to the asymmetric molecular structure of FTA ¹ . On the other hand, ionic liquid based BF 4 ¹ , which has simple and symmetric structure, exhibited with high reproducibility in XRD patterns as displayed in Fig. 7(b). BF 4 ¹ seems to get the most stable structure easily. We estimated the reproducibility of the obtained XRD patterns for each ionic liquid shown in Figs. 6 and 7 with the simple statistical method. If material exhibits polymorphic behavior, it would show XRD patterns in different angles randomly for each measurement  cycle, and the sample standard deviation tend to be large value. Thus, we can qualitatively compare the tendency to show polymorphism for each ionic liquid. The results are shown in Table 1. We confirmed the variations of diffraction angles were less in order of TMPAFSA 0.33 TFSA 0.67 , DEMEBF 4 , TMPAFSA, TMPATFSA and TMPAFTA. From the above estimation, conformational change by intramolecular rotation plays a critical role on crystalline structure of ionic liquid, and mixing different anions had a more significant influence. Figure 8 represents composition dependences of conductivities and fluidities for TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x at 60°C. The conductivities and fluidities increased with FSA ¹ mole fraction. It was due to the better mobility of the FSA ¹ . The inflection points were found near x = 0.3, and this suggests that there are two regions depending on anion composition; FSA ¹ is dominant in one region and TFSA ¹ is so in the another region. At each composition, temperature dependences of conductivity and fluidity showed obvious curvatures that follow VTF behavior as shown in Figs. 9(a) and 9(b), respectively. Figure 10 exhibits the composition dependences of T 0,· and T 0,º determined by conductivity and fluidity, respectively. Here, T 0 is known as an ideal glass transition temperature at which the fluidity would fall to zero. 31,45,46 Cohen et al. proposed from a series of their investigations that free volume means requirement for molecular movements, and it depends on the difference between T 0 and measuring temperature under constant pressure. [47][48][49][50] Although T 0,· in low FSA ¹ mole fraction and T 0,º were almost constant, T 0,· above the composition of x = 0.33 showed dependence with FSA ¹ content as shown in Fig. 10. The eutectic composition seemed to exhibit the lowest hindrance for ionic transport. The composition dependences of T 0,· and T 0,º corresponded with the results of XRD of Fig. 6. TMPATFSA, which kept glassy state and then crystallized at a temperature lower than T 0,· and T 0,º , seems to exhibit phase transition under highly restricted condition.

Phase transition of binary anion system containing lithium

cation For application of ionic liquids as lithium ion battery electrolytes, lithium salt is usually mixed with ionic liquids. However, the increase of salt concentration generally induces unfavorable changes such as increase of viscosity, decrease of ionic conductivity, and rise of melting point. Mixing ionic liquids can lower the phase transition temperature especially at the eutectic composition as above mentioned, therefore we compare the phase transition of the system with single anion and binary anion. As binary anion system composed of FSA ¹ and TFSA ¹ , FSA ¹ mole fraction of x = 0.33 was employed because this anion ratio, which was found as the eutectic composition in Fig. 2, seemed to be stable in TMPA + based system. Figure 11 represents two phase diagrams; Li y TMPA 1¹y TFSA and Li y TMPA 1¹y FSA 0.33 TFSA 0.67 . The liquidus and solidus lines of the binary anion system were lower than that of the single anion system. This means binary anion could keep liquid state in wider   temperature range. It is one good example of practical application for binary anion. In the binary anion system, y = 0.5 did not show definite phase transition and showed liquidity at room temperature, while the single anion system solidified completely.


Interaction between lithium cation and binary anion

In FSA ¹ and TFSA ¹ coexisting system, it is important to know which anion interacts with Li + preferably. From the results of Raman spectroscopy shown as Fig. 12, the band assigned to the CF 3 bending vibration of TFSA ¹ was observed at 742 cm ¹1 , while the new band in the higher frequency side appeared depending on mole fraction of Li + . The new band around 750 cm ¹1 is ascribed to the TFSA ¹ bound to the Li + . 51 This behavior was same whether FSA ¹ coexists or not. On the other hand, the Raman band of 1217 cm ¹1 assigned to the SO 2 symmetric in-phase stretches of the bulk ( free) FSA ¹ did not show any changes between lithium mole fraction of y = 0.1 and y = 0.0, although the band ascribed to the FSA ¹ solvated to the Li + appeared around 1242 cm ¹1 and overlapped with the decreasing band of free FSA ¹ above y = 0.3. 52 Previous reports say that the lithium ion may be coordinated through two TFSA ¹ and that of FSA ¹ is three in corresponding ionic liquids. 53,54 As Li + mole fraction of y = 0.1 in the binary anion system, there is enough TFSA ¹ to coordinate with Li + . However, Li + and TFSA ¹ lose numerical balance above y = 0.3 and consequently form a pair with FSA ¹ . Therefore, Li + seems to interact with TFSA ¹ preferably in binary amide anion-based ionic liquid. This consideration is appropriate to the findings proposed by Tsuzuki et al. that the    Electrochemistry, 86(2), 92-98 (2018) interaction between FSA ¹ and Li + is weaker than between TFSA ¹ and Li + . 55 Thus, in Li y TMPA 1¹y FSA 0.33 TFSA 0.67 , Li + solvated cluster composed of TFSA ¹ seemed to exist in somewhat FSA ¹ -rich binary ionic liquid.


Conclusions

Properties of binary amide anion-based ionic liquid; TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x were investigated in whole composition. From DSC measurements, TMPAFSA x TFSA 1¹x showed the binary phase diagram which had the eutectic point at the mole fraction of x = 0.33. In order to investigate the polymorphism which was suggested by thermal analyses, we carried out XRD measurements again and again with repeated heating and cooling using temperature controlling unit. Comparing each XRD pattern with the simple statistical method, polymorphic behaviors due to the intramolecular rotation of amide anion were confirmed. A relatively good reproducibility in XRD patterns of the eutectic composition seemed to be consistent with the composition dependence of heat of fusion and of ideal glass transition temperature determined from VTF equation. Motivated by desire for a lithium secondary battery electrolyte for high temperature operation, we studied the binary amide anion system containing lithium cation. The phase transition temperature of the binary anion system was lower than TFSA ¹ system. In addition, Raman spectroscopy suggested that lithium cation interacts more preferentially with TFSA ¹ than with FSA ¹ .
