Fe contribution to the magnetic anisotropy of L1 0 -ordered FePt thin films studied by angle-dependent x-ray magnetic circular dichroism
8 Oct 202523E777E11D5C6BC98EEF70186A2EFB9BarXiv:2505.24385v2[cond-mat.mtrl-sci]
Among magnetic thin films with perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA), L1 0 -ordered FePt has attracted significant attention because of its exceptionally strong PMA.However, the microscopic origin of its strong PMA has not been elucidated experimentally.We have investigated the contribution of the Fe 3d electrons to its magnetic anisotropy energy by angle-dependent x-ray magnetic circular dichroism at the Fe L 2,3 edge.By this technique, one can deduce the magnetic dipole moment m T , which represents the anisotropic spatial distribution of spin-polarized electrons, and the orbital moment anisotropy (OMA) of Fe 3d electrons.Detected finite m T indicates that the spin-polarized Fe 3d electrons are distributed preferentially in the out-of-plane direction of the films.This m T of Fe overwhelms the positive contribution of OMA to PMA, and reduces the PMA of L1 0 -ordered FePt thin films, consistent with a previous first-principles calculation.The present result implies that a large positive contribution of the non-magnetic element Pt rather than Fe governs the PMA of L1 0 -ordered FePt thin films.

6][7] By adjusting the deposition and post-annealing temperatures, one can control the degree of the long range chemical order (S) in a wide range, 6,8 a useful property for realizing desired device characteristics.[11][12] Despite the concentrated effort over decades, the origin of the huge PMA of L1 0 -FePt has not been fully elucidated.In general, the MCA of ferromagnetic thin films is expressed as a sum of two components within the second-order perturbation theory with respect to the spin-orbit coupling (SOC): [13][14][15][16] One is the term proportional to the anisotropy of the orbital magnetic moment (m orb ) between the out-of-plane and in-plane directions of the film, referred to as orbital moment anisotropy (OMA), 13 and the other is the term which reflects the asphericity of electron orbitals (i.e.[16] In the case of L1 0 -FePt, there have been controversies on which of these two terms contribute more strongly to the MCA and which of Pt and Fe has dominant contributions.Experimentally, the OMAs of both Fe 17,18 and Pt 12,18 have been revealed by x-ray magnetic circular dichroism (XMCD).However, some theoretical calculations show that the MCA energy from Fe is negligibly small or even negative despite the large OMA. 19,20In addition, although many studies suggest that the Pt contribution is dominant in the MCA of L1 0 -FePt, 19,20 some first-principles calculations show that the MCA energy from the Fe site can be larger than that from the Pt site and that the large SOC of Pt only indirectly participates through the Fe 3d-Pt 5d band hybridization. 21,22Thus, consensus has not been reached yet about the microscopic origin of the PMA of L1 0 -FePt.

In order to elucidate the origin of the PMA in L1 0 -FePt, it is essential to experimentally probe the orbital asphericity as well as m orb of the Fe 3d and Pt 5d electrons.XMCD is an elementspecific technique which can separately probe the orbital and spin magnetic moments of ferromagnetic materials by using XMCD sum rules. 23,247][28][29][30][31] In the present study, in order to reveal the orbital asphericity of the Fe 3d electrons, we have performed angle-dependent XMCD (AD-XMCD) experiments at the Fe L 2,3 edge of FePt thin films with different degrees of L1 0 order and hence different MCA energies.From the magnetic field-angle dependence of m eff spin , it has been shown that the spin-polarized electrons of Fe preferentially occupy orbitals elongated along the z axis (the out-of-plane direction).The deduced MCA energy due to the orbital asphericity is negative, and its magnitude is larger than that due to the OMA.This means that the Fe sites favor the in-plane magnetic easy axis and that Pt should play a dominant role in the PMA of L1 0 -ordered FePt thin films.

FePt thin films were grown on MgO (100) substrates by the ultrahigh vacuum magnetronsputtering method.The details of the sample preparation methods are described in the previous report. 6The stacked structure of the sample was MgO substrate/Fe (1 nm)/Au (30 nm)/FePt (20 nm)/Au (2 nm).The Au 2 nm layer at the top was deposited as a capping layer in order to prevent the sample from oxidization.The 30-nm-thick Au layer was deposited as a buffer layer to ensure that the lattice constant of FePt is not influenced by that of the substrate, and the 1-nmthick Fe layer is the seed layer.One can control the degree of the L1 0 order S (the definition of which is described elsewhere 32,33 ) by changing the substrate temperature during deposition T S or the subsequent annealing temperature after the deposition T A . 6 Table I summarizes the samplepreparation conditions, the degree of the L1 0 order S deduced from the intensity of the (001)

This corresponds to the change in the magnetization angle (θ M ) with varying θ H , since the XMCD intensity is generally proportional to the projected magnetic moment onto the light axis [see Fig. 1(a)].It should be noted that the external magnetic field H and the magnetization M are not necessarily parallel (i.e.θ H = θ M ) in the presence of magnetic anisotropy, because M tends to be oriented along the magnetic easy axis.Based on this principle, one can deduce the MCA energy (K u ) of each film from the angular dependencies of the XMCD intensity, as shown below.

In order to analyze the angular dependence of the XMCD intensity more quantitatively, we have deduced the spin magnetic moment by applying the XMCD spin sum rules 24 to each spectrum in Figs.1(e-g).By using the XMCD spin sum rule, the 'effective' spin magnetic moment (per Fe ion) m eff spin ≡ m spin + (7/2)m T projected onto the x ray propagation vector ( P ), i.e., P • m eff spin , is deduced 24 .5][26][27] By definition, m T is a vector related to both the spin magnetic moment and the asphericity of the charge density, i.e., elongation or shrinkage of the electron orbitals.[30][31] The θ H dependence of P • m eff spin for the film with S = 0.4 obtained by the sum rule is shown in Fig. 2(a), and is analyzed based on the Stoner-Wohlfarth model, 39 in which one assumes the uniaxial MCA and single magnetic domain.Under these assumptions, the magnetostatic energy density E m is given by:
E m = −µ 0 MH cos(θ M − θ H ) + µ 0 2 M 2 cos 2 θ M − K u cos 2 θ M ,(1)
where M is the total magnetization (the sum of the spin and orbital magnetic moments of Fe and Pt).The three terms in Eq. ( 1) represent the Zeeman energy, the general magnetic shape anisotropy for thin films, and the MCA energy.By minimizing E m with respect to θ M , one can deduce the value of θ M for each angle θ H , and thus the effective spin magnetic moment can be deduced by the following equation: 30,31,35
P • m spin + (7/2) P • m T ≃ m spin cos(θ M − θ inc ) + (7/4) Q zz m spin (2 cos θ M cos θ inc − sin θ M sin θ inc ),(2)
where θ inc = 60 • is the x-ray incidence angle.In the simulation, we have assumed that the an- gular dependence of m spin + m orb is negligible and that the ratio of m Pt spin to m Fe spin is constant (m Pt spin /m Fe spin ≃ 0.11) 18 .The curves in Fig. 2(a) represent thus simulated angular dependencies under various assumptions: (i) both MCA (K u ) and quadrupolar moment Q zz are absent, (ii) MCA is incorporated but Q zz is absent, and (iii) both MCA and Q zz are taken into account.

Simulation (i) clearly deviates from the experimental data, showing that one needs to incorporate MCA in order to reproduce the experimental angular dependence.Comparing simulations (ii) and (iii), the latter (simulation with Q zz ) better agrees with the experimental data.Figure 2(b) shows the experimental and simulated angular dependencies of the effective spin magnetic moment for all the samples based on simulation.All the data could be well reproduced based on the Stoner-Wohlfarth model, suggesting that the assumption of the magnetic single domain is valid.The fitted values of m spin , K u , and Q zz are summarized in Table II.The MCA energy K u increases with S, consistent with the magnetization measurements (Fig. S2 of Supplementary Materials) and the previous magnetization studies 6,40 .One can see that Q zz is negative for the films which exhibit PMA (S = 0.3, 0.4) and that the magnitude of | Q zz | increases with S and K u .Figure 3 summarizes the S dependence of Q zz .The negative Q zz values show that the charge density in Fe ions is elongated along the out-of-plane direction, as shown in the bottom of Fig. 3.We note that the decrease of m spin with S in Table II is consistent with the previous experimental study 18 and the theoretical calculations based on the coherent potential approximation. 41cording to the previous theoretical studies 14,15 , the MCA energy (K u ) can be expressed as the  sum of two terms originating from spin-conserved and spin-flip virtual excitations in the secondorder perturbation theory with respect to SOC: specifically, in the well-known formula of secondorder perturbation theory 13-15
E 2 = ∑ ex gr|H SO |ex ex|H SO |gr E gr − E ex , |ex = c † n 2 ,σ 2 c n 1 ,σ 1 |gr (σ 1 , σ 2 = ±1/2),
the terms with σ 2 = σ 1 and σ 2 = −σ 1 are referred to as spin-conserved and spin-flip terms, respectively.Under the assumption that the majority-spin band is fully occupied, the explicit form of K u within the perturbation theory is: 14,15
K u = E 2 − E ⊥ 2 = ζ 4µ B m ⊥ orb − m orb + 21 4µ B ζ 2 ∆E ex m ⊥ T − m T = ζ 4µ B m ⊥ orb − m orb + 21 4µ B ζ 2 ∆E ex m spin Q zz ,(3)
where ζ is the SOC constant and ∆E ex is the exchange splitting between the majority-and minority-spin bands.The first term, originating from spin-conserved excitations, is proportional to the orbital moment anisotropy (OMA) between the film out-of-plane and in-plane directions.The second term, originating from spin-flip excitations, is proportional to the anisotropic charge distribution Q zz .Using the OMA and the Q zz values deduced in the present study (Table II and Fig.

), we have estimated the contributions of Fe to the MCA of FePt thin films and have plotted them in Fig. 4 as functions of the L1 0 order degree S. Here, ζ and ∆E ex of Fe have been assumed to be 0.054 eV and 3 eV, respectively 15,42 .The OMA is positive and gives a small positive contribution to the PMA.In contrast, the negative spin-flip term of Fe favors the in-plane magnetic easy axis.

Besides, its magnitude is significantly larger than that of the spin-conservation term.Therefore, the sum of the spin-conservation term and the spin-flip term is also negative with increasing S, as shown in Fig. 4.

As stated above, there are discrepancies on the origin of the PMA of L1 0 FePt thin films among the different theoretical and experimental studies.Some studies suggest that Fe has a dominant contribution to the PMA 21,22 , while others suggest that Pt contribution is more significant 19,20 .

The present XMCD results show that the overall MCA energy from the Fe ions is negative, which rules out the former standpoint.The negative MCA energy due to the orbital asphericity (m T ), revealed by the present AD-XMCD measurements, dominates over the positive contribution due to the OMA of Fe.

The present results also indicate that the PMA of L1 0 FePt thin films mainly originates from the Pt sites.Our previous XMCD study at the Pt L 2,3 edge 18 shows that the OMA of Pt is negative (m ⊥ orb < m orb ), suggesting that the OMA favors the in-plane magnetic easy axis.The study also suggests that m eff spin of Pt along the out-of-plane direction is slightly larger than that along the inplane direction for highly ordered samples, which might be explained by the increase of m ⊥ T − m T with increasing S. 18 According to Eq. ( 3), this has a positive contribution to PMA.Therefore, it follows that the PMA of the L1 0 FePt thin films is primarily due to the orbital asphericity of the Pt sites.These results are consistent with the theoretical calculation using the Green's function method 19 .To verify this hypothesis, AD-XMCD experiments at the Pt L 2,3 edge using hard x rays could provide a direct evidence, which remain to be performed in the future.

In summary, we have performed AD-XMCD measurements of FePt thin films with various L1 0 order degrees S. The MCA energy K u and the electric quadrupole moment Q zz , i.e. the chargedensity anisotropy, have been quantitatively deduced from the angular dependence of XMCD.The deduced Q zz is negative for the films with larger S, suggesting that the Fe 3d orbitals elongated along the z direction are preferentially occupied in the L1 0 -ordered films.The MCA energy due to this charge-density anisotropy of Fe ions is negative and dominates over the positive contribution by OMA, consistent with the previous first-principles calculation 19 .Considering that the OMA of Pt also favors the in-plane magnetic easy axis, 18 one has to take into account the charge-density anisotropy of Pt in order to explain the large PMA of L1 0 -ordered FePt thin films.
