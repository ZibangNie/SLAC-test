On generating direct powers of dynamical Lie algebras

The expressibility and trainability of parameterized quantum circuits has been shown to be intimately related to their associated dynamical Lie algebras (DLAs).From a quantum algorithm design perspective, given a set A of DLA generators, two natural questions arise: (i) what is the DLA gA generated by A; and (ii) how does modifying the generator set lead to changes in the resulting DLA.While the first question has been the subject of significant attention, much less has been done regarding the second.In this work we focus on the second question, and show how modifying A can result in a generator set A ′ such that g A ′ ∼ = K j=1 gA, for some K ≥ 1.In other words, one generates the direct sum of K copies of the original DLA.In particular, we give qubit-and parameter-efficient ways of achieving this, using only log K additional qubits, and only a constant factor increase in the number of DLA generators.For cyclic DLAs, which include Pauli DLAs and QAOA-MaxCut DLAs as special cases, this can be done with log K additional qubits and the same number of DLA generators as A.

Introduction


Background

Variational quantum algorithms are based on parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) consisting of tunable gates from some finite set.By adjusting the parameters to optimize a given loss function, a wide variety of problems can, in principle, be solved via this framework.However, in practice, the quality of solution depends on the expressibility of the PQC, i.e., the set of states that the PQC can output (for a given input state), while the efficiency with which a good solution can be found depends on the ease with which the parameters can be optimized.In certain cases, trainability can be critically hindered by the presence of so-called barren plateaus: regions of parameter space where the loss function becomes exponentially flat as the problem size increases.

Recently, an important connection was made between the properties of variational quantum algorithms and their associated dynamical Lie algebras (DLAs) [1,2].At a high level, DLAs with large dimensions (e.g.those growing exponentially with the number of qubits) favor expressibility, but lead to barren plateaus.Conversely, barren plateaus may be avoided if the DLA dimension grows only polynomially with qubit number, but at the cost of restricting the space of states expressible by the PQC.The dimension of DLAs has also been shown to upper-bound the number of parameters required for overparameterization to occur [3], where the PQC can explore all relevant directions in state space and which facilitates fast convergence to global optima.

The analysis of DLAs is therefore of central importance to understanding the feasibility of variational quantum algorithms and to aiding in their design.In this regard, given a set A of DLA generators (corresponding to the set of tunable gates in a PQC), two natural questions arise:

(i) What is the DLA gA generated by A?

(ii) How does modifying A lead to changes in the resulting DLA? 1.A tool to aid DLA analysis.Direct power DLA structures naturally appear in various situations, e.g. in the DLA corresponding to cycle graphs in the quantum approximate optimization algorithm for MaxCut [9,10], or when the DLA generators are strings of Pauli operators [5].

The analysis of these DLAs can be involved, and a question is whether, in some cases, their structure can be understood as a modification of a simpler DLA.

2. Constructing DLAs that avoid barren plateaus.From a quantum control theory perspective, one often seeks full controllability of a system which, for n qubits, corresponds to a DLA equal to su(2 n ).However, for variational quantum algorithms, full controllability can be problematic.Consider a variational algorithm with initial state ρ, measurement operator O, and vector θ of tunable parameters.The corresponding DLA gA decomposes into direct sum of simple subalgebras g1, ..., g k (for k ≥ 1) and a center c, and it is known [12] that if ρ ∈ igA or O ∈ igA, then the variance of the loss function ℓ(ρ, O, θ) satisfies
Var θ [ℓ(ρ, O; θ)] = k j=1 Pg j (ρ)Pg j (O) dim(gj) ,
provided the circuit is deep enough to form a 2-design.Here Ps(H)
def = dim(s) j=1 | tr(E † j H)| 2 , and {Ej } dim(s) j=1
is an orthonormal basis for a Lie algebra s.Avoiding barren plateaus requires, by definition, that Var θ [ℓ(ρ, O; θ)] = Ω(1/ poly(n)).However, for su(2 n ), which is a simple Lie algebra (and hence has no non-trivial simple subalgebras) of dimension 4 n − 1, the variance vanishes exponentially quickly in n.The ability to construct DLAs which decompose into multiple copies of smaller subalgebras can therefore be used as a tool in designing algorithms which avoid barren plateaus.


3.

Finding qubit-and parameter-efficient ways of constructing DLAs.Given a set of generators A on n qubits, it is trivial to define a DLA A ′ on nK qubits such that g A ′ = K j=1 gA: simply let A ′ consist of K copies of A acting on disjoint sets of qubits.However, in addition to the large number of qubits required, the cardinality of the modified set is |A ′ | = K |A|.As the number of tunable parameters in a PQC is lower-bounded by |A|, the variational optimization of such a system requires tuning at least K |A| parameters.On the other hand, the number of tunable parameters is only upper-bounded by the total number of gates in the PQC, which is independent of the DLA.Reducing the number of qubits required is clearly desirable from a quantum algorithm perspective, as is maintaining the minimum number of tunable parameters required for overparameterization.


Dynamical Lie algebras

A Lie algebra g is a vector space over a field F equipped with a bilinear map [•, •] called the Lie bracket that satisfies, for all A, B, C ∈ g, (i)
[A, A] = 0 and (ii) [A, [B, C]]+[B, [C, A]]+[C, [A, B]] = 0 (Jacobi identity).
We call a set of linearly independent and traceless anti-Hermitian operators on n qubits a dynamical generating set.For an integer L ≥ 2 and a dynamical generating set A = {A1, . . ., AL}, the dynamical Lie algebra (DLA) generated by A, denoted
gA = A Lie,R,
is the smallest Lie algebra over R containing A and closed by the Lie bracket, which is taken to be the commutator [A1, A2] def = A1A2 − A2A1.For s ≥ 2, define right-nested commutators of elements of A to be nested commutator of the form
[Ai 1 , [. . . [Ai s−1 , Ai s ] . . .]].
The Jacobi identity implies the following well-known fact, the proof of which is standard, but which, for completeness, we include details of in Appendix A.


Fact 1. All nested commutators of elements in A can be expressed as linear combinations of rightnested commutators.

Given a DLA gA, its semisimple component, or commutator subalgebra, is defined as [gA, gA] def = span{[A, B] : A, B ∈ gA}, and its center is defined to be Z(gA
) def = {A ∈ gA : [A, B] = 0 ∀B ∈ gA}.
As we will deal only with real Lie algebras, when we use the terms 'linear independence' and 'span' these will always mean over R. Fact 2 ([13]).As a subalgebra of su(2 n ), every dynamical Lie algebra admits a decomposition gA = [gA, gA] ⊕ Z(gA).


A Lie algebra admitting such a decomposition is called reductive.

We say that A is a Pauli dynamical generating set if, for all j ∈ [L], Aj = iPj where Pj is an n-qubit Pauli operator (i.e., an n-fold tensor product of the single qubit Pauli operators I, X, Y, Z), and we call the corresponding DLA a Pauli DLA.


Our Results

We consider two ways of modifying a DLA generating set.In the first (cardinality-increasing modifications), the number of generators in the modified set is larger than the original generating set.Theorem 1.Let A = {A1, . . ., AL} be a dynamical generating set, and χ a Hermitian operator with
K distinct eigenvalues. Define A ′ = {Ai ⊗ χ j : i ∈ [L], 0 ≤ j ≤ K − 1}. Then, g A ′ ∼ = K j=1 gA.
While the cardinality of the modified set is K |A| -the same as the naive approach mentioned in the introduction -only at most ⌈log K⌉ additional qubits are required to generate a DLA that is isomorphic to a direct sum of K copies of gA.This is because it is possible to define a Hermitian operator χ on ⌈log K⌉ qubits that has any K ≥ 1 distinct eigenvalues.The factor K increase in the number of generators may seem large.However, for any A, dim(Z(gA)) ≤ |A| [9], and this bound is tight.Thus, in the worst case, this multiplicative factor of K is necessary.Reducing the number of generators to fewer than K |A| therefore requires sacrificing copies of the center, and our remaining results focus on generating direct powers of the commutator subalgebra [gA, gA] instead of generating direct powers of gA.

Let V be a finite dimensional Hilbert space, χ : V → V a Hermitian operator and let I denote the identity operator on V .For A = {A1, . . ., AL} and S ⊆ [L], we denote by AS the set of operators {Ai ⊗ I : i ∈ [L]} ∪ {Ai ⊗ χ : i ∈ S}.To simplify notation, for 1 ≤ i ≤ L, we write Ai for A {i} .Theorem 2. Let A = {A1, . . ., AL} be a dynamical generating set, and χ a Hermitian operator with K distinct eigenvalues.Then,
[gA [L] , gA [L] ] ∼ = K j=1 [gA, gA] and dim(Z(gA [L] )) = 2 dim(Z(gA)).
By doubling the number of generators to 2 |A| and by adding only ⌈log K⌉ qubits, it is therefore possible to generate a DLA whose semisimple component is isomorphic to the direct sum of K copies of [gA, gA].

We are able to show slightly stronger results for two special cases.In both, the cardinality of the modified generating set need only be one larger than the original set A. The first case is when the elements of A are all n-qubit Pauli strings: Theorem 3.For every Pauli dynamical generating set A = {A1, . . ., AL} whose anticommutation graph is connected, we have, for every 1 ≤ i ≤ L,
gA i ∼ = K j=1
gA.

This theorem can be seen as generalizing part of the result of Aguilar et al. [5].There, the authors showed that all Pauli dynamical generating sets A satisfy gA ∼ = g B ′ = 2 nc i=1 gB, where nc ∈ Z+ (the number of 'control qubits') and B ′ = B ∪ C, where B = {B1, ..., BL−n c } and C = {C1, .., Cn c } are disjoint Pauli generating sets.The elements of B ′ are Pauli strings on n + nc qubits, for some integer n, where each B ∈ B has support on the first n qubits, and each Cj ∈ C has the form Cj = B2 ⊗ Pj , where Pj has support on qubit n + j.In other words, g ′ B can be seen as the DLA that results from repeatedly applying Theorem 3 to B, each time adding B2 ⊗ Pj to the generating set (i.e., setting χ = Pj ) for j = 1, ..., nc.As Pauli operators have eigenvalues ±1, this leads to a doubling of the DLA each time.

The second special case is when A has cardinality 2:

Theorem 4. For every dynamical generating set A = {A1, A2} we have, for i ∈ {1, 2},
[gA i , gA i ] ∼ = K j=1 [gA, gA] and dim(Z(gA i )) = dim(Z(gA)) if Ai ∈ [gA, gA], dim(Z(gA)) + 1 otherwise.
Our second way of modifying a DLA generating set (cardinality-preserving modifications) does so without increasing the number of generators, but applies only to certain DLAs that satisfy a property we call cyclic.We defer a technical definition of this property to Section 5, but note here that cyclic DLAs include all QAOA-MaxCut DLAs, all Pauli DLAs and all two-generator DLAs where one generator A satisfies A 2 = λI for some λ < 1.While for cardinality-increasing modifications we introduce a Hermitan operator χ, in this case we require a Hermitian operator Q that is sign unambiguous, i.e., if λ = 0 is an eigenvalue of Q, then −λ is not also an eigenvalue.

Theorem 5. Let A = {A1, . . ., AL} be a cyclic dynamical generating set, and Q a sign unambiguous Hermitian operator with K distinct non-zero eigenvalues.Set AQ = {A1 ⊗ Q, . . ., AL ⊗ Q}.Then,
[gA Q , gA Q ] ∼ = K j=1 [gA, gA],
and
Z(gA Q ) = {C ⊗ Q : C ∈ Z(gA)}.
In this case, the modified generating set AQ has cardinality |AQ| = |A|, and only an additional ⌈log K⌉ qubits are required to generate the K-fold direct sum of [gA, gA].

Cardinality-preserving generator set modifications

In this section we prove Theorem 5 and give a number of cases where it can be applied.

We say that a non-zero Hermitian operator Q is sign unambiguous if, for every pair of non-zero eigenvalues λ, λ ′ in the spectrum of Q, we have λ = −λ ′ .

Let A = {A1, . . ., AL} be a dynamical generating set.We say that A is cyclic if for every noncommuting Ai, Aj ∈ A, there exists a stable extension of (Ai, Aj).That is, there exist
A k 1 , . . . , A k ℓ s.t. [A k ℓ , [. . . , [A k 1 , [Aj, Ai]] . . .]]∝[Aj, Ai].
We say that an integer M is a common cycle length of a cyclic dynamical generating set A if, for every noncommuting Ai, Aj ∈ A, there exists a stable extension of (Ai, Aj) of length e(i, j) such that M is the least common multiple of {e(i, j) : [Ai, Aj] = 0}.We call a dynamical Lie algebra g cyclic if there exists a cyclic dynamical generating set A such that g = gA.

Discussion

In this work we have initiated a study of qubit-and parameter-efficient ways in which a DLA generating set A can be modified so that the resulting DLA is isomorphic to a direct sum of multiple copies of gA.For variational quantum algorithm design, it would also be desirable to have modifications to A effecting other DLA transformations.For example we have the following related questions:

• In a similar spirit to the work of [11], under what conditions would the change A → A ′ leave the DLA invariant?

• Given a specific subalgebra h ⊆ gA, what change A → A ′ would give g A ′ ∼ = h?

• Given generator sets A and B, what generator sets C would give gC ∼ = gA ⊕ gB?

As is the case for generating tensor powers of a DLA, it is easy to construct trivial ways to achieve each of the above.However, modifying the generator sets in a way that is either qubit-or parameterefficient, or else can be used to analyze the structure of other DLAs, may be more involved.But, if such tools can be developed, they would greatly aid the study and design of variational quantum algorithms where DLAs, other than those with Pauli generators, remain difficult to analyze in general.
