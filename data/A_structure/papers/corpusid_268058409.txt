M ET HODS


Study design

MagnetisMM-3 ( NCT04649359) is an open-label, multicentre, non-randomized, phase 2 registrational study evaluating the efficacy and safety of elranatamab monotherapy in patients with RRMM.The study was sponsored and designed by Pfizer Inc. and conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.The study protocol was approved by local or independent institutional review boards or ethics committees at participating sites.All the patients provided written informed consent.


Patients and treatment

Eligible patients were ≥18 years old with measurable disease based on the International Myeloma Working Group criteria 17 and had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance score of ≤2.Patients had to be refractory to ≥1 proteasome inhibitor, ≥1 immunomodulatory drug and ≥1 anti-CD38 antibody, and relapsed or refractory to their last anti-myeloma regimen.Patients enrolled in Cohort A had no prior exposure to a BCMA-directed therapy (i.e.BCMA naive), whereas patients enrolled in Cohort B had previously received a BCMA-directed therapy (e.g.antibody-drug conjugate [ADC] or chimeric antigen receptor [CAR]-T therapy; BMCA exposed).Patients were excluded if they had ongoing grade ≥2 peripheral sensory or motor neuropathy; a history of peripheral sensory or motor neuropathy with prior BCMA-directed therapy (Cohort B); an active, uncontrolled bacterial, fungal or viral infection; and/or previous treatment with an anti-BCMA bispecific antibody.Additional details on inclusion and exclusion criteria have been previously published. 18All patients received step-up doses of subcutaneous elranatamab (12 mg on Day [D]1 and 32 mg on D4), followed by the full treatment dose of 76 mg weekly starting on D8.Dose cycles were 28 days in length, and doses could be modified for the management of elranatamab-related toxicity or peripheral neuropathy.For both step-up and full doses, premedication including acetaminophen 650 mg (or paracetamol 500 mg), diphenhydramine 25 mg and dexamethasone 20 mg was administered 60 min prior to the elranatamab dose.


Patient-reported outcomes

PROs were an exploratory endpoint of the MagnetisMM-3 study.All PRO measures were administered electronically on D1 and D15 of the first three cycles (C) and D1 of each subsequent cycle through C12.Cancer-specific global health status and QOL, functioning, and symptom data were collected using the EORTC QLQ-C30 and the myeloma-specific QLQ-MY20 questionnaires.The QLQ-C30 is a validated cancer-specific questionnaire across five functional scales (physical, role, cognitive, emotional and social), one global health status scale, three symptom scales (pain, fatigue, and nausea or vomiting) and six single items (dyspnoea, insomnia, appetite loss, constipation, diarrhoea and financial difficulties).Better health is indicated by higher scores on the five functional scales and the global health status scale.Conversely, higher scores on the symptom scale indicate a greater degree of symptom severity. 16The QLQ-MY20 is a myeloma-specific questionnaire designed to assess disease symptoms, side effects of treatment, future perspectives and body image. 17ecreases in disease symptom and side effect domain scores and increases in future perspective and body image domain scores indicate improvement. 19The EQ-5D health questionnaire is a measure of health status assessing five domains (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain, and anxiety or depression). 20The Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) and Patient Global Impression of Severity were collected to assess the patient's overall sense of a change in their disease since starting treatment or a perception of their disease severity at a given point in time respectively.These PRO measures also enable the implementation of anchor-based methods to establish and interpret clinically meaningful changes in scores according to FDA guidance. 21,22Data cut-off was 14 March 2023, approximately 15 months after the last patient's initial dose.


Analysis

The PRO analysis dataset for each cohort included all patients in the safety analysis dataset who completed a baseline (last PRO assessment prior to or on the first dose of study treatment) and ≥1 post-baseline PRO assessment.PROs were summarized using descriptive statistics at each time point, and the relative changes from baseline in PRO data were calculated as the change in the least square means (LSM).This set of analyses is intended for descriptive purposes and hypothesis generation and is not meant to test any prespecified hypotheses.

R E SU LTS


Patient and disease characteristics

This analysis of PROs from the MagnetisMM-3 study included data from 123 BCMA-naive patients and 64 BCMA-exposed patients with a median follow-up of 14.7 months after the last patient's initial dose.Demographics and clinical characteristics were generally similar between the two cohorts (Table 1).BCMA-naive and BCMA-exposed patients had a median age of 68.0 and 67.0 years respectively.Notable differences between the cohorts included the incidence of extramedullary disease and the median number of prior lines of therapy, which were greater in BCMA-exposed patients (57.8% and 7.5 respectively) than in BCMA-naive patients (31.7% and 5.0 respectively).Most patients in both cohorts had triple-class refractory disease (BCMA naive, 96.7%; BCMA exposed, 96.9%), with 42.3% and 51.6% of BCMA-naive and BCMA-exposed patients, respectively, also having disease that was penta-drug refractory.Patients in Cohort B were exposed to a BCMA-directed ADC (71.9%),CAR-T therapy (32.8%) or both an ADC and CAR-T therapy (4.7%).The majority (57.8%) of BCMA-exposed patients were refractory to BCMA-directed therapies.


QLQ-30

Based on their QLQ-30 scores, BCMA-naive patients showed a transient worsening in the mean global health status score relative to baseline through C2D15 (LSM change [95% CI], −5.9 [−10.7 to −1.1]), followed by an improvement back to baseline levels at C3D1 that was maintained through C12D1 (Figure 1A).Overall, in BCMA-exposed patients, the LSM change in global health status score was maintained through C12D1 with a maximum deterioration at C1D15 (−2.0 [−8.0 to 4.0]) and a maximum improvement at C12D1 (9.9 [−7.6 to 27.3]) (Figure 1B).BCMA-naive patients had significant (i.e.95% CI did not cross zero) reductions in pain, starting at C4D1 (−6.7 [−13.0 to −0.4]), which were maintained through C12D1 (Figure 1C).Reductions in pain were evident by  | 1805

C3D15 and continued through C5D1 in BCMA-exposed patients.Thereafter, pain scores neared baseline values and were variable (Figure 1D).


QLQ-MY20

Results from the myeloma-specific QLQ-MY20 questionnaire revealed that BCMA-naive patients had significant reductions in disease symptoms starting at C5D1 (−6.9 [−10.6 to −3.1]), which were maintained through C12D1 (Figure 2A).BCMA-exposed patients also had a reduction in their disease symptoms, which was significant at select time points, largely ), followed by an improvement back to baseline levels at C3D1 that was maintained through C12D1 (Figure 2C).Scores in the side effects domain for BCMA-exposed patients remained at baseline levels until C9D1, when an improvement was reported through C10D1, returning to baseline levels by C11D1 (Figure 2D).There was little change in the body image domain for BCMA-naive patients over treatment cycles; however, a significant improvement in the body image domain was observed for BCMA-exposed patients beginning at C6D1 (16.7 [5.0-28.4])and largely maintained through C12D1 (Figure 2E,F).For both BCMA-naive and BCMA-exposed patients, significant improvements in the future perspectives domain scores were observed as early as C1D15 (5.2 [1.1-9.2] and 5.8 [0.9-10.8]respectively); these scores continued to improve or were maintained through C12D1 (Figure 2G,H).


EQ-5D

Overall QOL, as assessed by the EQ-5D questionnaire, remained at baseline levels until C11D1, when a slight improvement was observed and then maintained through C12D1 in BCMA-naive patients (Figure 3A).Trends for improved QOL were seen in BCMA-exposed patients (Figure 3B).


PGIC

By the first PGIC assessment (C1D15), 40.2% of BCMAnaive and 52.6% of BCMA-exposed patients were reporting an improvement in the disease symptoms (either 'a little better' or 'much better').These improvements continued through C5D1 for BCMA-naive patients and C2D15 for BCMA-exposed patients and were maintained in both groups through C12D1 (Figure 4A,B).


DISCUS SION

This analysis of PROs in BCMA-naive and BCMA-exposed patients with RRMM from the MagnetisMM-3 study demonstrates that in addition to its clinical benefit (objective response rate 61.0%), 18 elranatamab monotherapy maintains or improves QOL measures, including pain, disease symptoms, side effects of treatment, body image and future perspectives.Specifically, significant improvements in pain and disease symptoms were reported in both cohorts, regardless of prior BCMA exposure, although these improvements tended to be more variable in BCMAexposed patients in later treatment cycles, likely due to the smaller sample size.

4][25][26] Assessment of PROs has become an important part of managing patients with MM, as it provides valuable information on how the disease and its treatment impact health-related QOL. 5 However, data remain scarce for health-related QOL outcomes in patients with RRMM who are exposed to tripleclass or BCMA-directed therapy. 27In patients with RRMM, the toxic effects of previous treatments as well as the burden of continuous treatment may have long-term effects that decrease their QOL.In the MagnetisMM-3 trial, cytokine release syndrome (CRS) was reported in 57.7% of treated patients, with 98.8% of CRS events occurring with the first three doses, including 90.6% occurring with the step-up doses.The median time to onset of CRS relative to the most recent dose was 2 days (range, 1-9 days), and the median time to resolution was 2 days (range, 1-19 days). 18Elranatamab-induced CRS may have caused the transient worsening in the 'side effects' domain scores noted in early treatment; however, this QOL measure rapidly recovered, suggesting that elranatamab does not adversely affect physical disease burden in this population.Additionally, the maintenance or continued improvement of the future perspectives domain scores suggests that patients in this study may be less worried about death, their future health or MM. 19Improvements in patient QOL through validated PROs were further supported by patients' impression of improvement in their QOL captured by PGIC scoring.

Rapid and clinically meaningful improvements in health-related QOL were also seen in heavily pretreated, BCMA-naive patients with RRMM receiving the BCMA-CD3-bispecific antibody teclistamab. 28mprovements in the mean change from baseline for global health status and pain peaked at approximately 7 months (8 cycles) with teclistamab treatment in the MajesTEC-1 study (n = 110). 28Results of our analysis were similar, with improvements in global health status and pain peaking at approximately 11 months (12 cycles).Moreover, in both the MagnetisMM-3 and MajesTEC-1 patient populations, improvements in health-related QOL occurred early (i.e.median time to improvement was 1.5 months). 28However, these indirect comparisons should be interpreted cautiously considering the differences in patient clinical characteristics, sample size, dosing and timing of PRO collection between these two studies. 28,29hese types of PRO analysis are limited by the lack of a consensus on what PRO measure thresholds constitute clinically meaningful differences in MM studies, there being wide variability between studies in the definitions used. 28,30,31n this study, the size of the evaluable population in each cohort decreased over time and missing values were not imputed.Patients at later time points who did not complete questionnaires may have had different experiences than those who remained in the study.Additionally, lower questionnaire completion rates in later stages could be due to patient death, treatment discontinuation or loss to follow up, among other reasons.Therefore, data from later cycles should be interpreted with caution as scores could be overestimated if patients with better QOL were more likely to complete the questionnaires. 32,33Nonetheless, to determine the extent to which there might be a systematic bias that influenced the overall conclusions of the PRO outcomes reported here, we conducted a post hoc analysis.Regardless of the cycles analysed (early vs. late), the distribution of the mean change in global health status (as an example) did not vary substantially with patient attrition.No differential pattern was observed, suggesting that the maintenance or improvement in global health status was not merely a function of healthier patients remaining in the study.
