Background: Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is an infectious disease that has many adverse impacts on many systems including reproduction.The direct effects of COVID-19 on urogenital system are still open to argue.This study aimed to compare testosterone, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH) and estradiol (E2) hormone levels in COVID-19 infected male individuals with infertility suspicion.Methods: One hundred five control (healthy) and 105 COVID-19 infected males aged between 20 to 54 years old were enrolled in the study.All patients were either diagnosed with primary or secondary infertility suspicion.The COVID-19 infection was diagnosed via reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction test.Blood samples from patients were analyzed from the control and COVID-19 group to measure serum testosterone, FSH, LH, and E2 levels.Hormone levels were statistically compared between groups with the Independent T test.Results: In control and COVID-19 patients, no significance was determined for FSH and LH hormone values.Testosterone hormone were significantly decreased and E2 level was statistically increased in COVID-19 patients compared to that in the control group (P < .001).Conclusion: COVID-19 is a viral disease that affects organ including gonads.COVID-19 infection decreased testosterone levels and increased E2 levels, which leading to disorders in male and female reproductivity.

Introduction

A novel coronavirus (SARS-CoV-2) has caused global pandemic and spread "respiratory distress syndrome (SARS)" called coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19). [1]Studies showed that course of post COVID-19 syndrome over time causes many adverse effects on different organs such as vascular structures, brain, kidney, lungs, heart, gastrointestinal organs. [1,2]There is no direct evidence that show detection of SARS-CoV-2 in testes however inflammation, testicular damage and alterations in gonadotropin was reported. [3,4]Autopsy cases of testes with few numbers was shown to include viral particles with errors in sampling.However, it is known that fever can affect spermatogenesis. [5,6]Considering COVID-19 with fever symptoms, it is clear that the disease causes male infertility. [7,8]Koç et al [9] studied sperm quality and found negative impacts on percentage of total motility, percentage of progressive motility, and normal sperm morphology.Khalili et al [10] found that COVID-19 patients had degenerated seminiferous tubules and germ cells.Male patients had a significantly lower concentration of sperm who has recovered from a moderate infection, compared to mildly infected and noninfected men and concluded that sperm count was still in the normal range despite the difference.

The male hormonal status in patients with infertility suspicion is prominent for resuming pregnancy.Hormonal values in normal range are required for optimal male reproductive functions. [11,12]There are a few reports that show relationship of male hormones and COVID-19.COVID-19 infection causes a decline in circulating level of testosterone (T levels), leasing to severe clinical symptoms.There is still a gap in the literature to elucidate impacts of COVID-19 on male hormones with infertility suspicion. [13]his study analyzed the T levels, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), and estradiol (E2) hormone levels in COVID-19 infected male individuals with infertility suspicion.


Patients and methods


Patients sampling

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and ethical approval of Non-Interventional Clinical Researches Ethics Committee, Dicle University (2021/489).

All participants were informed about the research.They all signed and accepted written informed consent.One hundred five uninfected control (healthy) male participants and 105 COVID-19 infected patients were enrolled from December 2021 to May 2022 at the Infertility Clinic of Diyarbakır Gazi Yaşargil Training and Research Hospital.Samples from COVID-19 patients were collected after patient discharge/recovery.Patient number was calculated by G power according to Alviggi et al [14] .All participants were aged between 20 to 54 years and have diagnosed with suspicion of primary or secondary infertility.Demographic properties (marital status, infertility type, age, body mass index), smoking, medical history, and COVID-19 history (symptom, disease level, medication therapy, and hospitalization status) were recorded for all patients.Patients with varicocele, diabetes mellitus, chronic renal failure, and hypertension were excluded from the study.The COVID-19 diagnosis was detected via reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction test.All COVID-19 participants included in the study clinically diagnosed as mild COVID-19 infection according to the guidelines provided by the Turkish National Committee of COVID-19.


Measurement of hormone levels

Serum samples from control group and COVID-19 group analyzed for hormone measurements.T levels, FSH, LH, and E2, and levels were measured in the groups.Blood samples were taken from all participants, centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 15 minutes to get serum.Sex-related hormone levels were measured using Cobas 6000 (Roche Diagnostic, Germany) auto analyzer device and commercial kits (Roche Diagnostic, Germany) belonging to the same company [FSH: (10) 62576903, LH: (10) 62103005, T levels: (10) 68876501, E2: (10)67083805].


Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 25.0 statistic software package (IBM SPSS Inc., Armonk, NY).Shapiro-Wilk test was used for the normality of data distribution.The difference between the means in the groups was measured by the Independent T test.The data obtained in the study were expressed as number, percentage, arithmetic mean ± standard deviation.Significance was accepted for level P ≤ .05.

Age, Body mass index, smoking, education, infertility, and COVID-19 test results were shown in Table 1.

Demographical and clinical properties of patients were shown in Table 2. COVID-19 related symptoms in patients were fever (n = 51), dyspnea (n = 12), cough (n = 36), myalgia (n = 64), headache (n = 73), weakness (n = 69), diarrhea (n = 14), sore throat (n = 41), taste loss (n = 23).None of the participants were hospitalized during the illness.All COVID-19 patients included in the study were treated according to the Turkish COVID-19 National Committee guidelines.None of the participants complained about the testicles after the diagnosis of COVID-19.

Statistical analysis of sex-related hormones in patients were presented in Table 3. Statistical analysis of the T levels, FSH, LH, and E2 hormone levels were presented in Table 3.No statistically significant difference was seen between FSH and LH values among the groups.While the T levels level in COVID-19 group was significantly decreased (P < .001),E2 level significantly increased compared to the control group (P < .001).


Discussion

Male infertility is a common global reproductive health problem.Omo-Aghoja et al [15] studied on hormonal parameters and semen microbiological pattern of infertile males and reported that abnormalities of LH (79.1%) and FSH (26.8%) levels were the most seen hormonal disorders.T levels level disorder was least common, found in 5.1%.Geidam et al [16] found abnormal values in FSH (66.7%) and T levels (50%) level in infertile men.Tsolaki et al [17] showed that the common cause of male infertility was the increases or decreases in T levels levels.

COVID-19 pandemics alters many physiological and pathological changes in organs so researchers have focused on the relationship between hormone levels and COVID-19.Xu et al [4] studied on SARS-CoV-2 infection, sex-related hormones and testicular function in recovering patients.They found that sex-related hormone (T, FSH, and LH) in SARS-CoV-2 infected males were  improved during recovery period.The authors also stated that no significant relation was found between T levels level and duration or severity of disease.In the current study, we also observed no statistically significant difference between FSH and LH values among the 2 groups However, T levels levels were significantly reduced in COVID-19 group compared to the control (P < .001).

Additionally, E2 has increased significantly in COVID-19 group compared to the control (P < .001).Ma et al [18] demonstrated SARS-CoV-2 infection caused alterations in level of male sex hormones.They examined sex-related hormones of fertile patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection (n:81) and healthy group (n:100).

Level of serum T levels doesn't show statistically difference between groups, however serum LH levels (P < .0001)were significantly increased.Additionally, ratio of T levels: LH (P < .0001)and follicle-stimulating hormone: LH (P < .0001)were decreased in recovered patients compared to the healthy patients.Regression analysis of the T levels: LH ratio of patients with COVID-19 was negatively related with the severity of the disease (P = .0236).Rastrelli et al [19] revealed low total T levels levels in most COVID-19 recovered patients.Laenza et al stated that SARS-CoV-2 was closely related with excessive ROS production resulting in increased oxidative stress.The authors claimed that increased oxygen species impaired sperm function with lipid peroxidation in sperm membranes, and caused sperm DNA fragmentation.Furthermore, they stated low T levels levels, high LH levels, and decreased T levels/LH and FSH/LH ratios imply damage in primary testicular and Leydig compartment. [20]aida et al [21] showed that there was a correlation between severity of COVID-19 symptoms and semen analysis (sperm morphology, sperm count, motility) and male infertility.Mojgan Moshrefi et al [22] has showed the adverse effects of SARS-CoV-2 on male fertility potential and stated that the spermatogenesis as well as gonadotropin, androgens, and T levels secretion during SARS-CoV-2 infection were deteriorated.Sengupta et al [23] reported that COVID-19 might decrease T levels level, worsening the inflammatory response.Long-term effects of COVID-19 on male reproduction system remains unclear.


Limitations

The patients with COVID-19 were enrolled 3 to 4 months after recovery period.So, long-term effects of COVID-19 was not presented.Before-after semen analysis of patients could be recorded.A large-scale study of patients may be performed to better visualize effects of COVID-19 on male sex hormones.


Conclusion

It is clear that COVID-19 has adverse impacts on many organs including testis.This study showed that patients with COVID-19 infection had decreased T levels levels and increased E2 levels which may affect their fertility.Studies on male reproduction system and COVID-19 infection are vital since it is a global worry.We think that our results may guide researchers to understand the adverse effects of SARS-CoV-2 on the male reproductive system and its hormones.
