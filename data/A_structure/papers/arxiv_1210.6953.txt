ON A CONJECTURE FOR HIGHER-ORDER SZEGŐ THEOREMS
25 Oct 2012
We disprove a conjecture of Simon for higher-order Szegő theorems for orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle and propose a modified version of the conjecture.Note that, since log w ≤ w−1, (1.1) is equivalent to log w ∈ L 1 (∂D, dθ 2π ). This theorem has led the way for many related results on orthogonal polynomials and Schrödinger operators; see [11] for a book-length treatment. In this paper we focus on higher-order Szegő theorems, where (1.1) is replaced by a weaker condition. A conjecture from [9] describes the situation with finitely many singularities. Denote by S the shift operator on sequences, (Sx) n = x n+1 .

Introduction

In this paper we investigate probability measures µ supported on the unit circle ∂D = {e iθ | θ ∈ [0, 2π)}. If µ has infinite support, the sequence 1, z, z 2 , . . . is linearly independent in L 2 (∂D, dµ), so Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization provides orthonormal polynomials ϕ n (z), which obey the recursion relation zϕ n (z) = 1 − |α n | 2 ϕ n+1 (z) +ᾱ n ϕ * n (z) with ϕ * n (z) = z n ϕ n (1/z) and coefficients α n ∈ D called Verblunsky coefficients. Thus, to the measure µ there corresponds the sequence α = {α n } ∞ n=0 ∈ D ∞ . This is, by Verblunsky's theorem [13], a bijective correspondence; see [9,10] for more information.

With respect to Lebesgue measure on ∂D, µ can be decomposed into an absolutely continuous and a singular part,
dµ = w(θ) dθ 2π + dµ s .
The celebrated Szegő theorem for the unit circle (due in this generality to Verblunsky [14]) states that α ∈ ℓ 2 is equivalent to 
log w(θ) dθ 2π > −∞.(1.(1 − cos(θ − θ k )) m k log w(θ) dθ 2π > −∞ (1.2) is equivalent to l k=1 (S − e −iθ k ) m k α ∈ ℓ 2 and α ∈ ℓ 2 max k (m k )+2 . (1.3)
The conjecture originated with Simon's proof [9, Section 2.8] for the case l k=1 m k = 1; Simon-Zlatoš [12] proved the case l k=1 m k = 2, and Golinskii-Zlatoš [2] proved the equivalence under the assumption α ∈ ℓ 4 . However, we will see that it is not true in general.

In Theorem 2.1, we show that (1.3) is equivalent to the existence of sequences β (1) , . . . , β (l) such that
α = β (1) + · · · + β (l) (1.4)
and, for all k,
(S − e −iθ k ) m k β (k) ∈ ℓ 2 (1.5) β (k) ∈ ℓ 2 max j (m j )+2 (1.6)
We find this decomposition natural because for each critical point e iθ k in (1.2), there is a sequence β (k) which corresponds to this critical point through (1.5). However, the conjectured equivalence of (1.2) and (1.4)-(1.6) implies that the degree m k associated with e iθ k affects not only the conditions on the corresponding β (k) , but all on the β (j) through the max in (1.6). We find it more natural to replace (1.6) by
β (k) ∈ ℓ 2m k +2 . (1.7)
This suggests a modified version of the conjecture. Although this conjecture is incompatible with Conjecture 1.1, they coincide in all the cases which have been proved. However, in the very next case one would naturally proceed to verify, the conjectures differ. For the condition 
(1 − cos θ) 2 (1 + cos θ) log w(θ) dθ 2π > −∞,(1.(S − 1) 2 (S + 1)α ∈ ℓ 2 , α ∈ ℓ 6 .
Conjecture 1.2 is stated in terms of β's, but as we will explain in Section 2, it can be restated in terms of α's and predicts that (1.8) is equivalent to
(S − 1) 2 (S + 1)α ∈ ℓ 2 , α ∈ ℓ 6 , (S − 1) 2 α ∈ ℓ 4 . (1.9)
Due to the prohibitive nature of the calculations involved, we only prove a special case.
Theorem 1.3. Let (S −1)(S +1)α ∈ ℓ 2 and α ∈ ℓ 6 . Then (1.8) is equivalent to (S − 1) 2 α ∈ ℓ 4 .
In particular, Theorem 1.3 disproves Conjecture 1.1:
Corollary 1.4.
Let the Verblunsky coefficients of the measure µ be given by
α n = 1 + (−1) n 3(n + 1) 1/4 . (1.10)
Then (S − 1) 2 (S + 1)α ∈ ℓ 2 and α ∈ ℓ 6 , but
(1 − cos θ) 2 (1 + cos θ) log w(θ) dθ 2π = −∞. (1.11)
Proof. It is straightforward to verify α ∈ ℓ 6 and (S − 1)(S + 1)α ∈ ℓ 2 ; the latter also implies (S − 1) 2 (S + 1)α ∈ ℓ 2 . However, (S − 1) 2 α / ∈ ℓ 4 , so Theorem 1.3 implies (1.11).

Necessary and sufficient conditions for (1.2) in terms of Verblunsky coefficients have been proved by Denisov-Kupin [1], following work of Nazarov-Peherstorfer-Volberg-Yuditskii [8] for Jacobi matrices. However, these conditions are in a more complicated form which hasn't been successfully related to conditions such as those discussed here. For Jacobi matrices, analogs of the cases l k=1 m k = 1, 2 have been proved by Laptev-Naboko-Safronov [5] and Kupin [4].

We end on a pessimistic note: even though all the existing results, including Theorem 1.3, are compatible with Conjecture 1.2, we are not confident that it is true, either. In [7], we analyzed Verblunsky coefficients of the form (1.4), with (1.5) replaced by the stronger condition (S − e −iθ k )β (k) ∈ ℓ 1 , and with α ∈ ℓ p for some p < ∞. There, the measure is purely absolutely continuous except on an explicit finite set of points. However, this set of possible pure points increases with increasing p, and can contain points not in {e iθ k | k = 1, . . . , K} if p > 3. The possibility of these points was shown by Krüger [3] and Lukic [6]. If the analogous phenomenon is true here, it would mean that for large enough m k , the measure may have points outside of {e iθ k | k = 1, . . . , K} where log w is not locally L 1 , so (1.2) would be false.


Decomposition

If P 1 , . . . , P l ∈ C[x] are pairwise coprime, then there exist polynomials
U 1 , . . . , U l ∈ C[x] such that l j=1 U j i =j P i = 1. (2.1)
This is easily proved by induction on l, since C[x] is a principal ideal domain.

Theorem 2.1. Fix 2 ≤ p < ∞. Let P 1 , . . . , P l ∈ C[x] be pairwise coprime and U 1 , . . . , U l ∈ C[x] be such that (2.1) holds. Then the following are equivalent: (i) α ∈ ℓ p and P 1 (S) · · · P l (S)α ∈ ℓ 2 ; (ii) if we define β (j) = U j (S) i =j P i (S)α for j = 1, . . . , l, then β (j) ∈ ℓ p , P j (S)β (j) ∈ ℓ 2 and α = β (1) + · · · + β (l) (2.2) (iii) there exist sequences β (1) , . . . , β (l) ∈ ℓ p such that (2.2) holds and that P j (S)β (j) ∈ ℓ 2 for j = 1, . . . , l.

Proof. This proof repeatedly uses the following obvious fact: if γ ∈ ℓ n , then Q(S)γ ∈ ℓ n , for an arbitrary polynomial Q ∈ C[x].

(i) implies (ii): P j (S)β (j) = U j (S)P 1 (S) · · · P l (S)α ∈ ℓ 2 , and α ∈ ℓ p implies β (j) ∈ ℓ p . Finally, (2.1) implies (2.2).

(ii) implies (iii) trivially. (iii) implies (i): since β (1) , . . . , β (l) are in ℓ p , so is their sum α. Further, P j (S)β (j) ∈ ℓ 2 implies P 1 (S) · · · P l (S)β (j) ∈ ℓ 2 , and summing in j, P 1 (S) · · · P l (S)α ∈ ℓ 2 .

For example, in the case considered by Theorem 1.3, take P 1 (z) = (z−1) 2 , P 2 (z) = z + 1. Note that
1 4 (z − 1) 2 − 1 4 (z − 3)(z + 1) = 1, so take U 1 (z) = − 1 4 (z − 3), U 2 (z) = 1 4 .
Then
β (1) = − 1 4 (S − 3)(S + 1)α, β (2) = 1 4 (S − 1) 2 α. Notice that (S−1) 2 (S+1)α ∈ ℓ 2 is equivalent to (S−1) 2 β (1) , (S+1)β (2) ∈ ℓ 2 .
Further, β (1) ∈ ℓ 6 and β (2) ∈ ℓ 4 is equivalent to α ∈ ℓ 6 and β (2) ∈ ℓ 4 . Thus, Conjecture 1.2 predicts that (1.8) is equivalent to (1.9).
