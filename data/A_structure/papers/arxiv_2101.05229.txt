Two conjectured strengthenings of Turán's theorem
December 21, 2023A3C42DDFC21B95EEAD77B9A617A6FA24arXiv:2101.05229v3[math.CO]
We investigate two conjectured spectral graph theoretic strengthenings of Turán's theorem.Let µ 1 ≥ . . .≥ µ n denote the eigenvalues of a graph G with n vertices, m edges and clique number ω(G).The concise version of Turán's theorem is that n/(n − d) is a lower bound for the clique number ω(G), where d is the average degree.Our first conjecture is that d can be replaced in this bound with √ s + , where s + is the sum of the squares of the positive eigenvalues.We prove this conjecture for triangle-free, weakly perfect and Kneser graphs and for almost all graphs.We have also used various software tools to search for a counter-example.Nikiforov proved a spectral version of Turán's theorem that

µ 2  1 ≤ 2m(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) ,

and Bollobás and Nikiforov conjectured that for G = K n µ 2 1 + µ 2 2 ≤ 2m(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) .

For our second conjecture, we propose that for all graphs (µ 2 1 + µ 2 2 ) in this inequality can be replaced by the sum of the squares of the ω(G) largest eigenvalues, provided they are positive.We prove the conjecture for weakly perfect, Kneser, and classes of strongly regular graphs.We also provide experimental evidence and describe how the bound can be applied.Liu and Ning [20] published a wide-ranging paper entitled "Unsolved Problems in spectral graph theory", and these two conjectures were placed second and fourth in their list of such problems.


Notation

Let G be a graph with no isolated vertices, with n vertices, m edges, average degree d, chromatic number χ(G) and clique number ω(G).We also let A denote the adjacency matrix of G and let µ = µ 1 ≥ µ 2 ≥ . . .≥ µ n denote the eigenvalues of A. The inertia of A is the ordered triple (n + , n − , n 0 ), where n + , n − and n 0 are the numbers counting multiplicities of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues of A, respectively.Let
s + = n + i=1 µ 2 i and s − = n i=n−n − +1 µ 2 i .
Note that:
n i=1 µ 2 i = tr(A 2 ) = 2m = s + + s − .
2 Replacing µ 2 with s +

The maximum eigenvalue µ appears in many spectral bounds on graph parameters.For instance, Edwards and Elphick [9] proved that 2m 2m − µ 2 ≤ χ(G), and Ando and Lin [1] proved a conjecture due to Wocjan and Elphick [25] that
2m 2m − s + = 1 + s + s − ≤ χ(G).
Coutinho and Spier [7] recently proved the stronger result that 1 + s + /s − ≤ χ v (G), the vector chromatic number.

As another example of replacing µ 2 with s + , Hong [18] proved for graphs with no isolated vertices that µ 2 ≤ 2m − n + 1, and Elphick et al. [10] conjectured that for all connected graphs s + ≤ 2m − n + 1.Similarly, Favaron et al. [12] proved that ω(G) ≤ 2m/µ, and Wu and Elphick [26] strengthened this result by proving that χ(G) ≤ 2m/ √ s + (note that both ω(G) ≤ χ(G) and 1/ √ s + ≤ 1/µ).As a fourth example of replacing µ 2 with s + , Stanley [23] proved that
µ ≤ √ 8m + 1 − 1 2 ,
and Wu and Elphick [26] used the result of Ando and Lin [1] to prove the stronger bound that
√ s + ≤ √ 8m + 1 − 1 2 .
So in all of these cases we can (at least conjecturally) strengthen known spectral bounds by replacing µ 2 with s + .The next section considers the same replacement for a well known lower bound for the clique number.


Conjectured strengthening of Wilf 's bound

The concise version of Turán's theorem is that n/(n−d) ≤ ω(G).Wilf [24] strengthened this bound by proving that:
n n − µ ≤ ω(G).(1)
This bound was further strengthened by Nikiforov [21] who proved the following conjecture of Edwards and Elphick [9].
2m 2m − µ 2 ≤ ω(G).(2)
Wocjan and Elphick [25] noted that
2m 2m − s + ≤ ω(G).

Kneser graphs

The Kneser graph KG p,k is the graph whose vertices correspond to the k-element subset of a set of p elements, in which two vertices are joined if and only if the corresponding sets are disjoint.It is well known that:
n = p k , ω(KG p,k ) = p k , 2m = p k p − k k and p ≥ 2k.
The eigenvalues (see Godsil and Royle [13]) are :
(−1) i p − k − i k − i with multiplicity p i − p i − 1
, for i = 0, 1, 2, . . ., k.


Proof for the case k ≤ 2

Proof.The Kneser graphs with k = 1 are complete graphs.The Kneser graphs with k = 2 are strongly regular, with only three distinct eigenvalues.For these graphs
n = p 2 , ω(KG p,2 ) = p 2 , 2m = p 2 p − 2 2 and p ≥ 4.
The eigenvalues with k = 2 are :
(−1) i p − 2 − i 2 − i with multiplicity p i − p i − 1 , for i = 0, 1, 2.
Note that p −1 is defined to equal zero.We are seeking to prove that:
n n − √ s + ≤ p − 1 2 ≤ p 2 = ω(KG p,2

s + = 2m − s − ≤ n 2 (p − 3) 2 (p − 1) 2 = p 2 (p − 3) 2 4 .

p 2 p − 2 2 − (p − 1) p − 3 1 2 ≤ p 2 (p − 3) 2 4 .
Simple algebra reduces this to

which is true for all p ≥ 4.


Proof for the case k ≥ 3

Proof.For k ≥ 3 we can adopt a less detailed approach.We are seeking to prove that:
n n − √ s + ≤ n n − √ 2m ≤ p − k + 1 k ≤ p k = ω(KG p,k )

√ 2m(k + s + 1) ≤ n(s + 1).
Then squaring both sides and substituting values for 2m and n gives that:
(k + s + 1) 2 ((k + s)!) 2 ≤ (2k + s)!s!(s + 1) 2 .
With s = 0 this is true for all k ≥ 3.With s ≥ 1 and k ≥ 3 the inequality becomes stronger.


Proof for almost all graphs

We use the Erdős-Renyi random graph G p (n), which consists of all graphs with n vertices in which edges are chosen independently with probability p. Bollobás and Erdős [4] proved that the clique number is almost always x or x + 1 where
x = 2 log n log(1/p) + O(log log n).
Since almost all graphs have all degrees very close to n/2 we let p = 0.5.Therefore
s + ≤ 2m ≈ n 2 /2.
So for almost all graphs:
n n − √ s + ≤ n n − n/ √ 2 ≈ 3.4 < 2 log n log 2 ≈ ω(G).
4 Generalising a conjecture due to Bollobás and Nikiforov

We propose the following conjecture that provides a lower bound for ω(G) using up to ω(G) eigenvalues.It considers the sum of the squares of the ω(G) largest eigenvalues, provided they are positive.Conjectures 2 and 3 were placed fourth and third respectively in [20].

Conjecture 2. For any non-empty graph G, the clique number ω(G) satisfies the bound
µ 2 1 + µ 2 2 + . . . + µ 2 ℓ ≤ 2m(ω(G) − 1) ω(G)
, where ℓ = min (n + , ω(G)).

This conjecture is exact, for example, for complete regular multipartite graphs.

The obvious question is: why do we set ℓ = min (n + , ω(G))?The answer, referring to Section 4.2.1 below, is that if ℓ = n + then we have a lower bound for 2m(χ − 1)/χ, so ℓ ≤ n + .Below we provide an example showing that ℓ must be chosen such that ℓ ≤ ω(G).Conjecture 2 strengthens the following conjecture due to Bollobás and Nikiforov [5]:

Conjecture 3 (Bollobás and Nikiforov).Let G be a K ω(G)+1 -free graph of order at least ω(G) + 1 with m edges.Then
µ 2 1 + µ 2 2 ≤ 2m(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) .(4)
As noted in [5], Conjecture 3 is not valid for complete graphs.The conjecture also places a lot of emphasis on µ 2 .Zhang [27] proved Conjecture 3 for regular graphs, using that µ 2 > 0 in his proof.Lin, Ning and Wu [19] proved the conjecture for triangle-free graphs.It should be noted that the cycle C 7 provides an example of a triangle-free graph for which
µ 2 1 + µ 2 2 + µ 2 3 > 2m(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) .
This example demonstrates that ℓ must be chosen such that ℓ ≤ ω(G).It should be noted that there exist graphs G with n + < ω(G) < χ(G).For example, the complement of the folded 7-cube on 64 vertices has n + = 8, ω(G) = 22 and χ(G) = 32.

The cycle C 5 provides an example of a triangle-free graph for which µ 2 n + µ 2 n−1 > m.A proof of Conjecture 2 or 3 will therefore need to incorporate asymmetry between positive and negative eigenvalues.For example, the proof in [19] uses that µ 1 ≥ −µ n .This contrasts with the symmetry between positive and negative eigenvalues to be found in some lower bounds for the chromatic number ([1], [11], [25]).

We have the following experimental and theoretical results to support the conjecture.As proved in Section 4.2.1, Conjecture 2 is true when ω(G) = χ(G).In searching for a counterexample we therefore focus on graphs with ω(G) < χ(G).We also seek proofs for graph families with ω(G) < χ(G), but it is hard to find such families where both ω(G) and the spectrum are known.

There are over 3000 graphs with ω(G) < χ(G) in the Wolfram Mathematica database with up to 50 vertices, and we found no counterexample.For about 35% of these graphs, Conjecture 2 outperforms Conjecture 3.

Special strongly regular graphs (SRGs)

We do not know how to prove Conjecture 2 for all SRGs, but below are proofs for two classes of SRGs.Let G = SRG(n, d, λ, µ) with restricted eigenvalues r > s.

Cvetkovic [8] proved that ω(G) ≤ min (n
µ i ≤−1 + 1, n µ i ≥−1 )
, where n µ i ≤−1 and n µ i ≥−1 denote the number of eigenvalues ≤ −1 and ≥ −1 respectively.Therefore for SRGs:
ω(G) ≤ min (n − + 1, n + ) ≤ n + ; so ℓ = min (n + , ω(G)) = ω(G).
Therefore Conjecture 2 simplifies as follows for SRGs:
d 2 + (ω(G) − 1)r 2 ≤ nd(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) ,
which provides a quadratic inequality for ω(G).

For example the Schlafli graph SRG (27,16,10,8) has spectrum (16 1 , 4 6 , −2 20 ) and ω(G) = 6, so Conjecture 2 becomes:
d 2 + (ω(G) − 1)r 2 = 336 ≤ nd(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) = 360.
The difficulty of proving Conjecture 2 or 3 for SRGs is that we do not have a formula for the clique number, although it is known that for all SRGs, ω(G) ≤ 1 − d/s ≤ χ(G).

Roberson [22] demonstrated experimentally that numerous SRGs have ω(G) = 1 − d/s < χ(G), which he terms Type C SRGs.We can prove Conjecture 2 for these SRGs as follows.

Proof.We wish to show that
d 2 + (ω(G) − 1)r 2 = d 2 − dr 2 s ≤ nd(ω(G) − 1) ω(G) = nd 2 d − s .(8)
Haemers [16] and others have noted that (d − r)(d − s) = n(d + rs), and µ = rs + d, so (8) simplifies to: rµ ≤ ds(s + 1).(9) First, if λ ≤ µ, then |r| ≤ |s|, so in this case the inequality (9) easily follows from µ ≤ d and r ≤ s(s + 1).Hence, we may assume that λ > µ, which in particular implies that f < g, where f and g are the multiplicities of the eigenvalues r and s, respectively.Now, using the bound ω(G) ≤ min(n − + 1, n + ) = 1 + min(f, g) = 1 + f , combined with the assumption that G is a Type C SRG, and so ω(G) = 1 − d s , we obtain that G must satisfy d + sf ≤ 0.

We will now assume for contradiction that (9) does not hold for G, and show that this assumption leads to d + sf > 0, in direct contradiction to (10).Indeed, using the identities µ = rs + d and d + rf + sg = 0, the negated form of (9) becomes r(rs + d) > ds(s + 1) = (−rf − sg)s(s + 1).

Expanding both sides of the preceding inequality and rearranging gives
r(d + sf ) > −r 2 s − rf s 2 − s 3 g − s 2 g.
We focus now on the right-hand side of this inequality.Since by assumption g > f , and clearly g ≥ 1, we find that
−r 2 s − rf s 2 − s 3 g − s 2 g > −g(r 2 s + rs 2 + s 3 + s 2 ) = −g(rs(r + s) + s 2 (s + 1)).
But the last expression in the preceding line is positive, since by our assumption r + s = λ − µ > 0, so both the terms rs(r + s) and s 2 (s + 1) are negative, making the expression in parentheses negative.Thus, combined with the previous inequality, we have r(d + sf ) > 0, and since r > 0, also d + sf > 0, completing the contradiction.

Conclusions

The question posed by Conjecture 1 is whether Wilf's bound provides another example where µ 2 can be replaced by s + ?If so, are there further bounds in which this strengthening can be made?

The question posed by Conjecture 2 is whether the sign of eigenvalues is relevant to lower bounds for the clique number, since it is this refinement to Conjecture 3 that enables Conjecture 2 to be exact for K n , whereas K n is excluded in Conjecture 3. Can Zhang's [27] elegant proof of Conjecture 3 for regular graphs be adapted to enable a proof of Conjecture 2 for regular graphs?
