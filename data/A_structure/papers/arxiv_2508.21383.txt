Introduction

Let H be a Krull monoid or a Krull domain with class group G and suppose that each class contains a prime divisor.Then each (non-zero and non-invertible) element a ∈ H has a factorization into atoms, say a = u 1 • . . .• u k , where k is the factorization length and u 1 , . . ., u k are atoms (irreducible elements) of H.The set L(a) ⊂ N of all possible factorization lengths k is called the length set of a. Length sets and all parameters controlling their structure (such as elasticities and sets of distances) are the best investigated invariants in factorization theory (for a survey, we refer to [20]).To begin with the extremal cases, we have |L(a)| = 1 for all relevant a ∈ H if and only if |G| ≤ 2. If G is infinite, then every finite nonempty subset of N ≥2 occurs as a length set of some a ∈ H. Now suppose that G is finite with |G| ≥ 3. Then the length sets can be arbitrarily large, but they are all finite and well-structured (Proposition 2.2).Roughly speaking, they are sort of generalized arithmetic progressions.In addition, it is known that almost all length sets are intervals (Proposition 3.1).

For a finite nonempty set L ⊂ N, we denote by ρ(L) = max L/ min L the elasticity of L, and by ρ(H), defined as the supremum of ρ L(a) over all a of H, the elasticity of H.It is well-known that ρ(H) = D(G)/2, where D(G) denotes the Davenport constant of G (see Subsection 2.2).Recent research has seen much interest on the structure of length sets with maximal elasticity.It has turned out that these length sets have a much simpler structure than length sets in general (compare Propositions 2.2 and 2.4).

We continue the investigation of length sets with maximal elasticity and proceed in the more general setting of transfer Krull monoids (see Subsection 2.3).In analogy to the results on general length sets, we show that almost all length sets with maximal elasticity are intervals.We formulate the first of our main results.

Theorem 1.1.Let H be a transfer Krull monoid over a finite abelian group G with |G| ≥ 3. Suppose that G has the following Property P.

P. There are g 1 , g 2 ∈ G and minimal zero-sum sequences U 1 , U 2 over G such that
|U 1 | = |U 2 | = D(G), g 1 g 2 | U 1 , and (g 1 + g 2 ) | U 2 .
Then there exists some a * ∈ H such that for all elements a ∈ H with ρ(L(a)) = ρ(H), the length set L(a * a) is an interval with elasticity ρ(H).

In general, length sets L(a) with elasticity ρ(H) need not be intervals (in other words, the above statement does not hold for a * = 1; see Proposition 2.4).The proof of Theorem 1.1 will be given in Section 3. We provide two corollaries stating that almost all (in a combinatorial sense) length sets with maximal elasticity are intervals (Corollaries 3.8 and 3.9).Then, in Section 4, we study Property P. Theorem 4.2 shows that Property P is satisfied by cyclic groups of odd order, by groups of rank two, by p-groups, and others.The only known groups, for which Property P does not hold, are cyclic groups of even order.Our second main result shows that also the statement of Theorem 1.1 does not hold for them.But, their length sets still satisfy a quite strong structural property.

Theorem 1.2.Let H be a transfer Krull monoid over a cyclic group G of even order |G| ≥ 4.

1.There is no element a ∈ H with maximal elasticity such that max L(a) − 1 ∈ L(a).
2. If |G| + 1 / ∈ P, then there exist a * ∈ H and M ∈ N 0 such that for all a ∈ H with ρ(L(a)) = ρ(H), L(a * a) ∩ [min L(a * a), max L(a * a) − M ] is an interval and ρ(L(a * a)) has elasticity ρ(H).
The statement of Theorem 1.2.2 does not hold if |G| + 1 is a prime smaller than or equal to 11, see Remark 5.3.
