Self-adjoint quantization of Stäckel integrable systems
21 August 20251C8FAA8B09B6ADD8BC7EAAD3F4DF1A5610.1088/1751-8121/adf786Received 13 February 2025; revised 3 July 2025 Accepted for publication 4 August 2025separation of variablesStäckel systemsquantization
We show that quadratic Hamiltonians in involution coming from a Stäckel system are quantizable, in the sense that one can construct commutative selfadjoint operators whose symbols are the quadratic Hamiltonians.Moreover, they allow multiplicative separation of variables.This proves a conjecture explicitly formulated in Błaszak et al (2016 Ann.Phys., NY 371 460-77).

Introduction

We work in a simply-connected neighborhood W ⊂ R n with coordinates x 1 , . .., x n and denote the corresponding momenta by p 1 , . . ., p n .

It is known that given a nondegenerate n × n matrix S such that for every i the components S ij in the i row are functions of x i only, one can construct a family of functions H 1 , . . ., H n : T * W → R, given by (1), which commute with respect to the standard Poisson bracket
{H, F} := n i =1 ∂H ∂p i ∂F ∂x i − ∂H ∂x i ∂F ∂p i .
This construction, known as the Stäckel construction, appeared already in [8, sections 13-14], see also discussion in [9, pp 703-705] and was later independently found and intensively studied by Stäckel in e.g.[12], and further by e.g.Eisenhart [7].

Let us recall the construction.Consider the functions H α , α = 1, . . ., n, given by the following system of linear equations

It is known that the functions H α are in involution, that is, {H α , H β } = 0, and that they are functionally independent almost everywhere, so they generate an integrable system.We will call them Hamiltonians.They are clearly quadratic in the momenta p.

By quantization of the integrable system generated by H 1 , H 2 , . . ., H n we understand a system of commutative second order differential operators Ĥ1 , Ĥ2 , . . ., Ĥn such that for every i = 1, . . ., n the symbol of Ĥi is H i .We say that the quantization is self-adjoint, if all the operators Ĥi are self-adjoint with respect to the following inner product on the space of compactly supported smooth real-valued functions on W:

We denote the symmetric (2, 0)-tensor corresponding to the functions H α by H ij (α) .In the coordinates x i , H ij (α) is given by the diagonal matrix and its ith diagonal component H ii (α) equals ∆αi det(S) , where we denote by ∆ ij the components of the comatrix of S.

Our first result is the following theorem:
Theorem 2.
For the Stäckel integrable system constructed by an arbitrary Stäckel matrix S and for the function ϕ := det(S), the second order differential operators
Ĥα = i,j 1 ϕ ∂ ∂x i H ij (α) ϕ ∂ ∂x j , (5)
commute.

One more special case is related to orthogonal separation of variables in spaces of constant curvature, that is, when the Stäckel matrix is such that one of the Hamiltonians H α , say H 1 , is the kinetic energy corresponding to a metric g of constant sectional curvature.Recall that for metrics of constant curvature, the Robertson condition is trivially fulfilled, so the quantization corresponding to ϕ = | det(g)| produces commutative operators, see e.g.[1,2].The corresponding Stäckel matrices were described in [5, theorem 1.4].

Clearly, in all the cases above, elementary transformations with the rows of the Stäckel matrix correspond to taking linear combinations of the corresponding integrals and do not affect quantization.
