Photonic Horn Jets and Whispering Gallery Modes in Dielectric Micro-Cylinders With Two Point-Source Illumination

The interaction of light with micro-scale dielectric structures has garnered significant attention in recent years due to its potential applications in various fields ranging from photonics to biomedical imaging.In this context, this study investigates the generation of twin photonic jets from a dielectric micro-cylinder illuminated by two point-sources.The effect of different parameters on the jet's shape profile and length was analyzed.Those parameters include the cylinder's refractive index and radius, the source distance from the cylinder, and the vertical displacement between the sources.Innovatively, this study introduces the concept of 'Horn Jets' to characterize the twin photonic jets resulting from the illuminated dielectric micro-cylinder, marking a significant conceptual advancement in the field.Moreover, the generation of whispering gallery modes (WGMs) is investigated.The outcomes obtained through the spectral element method highlight the possibility of generating WGMs by meticulously selecting the cylinder's radius and refractive index, under the scenario of two-point-source illuminations.The results show that the magnitude of the WGMs resonance significantly lies on the vertical displacement due to the resulting interference pattern's dark and bright fringes with the cylinder edges.

[22] investigates the generation of t-PHs from two coherent illuminations of a single micro-cylinder; the authors find that t-PHs can be produced when the offset angle between the illuminating plane waves is large and explore properties like how the t-PH curvature depends on illumination parameters.Moreover, the study finds that asymmetry in the t-PHs can also be achieved by modifying the position of the cylinder.Other approaches were reported to generate the t-PHs [23], [24], [25].Ref. [23] used a polarized plane wave that illuminates a double-layer fan-shaped micro-cylinder to generate tunable twin photonic hooks (t-PHs); the study shows that the properties of t-PHs can be easily tuned by controlling the opening angles, sizes of the double-layer fan-shaped micro-cylinder, and surrounding medium refractive index.While in [24], the authors  studied a method to generate t-PHs from a single twin-ellipse micro-cylinder particle illuminated by a single plane wave light, the curvature, length, and intensity of the photonic hooks can be tuned by changing the refractive index and shape of the micro-cylinder particle.A liquid-immersed core-shell structure is also proposed to allow flexible tuning of the photonic hooks.Ref. [25] investigates two in-line micro-cylinders illuminated with a polarized plane wave to generate the t-PHs; the study states that the shape profiles of the t-PHs can be modulated by varying the two micro-cylinders' diameters and spacing.

Cylindrical and spherical shape particles can act as ring resonators under appropriate illumination conditions, with precise selection of the cylinder dimensions and the refractive index of the cylinder and the medium [31].The illuminating wave will be reflected inside the cylinder, which transforms the cylinder into an optical micro-cavity.Those generated resonance modes are called whispering gallery modes (WGMs) [32].Optical WGM micro-resonators are valuable for biological and physical sensing and optical communication [33], [34], [35], [36], [37], [38].

In this work, we investigate the generation of twin photonic horn jets (t-PHJs) by illuminating a dielectric micro-cylinder with two point-sources.The effect of different parameters, such as the cylinder's refractive index, radius, and source distances, were investigated using the spectral element method.Compared to presented literature, this article considers a simple structure for the t-PHJs generation with no complicated particle structures,  and it uses simple two point-sources without any restrictions on their characteristics.It is noteworthy to mention that a new term, "horn jets," is introduced for the twin photonics jets having the hook characteristics.

Most studies consider plane-wave illumination that is ideal and almost impossible to achieve in real-life applications.However, point sources, both single and double sources, are practically achievable as they present the real sources when they are even relatively close to the cylinder.In general, this study requires no specific restrictions for the two-point sources.

Furthermore and more importantly, we investigate the generation of the WGMs in dielectric micro-cylinder under two point-source illuminations.Different parameters were explored to find their impact on the WGMs characteristics.It is worth mentioning that the WGMs generation was not considered in the previous research with two point-source illuminations.The rest of this paper is organized as follows: Section II introduces the structure model and methodology.Section III discusses the simulation results for the t-PHJs and WGMs in Section III-A and III-B, respectively.Finally, conclusions are presented in Section IV.


II. STRUCTURE MODEL AND METHODOLOGY

The simulation model consists of a dielectric micro-cylinder (with a radius of R and a refractive index of n).Two identical point sources (separated by a distance of 2 s) are considered to illuminate the micro-cylinder (see Fig. 1).The sources are located at a horizontal distance of w from the cylinder's surface.It is important to point that n is a normalized refractive index with respect to surrounding medium, likewise, the dimensions including R are normalized with the incident wavelength (λ).

As the domain is unbounded, the perfectly matched layer (PML), whose mathematical formulation is introduced in reference [39], is utilized for domain truncation.Here, each source (Green's function) obeys the Helmholtz equation:
∇ 2 E inc + k 2 E inc = d( r i )(1)
where, d is the Dirac delta function, k is the wave number, and E inc stands for the incident field due to the point source at r i (i = 1 and 2 for the two point sources).Since we are addressing a two-dimensional problem, the fields are independent of the z coordinate.Therefore, the line source transforms into a point source, and the propagation direction is perpendicular to the z − axis.The exact solution to (1) can be expressed in terms of the zero-order Hankel function of the second kind H

The following frequency-domain Helmholtz equation must be satisfied and solved for the scattered field (E sca ) inside the computational domain (except the PML):
∇ 2 E sca + (nk) 2 E sca = k 2 1 − n 2 E inc (3)
Discretizing the electromagnetic domain by the Spectral Element Method (SEM) into elements requires more attention than in typical cases of finite difference or finite element methods.This is because irregular domains can only be discretized by deformed quadrilateral elements [40], [41].A special type of meshing grid, namely Gauss-Legendre-Lobatto (GLL) grids, is employed in the current work.To achieve the best accuracy offered by the SEM, reference [12] provides the optimum values of the attenuation factor associated with the number of nodes in GLLs.For the SEM formulation and SEM meshing, the reader may refer to references [39], [42], respectively, for further details.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

This section discusses the generation of the t-PHJs and WGMs in a dielectric micro-cylinder under two point-source illumination.Furthermore, it presents the effect of different parameters on the shape profile of the t-PHJs and WGMs.


A. Twin Photonic Horn Jets (t-PHJs) Generation

In order to investigate the twin photonics horn jets t-PHJs generation, two point sources are located at a relatively close distance from the dielectric micro-cylinder with refractive index (n) and radius (R), as shown in Fig. 1.Fig. 3 shows the evolution of twin photonic horn jets as the interfered waves from the sources illuminate the dielectric cylinder.In this figure, n = 1.4,R = 2.5λ, w = 7.25λ, and s = 5.5λ.As can be seen from the figure, t-PHJs are clearly formed with the shadow of the micro-cylinder being in between and a maximum normalized electric field magnitude of 0.07.

Fig. 4 illustrates a more pronounced generation of t-PHJs, showcasing an evident resemblance to a horn shape rather than a hook shape traditionally described in literature.Upon meticulous examination of the depicted shape, it becomes apparent that the term "horn" aptly characterizes the morphology of these photonic jets.Additionally, Fig. 4 includes a 3D plot, offering a detailed visualization of the generated t-PHJs' shape and the  magnitude of their associated field.Investigating the impact of various parameters on t-PHJs, including the radius and refractive index of the dielectric micro-cylinder, as well as the vertical distance between the sources, holds significant merit.This study aims to comprehensively analyze how altering these parameters influences the characteristics of t-PHJs.

In particular, we will examine the t-PHJs resulting from the illumination of two sources across different refractive indices for the dielectric micro-cylinder, specifically n = 1.45, 1.5, 1.55, and 1.65.The corresponding findings are depicted in Fig. 5, shedding light on the nuanced variations in t-PHJs morphology and behavior under varying experimental conditions.The findings illustrated in Fig. 5 highlight the significant influence of the micro-cylinder's refractive index on the shape profiles of t-PHJs.For n = 1.45, 1.5, 1.55, and 1.65, the t-PHJ lengths are 4.8λ, 4.5λ, 2.95λ, and 0.89λ, respectively.Notably, as n increases, the length of the shape profile of t-PHJs decreases, and the distinctive horn shape profile diminishes.Additionally, for refractive indices of 1.55 and 1.65, each t-PHJ appears divided into two distinct parts.

Furthermore, we'll investigate the impact of varying the vertical separation distance between the two sources.Fig. 6 displays the diverse shape profiles of t-PHJs corresponding to different values of s (s = 5λ, 5.2λ, 7.2λ, and 8.2λ); the t-PHJ lengths are 4.5λ, 3.3λ, 2.5λ, and 0.89λ, respectively.The results depicted in Fig. 6 vividly demonstrate that as the distance between the sources increases, the shape profile of t-PHJs becomes progressively shorter.Additionally, with increasing distance, the characteristic horn shape of the profile dissipates.This phenomenon may stem from alterations in the interference patterns Authorized licensed use limited to the terms of the applicable license agreement with IEEE.Restrictions apply.between the source waves, as evidenced by the changes in the field magnitude depicted in Fig. 6, particularly in front of the micro-cylinder.Fig. 7 illustrates the profile shapes of t-PHJs for both R = 2.5λ and R = 3.5λ; the lengths of t-PHJ are 4.4λ and 3.5λ, and their corresponding maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are 0.071 and 0.069, respectively.Notably, the t-PHJs shape profile appears more pronounced in the case of R = 2.5λ.Additionally, Fig. 8 offers a more detailed insight into the total field magnitude and the electric field along the x-axis.Noteworthy is the observation that all oscillations of the field along the x-axis occur before and inside the micro-cylinder.As we can see from Fig. 8(c) and (d), the maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are 0.1 and 0.08, respectively.Post-cylinder, the field experiences a decrease, as the field is calculated along the center line passing through the micro-cylinder (with the cylinder center at (0,0)).Furthermore, there are no distinct maximum peaks of electric field magnitude observed along the x-axis, indicating the absence of resonance oscillation.

The discussed results and figures underscore the author's advocacy for adopting the term "horn" instead of "hook" to describe the generated twin photonic nano-jets.Across all figures, it is evident that the shape profile of these jets closely resembles a horn rather than a hook.While previous research efforts have utilized the term "hook" [23], [24], it becomes apparent that "horn" is a more fitting descriptor in other studies [22], [25].Hence, the authors recommend employing the term "twin photonic horn jets" (t-PHJ) to denote these shape profiles in future research endeavors.

Furthermore, the notable presence of t-PHJs resulting from the illumination of two sources on a dielectric micro-cylinder is evident.The shape profile of these t-PHJs is significantly influenced by various parameters, as demonstrated in the previously discussed results.Subsequently, we will delve into the generation of Whispering Gallery Modes (WGMs) under the illumination of two point sources.


B. Whispering Gallery Modes (WGMs) Generation

As previously discussed, under precise illumination conditions, the phenomenon of t-PHJs ceases, giving rise to the excitation of Whispering Gallery Modes (WGMs).This transition occurs only at specific combinations of refractive index (n) and micro-cylinder radius (R), leading to the emergence of resonances associated with WGMs [31].Fig. 9 facilitates a comparison between t-PHJs and WGMs.It is evident from the figure that even a minor change in the refractive index (Δn = 0.01) results in the disappearance of t-PHJs and the excitation of WGMs.Additionally, the electric field magnitude along the x-axis serves to illustrate this transformation from t-PHJs to WGMs.Specifically, in the case of t-PHJs, there are no discernible peaks in the electric field.Conversely, in the scenario of WGMs, clear peaks are observed.Fig 9(d) highlights this observation with two distinct sets of peaks, each representing the two rings of WGMs encircling the edges of the cylinder.At the cylinder's edges, close to and far from the sources, the maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are 0.165 and 0.19, respectively.As we noted, those magnitude values are higher than the t-PHJ values due to the resonance modes of the WGMs.Fig. 10 presents the results illustrating the formation of Whispering Gallery Modes (WGMs) in a more precise and comprehensible manner.Under the simulation parameters (n = 1.7,R = 3.5λ [31]), the dielectric micro-cylinder functions akin to a ring resonator, displaying resonances both inside and outside the cylinder's edges.At the cylinder's edges, close to and far from the sources, the maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are 0.51 and 0.45, respectively.As we can see, Authorized licensed use limited to the terms of the applicable license agreement with IEEE.Restrictions apply.those magnitude values are higher than those in Fig. 9. Notably, with the excitation of WGMs, the field intensity inside the cylinder diminishes significantly, approaching zero.It is worth mentioning that WGM can appear at different n and R subject to satisfying the resonance condition.For additional information, the reader may refer to [31] and [43].

Fig. 11 showcases the shape profile of WGMs for varying vertical distances between the sources (s = 2.25λ, 2.5λ, 2.75λ).At the cylinder's edges, close to and far from the sources, the maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are (0.51, 0.45), (0.49, 0.44), and (0.29, 0.27), respectively.As evident in the figure, as the separation distances between the sources decrease, the magnitude of WGMs resonance also diminishes;.This phenomenon arises from the influence of s on the interference pattern and the magnitude of fringes on the excited WGMs.The impact of the distance w on the behavior of WGMs is explored in Fig. 12, where the distance is increased to 30λ.As depicted, acceptable WGMs are still observed, indicating that the distance between the sources and the cylinder has minimal effect on the generation of WGMs.

An intriguing observation emerged at s = 3λ, where t-PHJs were generated while no WGMs were excited.Upon increasing s to values of 4.5λ, 5λ, and even 5.5λ, WGMs continued to be excited, with maximum normalized electric field magnitudes of 0.31, 0.55, and 0.19, respectively.These noteworthy findings are depicted in Fig. 13, prompting a deeper investigation into Authorized licensed use limited to the terms of the applicable license agreement with IEEE.Restrictions apply.

the underlying reasons for such behavior.Fig. 14 (left column) displays the illuminating wave pattern without the presence of the micro-cylinder, with the circle added solely for clarification purposes, across different y values.

Of particular significance in Fig. 14 is the positioning of the cylinder's edges concerning the interference pattern of the two sources.Notably, for s = 2.25λ and 5λ, the edges of the cylinder coincide with the dark fringes of the interference pattern.The corresponding maximum normalized electric field magnitudes are 0.55 and 0.54, respectively.Conversely, for s = 3λ, the outer edges of the cylinder fall within the bright fringes propagation path.This observation suggests that under suitable parameters (n and R), WGMs can occur when the cylinder's edges align with the propagation path of the interference pattern generated by the two sources.This phenomenon may arise from the resonance modes generated inside the cylinder and their interaction with the interference pattern of the sources.

To enhance the reader's understanding, it is crucial to highlight the following formula governing the constructive interference condition for resonance modes:
2π λ • n(2πR) = m(2π) (4)
Where m is an integer, λ is the source wavelength, n and R (cylinder refractive index and radius, respectively), while (4) suggests that the product of the radius and the refractive index is approximately constant, it overlooks several critical aspects of the resonance phenomena discussed in this article, such as field magnitudes and the number of rings (types of WGMs).More importantly, this formula alone cannot achieve the necessary fine-tuning of the micro-cylinder's radius or the refractive index.Additionally, (4) does not account for the field magnitudes when considering two-point source illumination.Furthermore, as evidenced by the figures related to WGMs, the distance between the sources significantly influences the emergence of WGMs or horn jets.For these reasons, this study's governing wave equation derived from Maxwell's equations and solved using SEM, which offers superior accuracy compared to other numerical methods, is an excellent choice to investigate WGM and horn jets.As a final observation, in Figs.11, 13, and 14, the twin faint field pattern observed in the cylinder's shadow is attributed to the presence of two point sources.Conversely, in single pointsource illumination, a distinct pattern featuring a single faint field emerges, as depicted in Fig. 15.

The study provides an in-depth analysis of the formation of whispering gallery modes (WGMs) under two-point source illumination in micro-cylinders.Our objective is to highlight this specific phenomenon and its underlying physics, aiming to enhance both practical applications and theoretical understanding.Moreover, the manuscript references numerous studies focused on the generation of WGMs using micro-cylindrical particles.


IV. CONCLUSION

This study investigates the generation of twin photonic horn jets (t-PHJs) from a dielectric micro-cylinder under the illumination of two point sources.The resulting twin nano jets exhibit a distinct horn-shaped profile, prompting the introduction of the term "horn jets" to describe this phenomenon.Our findings reveal that increasing the refractive index of the cylinder and the vertical separation between the sources leads to a reduction in the length of the horn jets and distortion of their shape profile.Subsequently, we explore the generation of Whispering Gallery Modes (WGMs) under the same illumination conditions.Our results demonstrate that the magnitude of WGMs resonance diminishes with an increase in the vertical displacement of the sources.This decrement is attributed to the interference pattern's interaction with the cylinder edges, specifically the dark and bright fringes.Notably, t-PHJs exhibit remarkable capabilities in intensely focusing light into subwavelength dimensions, showcasing robust field confinement and high transmission efficiency.These attributes render t-PHJs promising candidates for various applications, including super-resolution imaging, single-molecule spectroscopy, and optogenetics.Furthermore, t-PHJs present novel opportunities for advancing techniques in these fields, opening avenues for further research and innovation.
