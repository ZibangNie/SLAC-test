Related Work

Nagi et al [7] developed a CNN-based recognition system for gesture applications in human-robot interactions.Work on the realization of convolution neural networks for real-time translation of various hand gestures can be considered vital towards finding effective communications between humans and robots.In truth, the system predominantly deals with well-posed gestures and, at times, misinterprets some of the hand signs, which are parts of sign language.Van den Bergh [8] and his colleagues developed a hand posture recognition system by Haar wavelet-based feature extraction and database searching.They had to find a match for features extracted using Haar wavelets inside a database of gestures.They had excellent results, though in the study, they only tested for six classes of gestures.Kang et al. [9]have developed in support of CNN, a natural time recognition system for ASL, using 31 hand symbols, which are acquired by taking 31,000 images using the Creative Senz3D Camera towards the acquisition of depth maps.The system achieved an accuracy of around 85.49% by excluding numerous gestures, which mainly require hands to be gripped closer.Gesture recognition system, hand gesture interpretation, development of the system: The system was developed by Song et al. using the Creative Senz3D depth map camera to interpret hand gestures.Their recognition methodology was worthy of the whole solution, yet the equipment on which the research was done is relatively expensive and does not make it practical for everyday use.Salian et al. [4] developed a hand gesture recognition system using Haar-like features with a cascade of classifiers coupled with a convolutional neural network engine.They trained a cascade function with a huge number of positive and negative images, which made the observation of hand gestures quite effective.The case of Rahman et al. [10] affirms a model designed through SLR-Net with eight layers over the CNN architecture for the problem of identification of hand gestures, which could attain a performance of 67% for letters and 82.5% for MU HandImages ASL numbers using the developed ASL dataset.The developed system consists of 6 convolution layers, three pooling layers, and one fully connected layer and can recognize ASL and finger-spelling gestures.In their research, two datasets were employed: one that was generated by themselves and another dataset called MU HandImages ASL [12] (Vivek Bheda et al., 2010).In case of letters their method achieved 67% accuracy while raising this number up to 82.5% when dealing with number recognition as well (Bheda et al., 2010). .In this regard, Starner et al. [17] proposed two real-time ASL recognition systems.One was done using a desk-mounted camera with a recognition rate of 92%, and another was done using a cap-mounted camera, which reached 98% recognition.Both the two implementations that they used had a 40-word lexicon, results indicating the efficiency of the recognition of continuous ASL gestures was high.Suk et al. [15] developed a dynamic Bayesian network model for ASL recognition from video streams.His evaluated system, with ten isolated gestures, performed better-a recognition rate of more than 99%-using the observed skin extraction technique of modeling and motion tracking.Later, W. Huang et al. [13] designed a 3D Hopfield neural network to recognize hand gestures.The system processes a gestural hand-tracking process, feature extraction, and recognition; the average recognition rate reached 91% for 15 hand gestures.T. Starner et al. [14] developed a real-time system for recognizing sentence-level continuous ASL.The test systems were mounted on the desk with 92% accuracy and the other mounted in the form of a cap with 98% recognition.Both of these systems used the 40-word lexicon, and this approach showed effectiveness.

Another study employed deep learning, by designing a feed-forward neural network to classify a sign.Well-known image pre-processing methods require background subtraction, image normalization, image segmentation, and contrast optimization.Gabor filters used Principal component analysis for image feature extraction.Accuracy was achieved in this case is 98.5% [18].They developed a CNN-based hand gesture recognition over the fingerspelling data of Rasha et al. [19] research, showing the possibility of surpassing 99% recognition in 28 isolated gestures.Their system has shown itself to work with high accuracy during real-time ASL recognition, thereby promising CNN for gesture interpretation.But the generally recommended system is the hand gesture recognition application in which SSD, the Inception v3, and the SVM are combined.Their system had pointed toward the automatic recognition of hands from camera images and the classification of those into 24 different ASL signs.Thus, interestingly, the system shows the incorporation of different machinelearning models into that just for effective gesture recognition.A simplified sign language recognition model using the MediaPipe framework and state-ofthe-art machine learning algorithms was proposed in this work by Arpita Halder et al. Data Collection

In this paper, we use ASL datasets to analyze the performance of system.The ASL data set is the ASL Alphabet data set [21], which contains over 87,000 samples of 29 classes: 26 for the letters A-Z, and three special characters: delete, nothing, and space.Some samples of the data set are shown in Fig.The large size of such a dataset would enable the proposed architecture in this work to learn features supported by a great deal of data during training.Thus, it would genuinely learn even small details of hand movements and subtle differences between ASL signs.Using a data set of this size allows the system to grasp the subtle nuances and features between one another.


Detection of hand landmarks using MediaPipe

Mediapipe: MediaPipe hand keypoint detection runs in a machine-learning pipeline.The models in the MediaPipe ML pipeline can execute palm detection on an entire image in a single forward pass to output a bounding box around the hand.This is followed by accurate 3D hand keypoint [23] detection on the region of interest.In the same line, the ML pipeline goes on to crop the hands using the hand landmarks from the previous frames, so detection of the palm will be applied to localize only if the last step failed.Figure 3: 21 hand landmarks can be tracked using MediaPipe's hand landmark detector.This can be used for hand gesture classification, as shown by Arsheldy Alvin et al. [24], using both MediaPipe and the K-Nearest Neighbors algorithm.Here, in the use of MediaPipe is for extracting hand key points, while the training/deployment is based on CNN to detect the applied ML algorithm.It runs on both CPU and GPU; MediaPipe uses no added-on processing power.In the MediaPipe hand tracking library that is used to generate the data, there are almost 21 critical features identified in the hand.The data contains all 26 alphabets and three unique characters, namely delete, nothing, and space, corresponding to hand gestures with 30 frames of each hand gesture.Also, the data constitutes 30 variations of each alphabet.[22]

CNN Model Description

The subsequent action involves employing the dataset to train the CNN Model.

CNN: Convolutional Neural Networks belong to the class of deep learning neural network architecture.This is where computer vision using artificial intelligence is made for the computer to be able to read photo or image data.The architecture design is set for CNN to ensure good performance and consideration of the matching chosen convolution layers and the number of neurons.No international standard has been accepted for selecting the count of neurons and convolution layers.Some of the CNN's various results are shown based on the variation of the number of layers and the number of neurons within each layer.Here, we have designed a maximization of recognition accuracybased CNN.The newly proposed CNN consists of three convolution layers, two pooling layers, and a fully connected layer, apart from the input-output layers.The primary significant steps of the proposed ASL recognition method are illustrated in Fig. 6.

Figure 5: Architecture of CNN [33] Input Layer: The pre-processed images are fed directly into the network via its input layer.An input layer is a very first layer of a neural network, which receives the raw input data-a kind of data, be it images or just text.The neurons in this layer represent the features or dimensions of the input data, and the number of neurons in this layer is derived from the shape of the input data.In most cases, there is no activation function implemented to the input layer, making the first stop in the processing pipeline of the neural network.

Convolution Layer: The first layer is the convolution in the CNN: one of the basic building blocks aimed at feature extraction from input data.The kernels in the salient feature extraction layer apply themselves to the input data from the input signal via forward and backward propagations.In our study, convolutional layer convolves over input data to provide filters.It computes a dot product along its sliding window with local regions, producing feature maps belonging to different patterns or characteristics of the input.Key features of a convolutional layer are weight sharing and the fact the filter is slided across the input.Spatial hierarchies of features are captured in the now evident feature maps.Optionally available parameters include padding, which can control the spatial dimensions of the output feature maps.In general, convolutional layers extract effective local patterns from input data, which allow CNNs to learn a hierarchy of representations for various tasks like image recognition or gesture recognition.
Y i,j,k = u,v,c X (i×S+u−P ),(j×S+v−P ),c ×W u,v,c,k +b k (3)
,where Y i,j,k is the value of the output feature map at position (i ,j ) in the kth channel.X (i×S+u−P ),(j×S+v−P ),c is the value of the input feature map at position (i×S +u−P, j ×S +v −P ) in the cth channel.X (i×S+u−P ),(j×S+v−P ),c is the weight parameter of the filter at position (u,v) in the cth input channel and the kth output channel.b k is the bias term associated with the kth output channel.F represents the spatial dimension of the filters.S is the stride.P is the amount of zero padding applied to the input feature maps.C in is the number of input channels.

Activation Function: Activation functions decide in which way the next node should get activated.We use the ReLU activation function [27], which makes all the negative numbers zeros and retains the positive numbers as it is.The choice of ReLU I make directly is because this model is getting trained.As defined, during training, ReLU is many orders of magnitude faster than bounded activation functions like the sigmoid or hyperbolic tangent function [28] and allows training deep models without suffering from the vanishing gradients problem.Definitions of a function like this:
ReLU(x) = max(0,x)(4)
where x refers to the input to a neuron.

Pooling Layer: In this case, the process of pooling is integral to the working of a deep-learning system.It allows for super-fast training in a CNN, reduces the network memory size, and reduces the number of connections between the convolutional layers.We have implemented max-pooling in this regard.Through max pooling, a sliding window is swept across an input space to output the most significant value within that window.We have used a window size of 2x2 for max pooling, assuming that the stride size would have been taken as 2, so there would have been no problem of overlap.The formula of max pooling is:
N out = floor N in − F S + 1(5)
where, N o ut: The output dimension (height or width) of the feature map after the convolution operation.

N i n: The input dimension (height or width) of the feature map before the convolution operation.F: The size of the filter (kernel) used in the convolution operation.If the filter is square, F is the length of one side.S: The stride of the convolution operation, which is the number of pixels by which the filter moves at each step.

Global Average Pooling Layer: The global average pooling (GAP) layer is a lot like the max-pooling layer.The dissimilarity is that the mean value, instead of the maximum value, replaces the whole region.Global average pooling reduces the number of total model parameters, which might prevent overfitting and improve generalization.These coefficients are very effective in providing spatial information for the network and are used in tasks like object localization and detection.Global average pooling keeps the important spatial information of feature maps in the CNNs but reduces their dimensionality.
GAP(X) = 1 H × W H i=1 W j=1 X ij(6)
X ij is the value of the feature map X at spatial location (i,j) and channel c,H represents the height dimension of the feature map, while W denotes its width.

Fully Connected Layer The Fully Connected (FC) layer is fed with the MAP features generated by the GAP layer, to which it applies the Hard Max Layer.In the FC layer, neurons connect with neurons from other layers in one-to-one style.Because that's what its filters of size 1x1 represent, one may think of fc layer as a convolution layer.
Y = H i=1 W j=1 X ij × W ij + b (7)
where Y is the output of the fully connected layer, Xij are the input features from the previous layer, W ij are the weights connecting each input feature to the output neuron, b is the bias term, H and W represent the dimensions of the input feature map, denoting height and width, respectively.

Dropout Layer: Dropout is a regularization technique most widely applied to reduce overfitting in a neural network model, mainly in LSTMs.Each element is dropped to zero with a probability of per iteration.Training of a model tends to overfit, i.e., it overfits because the accuracy is way too high concerning the testing accuracy.Till today, an example of a dropout layer in the final fully connected layer of all the models, as in the picture above, helps save the model from overfitting [20] and, therefore, enhances the accuracy by randomly nulling the activation.The process conducted in this study used a Dropout Probability of 0.5 [21].

Training

In our study, Adam optimizer has been used, as it is an optimization algorithm adjusting learning rates in adaptive manners.We placed the learning rate at 0.001 because a smaller learning rate will make the dynamics of training stable.Besides, we adopted categorical cross-entropy loss function suitable for multi-classification tasks.This is a function that computes the difference between the predicted probability distribution and the true probability distribution of classes.Therefore, we picked a batch size of 32 for training the model since smaller batch sizes, like 32, are well balanced between memory consumption and computational efficiency.The number of epochs was also fixed at 200.Epochs determine the count of how many times an entire training dataset is passed forward and backward through the neural network.We use early stopping function to prevent overfitting and it monitores validation loss, and stop training if the metrics stop improving for a certain number of epochs.We train our model for as long as it can take, that is, 200 epochs, so that we can get improved accuracy on the test dataset.We usually use a validation split of 10%.

It does this by splitting up the validation data from the training set in such a way that it creates a form of monitoring performance during training, which represents 10% of the whole data.That validation set helps to do early stopping and hyperparameter fine-tuning to curb overfitting.Secondly, we instantiated two callback functions: EarlyStopping and ModelCheckpoint.The function 'EarlyStopping' stops the training of a model when there are no further improvements beyond a certain limit in order to avoid overfitting performance on the validation dataset.On the other hand, the 'ModelCheckpoint' function saves the weights of the model for later use by, let's say, the best model depending on validation performance.The accuracy of the trained model we get is 99.12%.The performance of the proposed model was evaluated for recognition of ASL sign language.

Discussion

This paper presents an emergent approach to sign language detection using the combined solutions by MediaPipe and Convolutional Neural Networks, which is used for interpreting hand gestures.It is really going to be important in assistive technology, aids for communication, and even human-computer interaction.CNNs process the image of the hand sign to be classified.Use MediaPipe to perform hand tracking.Data collection should initially be done, like recording of sign language gestures.After that, preparatory steps must also be done like normalization and augmentation.CNN architecture: convolutional layers for the extraction of features, and pooling layers for downsampling, combined with fully connected layers for classification.In general, the CNN architecture uses convolutional layers to help carry out features from the data.The pooling layers help in the downsampling process, while the fully connected layers are used to give the output after classification.Training optimizes model parameters with gradient-based algorithms, and metrics are evaluated to assess performance.The final trained model is now deployed into the real-time interpretation using MediaPipe for efficient hand tracking.Challenges: hand shape, movement, changes in lighting.Future research will be maintained to develop more robust models for wider applicative usage on sign language detections.

Conclusion

In short, the proposed ASL recognition model provides an accuracy of 99.12% under a real-time system.The classification, through the features of Mediapipe plus CNN as proposed in the model, is robust and effective in identifying the hand motion with the American Sign Language.In this study, we applied MediaPipe to identify landmarks of the hand; afterward, important input features are extracted for the inputs of the CNN model to capture spatial relationships and patterns of sign language gestures.The approach yielded higher classification performance and thus holds immense potential for use as a communication aid, educational tool, and human-computer interaction system.The idea can be further extended in future work: improving performance with the use of a more sophisticated CNN architecture, adding more contextual information, and expanding the dataset.


Future Work

In the future, researchers should focus on a database with different sign languages, dynamic symbols and constant sentences.Fundamentally, the model needs to be enhanced so that it is better capable at translating texts in real-time, employs both hands and remains secure amidst different environments.To achieve this it is necessary to integrate gesture recognition with NLP for text or speech translation and make the appropriate adjustments based on individual signing styles resulting from transfer learning processes.User studies are expedient for feedback and correction of ethical concerns on data privacy that would bring about the acceptance and ethical deployment of sign language detection systems.
