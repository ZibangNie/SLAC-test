Dispersive estimate for the fourth order Schrödinger operator with a class of scaling-critical magnetic potentials in dimension two was obtained by the construction of the corresponding resolvent kernel and the stationary phase method.

A typical example of A is given by the Aharonov-Bohm potential
A(x) = α|x| −1 (−x 2 , x 1 ), x = (x 1 , x 2 ) ∈ R 2 \ {0}, α ∈ R \ Z.
If we write A(x) = (A 1 , A 2 )(x), then the usual second order operator L A,2 = (−i∇ + A/|x|) 2 represents the quantum Hamiltonian of a non-relativistic electron interacting with the singular magnetic field B(x)
B(x) = ∂ ∂x 1 A 2 (x) |x| − ∂ ∂x 2 A 1 (x) |x| , x ∈ R 2 .
Since the potential A(x)  |x| is singular at zero, we should extend the operator L A,2 to a self-adjoint operator on L 2 (R 2 ) so that the Schrödinger semigroup e −itLA,4 generated by L A,4 (in the spectral sense) is well-defined.To be specific, we regard L A,2 as the Friedrichs extension of the symmetric operator (−i∇+A/|x|) 2 (initially defined on C ∞ c (R 2 \ {0})) throughout the whole paper.Hence, the spectrum of the Hamiltonian L A,4 consists only of the absolutely continuous part [0, +∞).If A ≡ 0, then L A,4 reduces to the free fourth order Schrödinger operator L 0,4 = ∆ 2 .By the Fourier transform, one can easily derive the sharp pointwise time-decay estimate for any x, y ∈ R d (see e.g.[2])

Remark 1.2.Theorem 1.1 is a natural generalization of the dispersive estimate (1.4) for the free operator ∆ 2 .We do not include an additional rough potential V (i.e.L A,4 + V with |V (x)| (1 + |x|) −β ) here since once we have proved the dispersive estimate (1.5) for the magnetic operator L A,4 , then, viewing L A,4 + V as a perturbation of L A,4 (similar to the treatment of ∆ 2 + V ), one may closely follow the steps of Li-Soffer-Yao [32] (with some obvious modifications) to obtain the dispersive estimate for L A,4 +V .The paper [32] is quite lengthy (but the idea is the classical perturbation method), so we only consider the purely magnetic case L A,4 to avoid repeating.Apart from the case of an additional perturbation V , there are also many interesting situations to be considered.For example, one may consider the dispersive or smoothing estimates for the general higher order Schrödinger operators (with electromagnetic potentials) in two and higher dimensions.
