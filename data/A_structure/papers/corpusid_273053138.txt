1 Supplementary Methods


Induced self-immunity assays

Overnight cultures of each producer strain in 50% THY and 50% CDM were pelleted at 3250×G for 5 minutes.The supernatants were removed and each pellet was resuspended in fresh CDM.An aliquot (80 l) of the washed overnight culture was then added to 5 mL of fresh THY and incubated until the culture reached mid-log phase.While the strains were growing, fresh induction and control plates were made by spreading 200 l of 1 mM MSP or 200 l of DMSO over the surface of THY agar plates, respectively.Upon reaching mid-log phase (OD 600 between 0.3 and 0.5), 20 l of each producer strain was spotted onto THY/MSP and THY/DMSO plates (=3-4) and allowed to dry for 20 minutes prior to overnight incubation, agar side up, at 37 • C, 5 % CO 2 .After 18 hours, the plates were exposed to UV light for 20 minutes to ensure subsequent antagonism was not due to actively growing producer strains.Target strain overnight cultures grown in 50% THY and 50% CDM were pelleted at 3250×G for 5 minutes.Cell pellets were resuspended and washed with fresh CDM.An aliquot (80 l) of the washed overnight culture was then added to 5 mL of fresh CDM and incubated until the culture reached mid-log phase.Cells were then either dosed with 5 l of the appropriate MSP or an equal volume of DMSO and allowed to grow until they had reached stationary phase ( 3-4 hours).1.25 mL of target strain was then transferred to a prewarmed, 50mL conical tube containing 25 mL of 45 • C, soft (0.75%) THY agar.The tube was inverted five times to ensure proper mixing and 5 mL of the suspended target strain was added as an overlay to producer strain plates.After 20 minutes, the plates were transferred agar side down to a 37 • C, 5% CO 2 incubator.After 24 hours, the diameter of the resulting inhibition rings were measured using digital calipers.The zone of inhibition was defined as the difference in the areas of the inhibition ring and the producer spot.
