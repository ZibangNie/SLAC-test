The rise of large language models (LLMs) has transformed natural language processing (NLP) and generative artificial intelligence (AI) applications.However, deploying these transformer-based models in resource-constrained environments poses a significant challenge due to their high computational and memory demands.To address this, we introduce in this article generative AI (GenAI) on the Edge, a comprehensive benchmarking dataset designed to evaluate the performance of LLMs deployed on edge devices.Leveraging a distributed testbed of Raspberry Pi 5 devices orchestrated with lightweight Kubernetes (K3s), the dataset captures a broad range of performance metrics essential for assessing the feasibility of local inference in constrained environments.These metrics include detailed measurements of throughput, inference latency, memory utilization, and computational efficiency, along with granular timing data for key stages of the inference pipeline-sample, prefill, and decode phases.We systematically evaluate LLMs of varying sizes under real-world deployment scenarios, with a particular emphasis on CPUbased edge platforms.By conducting multiple runs of conversation-based evaluations, GenAI on the Edge provides actionable insights into the tradeoffs between performance and resource efficiency, enabling better decision-making for LLM deployment in edge environments.IEEE SOCIETY/COUNCIL Communications Society (ComSoc) DATA TYPE/LOCATION Structured Text Data (CSV); Leeds, U.K. DATA DOI/PID 10.21227/7d08-8655 INDEX TERMS Benchmarking dataset, edge artificial intelligence (AI), generative AI (GenAI), large language model (LLM), open radio access network (ORAN), performance evaluation.

BACKGROUND

The vision of 6G networks as artificial intelligence (AI)-native systems emphasizes embedding intelligence directly within network infrastructure, enabling decentralized AI processing closer to end users [1].Supported by paradigms such as open radio access network (ORAN) 1 and 1 O-RAN Alliance, https://www.o-ran.org/,accessed Nov. 2024.network-in-a-box, this approach advocates for cost-effective, off-the-shelf hardware, particularly in scenarios such as rural connectivity provisioning.Complementing this vision, the integration of generative AI (GenAI) and LLMs into modern applications-spanning NLP to real-time decision support-is revolutionizing the technological landscape.However, the substantial computational resources required for LLM inference often make local deployment impractical.Consequently, these models are predominantly hosted on remote servers, with users accessing their capabilities via APIs [2], [3].

While the cloud-based architecture reduces resource demands on local devices, it introduces significant challenges, including increased latency, privacy concerns, and dependence on stable internet connectivity.These limitations underscore the urgent need to evaluate and optimize LLM performance on resource-constrained edge devices [4].According to Gartner [5], more than half of the deep neural network data analysis will occur on edge devices by 2025, highlighting the growing role of edge AI in processing data closer to its source.This shift promises to democratize access to AI, particularly in bandwidth-constrained environments.However, systematic evaluation of LLMs on such hardware-especially under the computational and memory constraints of edge devices without GPUs-remains largely unexplored.Existing benchmarks and frameworks [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12] focus primarily on centralized deployments and often overlook comprehensive evaluations on CPU-based, resource-constrained edge platforms.

To address this gap, our research presents a benchmarking dataset, GenAI on the Edge, that enables research community to systematically evaluate the performance of LLMs on a Raspberry Pi 5 cluster configured as an edge testbed [13].Leveraging a lightweight Kubernetes distribution (K3s) and a modular prompting framework, we assessed LLMs of varying sizes across key performance metrics, including throughput, latency, memory utilization, and computational efficiency.The dataset captures the feasibility of deploying LLMs on resource-constrained edge devices while highlighting the tradeoffs involved in achieving efficient local inference.

This dataset makes several critical contributions to the fields of edge computing and AI.

1) Standardized Benchmarks: It establishes reproducible benchmarks for evaluating LLMs on edge devices, enabling comparative analyses and consistent evaluation methodologies.2) Practical Insights: It provides actionable insights into the performance and efficiency of LLM deployments on resource-constrained edge platforms.3) Enabling Broader Edge AI Adoption: By informing the design of cost-effective, localized deployments, it supports real-world applications requiring low latency and privacy-sensitive AI.By facilitating localized inference in bandwidth-constrained environments, this dataset aligns with the 6G vision of ultralow latency, privacy-sensitive AI applications. 2It serves as a foundational resource for researchers and practitioners in AI, telecommunications, and edge computing, advancing the integration of AI in resource-limited and decentralized environments.


COLLECTION METHODS AND DESIGN

Fig. 1 provides an overview of the system setup for GenAI data collection on edge devices.Fig. 1(a) depicts the benchmarking testbed architecture [13], which forms the foundation for data generation.The testbed is specifically designed to evaluate the inference performance of LLMs within resource-constrained edge environments, focusing on their deployment in a small base station (SBS) architecture.The architecture features two compute clusters built using Raspberry Pi single-board computers (SBCs), providing a cost-effective and scalable platform for experimentation.Cluster 1 supports containerized ORAN deployment and configuration, while cluster 2 focuses on LLM deployment and edge offloading for PromptAI generative tasks.This article utilizes cluster 2 for generating the benchmarking dataset.The PromptAI system architecture is organized into four layers: hardware, middleware (orchestration), containerization, and application.It features an APIbased edge computing testbed, leveraging a Raspberry Pi 5 cluster as edge nodes orchestrated by K3s.Docker containers enable the delivery of GenAI services within an isolated environment, ensuring scalability, flexibility, and efficient LLM deployment on resource-constrained hardware.Further details on the testbed and its functionalities can be found in our prior work [13].The testbed incorporates a set of LLMs and two datasets for its operation.

1) Language Models: As outlined in Table I, a diverse collection of pretrained LLMs, sourced from Hugging Face, containerized using Docker, and stored in both a local repository and on DockerHub, is used for benchmarking.These models are deployed on the testbed using Kubernetes pods/services for orchestration.2) OpenAssistant/oasst1 Dataset: A subset of the Ope-nAssistant/oasst1 dataset [14], [15], which contains dialogues designed for conversational AI research is employed for the examination of LLMs performance.3) Winogrande Dataset: A large-scale benchmark dataset [16] for evaluating natural language understanding, specifically designed to assess a model's ability to solve common-sense reasoning problems.It consists of 44 000 multiple-choice questions, each requiring the model to choose the correct answer based on contextual understanding and reasoning.The large language models (LLMs) selected for evaluation are sourced from the Open LLM Leaderboard available on the Hugging Face platform [17].These models represent the latest popular and regularly updated open-source options.

VALIDATION AND QUALITY

The generated dataset is comprehensive, capturing both performance metrics (e.g., latency and throughput) and resource utilization statistics, making it a valuable resource for understanding the behavior of LLMs in edge com-  puting environments.A subset of 50 diverse dialogues (conversations) from the OpenAssistant/oasst1 dataset, each consisting of at least five interaction turns, was used to enhance the realism of the input.The dialogues offer realistic prompts with a moderate average prompt length of 25 words (prompt lengths ranging from 1 to 241 words) and high variability, with a standard deviation of 30.67 words.

To ensure validity and reliability, all experiments were conducted three times, and the dataset includes the full results of all runs for each prompt, preserving the variability inherent in the individual measurements.To maintain consistency in model outputs, the maximum number of generated tokens was set to 500 for all models, ensuring deterministic responses.Each experiment was carried out in a controlled environment, with models deployed in isolated containers on a single Raspberry Pi, free from competing workloads.Additionally, the dataset provides the number of tokens used during inference, allowing users to perform normalization on a per-token basis for fair and standardized comparisons across models.This approach ensures that users can evaluate the models consistently and equitably.


RECORDS AND TOOLS

We provide a detailed overview of the files included in the GenAI on the edge dataset in Tables II and III.In addition to the performance and inference time-series data, the dataset includes a README file with instructions for usage.

We also release Docker images of the PromptAI system, benchmarking scripts, and Python scripts for results visualization.This combination of data and tools aims to facilitate reproducibility and further research.With the increasing relevance of GenAI and edge computing, we anticipate this benchmarking dataset will serve as a valuable resource for advancing the deployment and evaluation of language models in edge environments.

The Inference Files contain detailed, time-series performance metrics as outlined in Table II.These files track the performance of LLMs by documenting the evaluation of individual prompts for each model, enabling thorough analysis.The captured metrics provide key insights into LLM inference performance.For example, load time, prefill time, and decode time highlight the efficiency of various stages in the response generation process, while prefill throughput, sample throughput, and decode throughput reveal token processing speeds.High-level metrics, such as total time and total tokens, summarize the overall inference process.In addition, metrics such as sample runs and decode runs offer insights into iterative computations, and the LLM response retains the qualitative output for further analysis.Together, these metrics help identify performance bottlenecks, optimize resource usage, and assess the scalability of LLMs for edge deployments.The dataset also includes CPU consumption files and memory consumption files (outlined in Table III), which capture time-series data on system resource utilization.


INSIGHTS AND NOTES

This work introduces the GenAI on the edge dataset, an open-source resource designed for developing and benchmarking language models in edge computing environments.Generated using a specialized testbed, llama.cpp[19], and Prometheus [20], and leveraging a modular GenAI/ORAN deployment model, the dataset bridges theoretical benchmarking with practical GenAI applications.It specifically focuses on key edge computing metrics such as inference latency, throughput, and resource utilization, addressing challenges in resource-constrained environments typical of edge scenarios.This makes it distinct from other benchmark datasets, which often focus on more traditional server-based or cloud-centric evaluation, not accounting for the limitations and requirements of edge environments.The GenAI on the edge dataset aims to support AI advancements in telecommunications and edge computing, emphasizing system-level evaluation and deployment readiness, thus offering a complementary approach to existing datasets that focus on more generalized or theoretical model performance.

Several related datasets in the field of LLMs and AI applications have contributed to specific aspects of model evaluation.The Thota dataset [10] addresses biases in LLMs but does not emphasize computational performance or systemlevel evaluation metrics such as latency and resource usage.Similarly, the Text2RDF dataset [11] is designed knowledge extraction from LLMs and focuses on accuracy and structured data, with no focus on the runtime performance of models in constrained environments.Other datasets, such as RAI-30k [12] and fuzzing Python functions dataset [21], emphasize performance (e.g., reducing hallucinations and automating software testing), yet none provide a comprehensive system-level benchmark that includes the vital metrics of inference latency, throughput, and resource utilization needed for edge deployment scenarios.

Datasets such as the multimodal sentiment analysis dataset for Urdu [22] and ChatGPT for load flow studies [23] contribute significantly to domain-specific tasks but do not emphasize performance metrics necessary for edge deployment.The multimodal sentiment analysis dataset for Urdu [22] supports sentiment analysis for Urdu-language opinion videos, advancing research in non-English natural language processing.Similarly, ChatGPT for load flow studies [23] explores GPT models' potential in generating power system models for load flow studies, assessing their ability to solve engineering problems.Finally, the FormAI dataset [24] provides 112 000 C programs with labeled software vulnerabilities, designed for training LLMs in vulnerability detection, but again lacks the system-level evaluation metrics necessary for edge-based use cases.

In comparison with existing datasets, GenAI on the edge uniquely offers the ability to perform system-level evaluations, focusing on the holistic performance of LLMs, from inference efficiency to resource demands.This makes it a complementary resource to current datasets that typically focus on task-specific benchmarks, model accuracy, or domainspecific performance but do not consider the full spectrum of metrics required for efficient model deployment in edge computing.Thus, GenAI on the Edge is poised to significantly contribute to research and practical deployment of AI models in edge computing environments, providing essential insights into efficiency, deployment readiness, and resource optimization that are critical for real-world applications.

The dataset empowers users to conduct detailed analyses of LLM behavior across multiple runs and models.Leveraging tools such as Python's matplotlib and seaborn, users can create visualizations to illustrate trends in throughput, latency, and resource usage.Key exploration areas include benchmarking models for intra-and intermodel comparisons, analyzing metrics, identifying performance patterns, finetuning LLMs, detecting anomalies, simulating deployment scenarios, and performing exploratory data analysis.Insights derived from this dataset can help users to pinpoint models optimized for speed and low resource usage, assess consistency under varying conditions, and determine the bestsuited LLMs for edge computing applications, balancing performance and efficiency.
