Definition of Statin Intolerance and Clinical and Biochemical Phenotypes 22

Trial participants were monitored for adverse drug events. Investigators could attribute 23 adverse events during the trial to the study drug and/or background therapies (eg, 24 atorvastatin or rosuvastatin). Laboratory testing, including for creatine kinase (CK) 25 levels, was performed centrally at baseline and at protocol-specified intervals after 26 randomization. 27

Patients were classified as statin-intolerant at baseline if the investigator 28 documented that they had discontinued at least 2 different statins prior to enrollment or 29 during run-in due to muscle symptoms and/or elevated CK. For the present 30 pharmacogenomic analysis, we defined 2 separate phenotypes. The first was a clinical 31 phenotype based upon documented statin intolerance prior to randomization or an 32 investigator-documented adverse event of statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS; 33

i.e., cramping, myalgia, or myopathy attributed to statin use by the investigator) 34 developing after randomization. We also defined a biochemical phenotype as the 35 maximum value of CK determined on regular, protocol-specified laboratory testing 36

Hence, all known "statin intolerant" cases documented during the run-in period were 44 included in the genetic sub-study. Only adverse events and laboratory measures prior to 45 study drop-out or completion were considered for analysis. See Table I for additional  46 exclusion/inclusion criteria for each phenotype. Both phenotypes were assessed for 47 accuracy/quality using R Studio (Vienna, Austria). 48

These phenotypes were tested for association against 8,921,030 (minor allele 49 frequency [MAF]>0.01) genetic variants genome-wide, including several candidate 50 genes and variants based on previously reported studies. 51

Patients with available genetic data and documented statin intolerance due to muscle 54 symptoms (e.g., cramping, myalgia, or myopathy) and/or elevated CK prior to 55 randomization (n=657) were included as cases for the baseline statin intolerance 56 phenotype. Of these, 555 were intolerant to both atorvastatin and rosuvastatin during 57 the run-in period, while 102 subjects had documentation of prior intolerance to at least 2 58 statins (not necessarily atorvastatin and rosuvastatin) prior to study enrollment. the 657 subjects with statin intolerance at randomization, comprised the overall case 65 population (n=894) for this phenotype. All subjects taking high-dose atorvastatin or 66 rosuvastatin at randomization, who were followed for at least 4 months and had no 67 indication of investigator-documented SAMS or statin intolerance prior to randomization, 68 comprised the control group (n=9,723). Additionally, all high-dose atorvastatin-and 69 rosuvastatin-treated subjects with documented myalgia or myopathy after randomization 70 due to unknown etiology or not directly attributed to statin use by study investigator 71 were excluded from the analysis (n=472; Table I). 72


CK Maximum Value During the Treatment Period 73

All trial participants included in the pharmacogenomic analysis (n=11,880) had CK 74 levels drawn at least once during the randomized treatment period; 11,865 also had a 75 baseline CK measurement at randomization. Of this population, 10,653 (89.7%) patients 76 were taking high-dose atorvastatin or rosuvastatin at randomization. Of these 10,653 77 subjects, 810 had a myocardial infarction during the treatment period and were 78 excluded to avoid false/confounding CK elevations due to myocardial damage/injury 79 (Table I). Additional subjects were excluded from analysis due to less than 4 months of 80 follow-up. Overall, 9,630 subjects were included in the analysis for the maximum CK 81 phenotype. Each patient's maximum CK level during the treatment period was utilized 82 for the analysis and treated as a continuous variable. Maximum CK, rather than 83 baseline CK, was used for the following reasons: 1) Using baseline CK would have 84 limited our analysis to a single point in time, making the phenotype more susceptible to 85 inherent biases. 2) Baseline CK was drawn at randomization, which occurred only two weeks following statin initiation for some patients and may have been too early to detect 87 statin-associated maximum CK given the median time to SAMS with CK elevation 88 following statin initiation is reported to be 6.3 months 45 (Table II). 145


Gene Burden Tests 146

In each Ensembl-defined gene, 51 nonsynonymous variants were filtered according to 20 147 masks, 5 different masks/criteria based on 5 allele frequency thresholds (MAFs: <5%, <1%, <0.1%, and <0.01%, and singletons only) and 4 based on the predicted protein 149 coding impact of the variant. For the coding impact, the masks ranging from most 150 restrictive to least restrictive are: 1) predicted loss of function (pLoF); 2) pLoF or 151 missense predicted to be damaging by 5/5 deleterious algorithms; 3) pLoF or missense 152 predicted to be damaging by at least 1/5 deleterious algorithms; and 4) pLoF or 153 missense. The 5 algorithms used to assess deleteriousness were SIFT, 52 Polyphen2 154 HDIV 53 , Polyphen2 HVAR 53 , LRT, 54 and MutationTaster. 55 For the variants that met 155 filtering criteria for a given mask, the variants were collapsed into a single summary 156 measure per individual (eg, 0, 1, or 2) and analyzed in a similar manner to single-point 157 analysis. A gene burden analysis of rare-variants in 18,878 Ensembl-defined genes 51 158 was then performed for both phenotypes using the same covariates and regression 159 methodology described above (Table I). An exome-wide significance threshold of 160 P<1x10 -6 was used for gene burden analysis. 161

Rs6667912, an intronic variant in TMEM9 was associated with an increased risk of SAMS in the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES pharmacogenomic subgroup (cases=894, controls=9,723), the largest atorvastatin/rosuvastatin SAMS cohort to date. The exact physiological mechanisms by which TMEM9 may modulate SAMS is unknown, and the impact of rs6667912 on TMEM9 expression/function has yet to be elucidated. Yet, there are limited preclinical and in vitro data suggesting its role in skeletal muscle regeneration/proliferation. Increased Tmem9 expression was observed in skeletal muscle of a muscular atrophy mouse model, suggesting a role in myocyte energy conservation 29 . TMEM9 was also shown to amplify the Wnt-beta catenin pathway in vitro, a finding that was validated ex vivo and in a mouse model 28 . A role of the Wnt-beta catenin pathway in skeletal muscle regeneration/repair and myocyte proliferation following injury has been proposed 60,61 , but remains controversial 62 .
