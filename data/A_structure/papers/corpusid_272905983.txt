The design strategy and testing of an efficient floor antifreezing ventilation system in a large cold store
2374-4731 print / 2374-474X81BE36E992E0170A3B3C13B15C91B92010.1080/23744731.2024.2404813Received December 8, 2023; accepted August 21, 2024
In this study, an optimization strategy of mechanical ventilation was carried out to reduce frost heaving and heating power in a large cold storage operation.Then, a response surface method is used to determine influential factors and mechanisms of mechanical ventilation by using a standard pre-assembled cold storage system as a test case.Results show that the main factors affecting the soil surface temperature critical to frost-heaving prevention include the soil temperature, airflow speed, pipe diameter, and pipe spacing.For a case study, the spacing of the ventilation pipes has the most significant influence on the surface temperature of the soil layer.In contrast, the soil temperature at a depth of 3.2 m has a minor impact on the surface temperature.Especially for the design criteria that the soil surface temperature cannot be less than 2 C, the diameter of the ventilation pipe should be greater than 0.28 m, the spacing of the ventilation pipe should be less than 1.01 m and the airflow speed should be greater than 2.09 mÁs −1 .

Introduction

With the recent rapid development of logistics technology, the demand for quick-frozen food and cold storage for processing and storage is increasing (Cao, Han, and Gu 2011;Delele et al. 2009).The cold storage can be divided into residential and commercial assembled cold storage.Due to the advantages of a short construction period and flexible usage, prefabricated cold storage buildings have been widely used worldwide.In the assembled cold storage structure, especially in the meat-based food freezing and processing rooms, the temperature in the storage room could be as low as −40 C, which can easily cause the water in the soil underneath the floor of the freezing and processing room to freeze, resulting in ground frost heaving.The ground frost heaving can cause wall cracking and other phenomena to damage the building structure and affect the quality of the frozen food (Hao and Ju 2011;Hoang et al. 2015;Carneiro, Gaspar, and Silva 2017).

Aerial-terracing natural ventilation, mechanical ventilation, embedded hot-oil pipes, and electric heating wires, among others, are generally adopted to minimize the effect of the cold temperature of the freezing warehouse on the soil layer below and to avoid the frost-heaving problem of the cold storage terrace (Laguerre, Hoang, and Flick 2013;Evans et al. 2014a;Li et al. 2017).The most common approach is to use mechanical ventilation pipes in the assembled cold storage to ensure the soil surface temperature is above 0 C. The design of mechanical ventilation is a complicated engineering undertaking involving parameters such as the diameter of the ventilation pipe, the spacing of the ventilation pipes, and the airflow speed in the ventilation pipe (Evans et al. 2014a;de Freitas and da Cunha 2018;Al-Sanea and Zedan 2012).If the airflow speed in the ventilation pipe is too low, the soil surface temperature will be lower than 0 C. On the other hand, if the airspeed is too high, the temperature in the warehouse will be too warm.With the increasing popularity of prefabricated cold storage, a reasonable and accurate selection of mechanical ventilation parameters for the cold storage floor is of great significance to ensure the safe and energy-saving operation of the cold storage facilities (Foster et al. 2016;Garrido et al. 2018;Reilly and Kinnane 2017;Baldinelli et al. 2018;Ge and Tassou 2001).

An alternative to the techniques as mentioned above is installing the terrace's insulation layer to prevent soil freezing.However, this approach may postpone the occurrence of soil freezing but cannot stop it (Bianco et al. 2015;Galliano et al. 2016).According to the relationships between the temperature in the cold room, the insulation layer thickness with a material thermal conductivity of 2.33 WÁ(mÁ C) −1 , and the occurrence time of soil freezing (Prata, Simões, and Tadeu 2018), when the storage room temperature is −40 C and the thickness of the insulation layer is 0.25 m, the soil will freeze after 3.5 months (about 2500 h) in operation.Furthermore, if the design condition is that the soil would not freeze in 10 years, the insulation layer thickness will need to be increased to 2.1 m.Overall, the cost will be prohibitively high and the building structure cannot accommodate this thickness (Carbonaro et al. 2016;Ascione et al. 2014).However, mechanical ventilation can improve energy consumption to shrink the cost.Very few reports are available in the literature on the design of mechanical ventilation pipes (Evans et al. 2014b;Capozzoli, Gorrino, and Corrado 2013;Smale, Moureh, and Cortella 2006).Although some engineering data might provide cold storage design specifications for mechanical ventilation pipes, the climate characteristics of the cold storage facilities and the design temperature in the cold storage areas were not available for the floor design in different regions (O'Grady, Lechowska, and Harte 2017;Ibrahim et al. 2014).Some researchers simulated the operation of cold storage facilities using CFD methods (Smale, Moureh, and Cortella 2006;Kondrashov and Tyukov 2006;Norton and Sun 2006), including Jing et al. (Xie et al. 2006) for the flow and temperature fields in the warehouse, Hao et al. (Hao and Ju 2011) for the velocity and temperature fields by using the finite-volume method and two standard k-e equations, and Hoang (Hoang et al. 2015) for the airflow and heat transfer in a cold room filled with the apple pails.They also used the CFD method to study the heat and mass transfer in the cold storage.Additionally, Carneiro et al. (Carneiro, Gaspar, and Silva 2017) developed a 3-D CFD model of the opening/closing cycle of the cold room's doors.They found that the air infiltration rate through the sliding door is 20% lower than that through a hinged door and the air temperature inside the cold room with a sliding door is 17% lower than the hinged door.Then, Tian et al. (Tian et al. 2018) developed an unsteady analytical model for predicting the infiltration flow rate through the doorway of refrigerated rooms.

Although the studies mentioned above fully illustrated that CFD technologies can solve some heat and mass transfer problems in cold storage, there were few relevant reports on anti-freezing issues of cold storage floors.These studies often face limitations in accurately controlling soil surface temperature and preventing ground frost heaving effectively.The design parameters such as ventilation pipe diameter, spacing, and airflow speed are challenging to optimize.Suboptimal designs may lead to insufficient heating of the soil surface, resulting in ground frost heaving issues.Additionally, insulation layer installation has been proposed as an alternative approach, but it may only delay soil freezing rather than prevent it entirely.

The present model introduces an innovative approach to address the limitations of previous studies by optimizing mechanical ventilation using the Response Surface Method (RSM).Unlike traditional methods, which rely on empirical design or insulation layers, the RSM-based optimization scheme offers a systematic and data-driven approach to determine the optimal ventilation parameters.By considering factors such as pipe diameter, airflow rate, and spacing, the model aims to achieve precise control of soil surface temperature to prevent ground frost heaving effectively.This application of RSM to mechanical ventilation design in cold storage facilities represents a novel contribution to the field and offers potential improvements in energy efficiency, operational safety, and cost-effectiveness compared to existing literature models and previous approaches.

The paper is composed of five sections.First, a simulation model is established for a cold-storage ventilation pipeline of a pre-assembled cold storage area, and the accuracy of the simulation model is verified according to the measured data.Then, by using the soil surface temperature as the design criterium, the influence of soil temperature, airspeed, pipe diameter, and pipe spacing on the ventilation effect at different areas is analyzed, and the optimal design scheme of the mechanical ventilation of the ground floor of the freezing room is obtained.


Problem descriptions

The design temperature of a freezing room is much lower than 0 C and the soil temperature underneath the floor can easily be lower than the freezing point, which can cause frost heaving.A schematic diagram of a cold storage area is shown in Figure 1.

From Figure 1, the cold storage area employs a mechanical ventilation system between the freezing room and the soil layer for frost heaving prevention.Due to the low temperature (from −30 to −40 C) in the freezing room, the soil below the storage room may be below 0 C. If this occurs, the moisture in the soil would freeze into ice and expand in volume, thus damaging the building structure and cause energy loss in the storage area.

Figure 1 shows the present mechanical ventilation system and the construction structures between the floor of the freezing room and the ventilation system.The construction structures include a reinforced concrete layer, a waterproof layer, a thermal insulation material layer, an air barrier layer, another concrete layer, and a filler layer including ventilation pipes.The role of the ventilation pipeline is to provide sufficient heating effect to avoid freezing the water in the soil layer beneath the pipeline.The design parameters, such as the diameter, spacing, and airflow speed of the ventilation pipe, would have an essential influence on the system design.


Building model and Verification


Building model

To explore the effect of the ventilation system on the cold storage floor, a real assembled cold storage area is selected from Guangzhou, China.In addition to the freezing room, the cold storage building has a platform area and an anteroom area and its horizontal layout is shown in Figure 2. The lengths of the platform, anteroom, and freezing room are, respectively, 6, 6, and 48 m, and the widths of all three areas are 22.5 m, which gives an area of 1080 m 2 for the cold storage room.The design temperature of the cold storage room is −18 C, the design temperature of the anteroom is 7 C, and the platform is open to ambient conditions.Then, the ventilation pipe will run through the length of all three areas.

Verification of the simulation results

For the present work, the ventilation system of a cold storage floor is put forward by using a 3-D numerical solution.

The simulation results are compared with the measured experimental data to verify the accuracy of the simulation software and the model.The literature in (Liu 1994) provides the measured air temperature data of a ventilation pipe on the floor of a prefabricated cold storage area in Guangzhou.Based on the same simulation platform and the cold storage structure parameters, the ventilation pipe air temperatures are generated through the simulation model and are compared with the measured data in Figure 4.

As shown in Figure 4, the simulation results and the experimental data are in good agreement.After the air enters the ventilation pipe, its temperature drops sharply in the range of 0-10 m, varies mildly in the range of 20-50 m, and then rises rapidly in the range of 50-60 m.The simulation and measured temperatures reach the lowest value between 40-50 m.Therefore, the soil-layer surface temperature at a distance of 45 m from the inlet of the ventilation pipe is used as the critical temperature to evaluate the ventilation effect.Good agreement between the simulation and experimental temperatures can be seen along most of the pipeline, and a significant deviation of the simulation temperature from the measured one occurs only near the outlet of the ventilation pipe.Therefore, the simulation temperature can accurately reflect the actual temperature on the floor of the cold storage area, especially in the middle section of the ventilation pipe that is directly under the cold storage room, with a design temperature in the warehouse of −18 C.


Experimental design

The Response Surface Method (RSM) is a statistical method used to find the optimum process parameters and solve multivariate problems using a reasonable experimental design method.It obtains specific data through experiments and fits the functional relationship using a multivariate quadratic regression equation between factors and response values.Many studies have demonstrated that RSM can effectively solve complex engineering problems.Mojaver (Mojaver, Khalilarya, and Chitsaz 2019) used an RSM analysis for a novel integrated biomass-gasification solid-oxide fuel cell and high-temperature sodium heat pipe system.Abdullah (Al-Rashed 2019) optimized the heat transfer and pressure drop of nano-antifreezing using the statistical method of the RSM.Khalajzadeh (Khalajzadeh, Heidarinejad, and Srebric 2011) optimized the parameters of a vertical ground heat exchanger based on the RSM.

The Response Surface Method is suitable for solving problems related to nonlinear data processing, which employs experimental and statistical techniques to seek the best combination of operational conditions.Through the regression fitting of the process and the drawing of the corresponding surface and contour, the response value corresponding to each factor level can be conveniently obtained.Response surface optimization methods mainly include central composite design (CCD) and Box-Behnken design (BBD).In this paper, the BBD response surface optimization method is selected to design the test points, and the Design-Expert, the most commonly used RSM software, is used to design and produce correlations between important parameters.According to the research subjects of this paper and relevant parameters of the ground structure between freezing blocks, the value range is determined as shown in Table 2.

Influence of the interaction of various factors

Eq. 8 shows that the soil surface temperature is mainly affected by four factors: A, B, C, and D. However, these four parameters may also be coupled to produce an impact on the soil layer surface temperature.To show the influence of these four parameters on the soil-layer surface temperature graphically, Figure 6 presents 3D plots for the surface temperature of the soil layer as a function of two of the four parameters while keeping the other two parameters constant.Through the inspection of Figure 6, it can be seen that within the given ranges of the other parameters, B (spacing of ventilation pipes) has the most significant influence on the soil surface temperature, which is manifested by the steepest changes of the soil surface temperature vs. B. On the other hand, the soil temperature at a depth of 3.2 m exhibits the slightest influence on the change in the surface temperature of the soil layer.With a decrease in the spacing of the ventilation pipes, the surface temperature of the soil layer in most cases is increased significantly.With an increase in the airflow speed, the surface temperature of the soil layer also increased.The larger the diameter of the ventilation pipe, the higher the surface temperature of the soil layer.Considering that the temperature in the deep soil layer is natural and cannot be changed, only the other three parameters may be optimized.It is not difficult to conclude that to improve the surface temperature of the soil layer, the diameter of the ventilation pipe and the airflow speed should be increased as much as possible.But these increases will result in an increase in airflow rate, accompanied by an increase in fan power consumption.

Therefore, a tradeoff between the surface temperature and power consumption should be considered to select the appropriate pipe diameter and airflow speed.So long as the surface temperature of the soil layer reaches the required level, the heating power of the ventilation system should be reduced as much as possible.According to the results shown in Table 3, when comparing the third and 25th experiments under the conditions of the same airflow pipe diameter, airflow speed and deep soil temperature, when the airflow pipe spacing changed from 2 m to 1 m, the heating power decreased from 1737.80 W to 1180.07 W, which is a decrease of 32.09%.At the same time, the soil surface temperature increased from −0.9 C to 3.22 C.This result shows that reducing the airflow pipe spacing is beneficial for increasing the soil surface temperature and reducing the heating power as well.


Optimization schemes

The influence of each parameter on the soil surface temperature has been studied above.For the mechanical ventilation pipe design of a frozen floor in mild climates regions, the temperature in the deep soil layer may be treated as constant.Therefore, the optimization of the ventilation pipe design parameters is conducted under the premise of constant temperature at a 3.2 m depth of a fixed soil layer.At the same time, the temperature to prevent ground frost heave should not be less than 0 C on the surface of the soil layer.In view of uneven temperature distribution, it is necessary to consider a certain safety margin.Here, the surface temperature of the soil layer is set to at least 2 C as an optimization criterium.

Using the cold storage in Guangzhou, China, as a case study, the temperature at a depth of 3.2 m of soil in Guangzhou is 22 C. To ensure the surface temperature of the soil layer is not lower than 2 C, the optimal parameters given by the Design-Expert software are 0.28 m in diameter of the ventilation pipe, 1.01 m spacing of the ventilation pipe, and 2.09 mÁs −1 airflow speed.The optimal results here   Volume 31, Number 1, January 2025 135 cross-sectional area.The temperature around the ventilation pipes varies greatly.The temperature directly above the ventilation pipes is approximately −2 C. Conclusions

This paper established an accurate and fast way to optimize the anti-freezing mechanical ventilation parameters for a refrigerated freezing-room floor, which used a design method of Response Surface Method (RSM) for safe and energy-conservation operation.Through comprehensive analyses of mechanical ventilation parameters for frost-heaving prevention, the main conclusions can be made as follows:

The soil temperature of the cold storage floor and the design temperature of the freezing room in the cold storage must be considered simultaneously for the ventilation and cooling pipeline of the cold storage floor.These two temperatures will have a significant impact on the cold storage capacity loss and the soil freezing.

The main factors affecting the soil surface temperature critical to frost-heaving prevention include the soil temperature, airflow speed, pipe diameter, and pipe spacing.The RSM based on the Design-Expert software can design the orthogonal test schemes, identify the influence of the various parameters on the surface temperature of the soil layer, and quickly optimize the parameters of the floor ventilation-pipe system for a given soil temperature at a certain depth.For a given design temperature in the cold storage, the effect of ventilation pipe cooling (i.e., the soil temperature under the ventilation pipe) from the primary to secondary order is: ventilation pipe spacing > air velocity > ventilation pipe diameter > soil temperature.

For the case study of this paper, a cold storage facility in Guangzhou, the spacing of the ventilation pipes has the most significant influence on the surface temperature of the soil layer.In contrast, the soil temperature at a depth of 3.2 m has a minor impact on the surface temperature.For the design criteria that the soil surface temperature cannot be less than 2 C, the diameter of the ventilation pipe should be greater than 0.28 m, the spacing of the ventilation pipe should be less than 1.01 m and the airflow speed should be greater than 2.09 mÁs −1 .
