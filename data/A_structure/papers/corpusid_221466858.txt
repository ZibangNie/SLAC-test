14This paper focusses on the examination of the fishing ground footprint in a group of 89 15 countries using fractional integration. The fishing ground footprint is one of the 16 components of the ecological footprint. Nevertheless, it has not been investigated very 17 much from an empirical viewpoint. We contribute to the existing literature on fishing 18 ground footprint by using fractional integration techniques to examine the persistence of 19 the series. Our results are very heterogeneous across countries though we find that most 20 of the series are nonstationary and non-mean reverting, with most of the countries 21 belonging to the upper-middle and high income levels. On the other hand, most of the 22 14.4% of countries that show a stationary pattern belong to lower-middle and low income 23 countries. One of the implications of the study is that policies aimed at reducing fishing 24 grounds footprint are likely to be effective in most of the investigated countries.25 26

Nevertheless, some conclusions can be inferred from our results. We find 17370 countries where the order of integration is constrained between 0.5 and 1, implying 371 nonstationarity though mean reversion. Thus, the no applicability of the appropriate 372 differentation before conducting Granger causality test or ordinary least squares (OLS), may cause erroneous conclusions in the results. Moreover, the conventional diagnostic 374 statistics which are utilised to evaluate OLS estimates will suggest a statistically 375 significant relationship between the series when there is no relationship and ultimately 376 the procedure may yield inappropriate policy actions. Moreover, 77 out of the 90 series 377 investigated (85.5%) display a nonstationary pattern and more than half of them belong 378 to the upper-middle-and high-income levels. This is in line with the results provided in 379 Yilanci et al. (2019). On the other hand, 14.4% of the series appear to be stationary (with 380 an order of integration below 0.5), and most of the them belong to lower-middle-and 381 low-income countries. 382 A policy implication of the results is that there is a path dependence or hysteresis 383 in the fishing grounds footprint of the upper-middle-and high-income levels and therefore 384 shocks resulting from policies implemented to stem the fishing grounds footprint per 385 capita will have long lasting impacts on their growth, suggesting that policies aimed at 386 changing the path of the fishing grounds footprint will be effective. On the other hand, 387 the policies aimed at ensuring conservation of fishery resources and protecting essential 388 fish habitats, especially the fishing grounds footprint per capita in the lower-middle-and 389 low-income countries might not be effective. Policies that can protect fishing habitats 390 include regulating or prohibiting trawling and other forms of destructive fishing in 391 countries where these activities are common.
