SGLT2i-induced euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis in a heart failure patient with acute myocardial infarction: a case report

Introduction: Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i) are widely used as oral hypoglycemic agents in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) patients because of their cardiovascular and renal benefits.Euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) is a serious and increasingly recognized complication of SGLT2i therapy.The atypical symptoms of euDKA may lead to delayed diagnosis and life-threatening outcomes.Case Presentation: Here, we report a 48-year-old woman with T2DM and hypertension who was diagnosed with acute myocardial infarction (AMI).The patient underwent emergency percutaneous coronary intervention and received canagliflozin for heart failure.However, the patient presented with tachypnea, dyspnea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain during the postoperative period.SGLT2i-associated euDKA was suspected to be the underlying cause of mild hyperglycemia, high anion gap metabolic acidosis, and ketonemia.The patient was successfully rescued by rapid correction of dehydration and electrolyte abnormalities and an insulin drip to normalize acidosis and bicarbonate levels.Discussion and Conclusion: Although the early use of SGLT2i may improve cardiac function in patients with AMI and heart failure, our findings suggest that clinicians should be aware of the association between SGLT2i therapy and euDKA.SGLT2i should be avoided in acute cardiovascular crises until patient stabilization.

Background

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is myocardial damage caused by acute and persistent ischemia and hypoxia in the coronary arteries.The estimated annual incidence of AMI in Europe is 293.3/100000 individuals [1] .Although coronary revascularization has dramatically improved patient survival, AMI remains the leading cause of mortality due to cardiovascular diseases worldwide.Hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), hyperlipidemia and smoking are high-risk factors for AMI.T2DM is present in approximately 30% of AMI cases, contributes to the development of AMI and worsens its prognosis [2] .The commonly used hypoglycemic agents in clinical practice include insulin and its analogues, biguanides, sulfonylureas, glinides, thiazolidinediones, α-glucosidase inhibitors, dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitors, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists, and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitors (SGLT2i).The choice of hypoglycemic agent in AMI patients should consider cardiovascular outcomes.

SGLT2i are a new class of oral hypoglycemic agents that reduce glucose levels by inhibiting the reabsorption of glucose in the proximal renal tubules.SGLT2i have recently become a preferred option for patients with cardiovascular diseases and T2DM because of their advantages in heart failure therapy [3] .Previous clinical trials have shown that SGLT2i reduce the combined risk of cardiovascular death or hospitalization in patients with heart failure [4] .Importantly, SGLT2i-associated cardiovascular benefits are not directly related to glycemic control, suggesting that these effects could be extended to patients without T2DM [5] .Moreover, SGLT2i can improve the functionality of pancreatic β cells,

Here, we report a rare combination of postoperative AMI, heart failure, and euDKA associated with SGLT2i therapy in a patient that underwent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).


Case presentation

A 48-year-old woman with constrictive chest pain and dyspnea for 20 h was sent to our hospital.She had a previous diagnosis 10 years prior of T2DM and hypertension and was treated with acarbose 50 mg thrice daily and nifedipine 20 mg daily.Her vital signs included a temperature of 36.5°C,heart rate of 85 beats/ min, blood pressure of 142/94 mmHg, and respiratory rate of 20 breaths/min.Physical examination did not reveal any pathological findings.Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for publication and any accompanying images.A copy of the written consent is available for review from the Editor-in-Chief of this journal upon request.This work has been reported in line with the Surgical Case Report 2025 criteria [10] .The arrival electrocardiogram revealed ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF (red arrow), and ST-T depression in leads I and aVL (blue arrow) (Fig. 1).Her cardiac troponin I (cTnI) level was 19 429.2 pg/mL and her N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) level was 634 pg/mL.Transthoracic echocardiography revealed diffuse ventricular hypokinesia (left ventricular ejection fraction 48%).AMI was diagnosed on the basis of these results.Urgent coronary angiography revealed triple-vessel disease, including diffuse severe narrowing and calcification of the left anterior descending artery (red arrow) and left circumflex artery (blue arrow), and occlusion of the distal right coronary artery (green arrow).Two stents (yellow arrow) were immediately implanted in the right coronary artery (Fig. 2).

Subsequently, the patient was transported to the cardiac care unit (CCU) for postoperative management, and her chest pain was significantly relieved.Routine blood tests revealed a leukocyte count of 12.94 × 10 9 /L, a neutrophil count of 11.61 × 10 9 /L, hemoglobin level of 136 g/L, and a platelet count of 348 × 10 9 /L.Serum biochemical tests revealed the following levels: alanine aminotransferase 25 U/L, aspartate aminotransferase 140 U/L, total cholesterol 6.66 mmol/L, low-density lipoprotein 2.78 mmol/L, creatinine 53 µmol/L, and lactic acid 1.75 mmol/L.The intravenous blood glucose level was 17.25 mmol/L and the glycosylated hemoglobin was 10.9%.Arterial blood gas analysis revealed that the potential of hydrogen (pH) was 7.342, the partial pressure of CO 2 (PaCO 2 ) was 26.6 mmHg, the partial pressure of O 2 (PaO 2 ) was 174 mmHg, HCO 3 was 13.8 mmol/L, blood base excess (BE) was −11 mmol/L, and the anion gap (AG) was 13.2 mmol/L (Table 1).We administered canagliflozin (100 mg daily) because the patient experienced heart failure.Additional oral medications included ticagrelor 90 mg twice daily, aspirin 100 mg daily, atorvastatin 20 mg nightly, metoprolol 47.5 mg daily, and perindopril 8 mg daily.Over 3 days postoperatively, her oral food intake decreased, and the patient developed tachypnea, dyspnea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain.The abdomen was soft and had mild tenderness, with a heart rate of 126 beats/min and a blood pressure of 156/ 98 mmHg.There was no increase in cTnI (7704.8pg/mL) or ST elevation on the electrocardiogram.However, the NT-proBNP level increased to 1172 pg/mL, with alanine aminotransferase at 114 U/ L, aspartate aminotransferase at 179 U/L, amylopsin at 39 U/L, pancrelipase at 89.2 IU/L, creatinine at 70 µmol/L, and lactic acid at 2.50 mmol/L.The patient was administered furosemide 20 mg for suspected heart failure, but her symptoms continued to worsen.Arterial blood gas analysis revealed high anion gap metabolic acidosis (pH 7.097, PaCO 2 10.4 mmHg, PaO 2 129 mmHg, HCO 3 3.1 mmol/L, BE −27 mmol/L, and AG 35.8 mmol/L) (Table 1).The intravenous blood glucose level was 13.46 mmol/L and the beta-hydroxybutyrate level was 7.7 mmol/L, with strongly positive urinary glucose (3 +) and ketone (4 +) levels.A diagnosis of euDKA in this case was established on the basis of mild hyperglycemia, a high anion gap, severe metabolic acidosis, blood and urinary ketones, normal lactic acid and renal function, and the use of canagliflozin post-PCI.

Oral canagliflozin was discontinued immediately.She was started on intravenous fluids and dextrose, insulin infusion at 4 U/ h, intravenous sodium bicarbonate, and hourly glucose monitoring.Within the first 24 h of treatment initiation, the intravenous blood glucose level slightly decreased (11.02 mmol/L), and acidosis gradually resolved (pH 7.290, PaCO 2 29.8 mmHg, PaO 2 143 mmHg, HCO 3 13.9 mmol/L, BE −11.3 mmol/L, and AG 28.2 mmol/L) (Table 1).The patient experienced alleviation of dyspnea, nausea, and vomiting 2 days later.At this time, arterial blood gas analysis revealed a pH of 7.368, PaCO 2 of 33.4 mmHg, PaO 2 of 105 mmHg, HCO 3 of 18.7 mmol/L, BE of −5.4 mmol/L, and AG of 20.6 mmol/L (Table 1).The intravenous blood glucose level was 7.95 mmol/L, and the serum beta-hydroxybutyrate level was <0.3 mmol/L.Urinalysis revealed a reduction in urinary glucose (1 +) and ketone (1 +) levels.The patient spent 7 days in the CCU and was transported to the general ward with subcutaneous insulin.

The patient was discharged home on day 12 of hospitalization.At this time, the arterial blood gas showed a pH of 7.375, PaCO 2 of 39.0 mmHg, PaO 2 of 102 mmHg, HCO 3 of 22.6 mmol/L, BE of −2.3 mmol/L, and AG of 14.6 mmol/L (Table 1).Blood glucose (7.05 mmol/L) was well controlled by subcutaneous insulin therapy using aspart and glargine.Other therapies included drugs for coronary heart disease.One month later, the patient underwent left-coronary PCI.She is currently undergoing regular clinical follow-up and has no further complications associated with her medications.


Discussion

SGLT2i, including empagliflozin, canagliflozin, and dapagliflozin, constitute a new class of oral hypoglycemic agents.Previous randomized controlled trials have shown that SGLT2i markedly reduce cardiovascular mortality, the risk of hospitalization for heart failure, and major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with or without T2DM [11] .In addition, in patients with chronic kidney disease, SGLT2i substantially slowed disease progression, including glomerular filtration rate reduction and albuminuria [12] .The focus of T2DM treatment has recently shifted from blood glucose control to the comprehensive management of cardiorenal metabolism.Therefore, SGLT2i are currently recommended as first-line hypoglycemic agents for patients with cardiovascular disease.In this study, we report the occurrence of SGLT2i-associated euDKA in a patient with AMI and heart failure, suggesting that early use of SGLT2i should be performed cautiously in patients with acute cardiovascular diseases.

In 2015, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration issued a warning that SGLT2i may lead to euDKA.A multicenter cohort study of T2DM patients taking SGLT2i revealed a rare prevalence of euDKA of approximately 0.25% [13] .The pathophysiological mechanism underlying SGLT2i-associated euDKA involves a decreased insulin/glucagon ratio [14] .SGLT2i not only reduces endogenous insulin secretion and sensitivity, but also stimulates the secretion of counterregulatory hormones (e.g.glucagon, growth hormone, cortisol, and catecholamine) [8] .Insulin reduces blood glucose, inhibits lipolysis, and promotes the tissue utilization of ketone bodies, whereas glucagon and catecholamine promote lipolysis and increase the levels of ketone bodies.Under conditions of AMI/stress, starvation, pregnancy, surgery, or sepsis, glycogen depletion decreases the hepatic production of glucose, whereas the acceleration of lipolysis and free fatty acid oxidation results in the production of excessive ketone bodies.SGLT2i promote lipolysis and oxidation of free fatty acids, thus increasing ketogenesis [14] .Moreover, SGLT2i facilitate urinary glucose excretion and sodium loss in the proximal convoluted tubules of the kidney, which contribute to osmotic diuresis [15] .The effects of circulating blood volume reduction and ketone body accumulation during SGLT2i therapy increase the risk of euDKA.

Although rare, euDKA is a serious metabolic complication associated with SGLT2i therapy.Patients with SGLT2i-associated euDKA often present with atypical symptoms, such as malaise, nausea, vomiting, hyperpnea, fatigue, hypotension, or even shock [9] .In this case, the patient had AMI and had recently underwent emergency PCI, which increased the risk of euDKA.Furthermore, the reduction in the postoperative diet aggravated fluid loss and ketone body production in the patient.However, the mild increase in blood glucose levels and atypical symptoms contributed to a delayed diagnosis and life-threatening complications.The management of euDKA involves supplementation with insulin and fluids, electrolyte imbalance correction, and monitoring of blood glucose and hemodynamics until acidosis and ketonemia are corrected [16] .

Although several ongoing clinical studies have revealed beneficial links between SGLT2i and AMI, the initiation of SGLT2i therapy in acute cardiovascular diseases is controversial.The SGLT2i AMI PROGECT registry revealed that the use of SGLT2i in patients with AMI was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular death, arrhythmias, and contrast-induced acute kidney injury during hospitalization, with reduced cardiovascular mortality and heart failure rehospitalization during long-term follow-up [17] .In addition, Paolisso et al [18] reported that AMI patients with T2DM taking SGLT2i exhibited a significantly reduced inflammatory response and a smaller infarct size than did those taking other hypoglycemic agents.These effects are related to improved cardiomyocyte energy metabolism, attenuated cardiomyocyte necrosis, reduced reperfusion injury, and prevention of heart failure [19,20] .However, patients with euDKA are relatively underrepresented in these trials.The potential benefits derived from SGLT2i usage in the inpatient setting cannot mitigate the possible risks [21] .A previous study reported that a patient with T2DM who was receiving longterm treatment with empagliflozin developed asymptomatic euDKA after AMI [22] .In this case, although SGLT2i therapy was initiated early for the benefit of heart failure after AMI, it may have triggered euDKA.Thus, clinicians should be cautious when initiating SGLT2i during acute illness such as AMI.SGLT2i therapy should be withheld during the perioperative period and started after the patient is stabilized.

In conclusion, even though the early use of SGLT2i in AMI patients may improve cardiac function and reduce cardiovascular death, the potential adverse effects of SGLT2i such as euDKA should not be underestimated in clinical practice.We need to discontinue SGLT2i during acute AMI and monitor ketones if therapy is unavoidable.To minimize the risk of euDKA, clinicians should raise awareness of high-risk situations (surgery, low-carbohydrate diet, insulin withdrawal or reduction, acute medical events), discontinue SGLT2i therapy 3 days prior to scheduled surgery, start SGLT2i therapy with small doses, and close monitor blood glucose and ketone bodies.
