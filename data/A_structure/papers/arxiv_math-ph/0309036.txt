arXiv:math-ph/0309036v1 15 Sep 2003 On the C λ -extended w ∞ -symmetry
Starting from the C λ -extended oscillator algebras, we obtain a new deformed w ∞algebra. More precisely, we show that the C λ -extended w ∞ -algebra generators may be expressed via the annihilation and creation operators of the C λ -extended oscillator algebras a and a † as an infinite-dimensional extension of the realization of sp(2) algebra.

Introduction

Over the past decade, much attention has been paid to the oscillator algebras in connection with the important role investigated in many physical systems, and they provide an useful tool in the theory of Lie algebra representations. On the other hand, their deformations and extensions have given new mathematical objects called Quantum Groups or Quantum Algebras [1] which have found applications in various physical problems, such as the description of the fractional statistics [2], the bosonization of supersymmetric quantum mechanics [3,4] and in the n-particle integrable systems [4,5]. Among these various deformations and extensions, we mention the generalized deformed oscillator algebras (GDOA's) [6] and the G-extended oscillator algebras [7] where G is some finite group, e.g in the case of Calogero-model C λ = Z λ is the cyclic group of order λ. This latter, have recently proved very useful in the context of supersymmetric quantum mechanics and some of its variants [7,8], and also in the study of coherent states in nonlinear quantum optics [2].

In this paper, we recall some facts concerning the C λ -extended oscillator algebras which were introduced as a generalization of Calogero-Vasiliev algebras [4,9]. In the second section we give some of their mathematical properties, and we extend the commutation relation between annihilation and creation operators a and a † to any indices n and m, as we will see for the positive indices it is straightforward. However, for the formal negative powers one consider the Bargmann representation [9], indeed in such representation the generators a and a † are identified with differential operators acting on the Bargmann space. Section three is devoted to the construction of w ∞ -algebra generators in terms of the pair a and a † of the C λ -extended oscillator algebras, we begin by the case of the Virasoro generators. We enclose by a conclusion and remarks.

2 Review on C λ -extended oscillator algebras As a generalization of the Calogero-Vasiliev algebras, the C λ -extended oscillator algebras (also called generalized deformed oscillator algebras (GDOA's)), denoted A λ , λ = 2, 3, ..., are defined by
[N, a † ] = a † , [N, P µ ] = 0, [a, a † ] = I + λ−1 µ=0 α µ P µ , a † P µ = P µ+1 a † ,(1)
together with their hermitian conjugates, and
P µ = 1 λ λ−1 ν=0 e 2πiν(N−µ) λ , λ−1 µ=0 P µ = 1 P µ P ν = δ µ,ν P ν λ−1 µ=0 α µ = 0, µ−1 ν=0 α ν > −1, µ = 1, ..., λ − 1. ,(2)
where α µ ∈ R, N is the number operator and P µ are the projection operators on subspaces F µ = {|kλ − µ |k = 0, 1, 2, ...} of the Fock space F which is portioning into λ subspaces.

The operators a and a † are defined by
a † a = F (N), aa † = F (N + 1),(3)
where
F (N) = N + λ−1 µ=0 β µ P µ , β µ = µ−1 ν=0
α ν , which is a fundamental concept of deformed oscillators. Let's denote the basis states of subspaces F µ by |n = |kλ + µ ≃ (a † ) n |0 where a|0 = 0, |0 is the vacuum state. The operators a, a † and N act on F µ as follows
N|n = n|n , a † |n = F (N + 1)|n + 1 , a|n = F (N)|n − 1 .(4)
According to these relations, a and a † are the annihilation and the creation operators respectively.

Particularly, if λ = 2, we have two projection operators P 0 = 1 2 (I + (−1) N ) and P 1 = 1 2 (I − (−1) N ) on the even and odd subspaces of the Fock space F , and the relations of (1) are restricted to
[N, a † ] = a † , [a, a † ] = I + κK, {K, a † } = 0,(5)
with their hermitian conjugates, where K = (−1) N is the Klein operator and κ is a real parameter. These relations define the so-called Calogero-Vasiliev algebra.

The C λ -extended oscillator algebras are seeing as deformation of G-extended oscillator algebras, where G is some finite group, appeared in connection with n-particle integrable models. In the former case, G is the symmetric group S n . So, for two particles S 2 , can be realized in terms of K and S 2 -extended oscillator algebra becomes a generalized deformed oscillator algebra (GDOA) also known as the Calogero-Vasiliev or modified oscillator algebra. In the C λ -extended oscillator algebras, G ≡ C λ is the cyclic group of order λ, C λ = {1, K, ..., K λ−1 }. So, these algebras have a rich structure since they depend upon λ independent real parameters, α 0 , α 1 , ..., α λ−1 .

Thus, these algebras can be also rewritten in terms of Klein operator, and instead of (1) we have [N,
a † ] = a † , [N, K] = 0, K λ = I,(6)

a † ] = I + λ−1 r=1 κ r K r , a † K = e −2πi λ Ka † ,(7)
together with their hermitian conjugates, where κ µ ∈ C and κ * µ = κ λ−µ .

To see the equivalence between (1) and (6-7), we note that C λ has λ inequivalent one-dimensional unitary irreducible matrix representations Γ µ , µ = 0, 1, ..., λ − 1. The projection operator on the carrier space of Γ µ can be written as
P µ = 1 λ λ−1 ν=0 (Γ µ (K ν )) * K ν = 1 λ λ−1 ν=0 e −2iπµν K ν ,(8)
and α µ can be given in terms of κ µ as follows
α µ = λ−1 ν=0 e −2iπµν λ κ ν , µ = 0, 1, ..., λ − 1.(9)
The C λ -extended oscillator Hamiltonian defined in the bosonic Fock space representation as usual by
H 0 = 1 2 {a, a † }.(10)

In each subspace F µ , the spectrum of H 0 is harmonic, but the λ infinite sets of equally spaced energy levels, corresponding to µ = 0, 1, ..., λ − 1, can be shifted with respect to each other by some amounts depending upon the parameters α 0 , α 1 , ..., α λ−1 .

Classical conformal symmetry and its representations

For reader convenience we remind some information about the conformal symmetry termed also Virasoro symmetry which was first introduced in the context of string theories and it is relevant to any theory in (2+1)-dimensional space-time which possesses conformal invariance. The conformal symmetry consists of all general transformations
z → z + ǫ(z),z →z +ǭ(z)(20)
where ǫ(z) and (ǭ(z)) are an infinitesimal analytical (anti-analytical) functions. It can be represented as an infinite Laurent series
ǫ(z) = n ǫ n z n+1 , n ∈ Z,(21)
and an analogous formula holds forǭ(z).

These mapping are generated by the differential operators
ℓ m = −z m+1 ∂ z andl m = −z m+1 ∂z.(22)
The operators ℓ m satisfy the commutator relations
[ ℓ m , ℓ n ] = (m − n)ℓ m+n ,(23)
and an analogous formula holds for thel m . Hence, this means that both the ℓ m and thē ℓ m span an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra; moreover, these two algebras are combined as a direct sum, [ℓ m ,l m ] = 0. The algebra defined by (23) is known as the classical conformal or Virasoro algebra. We shall denote that the above algebra admits a unique 1-dimentional central extension
L c = L ⊕ Cc,(24)
with the following commutation relations
[ ℓ m , c] = 0, [ℓ m , ℓ n ] = (m − n)ℓ m+n + c (m 3 − m) 12 δ m+n,0 ,(25)
where the value of the central charge c is the parameter of the theory in the quantum field theory context (e.g c = 1 for the free boson). The unitary representation of the above algebra are well know (see e.g [10]). In what follows, we give some representations discussed in the literature, 
ℓ m = j∈Z E m,m−j ,
where E k,j are the infinite dimensional matrix with value 1 at the (k, j) entry and 0 at all other entries [10].

Here we consider the simplest one which is given by oscillatory representation. In fact using the commutation relations of the quantum mechanics, we obtain the following commutator
[a, (a † ) m ] = m(a † ) m−1 .(26)
From this commutator, one see easily that the Virasoro operators for the unidimensional harmonic oscillators can be defined by
ℓ m = (a † ) m+1 a,(27)
which satisfy the following centerless Virasoro algebra [ ℓ m , ℓ n ] = (m − n)ℓ m+n .

In this section, we will extended the construction done in the previous section on w ∞algebra which is seeing as a generalization of Virasoro algebra. This algebra is interesting in the conformal field theory point of view because it contains the centerless Virasoro algebra as subalgebra and thus may be viewed as an extended conformal symmetry with an infinite set of additional symmetries. The w ∞ -algebra contains generators ω s m of spin 2, 3, 4, ... The defining commutation relations are
[ω s m , ω t n ] = (t − 1)m − (s − 1)n ω s+t−2 m+n .(43)
The generators ω 2 m generate a classical Virasoro subalgebra. The conformal properties of these generators can be viewed from the commutation relation
[ω 2 m , ω t n ] = (t − 1)m − n ω t m+n .(44)
which implies that ω t m can be regarded as the algebra Fourier modes of primary fields with the conform spin t. On the other hand w ∞ -algebra can has a natural geometric interpretation as area preserving diffeomorphisms of 2-manifolds e.g on the two-dimensional cylinder R × S 1 with canonical coordinates x and y it can be represented by Poisson bracket of the smooth function ω s m = e mx y s−1 . However, the standard central extension of Virasoro algebra can not extended to the full algebra w ∞ , which should therefore be viewed as a classical W -algebra. The W ∞ -algebra, which was first introduced in refs [18], is a deformation of w ∞ which is such that the standard central term in the Virasoro subalgebra can be extended to the whole algebra. W ∞ -algebra can thus be viewed as the quantum version of w ∞ . This has been nicely illustrated in the context of W gravity. Conclusion and remarks

In the present paper, we have found the new deformed w ∞ -symmetry by adopting the approach for undeformed case where the elements of the w ∞ -algebras ω s m may be expressed via the pair a † and a of quantum oscillator. We have shown that in the Calogero-Vasiliev case λ = 2, only in the case associated with odd and even indices the obtained symmetries are changed. Finally, note that in the same way one can construct the C λextended oscillator algebras of the supersymmetric extension of w ∞ -algebra by using the super-realization of C λ -extended oscillator algebras.
