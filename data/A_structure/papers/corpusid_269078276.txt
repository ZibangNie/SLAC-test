(1620) − production in K − p scattering process
9 April 2024E3DBF13671CB5D7A71A77E0CC4420F9810.1140/epjc/s10052-024-12754-1Received: 17 February 2024 / Accepted: 1 April 2024 /
In the present work, the production of (1620) − in the K − p scattering process is investigated by using an effective Lagrangian approach, where (1620) − is considered as a K − molecular state.Our estimations indicate that the cross sections for K − p → K + (1620) − are (1.48 +1.12  −0.69 ) µb at P K = 2.8 GeV, where the uncertainties are resulted from the variation of the model parameter.As for the K − p → π 0 − K + process, the cross sections are estimated to be (0.61 +0.47  −0.29 ) µb at P K = 2.87 GeV, which is consistent with the experimental measurements.

Introduction

In the recent two decades, some multiquark candidates have been observed, and the majority of these states contain at least one heavy quark (see Refs. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8] for more details).The investigations of the heavy pentaquark systems have undoubtedly expanded our comprehension of the quark model.Such expansion of the quark model inherently predicts the multiquark states composed of only light quarks, whose masses are usually overlapped with the excited states of the traditional hadrons.Consequently, the identifications of such kind of light exotic states experimentally or theoretically are rather difficult comparing to those with heavy quarks.Even so, some exotic candidates have been observed in the light sector.For example, the Y (2175) could be considered as a tetraquark state [9][10][11][12], the (1405) is resulted from the K N interaction [13][14][15][16], while the (2012) is generated from the K * interactions [17][18][19][20].

In addition to the aforementioned examples, another states with strangeness S = −2, known as (1620), could also be a promising candidate of exotic state.The experimental observations of (1620) could date back to nearly half a century ago.In Ref. [21], the cross sections for the process a e-mail: chendy@seu.edu.cn(corresponding author) K − p → − π + K 0 had been measured at 2.87 GeV, and significant structures were observed in the − π + invariant mass spectrum at a mass of 1630 MeV.The mass and width of 0 (1620) were reported to be 1628 ± 5 MeV and 15 ± 5 MeV, respectively.Subsequent measurements of the same process at 3.13, 3.30, and 3.58 GeV confirmed the existence of 0 (1620), and the mass and width of 0 (1620) were measured to be 1605.5 ± 5.6 MeV and 20.8 ± 7.4 MeV respectively [22].In addition, investigations of * productions in the K − p interactions were performed with the data at 2.87 GeV in the π , π π , (1530)π , Y K and Y K π invariant mass distributions [23].In these data, the resonance (1620) 0 was observed in the process K − p → − π + K 0 , however, no clear signal of (1620) − was observed in the process K − p → − π 0 K 0 , and the upper limit of the cross sections for K − p → (1620) − K + → − π 0 K + at P K = 2.87 GeV was measured to be 1.2 µb.

After a long silence, the experimental breakthrough about (1620) was appear in 2019, when the Belle Collaboration announced their observation of (1620) via the invariant mass spectrum of − π + in the process + c → − π + π + [24].With the number of (1620) 0 events be 2 orders of magnitude larger than that in previous experiments, the mass and width were precisely determined to be
m = 1610.4 ± 6.0(stat.) +6.1 −4.2 (syst.) MeV, = 59.9 ± 4.8(stat.) +2.8 −7.1 (syst.) MeV,(1)
respectively.

As depicted in Fig. 1, the (1620) is situated very close to the K threshold, a feather it shares with (1405) and (2012), which are slightly below the K N and K * thresholds, respectively.This similarity has led to theoretical proposals of a K molecular interpretations.By using the unitary extension of the chiral perturbation theory, the lowenergy meson-baryon scattering in the strangeness S = −2 sector was investigated, and a scattering-matrix pole was Fig. 1 A comparison of the possible molecular state with the corresponding thresholds found around 1600 MeV with J P = 1/2 − , which could be identified as (1620) [25,26].The estimations with Bethe-Salpeter equations in Ref. [27] indicated that the (1620) could be interpreted as K and K bound states with J P = 1/2 − , and the decay properties of (1620) in the K − K molecular scenario also favored the J P = 1/2 − assignment [28,29].In the framework of the one-bosonexchange model, the authors in Ref. [30] suggested (1620) as a K molecule with I (J P ) = 1/2(1/2 − ), which could be an analogue of (1405).However, there are different views of the inner structure of (1620).For instance, in Ref. [31], the analysis by using the chiral unitary model indicated that it is incompatible with the K − scattering length extracted from the ALICE experimental data when considering (1620) as a shallow quasi-bound state [32].

So far the nature of (1620) is still under debate.Investigations from different aspect are necessary to decode its inner structure.Besides the mass spectrum and decay behavior, the production properties of (1620) also have strong connection with its inner structure.It should be noted that the threshold of K and K − 0 are about 1610 MeV and 1686 MeV, respectively.Since the large discrepancy between the K threshold and (1620) mass, the coupling between (1620) and K should be suppressed comparing to that between (1620) and K .For instance, in Ref. [25], the coupling constant |g K |2 is about 7 times larger than |g K | 2 (Hereafter refers to (1620)), and similar conclusion can be obtained from the estimations in Ref. [26].Thus in the present work, we consider (1620) as a shallow bound state dominantly composed of K with J P = 1/2 − , and investigate the production process K − p → (1620) − K + by using an effective Lagrangian approach, in which (1620) could further decays into (1314)π .

2 (1620) production in the K − p scattering process 2.1 Molecular structure of (1620)

In the molecular frame, (1620) is considered as a S-wave K bound state.Here, we employ an effective Lagrangian approach to describe the interaction between (1620) and its components, which is,
L K = ig K ¯ K . (2)
The coupling constant g K could be determined by the compositeness condition with non-relativistic limit [33,34], which is,
g 2 K = 4π 4M m (m K + m ) 5/2 (m K m ) 1/2 √ 32 ,(3)
with = m K + m − m to be the binding energy of (1620).In the present work, we take = 6 MeV, and consequently, the coupling constant is estimated to be g K = 1.85. 1s a cross check of the reliability of the non-relativistic limit, this coupling constant is also estimated by the compositeness condition with local interaction, which has been widely applied to investigate the decay properties of the molecular states [35][36][37][38].In this approach, the local interaction is described by a correlation function with a model parameter , which should be the order of 1 GeV.With = 1 GeV, we get g K ∼ 1.7, which is consistent with the one estimated in the non-relativistic limit.

Cross sections for K
− p → − π 0 K +
In addition to the (1620) − production in the K − p scattering process, we have also investigated the process K − p → − π 0 K + , where − π 0 are the daughter particles of (1620) − .In Fig. 6, we present the cross sections for the process K − p → − π 0 K + depending on the momentum of the incident kaon beam.The P K dependences of the cross sections are very similar to those of the cross sections for K − p → (1620) − K + .In particular, the cross sections increase rapidly near the threshold of − π 0 K + , and then smoothly decrease with the increasing of P K .In the considered model parameter range, we find the cross section for K − p → − π 0 K + is estimated to be (0.61 +0.47  −0.29 ) µb at P K = 2.87 GeV, which is safely under the upper limit of the experimental measurement, which is 1.2 µb [23].


Summary

The (1620) state has been studied continuously over the years.Recently, the experimental observation of the double strange baryon (1620) from the Belle Collaboration is of great significance to its research, through which we are able to obtain precise mass and width data for the (1620) particle.The observed mass of (1620) is very close the the threshold of K , which indicates that (1620) could be a good candidate of molecular state composed of K .

Besides the resonance parameter, the production process could also provide some crucial information of the inner structure of (1620).In the present work, the production process K − p → (1620) − K + are investigated.The cross sections and the differential cross sections for K − p → (1620) − K + are evaluated.Our estimations indicate that the cross sections for K − p → (1620) − K + increase sharply near the threshold of (1620) − K + , and then decrease slowly with the increasing of P K .In particular, the cross section is estimated to be (1.49+1.12 −0.70 ) µb at P K = 2.87 GeV.In addition, we find the differential cross sections reach the maximum at the forward angle limit.

Consider the kinematics limit, (1620) should dominantly decay into π , thus in the present work, we also estimate the cross sections for K − p → − π 0 K + , where − π 0 come from the decay of the (1620) − .So far the upper limit of the cross section for K − p → 0 π − K + was measured to be 1.2 µb at P K = 2.87 GeV, and this cross section is estimated to be (0.61 +0.47  −0.29 ) µb, which is safely under the upper limit of the experimental measurement.Thus, based on the estimations in the present work and the experimental measurement, we can conclude that the molecular interpretations of (1620) cannot be rule out.
