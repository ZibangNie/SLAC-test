An identity theorem for the Fourier transform of polytopes on rationally parameterisable hypersurfaces
Jul 2020

Konrad Engel 
An identity theorem for the Fourier transform of polytopes on rationally parameterisable hypersurfaces
29Jul 2020
A set S of points in R n is called a rationally parameterisable hypersurface if S = {σ(t) : t ∈ D}, where σ : R n−1 → R n is a vector function with domain D and rational functions as components. A generalized n-dimensional polytope in R n is a union of a finite number of convex ndimensional polytopes in R n . The Fourier transform of such a generalized polytope P in R n is defined by FP (s) = P e −is·x dx. We prove that FP 1 (σ(t)) = FP 2 (σ(t)) ∀t ∈ O implies P1 = P2 if O is an open subset of D satisfying some well-defined conditions. Moreover we show that this theorem can be applied to quadric hypersurfaces that do not contain a line, but at least two points, i.e., in particular to spheres.

Introduction

A generalized n-dimensional polytope in R n is a union of a finite number of convex n-dimensional polytopes in R n . In this paper we study the Fourier transform of n-dimensional generalized polytopes P in R n F P (s) = P e −is·x dx , restricted on rationally parameterisable hypersurfaces. Here bold symbols denote vectors in R n or R n−1 and the product · is the standard scalar product. For example, using polar resp. spherical coordinates and the standard substitution t = tan(α), which implies cos(α) = 1−t 2 1+t 2 and sin(α) = 2t 1+t 2 , one obtains that the unit circle

The main aim of the paper is the proof of the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1. Let P 1 and P 2 be generalized n-dimensional polytopes in R n and let S = {σ(t) : t ∈ D} be a rationally parameterisable hypersurface in R n . Assume that there exists an open subset O of D \ σ −1 1 (0) in R n−1 that satisfies the hyperplane and the inner point condition. If
F P1 (σ(t)) = F P2 (σ(t)) ∀t ∈ O , then P 1 = P 2 .
In particular we apply Theorem 1.1 to quadric hypersurfaces, i.e., zero sets of an equation of the form
1 2 s T As + b T s + c = 0 ,(2)
where A is a symmetric matrix different from the zero matrix.

Theorem 1.2. If S is a quadric hypersurface that does not contain a line but at least two points, then, up to an exceptional set of hypersurface measure zero, it is an rp-hypersurface with some parameter domain D and every open subset O of D \ σ −1 1 (0) in R n−1 satisfies the hyperplane and the inner point condition.

It is easy to construct a counterexample to the assertion in Theorem 1.1 if σ(O) is contained in a hyperplane h given by a · x = 0. If P 2 is the mirror of P 1 with respect to h, then, for all s ∈ h, the product s · x is constant on the lines orthogonal to h, i.e., lines of the form {x = x 0 + λa : λ ∈ R}, and hence by Cavalieri's principle F P1 (s) = F P2 (s) for all s ∈ h, and, in particular,
F P1 (σ(t)) = F P2 (σ(t)) for all t ∈ O.
In Section 2 we present several auxiliary results on rational functions and exponential functions. The proof of Theorem 1.1 is contained in Section 3 and Theorem 1.2 is proved in Section 4. Finally some open problems are presented in Section 5.

An important tool is the explicit representation of the Fourier transform of a convex polytope which is a central subject in the theory on the exponential valuation of polytopes built by Brion, Lawrence, Khovanskii, Pukhlikov, and Barvinok [3,7,16,21] and has origins in results of Motzkin and Schoenberg (mentioned by Davis [9]) as well of Grunsky [14]. We recommend [2,4] for studying this theory.

We show that the equality F P1 (s) = F P2 (s) for all s ∈ σ(O) can be extended to F P1 (s) = F P2 (s) for all s ∈ R n . This is by no means clear in view of the preceding example. But having this extended equality the assertion in Theorem 1.1 follows from the injectivity of the Fourier transform, see e.g. [19].

The reconstruction of polytopes from sparse data of moments as well as from sparse data of the Fourier transform has been studied intensively in recent years. This concerns convex polytopes [8, 10-12, 15, 17] and also non-convex polygons [20] and generalized polytopes [13].

This paper should be considered a first step to solve Problem 3, i.e., the case that we do not know the values of the Fourier transform, but only the absolute value of it. In small-angle X-ray scattering and partly also in wide-angle X-ray scattering one can detect the absolute value of the Fourier transform on a sphere, the Ewald sphere, see e.g. [1,22]. Therefore uniqueness questions and phase retrieval algorithms are important subjects for application in crystallography and other domains. Related results (without the restriction to hypersurfaces) can be found e.g. in [5,6,18].

Proof of Theorem 1.1

First let P be a convex n-dimensional polytope in R n . Let V P be its vertex set. Here we consider each vertex as an element v of R n . Moreover let L P = {v−v ′ : v, v ′ ∈ V P }. From the theory on the exponential valuation of polytopes [3,7,16,21] it is known that
F P (s) = v∈VP Q P,v (s)e −iv·s ∀s ∈ C n \ Z P ,
where each Q P,v (s) is a rational function of the form
Q P,v (s) = I∈IP λ P,v,I ℓ∈I ℓ · s ,
the numerators λ P,v,I are real numbers, I P is a family of n-element linearly independent subsets of L P and Z P contains those s for which a term ℓ · s in the denominator is zero. Note that F P is an E-function of degree −n.

Now let P be a generalized n-dimensional polytope, i.e.,
P = P 1 ∪ · · · ∪ P m with convex n-dimensional polytopes P 1 , . . . , P m in R n . For J ⊆ [m] let P J = j∈J P j and let J = {J ⊆ [m] : P J is n-dimensional}.
Recall that the characteristic function χ T of a subset T of R n is defined by
χ T (x) = 1 if x ∈ T , 0 otherwise . It is well-known that 1 − χ P1∪···∪Pm = m j=1
(1 − χ Pj ) and χ ∩j∈J Pj = j∈J χ Pj which leads to the inclusion-exclusion formula
χ P1∪···∪Pm = ∅ =J⊆[m] (−1) |J|+1 χ PJ .
Integration over R n and omission of zero terms yields
F P (s) = J∈J (−1) |J|+1 F PJ (s) ,
i.e., a linear combination of E-functions of degree −n, which is again an Efunction of degree −n. Now let P 1 and P 2 be the two generalized n-dimensional polytopes given in the theorem.

By the assumption of the theorem
F P1 (σ(t)) − F P2 (σ(t)) = 0 ∀t ∈ O . Since F P1 − F P2 is an E-function of degree −n, Theorem 2.3 implies F P1 − F P2 = O .
Since the Fourier transform is obviously continuous in s we finally obtain
F P1 (s) − F P2 (s) = 0 ∀s ∈ C n .
The injectivity of the Fourier transform, see e.g. [19], implies P 1 = P 2 .
