Transient analysis of earthing electrodes considering soil ionization phenomenon under lightning impulse condition

This paper presents the transient behavior of an earthing system under the influence of lightning current injected in to it.Vertical rods and horizontal electrodes buried in homogeneous soil are considered in this analysis.State-space representation (SSR)based transmission line methodology (TLM) is adopted with nonlinear parameters for modeling the earthing electrodes in the time domain.Soil ionization phenomenon is taken up with the presence of residual resistivity.In this method, soil resistivity variation is adopted to evaluate the soil ionization phenomenon that occurs due to changes in the electric field.This study incorporates mutual coupling between the electrode segments for low and high soil resistivity.Earthing electrodes are subjected to high-magnitude lightning current impulse with different peaks.Transient voltage and impulse impedance are calculated to analyze the transient behavior of earthing electrodes with soil ionization.The impulse impedance of vertical earthing rods and horizontal electrodes is calculated and compared with the power frequency impedance.Percentage ohmic reduction between them is also reported.The influences of a critical electric field, soil resistivity and lightning magnitude on the transient performance of earthing electrodes are calculated.Also, an effect of the front time of lightning current impulse for a fixed peak on the impedance of the earthing electrode is evaluated and significant changes are observed.Simulated results are validated with the experimental and theoretical results reported in the literature, and adequate agreements are achieved.

Introduction

An earthing system comprising a vertical rod and horizontal conductor provides a low-impedance path that ensures a smooth distribution of the earth potential that develops due to high-magnitude current passing through it [1].An effective earthing system disperses the abnormal current in to the earth without increasing the potential difference and induced voltage that might be dangerous for operators and equipment [2].The performance of an earthing system associated with lightning return strokes differs from low-power frequency signals mainly owing to the soil ionization and conductor reactance [3][4][5].Analyzing the performance of the earthing system is necessary during high impulse current injection to find the best electrical system design that provides safety to the power system equipment and humans against unusual events.The representation of the earthing conductor as an equivalent impedance is required due to the lightning current frequency that makes the reactive effect very relevant.The attenuation of lightning current results in a non-uniform distribution of potential along the earthing electrode [6].Several numerical simulations and tests focus mainly on analyzing the impulse performance of an earthing system [7,8].In [8], the simulation model results include mutual coupling effects for the earthing system's performance.Many numerical and hybrid techniques such as an improved circuit-based model [9], transmission line theory [10], electromagnetic field approach [11] and hybrid approach [12] have been proposed to simulate the transient behavior of earthing electrodes under lightning current energization conditions.Transmission line modeling (TLM) considering the mutual effect between different segments of earthing electrodes has been applied in studies for predicting the surge propagation delay [8,13].Computer simulation and earthing system modeling help to analyze soil ionization behavior even with high frequency and fast varying lightning current conditions [14].Also, complex field equation-based models have recently been used in timedomain and frequency-domain studies [15][16][17][18].The effects of soil permittivity and resistivity on earthing electrodes under subsequent lightning stroke have been analyzed [15].A new formula to analyze the surge characteristic, including the frequency-dependent inductive and soil ionization phenomena in the time domain, has also been proposed in [16].In [17], a new model was proposed to analyze the transient behavior of earthing grid that deals with frequencydependent electrical parameters.Time-domain approaches to study the transient behavior of earthing electrodes by considering the mutual inductance and capacitance are also reported in existing literature [18].Experimental investigations of soil ionization and breakdown delay of a two-layer soil model with varying moisture contents have also been reported [19,20].Consideration of soil residual resistivity and soil dynamic behavior makes the soil ionization phenomenon more accurate during the breakdown period [21,22].The transient performance of vertical earthing rods subjected to the sum of two double exponential current functions has been reported [23].However, the complex nonlinear dynamic phenomenon of soil ionization surrounding the earthing electrode is simulated using the enlargement of an electrode radius during the injection of high impulse current on the grounding system [24][25][26][27][28][29][30].Design of any substation's earthing system should be safe and economical [31].The transient analysis helps to design a safe and economic earthing system.Moreover, studies based on experimental simulation of soil ionization demonstrate the variation in ionization with respect to time-space and non-uniformity of the ionization zone surrounding the electrode [32][33][34].Related literature reveals the scope to improve the transient model of earthing electrodes under nonlinear conductance conditions while considering the residual resistivity of ionized soil.Investigating the impulse impedance of the soil under the influence of impulse current's front time is another prospective research.

The method proposed in this study to analyze the transient behavior of earthing electrodes is a modification of the technique used in [35].In [35], soil ionization model is used without consideration of soil residual resistivity and spark discharge region.

Hence, in this work, a numerical approach combining TLM with SSR is used to analyze the transient behavior of earthing electrodes during soil breakdown.Space-time variable soil resistivity is used to simulate the soil ionization phenomenon where soil resistivity is controlled according to its relationship with the local instantaneous value of the electric field.The space-time variable soil resistivity approach is embedded in the TLM-SSR approach to simulate the nonlinear effect of the soil ionization process.The spark discharge region surrounding the earthing electrode is also considered in the analysis.The concepts of residual resistivity and nonlinear parameters are adopted in the soil ionization phenomenon, generating satisfactory results.Horizontal electrodes and vertical rods with varying lengths are subjected to high-magnitude impulse current with variable front times, and impulse impedance is calculated for the different critical electric fields.The resistances under power frequency are evaluated for horizontal and vertical earthing electrodes and obtained results are compared with impulse impedance.Elementary results of the proposed method were verified with the results in existing literature and additional results were reported in Sect. 5.


Soil ionization phenomenon

Injection of high-magnitude lightning current on the earthing system results in a high current density that generates an electric field E(t) surrounding the electrode segments.As the generated electric field exceeds the soil breakdown strength or critical electric field (E c ), ionization occurs in the earthing electrode's surroundings.An arc emerges during this process that generates the tracking on the surface of the soil grains.The tracking increases along with increase in current density until it reaches a threshold value.If current density increases above the threshold value, the soil is punctured and sparked over is generated [32][33][34].In this study, soil resistivity function uses a space-time variable approach to analyze the ionization phenomenon.The electrode part influenced by the ionization becomes a good conductor [18].Several theoretical and numerical studies regarding soil ionization have been reported in existing literature [36,37].In [36], the soil model adopts an increasing earth conductor radius during the soil ionization period.Investigations of ionization in the sand with varying water content were carried out by [37].Soil surface ionization may be relevant when analyzing an earthing system subjected to lightning flash, as given in [38,39].Another study presented a theory of residual resistivity retention by the soil in the ionization zone and proposed a model for residual resistivity [21].The soil's residual resistivity affects its earthing conductance, which in turn affects the performance of an earthing system during soil ionization [14].A typical residual resistivity value in the ionization region that is 7% of steady soil resistivity can be considered to simulate the ionization phenomenon as reported in [33,40].For different soil models with varying resistivities (50-827 m) and variable magnitudes of lightning currents, the residual resistivity in the ionization region belongs in the range of 1.7-47% of the original soil resistivity as demonstrated in [21,[41][42][43].In this study, the electric field was computed at every time step.The time-dependent electric field for each segment of the earthing conductor was Fig. Time-dependent state-space representation

The state-space representation (SSR) is generally used to evaluate the dynamic behavior of the system when it is subjected to any external excitation.TLM with SSR is used to evaluate the transient performance of the earthing system in [35].The same is applied here to evaluate the R ac , L, C and G for the earthing electrode.Due to the soil ionization phenomenon and mutual coupling between the electrode segments, time-dependent effects are included in the SSR.So parameters A, B, C and D given in [53,54] become time-variant state parameters A 1 (t), B 1 (t), C 1 (t), D 1 (t) and state-space equation can be represented as ( 20)

Validation of model


Validation with Geri [25]

In this section, the current impulses higher than 25 kA peak were injected on a 5 m long horizontal earthing electrode and 1 m long vertical earthing rod.Both electrodes are made of steel material with resistivity (ρ c ) 0.25 × 10 −6 m, and buried in homogenous soil with the 42 m soil resistivity and permittivity of 10.The value of E c for soil ionization phenomenon is considered as 350 kV/m.The radius of horizontal electrode and vertical rod is 4 mm and 25 mm, respectively.The horizontal earthing electrode is placed at the depth of 0.6 m from soil surface.Soil ionization phenomena occur under such a high current level.Figure 4a, b represents the transient voltage at the injection point of horizontal earthing electrode and vertical earthing rod, respectively.From Fig. 4a, b, it is obtained that calculated results of the proposed method closely match with the experimental results reported in [25] for both horizontal earthing electrode and vertical rod.


Validation with Gazzana et al. [14]

For other validation of the proposed method, a horizontal earthing electrode with the length of 12 m and radius of 0.0065 m is selected as in [14].The electrode is buried at a depth of 0.5 m in homogenous soil with a resistivity of 800 m and a relative permittivity of 6.A double exponential lightning surge of 50 kA (8/20 μs) is injected at one end of the horizontal earthing electrode.The value of a critical electrical field E c considered for the soil ionization is 1014.30kV/m.Results of induced voltage with and without soil ionization condition are plotted in Fig. 5. 35.97% reduction in transient voltage is noticed when soil ionization is considered for the non-ionization condition.The voltage obtained from the developed method is close to the results as reported in [14].

Influence of critical electric field on impulse impedance

In this section, a horizontal earthing electrode and a vertical rod with length of 10 m and a radius of 0.0065 m are used to analyze the transient behavior of the earthing system.Both electrodes are buried separately in different homogenous soil with different soil resistivity (50, 157, 310, 579 and 827 m).The burial depth of horizontal electrode in the soil is 0.5 m and ρ c 0.25 × 10 −6 m.A lightning impulse of 10 kA peak (8/20 μs) is injected at one end of the electrode.Impulse impedance of earthing electrode is calculated for different critical electrical fields, i.e., 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 kV/cm.Simulation results are reported in Tables 1 and 2 for horizontal earthing electrode and vertical rod, respectively.The results show that impulse impedance increases with increase in soil resistivity for the assumed length of the earthing electrode.The critical electrical field has a great influence on the impulse impedance of the earthing system.With an increment in the critical electrical field, the impulse impedance of the earthing system increases.For a high value of E c , impulse impedance of the system is high for both horizontal  electrode and vertical rod. Conclusion

The mathematical model of soil ionization was implemented with considering conductive and capacitive components of TLM and the residual resistivity of the soil.A hybrid approach of TLM and SSR is used to analyze the soil ionization phenomenon.The space-time variable soil resistivity approach is embedded with SSR-TLM methodology to simulate the nonlinear effect of soil ionization.The proposed method is validated with the experimental and computed results reported in literature and satisfactory agreement is reached.The influence of different soil resistivity, critical electrical field and current magnitude on transient analysis of earthing electrode is analyzed and reported.The influence of the front time of impulse current injected into earthing electrodes on impulse impedance is analyzed.The results of the study show that there is a change in transient impedance when an impulse with a shorter front time is injected on earth-  ing electrodes under high soil resistivity conditions.Study shows that proposed method is limited to single electrode.
