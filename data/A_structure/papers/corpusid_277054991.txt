Anemia is a significant public health concern among adolescent girls, particularly in developing countries, where iron deficiency is prevalent due to inadequate dietary intake and poor nutrient bioavailability.This study evaluates the impact of supplementing a nutrient-dense millet bar on the hemoglobin (Hb) levels, weight, and overall nutritional status of anemic adolescent girls.Millets, known for their high iron, fiber, and protein content, were combined with jaggery, roasted Bengal gram, and groundnuts to create a functional food aimed at addressing iron deficiency.A total of 30 adolescent girls (ages 15-19) with Hb levels below 9 g/dL were selected and divided into two groups: a treatment group (n=15), which received a 30g millet bar daily for 90 days, and a control group (n=15), which followed their regular diet.Pre-and post-intervention assessments measured hemoglobin levels, weight, and height.The results indicated a significant improvement in hemoglobin levels and weight in the treatment group compared to the control group, highlighting the potential of millet-based supplementation in combating adolescent anemia.This study underscores the importance of traditional, sustainable, and bioavailable food-based approaches to addressing iron deficiency.Given its cost-effectiveness, cultural acceptability, and nutritional benefits, millet-based supplementation can serve as a practical public health intervention, particularly in school feeding programs and community nutrition initiatives.Further research on large-scale implementation and long-term adherence can help establish millets as a key dietary solution for improving adolescent health and nutrition security.Objectives of the Study1. To assess the impact of millet-based supplementation on hemoglobin levels in adolescent girls diagnosed with anemia.2. To evaluate changes in anthropometric measurements (height and weight) following supplementation.3. To determine the acceptability and feasibility of millet-based nutrition bars as a sustainable dietary intervention.

Introduction

Adolescent girls are a critical population subgroup ensuring the health and well-being of future generations.Malnutrition in the form of stunting and thinness is widely prevalent among adolescent girls and is associated with obstetric complications and reduced work capacity.Anemia is a significant public health problem in developing countries, affecting a large proportion of adolescent girls.In this context, the current study was undertaken to assess the impact of supplementation with a nutrient-dense millet bar on the hemoglobin (Hb) levels of anemic adolescent girls.Adolescent girls are the cornerstone of future maternal and child health.Nutritional deficiencies during adolescence, particularly anemia, can have severe consequences, including stunted growth, reduced cognitive ability, and increased obstetric risks in later life (Bora, 2020) [1] .Iron deficiency anemia remains one of the most prevalent micronutrient deficiencies among adolescent girls in India, exacerbated by inadequate dietary intake, poor bioavailability of iron in plant-based diets, and menstrual blood loss (Kumar et al., 2019)  [2] .In response to this challenge, nutrient-dense supplementation has been identified as a sustainable strategy to improve hemoglobin levels and overall nutritional status.Millets, such as finger millet (Eleusine coracana), pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum), and sorghum (Sorghum bicolor), are rich in iron, calcium, and fiber and have been recommended as an effective dietary intervention for anemia prevention (Parameswaran & Sadasivam, 2021) [3] .This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of an energy-dense millet bar, rich in iron and protein, in improving the Hb levels, weight, and height of anemic adolescent girls over 90 days.


Materials and Methods


Study Design and Sample Selection

A total of 30 adolescent girls (aged 14-19 years) from a rural community were selected based on their Hb levels (<9 g/dL) and underweight status.The participants were randomly divided into two groups:

• Treatment Group (n=15): Received a 30g millet bar daily for 90 days.

• Control Group (n=15): Followed their regular diet without supplementation.Baseline anthropometric measurements (height and weight) and Hb levels were recorded for both groups before supplementation.The same parameters were reassessed after 90 days to evaluate the impact of the intervention.


Composition of Millet Bar

The energy-dense millet bar was formulated using:
• Roasted Finger Millet (Ragi) • Roasted Bajra (Pearl Millet) • Puffed Sorghum (Jowar) • Roasted Bengal Gram (Chickpeas) • Roasted Groundnut (Peanuts)
• Jaggery (Iron-rich natural sweetener) The combination of these ingredients ensured a rich supply of iron, protein, and essential micronutrients necessary for improving anemia.


Results and Discussion Anthropometric and Hemoglobin Changes

After 90 days, the treatment group showed noticeable improvements in weight and Hb levels compared to the control group.The mean increase in Hb levels was 0.35 g/dL in the treatment group, whereas the control group showed no significant improvement.

The results of the study showed that the treatment group had a significant improvement in Hb levels (p<0.05)compared to the control group.The mean Hb level increased from 7.4% to 8.3% in the treatment group compared to 7.3% to 7.2% in the control group.The weight-for-height scores also improved in the treatment group, although the difference was not statistically significant.


Discussion

The findings of the study suggest that supplementation with a nutrient-dense millet bar can improve the Hb levels of anemic adolescent girls.The ingredients used in the millet bar, including finger millet, Bajra, and Bengal gram, are rich sources of iron, which is essential for the production of Hb.The use of jaggery, a natural sweetener, as a binder in the millet bar adds to its nutritional value.The results of this study provide evidence for the potential of millet-based food products as a low-cost and effective intervention for preventing anemia in adolescent girls


Statistical Findings and Analysis

The results indicate that the millet supplementation had a positive impact on weight gain and hemoglobin levels, albeit marginal.The slight improvement in Hb levels suggests that regular consumption of iron-rich millet products, combined with vitamin C intake for enhanced iron absorption, can contribute to better anemia management among adolescent girls.These findings align with previous studies on the role of millet-based supplementation in improving adolescent nutritional status (Sharma et al., 2020) [4] .However, the short duration of the study and limited sample size were constraints in determining statistically significant results.


Conclusion

Adolescent anemia remains a significant public health concern, particularly in developing regions where dietary deficiencies, inadequate iron intake, and poor bioavailability of nutrients contribute to widespread micronutrient malnutrition.Addressing this issue requires sustainable, culturally acceptable, and nutrient-dense dietary interventions.

The supplementation of adolescent girls with a millet-based nutrition bar offers a promising and practical approach to improving their iron status, overall nutritional intake, and long-term health outcomes.

The study on the Assessment of Supplementation with Nutrient-Dense Millet Bar to Anemic Adolescent Girls provides valuable insights into the potential benefits of incorporating iron-rich millets into daily diets.Millets such as finger millet (ragi), pearl millet (Bajra), and sorghum (jowar) are not only rich in essential micronutrients like iron, calcium, and fiber but also possess a lower glycemic index, making them ideal for improving the dietary quality of adolescent girls without increasing the risk of metabolic disorders.

Additionally, the inclusion of jaggery as a natural sweetener enhances iron absorption, while roasted Bengal gram and groundnuts provide high-quality protein, essential for growth and development during adolescence.

Improvement in Hemoglobin (Hb) Levels: Regular supplementation with millet-based bars contributed to an increase in hemoglobin levels among the participants.This suggests that the bioavailable iron in millets, combined with jaggery, plays a crucial role in reducing anemia.The results align with previous studies emphasizing the role of biofortified and naturally iron-rich foods in combating anemia.Positive Impact on Weight and Nutritional Status: The study observed a slight improvement in the weight of participants post-supplementation, indicating enhanced overall nutrition.The balanced macronutrient profile of the millet bar-rich in complex carbohydrates, proteins, and good fats-ensured steady energy release and helped in maintaining healthy body weight.Sustainability and Acceptability: One of the most significant advantages of millet-based supplementation is cultural acceptability.Unlike synthetic supplements, which may cause side effects or adherence issues, millet bars are palatable, easy to consume, and cost-effective.Moreover, millets are locally grown, climate-resilient crops that contribute to food security and sustainable agriculture.Potential for Scaling Up as a Public Health Intervention: The positive nutritional outcomes from this study suggest that millet bars can be incorporated into government nutrition programs such as the Mid-Day Meal Scheme, Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS), and adolescent health programs.Fortifying school meals with millet-based products can significantly contribute to anemia reduction at the community level.

Traditional supplementation approaches, such as iron tablets and fortified foods, have been met with varying degrees of success, often hindered by low compliance, side effects, and accessibility issues.Hence, a food-based approach-leveraging locally available, nutrient-dense, and culturally accepted foodsemerges as a sustainable and effective strategy for tackling anemia.This study, titled "Assessment of Supplementation with Nutrient-Dense Millet Bar to Anemic Adolescent Girls," investigates the impact of millet-based nutrition bars on the health and hemoglobin (Hb) levels of adolescent girls.Millets, such as finger millet (ragi), pearl millet (Bajra), and sorghum (jowar), are rich sources of iron, calcium, fiber, and essential micronutrients, making them ideal candidates for dietary intervention.Additionally, jaggery serves as a natural iron-rich sweetener that enhances iron absorption, while roasted Bengal gram (chickpeas) and groundnuts (peanuts) provide high-quality protein, crucial for growth and development.Recommendations forFuture Research 1. Conducting a longer-duration study with a larger sample size to confirm the effectiveness of millet supplementation.2. Assessing iron bioavailability and absorption rates of millet-based foods in anemic individuals.3. Combining millet supplementation with vitamin C-rich foods to enhance non-heme iron absorption.
