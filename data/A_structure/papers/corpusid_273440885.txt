Background: 'Breast cancer' the most common cancer amongst females in India.Having good Quality of life (QoL) among the patients undergoing treatment is one of the vital and significant factors for ensuring holistic approach to treatment and well-being of cancer patients.The present study was conducted to assess the QoL and its determinants among breast cancer patients undergoing treatment at a tertiary care institute in western Maharashtra.Material and Methodology: 300 histopathologically confirmed breast cancer patients undergoing treatment at a tertiary care centre were divided into three groups to include 100 patients in each group.Group 1 (patients with duration of treatment 1 to 2 years) Group 2 (patients with duration of treatment 2 to 5 years) and Group 3 (more than 5 years).Validated tool Function Assessment of Cancer Therapy Breast specific (FACT-B) questionnaire was used to collect information on QoL and analysed using appropriate statistical tests.Results: Mean age of patients was 53.2 + 10.08 years, 136 (45.33%) were from age group 51 to 65 years.Majority of the patients 106 (35.33%) were educated till secondary schooling and 172 (57.33 %) were belonging to the middle-upper class.Lump in breast was most common presenting symptom in 235 (78.33%) cases followed by nipple retraction.275 (91.67 %) cases had infiltrating ductal Carcinoma and 144 (48%) patents were in Stage II cancer.Over all mean FACT-B score for QoL was 104.02 + 18.65 out of 141.Mean FACT-B score for Group 1, Group 2 and Group 3 was 88.18 +17.20, 105.89 + 11.78 and 118.28 + 12.45 respectively with (p< 0.05), suggests significant improvement in QoL with increase in time elapsed since treatment.Conclusion: Socio-demographic factors, type of surgery and duration of treatment had significant effect on QoL among breast cancer patients.Apropos physicians, paramedic staff and domestic care givers need to be sensitised on importance of QoL for collective and deliberate efforts to achieve holistic care.QoL can be made integral part of breast cancer treatment and management policy or programme.

BackgRound

'Cancer' is a large group of diseases that can affect almost any organ or tissue of the body and is the second most commonly diagnosed disease (11.6%) and the fourth leading cause of death (6.9%) as per global estimates of 2022 for both sexes.Breast cancer was the most common cancer in women, accounting 23.8%, and caused deaths in 15.4% females worldwide.In 2012, the number of breast cancer cases surpassed cervical cancer cases; since then, breast cancer has been the leading cause of cancer among Indian women.As per estimates of 2022 for India, breast cancer is leading in incidence with 13.6% cases and also causing most deaths (10.7%) for both sexes.Out of all cancers detected among females, breast cancer was leading with 26.6%, and out of all deaths, 22.0% were occurring because of breast cancer in India. [1]There is variation even for different geographic regions of India, and the incidence of breast cancer has been steadily increasing in India for the past 2 decades and more so in urban areas. [2,3]] Breast cancer patients undergo serious physical and psychological trauma first on diagnosis of cancer and then during various stages of treatment.Women who are newly diagnosed with breast cancer feel sad, anxious, and sometimes shocked and scared. [7]Furthermore, even treatment itself may also have adverse effects on fertility, libido, digestion, sleep disturbance, hair loss, mood swings, and so on.Collectively, it adversely affects their quality of life (QoL), which comprises multiple domains such as physical functioning, psychological and social well-being, and social support.Patients on chemotherapy suffer physical and psychological issues that adversely affect patients' QoL. [8]ually, patients and physicians may have differences of opinion and priorities regarding their illness, treatment, and possible outcomes.Final outcomes could be improved further by assessing QoL at regular intervals during treatment to detect and treat functional and psychological issues.Employing the patients' views into the decision making process not only will empower patients but also can improve the satisfaction level and compliance with treatment. [9]However, despite growing awareness on QoL, there is paucity of studies that are conducted in different geographic regions of the country.Therefore, the present study was undertaken to assess QoL and its determinants among breast cancer patients undergoing treatment at a tertiary care center.


MateRIal and Methodology

It is a descriptive cross-sectional study, in which 300 histopathologically confirmed breast cancer patients, who were undergoing treatment at a tertiary care center in western Maharashtra, were included.Patients were further divided into three groups as per the duration of treatment that patients were receiving at the time of data collection: Group 1 (duration of treatment 1 to 2 years), group 2 (duration of treatment 2 to 5 years), and group 3 (more than 5 years of treatment).The primary objective of the study was to describe QoL among female patients of breast cancer who have received treatment for 1 year and more and find association of QoL with duration of treatment.The secondary objective was to describe QoL with respect to selected sociodemographic and clinical factors and to find association if any.Data were collected over a period of 18 months.The sample size was calculated using the combined mean score and standard deviation (SD) from reference studies on the QoL. [10]The sample size was calculated for each domain of QoL, that is, physical well-being, social well-being, emotional well-being, functional well-being, and breast-cancer-specific domains, by considering the level of significance as 95% and the error of margin as 5%.The sample size calculated for the physical well-being domain was 205, that for emotional well-being was 133, that for functional well-being was 197, and that for breast-cancer-specific domains was 48.Out of all these domains, social well-being had the highest sample size of 205, which was taken for rest all other domains.However, a total of 300 patients were included into the study for better precision in outcome and they were further subdivided using the stratified random sampling method into group 1, group 2, and group 3 as per duration of treatment as explained earlier to include 100 patients in each group.Female patients of 18 years and above, who were diagnosed as cases of breast cancer by histopathology or FNAC and registered at a tertiary care hospital, were included in the study.Also, female patients who had completed at least 1 year after starting of any kind of treatment modality or in combination (radiotherapy and/or chemotherapy and/or hormonal therapy and/or surgery) were included in the study.Patients with psychiatric illness or suffering from comorbidities leading to major physical or psychological distress and also breast cancer patients suffering with terminal illness and unable to comprehend were excluded from the study.Information on QoL among breast cancer patients was collected by using the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-Breast specific (FACT-B) questionnaire (Version 4), which is available in English, Hindi, and Marathi languages.Data were collected together by the principal investigator and a medicosocial worker in the presence of the husband or relative accompanying the patient.3][14] FACT-B (breast-specific) version 4 has two parts; the first is the generic part, that is, Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy-General (FACT-G), and the other part is Breast Cancer Specific Subscale (BCS).FACT-G is composed of four subscales/domains: Physical well-being (PWB), Social well-being (SWB), Emotional well-being (EWB), and Functional well-being (FWB).There is also a Trail Outcome Index (TOI), which is composed of PWB, FWB, and BCS [Supplementary Figure 1].TOI efficiently summaries physical status, functional status, and side effects of treatment in breast cancer patients.[17] During collection and assessment of QoL, any patient found to have a very low score or in psychological distress was referred for assistance as deemed appropriate.

Collected data were maintained in an Excel sheet and analyzed using software IBM SPSS version 20.0.Descriptive statistics, mean, SD, percentage, range, and 95% CI, were calculated, wherever required for selective sociodemographic and clinical factors and also for domains of QoL.In inferential statistics, Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was applied as the test of normality, one-way ANOVA test was applied for domains and subgroups satisfying normality and homogeneity test, and Kruskal-Wallis test was applied if earlier tests failed.Student's unpaired T-test was applied whenever there were two groups.

Informed written consent was obtained from all study participants.The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee letter dated 10 Nov 2015/IEC/Nov 2015.


Results

The mean age of patients was 53.02 ± 10.8 (range: 29-80 years).106 (35.33%) patients were educated till secondary schooling, 68 (22.67%) were illiterate, and most of the patients were housewives [268 (89.33%)].172 (57.33%) were belonging to the middle-upper class, followed by 51 (17%) from the middle-lower class and 45 (15%) from the upper class, while 30 (10%) were from the lower upper class and only 2 (0.67%) patients were belonging to the lower class of SES.In the present study, the majority of subjects 246 (82%) were of religion Hindu, followed by Muslim 23 (8%), 288 (96%) patients were staying with family, 274 (91.33%) were married, 21 (7%) were widows, the majority of the patients [268 (89.33%)] were housewives, and only 32 (10.67%) were employed [Table 1].

Lump in breast [78.33% (235)] was the most common presenting symptom, followed by retraction of nipple 10.33% (31).The mean BMI of patients was 24.14 ± 6.58 (Kg/m 2 ), the majority of the patients 214 (71.33%) were having normal BMI, and 75 (25%) patients were overweight.As per pathological typing, infiltrating ductal carcinoma was the most common type diagnosed in 275 (91.67%) of patients

In a study conducted by Harminder Singh et al. [18] in Punjab (2010), the mean age of the study participants was found to be 49.05 ± 14.35 years, which is consistent with the age of study participants in our study (53.02 + 10.8) years.21] In our study, the majority of cancer patients were Hindu (82%).

Pandey M et al. at a tertiary care hospital in Kerala also found that 78% patients were of religion Hindu. [22]In our study, the majority of patients (35.33%) were educated till secondary schooling and 89.33% were housewives; similar demographic profiles have also been reported by studies conducted in Delhi. [10,23]In our study, 57.33% were belonging to the middle upper class, 17% were from the middle lower, 15% were from the upper class, 10% were from the lower upper class, and only 0.67% were belonging to the lower class of SES.A study conducted in Delhi by Kaur N et al. [10] reported that 74.8% of study patients were from the low-income class, 23.8% were from the middle social class, and 2.4% were from the upper class.This variation could have been because in our study, most of the cases were dependants of Government service personnel, who is invariably the head of family with a sustainable source of incomes and more or less good living conditions.

In the current study, the majority of the patients (78.33%) presented with a lump in the breast, and 5.0% of study participants presented with nipple discharge; findings are similar to results published by Kaur N. et al. [10] Recurrence of the breast cancer was only significantly associated with the social well-being domain and not with the rest of the domains; a systematic review by Heidary Z et al. commented on panic and fear of losing the peaceful life and also of metastatic cancer. [10,20] were similar to findings in our study; that is, 91.67% of patients were of infiltrating ductal carcinoma and 7.33% patients of infiltrating lobular carcinoma.

In the present study, the majority of the cancer patients, 48%, were in Stage II, 33% were in Stage III, and 18% in Stage I.  1].The present study also showed that the mean score for overall FACT-B was the highest (118.28 ± 12.45) among patients who took treatment for more than 5 years, followed by a score of 105.89 ± 11.78 in patients who had taken treatment for a duration of 2 to 5 years, and the score was the lowest (88.18 ± 17.20) for patients who were taking treatment for the duration of 1 to 2 years.Patients who were taking treatment for a longer duration were found to have a significant difference in QoL (P value < 0.001) [Table 3].

The highest level of education (postgraduation) had significantly (P value < 0.001) higher FACT-B and subdomains scores compared to lower levels of education.However, no significant difference in QoL score was observed based on the occupational status of the patients.There was statistically significant better QoL found among patients who were married compared to unmarried/divorced in domains like social, emotional, functional well-being, TOI, FCAT-G, and overall FACT-B.There was statistically significant better QoL found in domains like social well-being, functional well-being, and FCAT-G in patients who were living with family.Physical well-being, BCS subscale, and TOI domains were significantly associated with QoL with BMI [Table 4].Overall FACT-B and subdomains scores were significantly higher among patients who were detected in stage-I cancer than among patients detected with advanced stage cancer.Similarly, patients who had undergone either MRM (modified radical mastectomy) or RM (radical mastectomy) surgery had significantly lower FACT-B scores compared to patients who had undergone breast conservation surgery.However, no significant difference in overall score was observed for number of chemotherapy cycles received or radiotherapy and which side of breast involved, either right or left side.However, there was a statistically significant difference  [20,25] In the current study, the overall mean FACT-B score was 104.2 ± 18.65 and mean scores for all other subdomains were higher compared to studies conducted in India.In our study, the mean SWB score was high (22.62 ± 4.05), which suggests patients had better acceptance of illness; had better support from family, friends, and relatives; and also had satisfied sexual life.Similarly, patients had a high EWB score (17.28 ± 4.10), which indicates that they were less nervous and were satisfied with their coping efforts while undergoing treatment.The mean score of FWB in our study was 19.08 ± 4.14, which indicates that patients were able to do and enjoy house work, were happy in their daily life, and were having good sleep.Our study participants had high PWB and BCS scores, suggesting that they had lesser pain, nausea, and side effects and were less worried   [22] the overall FACT-B score mean score was 90.5 ± 18.4, the PWB mean score was 19.8 ± 4.7, the SWB mean score was 19.9 ± 5.3, the EWB mean score was 14 9 ± 4, the FWB mean score was 13 ± 5.7, and the BCS score was (23.7 ± 4.3), which were lower for all domains compared to the findings of our study.Similarly, a study conducted by Kaur N et al. [10] had also found lower QoL scores as compared to the present study.This difference may be because of the higher education status of patients (64% were higher secondary or higher schooling); most patients were married (91% were still married), with high SES (72% were from upper/middle upper class), and treatment was being taken in a dedicated hospital for government personnel.All these factors, that is higher-level education, [10] marriage, [22,26] and high SES, [10] are known to play a significant role in the improvement of QoL among breast cancer patients.Our study brought out that there was a significant association among breast cancer patients according to the type of surgery performed on the patient and all domains of QoL: PWB, SWB, EWB, FWB, BCS, TOI, FACT-G, and total FACT-B.Patients who underwent BCS surgery had a better quality of life compared to MRM and RM surgery.Similar higher QoL scores in patients with breast conservation surgery have been reported by studies from India and abroad. [27,28]This could be because of not only lesser damage to anatomical structures of the breast, faster recovery, and lesser side effects on the operated site like pain, swelling of arm, and impairment of motor activities but also lesser psychological trauma of losing an organ.

The present study showed that there was a significant difference in QoL as per the duration of treatment and the group who had taken the longest duration of treatment, that is, more than 5 years, had the highest score of QoL.A significant difference in all domains among patients who have received treatment for more than 5 years compared to the duration of treatment of 2 to 5 years and 1 to 2 years was observed.Similarly, higher scores are observed in patient group (elapsed since treatment more than 5 years, 89.83 ± 12.80) have been reported by study conducted in Delhi by Kaur N, et al., [17] study was conducted by Montazeri A. et al. [29] in Iran and by Wani SQ, et al. in  Srinagar. [30] a concept of 'Quality of Life', people are reasonably acquainted; however, we lack in depth assessment and understanding of QoL among cancer patients.Assessment of QoL carried out in different geographical regions of the country may provide insight into treating physicians, paramedics, care takers, and even policy makers.This study has studied QoL in a fairly large sample size but still has some limitations.First, the present study was hospital-based, so the extent of social reintegration and rehabilitation of women with breast cancer into society could not be determined.The role of medicosocial factors among women with breast cancer who were unable to reach health care facility could not be assessed.Last, changes that occur in QoL at different points of time such as before initiation of treatment, during treatment, and after completion of treatment cannot be commented upon with data being collected at one point of treatment.However, with intention to overcome this limitation to some extent and get valuable inputs with respect to improvement in QoL with an increase in duration of treatment, study participants were divided into three different groups.In the present study, we attempted to explore QoL of breast cancer patients in the western part of India.There is a necessity to undertake a detailed, well-planned, multicentric, follow-up study, which may provide comprehensive analysis and evaluation of an oversighted and neglected area of QoL among cancer patients, which may help in empowering patients, taking informed decisions, and formulating policies or programs designed for treatment and management of cancer patients.


conclusIon

The present study has brought out various sociodemographic factors; type of surgery and treatment duration had a profound effect on the QoL among breast cancer patients.Therefore, physicians, paramedical staff, and even care takers need to be sensitized on the importance and factors that affect the QoL among cancer cases undergoing treatment.In addition to clinical care, psychosocial support and counseling should be an integral part of the treatment plan to improve the patient's QoL as well as years of life.


Key Message

QoL is important to assess and to be considered while devising treatment plan to achieve holistic well-being among breast cancer patients.
