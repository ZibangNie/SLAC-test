While the single-layer operator for the Laplacian is well understood, questions remain concerning the single-layer operator for the Bilaplacian, particularly with regard to invertibility issues linked with degenerate scales.In this article, we provide simple sufficient conditions ensuring this invertibility for a wide range of problems.

Introduction

Let Γ be a smooth curve in the plane (we don't intend to be rigorous at this stage).For any density q ∈ H −1/2 (Γ ), the harmonic single-layer potential is defined by: S Γ q(x) = ˆΓ g 0 (x − y)q(y) ds(y) for all x ∈ R 2 , where g 0 is the fundamental solution of the Laplacian, that reads (κ is a positive parameter):
g 0 (x) = − 1 2π ln |x| κ for all x ∈ R 2 \ {0}.
The operator S Γ is bounded from H −1/2 (Γ ) into H 1 ℓoc (R 2 ) and so is the operator:
V Γ : H −1/2 (Γ ) −→ H 1/2 (Γ ) q −→ γ D Γ • S Γ q,
where γ D Γ stands for the usual Dirichlet trace operator on Γ .It is well known that V Γ is invertible if and only if κ ̸ = Cap Γ , where Cap Γ is a constant called the logarithmic capacity of Γ .Furthermore, according to [10,Theorem 8.16], if κ > Cap Γ , the operator S Γ is positive definite on H −1/2 (Γ ) and it has one negative eigenvalue if κ < Cap Γ .The value of Cap Γ is difficult to evaluate in general (it is explicitly known only for certain geometries such as a disk, an ellipse, a square...).However, one can use the following simple estimate: Cap Γ ⩽ R where R > 0 is the radius of any circle that enclosed Γ .The successful implementation of a BEM is therefore guaranteed by the verification of the criterion: κ > R.This criterion is elementary and applies to a wide range of problems.To our knowledge, no such simple criterion is available for the biharmonic single-layer potential.

In this paper we consider Jordan curves of class C 1,1 in the plane (see the last section for possible generalizations).We denote by Γ a disjoint union of a finite number of such curves.We define Ω − Γ the bounded domain consisting of the points enclosed by at least one curve and Ω + Γ its unbounded complement (see Fig. 1).The multi-connected curve Γ can be decomposed into Γ e , the boundary shared by Ω + Γ and Ω − Γ , and the Jordan curves included in Ω − Γ .On every Jordan curve, we define n the unit normal vector field directed towards the bounded domain enclosed by the curve (and we will stick to this convention throughout the paper).For every function u ∈ H 2 ℓoc (R 2 ), we can define the Dirichlet and Neumann traces on Γ denoted respectively by γ D Γ u and γ N Γ u (the Neumann trace is defined taking into account the orientation of n).The total trace operator is next given by:
γ Γ : H 2 ℓoc (R 2 ) −→ H 3/2 (Γ ) × H 1/2 (Γ ) u −→ γ D Γ u, γ N Γ u .
We will use the following expression for the fundamental solution of the Bilaplacian:
G 0 (x) = 1 8π |x| 2 ln |x| κ 0 + κ 1 for all x ∈ R 2 ,(1)
where the parameters (κ 0 , κ 1 ) ∈ ]0, +∞[×R are introduced to cover all the classical definitions available in the literature (see for instance [2,3,11]).We denote
H(Γ ) = H 3/2 (Γ ) × H 1/2 (Γ ) and H ′ (Γ ) = H −3/2 (Γ ) × H −1/2 (Γ ).
Using the usual abuse of notation to identify G 0 (x − y) with a two-variables function G 0 (x, y), the biharmonic single-layer potential is defined for every q = (q 0 , q 1 ) ∈ H ′ (Γ ) by: S Γ q(x) = ˆΓ G 0 (x, y)q 0 (y) + ∂ n(y) G 0 (x, y)q 1 (y) ds(y) for all x ∈ R 2 .

Our goal is to determine conditions that ensure the invertibility of V Γ .Even in the simplest case where Γ reduces to a single Jordan curve, the answer is not clear in general.Indeed, for any fixed parameters κ 0 , κ 1 (in identity (1)), it is known that there exist degenerate scales ρ > 0 for which V ρΓ is not invertible.Since the pioneering work [5], studies on degenerate scales (and more generally on the invertibility of V Γ ) have been reduced to questions of invertibility of a 4 × 4 matrix (known as the discriminant matrix).In this paper, we show that this matrix can be replaced by a simpler one, a 3 × 3 matrix called the Robin matrix (because of the similarity of its role to that of the Robin constant in potential theory).Recalling that the operator V Γ (from H ′ (Γ ) into itself) is self-adjoint, our main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.1.

1.The invertibility of V Γ depends on Γ e only.

2. To any multi-connected curve Γ (as described above) we can associate a 3 × 3 symmetric matrix Λ Γe (the Robin matrix).The operator V Γ is an isomorphism if and only if det Λ Γe ̸ = 0.

3. Let R > 0 be the radius of a circle C R such that Ω + C R ⊂ Ω + Γ (i.e. the circle C R enclosed Γ ).If κ 0 > eR and κ 1 > (κ 0 /e) 2 in the definition (1), then the matrix Λ Γe is positive definite and the operator V Γ is strongly elliptic on H ′ (Γ ).

Points 3 and 4 of Theorems 1.1 provide criteria (similar to those for the harmonic single-layer potential) ensuring the successful implementation of a BEM.These criteria are simple and apply to most of the problems that can be encountered.In particular, we emphasize that they apply to the case where Γ e is multi-connected, this case being the blind spot of most publications on the subject.Some authors focus on the particular case where κ 0 = 1 and κ 1 = 0.The most advanced results concerning degenerate scales in this case can be found in the recent paper [4] (to which we refer also for a detailed overview of the known results on this topic).The authors look for sufficient conditions to prevent the appearance of degenerate scales.They prove that when Γ is a single Jordan curve such that the domain Ω − Γ is star-shaped, or has symmetry properties, V Γ is invertible provided that either a circle of radius 1/e is included in
Ω − Γ or that Ω −
Γ is included in such a circle.They claim that when Γ e is not connected, no general conclusion can be drawn.Points 3, 4 and 5 of Theorem 1.1 above extend their results to the general case considered in this article, and thus removes their geometric restrictions.In the same paper [4], it is showed that when Γ e is a single Jordan curve, "holes" have no influence on the degenerate scales.The first assertion of Theorem 1.1 also extends this result, in particular to the case where Γ e is multi-connected.

The proof of Theorem 1.1 results straightforwardly from the combination of Theorems 4.1, 4.2, 5.1 and Property 5.1 established below.

Generalization

The C 1,1 regularity of Γ is the weakest for which the total trace operator from H 2 ℓoc (R 2 ) into H 3/2 (Γ ) × H 1/2 (Γ ) is well defined and onto.With weaker regularity, H 3/2 (Γ ) has no more intrinsic definition and the space H(Γ ) must be defined simply as the image of H 2 ℓoc (R 2 ) by the total trace operator.In general, this image is difficult to characterize (see [8,9]).Nevertheless, some cases are dealt with in the literature: curvilinear C 1,1 polygons in [7] and Lipschitz continuous curves in [6].For C 1,1 curvilinear polygons, a generalization of the work done in this document seems well within reach.

Another possible generalization would be to adopt the approach of [5], in which Γ is any compact set in R 2 .In this case, the spaces H(Γ ) and H ′ (Γ ) would be replaced by the spaces denoted respectively by H 2 γ (Γ ) and H −2 Γ in [5].The space W 2 (R 2 ) would obviously remain unchanged and W 2 Γ (R 2 ) would be defined as the closure of D(R 2 \ Γ ) in W 2 (R 2 ).With these settings, the domain Ω + Γ is the unbounded connected component of R 2 \ Γ and Γ e is the boundary of Ω + Γ .The details of the analysis still need to be verified, and this will be done in a future work.
