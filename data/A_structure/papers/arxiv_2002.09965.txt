Brownian flights over a circle
23 Feb 2020
The stationary radial distribution, P (ρ), of the random walk with the diffusion coefficient D, which winds with the tangential velocity V around the impenetrable disc of radius R for R ≫ 1 converges to the Airy distribution. Typical trajectories are localized in the circular strip [R, R + δR 1/3 ], where δ is the constant which depends on the parameters D and V and is independent on R.

Typical trajectories are localized in the circular strip [R, R + δR 1/3 ], where δ is the constant which depends on the parameters D and V and is independent on R.


I. INTRODUCTION

We study the behavior of the Brownian motion B in R 2 , evading a certain region. The questions of similar nature have been already considered in the literature. A notable example is the P. Ferrari and H. Spohn paper [2], where they discussed the limiting behavior of the (1+1)-dimensional Brownian bridge constrained to stay above the semicircular obstacle D R of the radius R. It has been found in [2] that in the limit R → ∞ the constrained Brownian trajectory converges to a certain diffusion process, which stays at a typical distance ∼ R 1/3 above D R and after rescaling by R 1/3 has a stationary distribution expressed in terms of the Airy function. In the later paper ( [4]) such a diffusion process (living in the halfspace R 2 + ) was named "Ferrari-Spohn diffusion". Recently in [5] we studied a related problem, in which the Brownian bridge is replaced by the true 2D Brownian motion, avoiding a disk (or a triangle) in R 2 . The following question was the subject of the work [5]: could a twodimensional random path pushed by some constraints to an improbable "large deviation regime", possess extreme statistics with one-dimensional Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) fluctuations? It has been shown that the answer is positive, though non-universal, since the fluctuations depend on the underlying geometry. We considered in details two examples of 2D systems: the semicircle and the triangle, for which imposed external constraints force the underlying stationary stochastic process to stay in an atypical regime with anomalous statistics.

In the paper [3] the question of the behavior of a planar Brownian loop capturing a large area A is studied. In particular, it has been established that a typical loop with a fixed area avoids a disc of area ∼ A, as A → ∞.

In the present paper we study a behavior of the Brownian motion B in R 2 which cannot penetrate inside a disc of radius R, winding around it with a given linear (tangential) velocity. We look at that problem from the viewpoint of the grand canonical ensemble. Namely, first of all, we find the drift field v (the tilt ) under which the (unconstrained) Brownian particle behaves typically as the atypical Brownian particle turning around the disc D R ; then, we find the stationary distribution of such a particle. Again, we find that the corresponding distribution, scaled by R 1/3 , is expressed via the square of the Airy function (as R → ∞) -see Eq.(37), which is the main result of our paper.


II. STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM

We study large deviation problem for a biased two-dimensional Brownian motion B on a plane in a radial geometry. We are looking for the rotation-invariant drift field v, which will impose the following constraints: i) the Brownian motion always stays outside the disk D R of radius R and ii) the Brownian motion winds around the disc with the mean angular velocity ω = V 2πR where the mean linear velocity V is fixed. The requested drift field v should produce the same behavior as that of the Brownian particle constrained to stay away from D R and making a full turn around D R during typical time 2πR V . In other words, we are looking for the angular tilt which would produce the constrained behavior as its typical one. Schematically, the considered system is shown on Fig. 1.

Our goal is to find the stationary distribution P (ρ, φ) of this process, where by ρ, φ we denote the polar coordinates on the plane. Clearly, P (ρ, φ) = P (ρ) , and the rotation-invariant drift field v is specified by its restriction to one ray.

The time-dependent distribution P (ρ, φ, t) of the Brownian motion in the rotation-invariant drift field v satisfies the nonstationary Fokker-Planck (FP) equation, which in polar coordinates can be written as follows:
∂P ∂t = D 1 ρ ∂ ∂ρ ρ ∂P ∂ρ + 1 ρ 2 ∂ 2 P ∂φ 2 − 1 ρ ∂ ∂ρ (ρv ρ P ) − 1 ρ ∂ ∂φ (v φ P )(1)
where v φ , v ρ are polar coordinates of the vector v(φ, ρ) and D is the diffusion coefficient.

Suppose the drift field v is such that the corresponding diffusion P (ρ, φ, t) stays away from the disc of radius R, is ergodic with a stationary distribution P (ρ, φ) = P (ρ) and the prescribed mean linear velocity V . Denote by Ω(R, V ) the set of all corresponding drift fields v. According to the Schilder theorem, the large deviation rate function for the Brownian motion is the square of the velocity, see [1,6]. Hence the drift field v ∈ Ω(R, V ) we are looking for, is the one which minimizes the action S (the "entropy" of ensemble of Brownian trajectories)
S(v) = ∞ R v 2 (ρ, φ)P v (ρ, φ) ρdρ dφ(2)
over Ω(R, V ).

First we get the closed expression for the action S in terms of P (ρ) only, expressing v ρ and v φ as functionals of P (ρ) by solving separately two auxiliary problems. Then we study the limiting behavior of the stationary distribution P (ρ, φ) as R tends to the infinity, and we see the emergence of the Airy distribution in that limit.

Our first remark is that if v is rotation-invariant and P is a stationary rotation-invariant solution of the equation (1), then the radial velocity v ρ (ρ) is defined by P. Indeed, the distribution P and velocity v φ do not depend on t and φ, so the Fokker-Planck (FP) equation (1) boils down to

IV. CONCLUSION

We have shown in the paper that the stationary radial distribution, P (ρ) of the random walk which winds with the tangential velocity V around the impenetrable disc of radius R for R ≫ 1 converges to the Airy distribution given by the expression
P (ρ) = V 2/3 2 4/3 π Ai ′ (a 1 ) 2 D 2/3 R 1/3 ρ Ai 2 V 2 2D 2 1/3 ρ − R R 1/3 + a 1 (ρ ≥ R),(37)
where a 1 ≈ −2.33811 is the first zero of the Airy function. Typical trajectories are localized in the circular strip [R, R + δR 1/3 ] where δ is the constant which depends on the parameters D and V and is independent on R.

It should be emphasized, that the presence of the impenetrable disc which restricts Brownian flights is crucial for the asymptotic behavior (37) and for the localization of trajectories within the strip of size R 1/3 . It seems instructive to compare two models which look pretty similar. The first model represents the "stretched" random walk of N = cR steps evading a disk of radius R and in main respects repeats the problem considered in our paper (see also [5] (definitely, in the lattice version on the square lattice c > 4). The second model discussed in [3], studies the deviations from the typical "Wulf shape" of strongly inflated random loop of N steps enclosing the algebraic area A = cN 2 . At N ≫ 1 the fluctuations in the first model scale as N 1/3 , while remain Gaussian with the typical width N 1/2 in the second model.
