Synopsis
This document sets out requirements
for the provision of information about
access to and alongside the railway. It
specifies where operational safety
signs for personnel on or near the
lineside are required.

Part 1
Purpose and Introduction
1.1
Purpose
1.1.1
This document is a standard on the provision of information and signs for access on
the railway, for members of RSSB to use if they so choose.
1.1.2
This document sets out the industry's agreed process for the conveyance of safety
and warning information on the Great Britain (GB) mainline railway.
1.2
Application of this document
1.2.1
Compliance requirements and dates have not been specified because these are the
subject of internal procedures or contract conditions.
1.2.2
If you plan to do something that does not comply with a requirement in this RIS, you
can ask a Standards Committee to comment on your proposed alternative. If you
want a Standards Committee to do this, please submit your deviation application
form to RSSB. You can find further advice in the ‘Guidance to applicants and
members of Standards Committee on using alternative requirements’, available from
RSSB’s website www.rssb.co.uk.
1.3
Health and safety responsibilities
1.3.1
Users of documents published by RSSB are reminded of the need to consider their
own responsibilities to ensure health and safety at work and their own duties under
health and safety legislation. RSSB does not warrant that compliance with all or any
documents published by RSSB is sufficient in itself to ensure safe systems of work or
operation or to satisfy such responsibilities or duties.
1.4
Structure of this document
1.4.1
This document sets out a series of requirements that are sequentially numbered. This
document also sets out the rationale for the requirement, explaining why the
requirement is needed and its purpose and, where relevant, guidance to support the
requirement. The rationale and the guidance are prefixed by the letter ‘G’.
1.4.2
Some subjects do not have specific requirements but the subject is addressed through
guidance only and, where this is the case, it is distinguished under a heading of
‘Guidance’ and is prefixed by the letter ‘G’.
1.5
Approval and Authorisation
1.5.1
The content of this document was approved by Traffic Operation and Management
Standards Committee on 03 July 2018.
1.5.2
This document was authorised by RSSB on 27 July 2018.

Part 2
Requirements for Provision of Information and Signs for
Access on the Railway
2.1
Responsibilities of infrastructure managers
2.1.1
Provision of information about access on the railway – authorised access points
2.1.1.1
Infrastructure managers (IMs) shall make information on authorised access points
available to transport operators (TOs).
Rationale
G 2.1.1.2
Authorised access points are approved locations at which people with a legitimate
reason to access the railway may do so.
G 2.1.1.3
The IM is responsible for managing the infrastructure, which includes the provision
and maintenance of these authorised access points. As some access points will be
used by staff other than the IM's employees, information relating to them needs to
be shared. This enables details of authorised access points to be incorporated into a
TO's safety management system (where appropriate).
Guidance
G 2.1.1.4
On the GB mainline railway, this requirement is met by publication of authorised
access points in the Hazard Directory.
G 2.1.1.5
Persons with a legitimate reason to access the railway may include, but are not
limited to:
a) IM and TO staff.
b) Contractors.
c) Emergency services personnel.
d) Utilities staff.
2.1.2
Provision of information about access on the railway – authorised walking routes
2.1.2.1
IMs shall make information on authorised walking routes available to TOs.
Rationale
G 2.1.2.2
Authorised walking routes allow people who do not hold a qualification in personal
track safety to access places of work.
G 2.1.2.3
Because authorised walking routes are designated routes between locations,
information regarding these routes is of use to people who are qualified in personal
track safety.
G 2.1.2.4
The provision of information on authorised walking routes by IMs ensures all
personnel concerned understand the location, limits and requirements for the safe
use of these routes. This enables staff who are not competent in personal track safety
to make sure they do not deviate from the authorised route. It also makes clear the
boundaries of responsibility for maintenance of these routes.

Guidance
G 2.1.2.5
On the GB mainline railway, this requirement is met by publication of authorised
walking routes in the Hazard Directory.
G 2.1.2.6
Authorised walking routes are designated routes that provide pedestrian access to
and from places of work that may only be accessible via IM property. These locations
may include, but are not limited to:
a) Depots.
b) Train crew booking-on points.
c) Signal boxes.
d) Locations where trains are stabled.
2.1.3
Provision of information signs – requirements for signs in tunnels
2.1.3.1
IMs shall, in tunnels of 100 m or more in length, provide signs at regular intervals to
indicate:
a) The distance to the nearest exit from the tunnel.
b) The direction in which the nearest exit may be found.
Rationale
G 2.1.3.2
Some emergency situations, such as a fire on a train in a tunnel, may require
passengers and staff to leave the tunnel as quickly as possible. Signs detailing the
location and direction of the nearest exit from a tunnel are a control measure for this
hazard as they allow rail staff and others to locate exits quickly and leave the tunnel
by the shortest possible route.
G 2.1.3.3
Readily-accessible information detailing the exits from a tunnel is important to rail
staff such as train crew placing emergency protection or conducting the emergency
evacuation of a train. These staff may have limited knowledge of the local area and
the location of the nearest exit.
Guidance
G 2.1.3.4
In addition to a tunnel portal, the nearest exit may also be a cross-passage to another
tunnel bore.
G 2.1.3.5
Section 2.4(a) of the Safety in Railway Tunnels Technical Specification for
Interoperability (SRT TSI) defines tunnels as being 100 m in length or longer.
G 2.1.3.6
Section 4.2.1.5.5 of the SRT TSI requires that the maximum distance between escape
signs shall be 50 m.
G 2.1.3.7
GIGN7634 references the design specification for ‘text’ signs (sign BB01), which
provide the direction and distance (in metres) to the nearest exit from a tunnel.
Displaying signs that meet this specification and are consistent across the network
helps rail staff to recognise and understand them easily.

2.1.4
Provision of warning signs – general requirements for warning signs at
obstructions
2.1.4.1
IMs shall:
a) Provide warning signs at obstructions, as required in 2.1.5, 2.1.6 and 2.1.7. These
signs shall be posted at each end of the obstruction and at any intermediate
authorised access points to the running lines, including the ends of platforms.
They shall be orientated to face people walking along the infrastructure towards
the obstruction.
b) Provide only one of the types of warning sign described in 2.1.5, 2.1.6 and 2.1.7 at
any one location.
c) Where appropriate, provide additional signs at intervals of not more than 40 m
along the obstruction. These shall be orientated to face the running line.
d) Position the warning signs at eye level above the walkway wherever practicable.
e) Not provide warning signs where the obstruction is at a station platform.
Rationale
G 2.1.4.2
The purpose of warning signs is to indicate the presence of an obstacle that obstructs
a position of safety at track level.
G 2.1.4.3
Warning signs are made as visually conspicuous as possible by:
a) Positioning them at eye level on each end of an obstruction.
b) Orientating them so that they face people walking towards the obstruction.
G 2.1.4.4
The meanings of the warning signs described in 2.1.5, 2.1.6 and 2.1.7 are different:
only one of these signs will apply at any given location.
G 2.1.4.5
There are no positions of safety at track level throughout the length of any station
platform. Warning signs are not required at these locations because rail staff are
trained that there is no safe access to the track within any station platform while
trains are running.
G 2.1.4.6
Other structures, such as bridge parapets or retaining walls, may or may not interrupt
a position of safety. The provision of warning signs at these locations shows whether
the position of safety is affected and allows rail staff to make appropriate
arrangements to protect themselves from trains.
Guidance
G 2.1.4.7
There is no guidance associated with this requirement.
2.1.5
Provision of warning signs – ‘Limited Clearance’
2.1.5.1
IMs shall provide ‘Limited Clearance’ signs where a position of safety is interrupted
for a distance of more than 2 m, and the criteria for ‘No Refuges’ or ‘Prohibition’
warning signs in 2.1.6 and 2.1.7 are not applicable.

Rationale
G 2.1.5.2
‘Limited Clearance’ signs warn staff that the position of safety is interrupted by an
obstruction and that there is no safe access to the designated area whilst trains are
running.
G 2.1.5.3
The consistent provision of ‘Limited Clearance’ signs is important to rail personnel
such as train crew carrying out emergency protection or evacuating trains, where
train movements may be continuing and there may be restricted local familiarity with
limited clearances.
Guidance
G 2.1.5.4
GIGN7634 references the design specification for ‘Limited Clearance’ signs (sign
BA04). Displaying signs that meet this specification and are consistent across the
network ensures rail staff will recognise and understand them.
2.1.6
Provision of warning signs – ‘No Refuges’
2.1.6.1
IMs shall provide ‘No Refuges’ warning signs at locations meeting the following
criteria:
a) Where positions of safety or refuges are not provided at least every 20 m along
the side of the running line in question.
b) Where positions of safety or refuges are provided at least every 20 m along the
opposite side of the running lines.
c) Where the criteria for ‘Prohibition’ warning signs in 2.1.7 are not applicable.
Rationale
G 2.1.6.2
‘No Refuges’ signs warn staff that the position of safety is interrupted by an
obstruction on one side of the running line, and therefore there is no safe access to
the designated area on that side of the line whilst trains are running. These signs
positively confirm that a position of safety is available on the opposite side of the
line.
G 2.1.6.3
The consistent provision of ‘No Refuges’ signs is important to rail personnel such as
train crew carrying out emergency protection or evacuating trains, where train
movements may be continuing and there may be restricted local familiarity with the
availability of refuges.
Guidance
G 2.1.6.4
GIGN7634 references the design specification for ‘No Refuges’ signs (sign BA05).
Displaying signs that meet this specification and are consistent across the network
ensures rail staff will recognise and understand them.
2.1.7
Provision of warning signs – ‘Prohibition’
2.1.7.1
IMs shall provide ‘Prohibition’ warning signs at the following locations:
a) Where there is not adequate time to reach a position of safety or refuge because
of sighting distances or other reasons.

Definitions
Authorised access point
A designated point where authorised people may gain access to
the railway.
Authorised walking route
A designated walking route providing pedestrian access to and
egress from places of work (including booking-on points and
stabling points) and which is suitable for use by people not
certficated as competent in personal track safety.
GB mainline railway
Mainline railway has the meaning given to it in the Railways and
Other Guided Transport Systems (Safety) Regulations 2006 (as
amended) and the associated exclusions. GB Mainline Railway is
the mainline railway network excluding any railway in Northern
Ireland, the Channel Tunnel, the dedicated high speed railway
between London St Pancras International Station and the Channel
Tunnel, and any other exclusions determined by the member state.
Permissible Speed
The authorised maximum speed over a section of line, either for all
trains or (where differential or enhanced permissible speeds are
applied) for specific types of trains, as set out in the Sectional
Appendix.
Position of safety
A place where people may stand safely while trains pass, as set out
in GERT8000.
Refuge
A position of safety provided in an area of limited clearance,
normally a specially constructed recess.
Tunnel
An excavation or a construction around the track provided to allow
the railway to pass for example higher land, buildings or water. The
length of a tunnel is defined as the length of the fully enclosed
section, measured at rail level. A tunnel in the context of this TSI is
0.1 km or longer. Source: SRT TSI
